## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Forest without Trees is still Fruitful: Constraints on the thermal state of the neutral IGM at z≈5.6 with the 21-cm forest power spectrum](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15341v1)

Tomáš Šoltinský, Arnab Chakraborty, Girish Kulkarni et al. (incl. Matteo Viel, an interest-file high-value co-author)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This is the first application of a 21-cm forest 1D power-spectrum inference framework to real observational data: 17.5 hr of archival uGMRT Band-2 observations of the brightest known z>5.5 radio-loud quasar J352−15, reaching σ_N = 3.62 mJy/beam per 6.1 kHz channel over 5.4 ≤ z ≤ 5.82. They measure the 1D power spectrum of the residual quasar spectrum, find no statistically significant 21-cm forest detection (KS p = 0.353 against off-source sightlines), and then run Bayesian inference under the null-detection to jointly constrain the mean neutral fraction ⟨x_HI⟩ and mean temperature of the neutral IGM ⟨T_HI⟩. The forward model uses 21cmFAST across a wide grid of ionization and X-ray pre-heating scenarios. At ⟨x_HI⟩ = 0.1 they disfavour ⟨T_HI⟩ < 26.9 K, and more generally exclude the coldest, most-neutral IGM models — implying substantial pre-heating of even the neutral IGM above the adiabatic cooling floor.

This is a direct hit on the IGM/reionization core of the interest file (§2): it constrains the thermal and ionization state of the end-of-reionization neutral IGM using a 1D power-spectrum estimator and Bayesian forward-modelling machinery — the exact methodological toolkit used for the Lyα forest P1D — and it is explicitly complementary to Lyα-based x_HI constraints, which only indirectly probe the neutral regions.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Standard Reconstruction Shifts the Optimal Input Scale for CNN-Based Density-Field Reconstruction](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15850v1)

Koichiro Nakashima, Kiyotomo Ichiki, Atsushi J. Nishizawa
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using dark-matter-only N-body simulations, the authors study CNN reconstruction of the high-redshift (initial) density field from the evolved late-time field, and ask how the optimal physical scale of the CNN input sub-box changes when standard first-order (perturbative) reconstruction is applied first. Without pre-processing, the CNN performs best on inputs of L_sub ~ 150–200 h⁻¹Mpc; after standard reconstruction the preferred scale shifts down to ~38–114 h⁻¹Mpc, and the CNN-after-standard-reconstruction pipeline beats both single- and dual-input CNNs across loss, PDF, KL divergence, residual field, and Fourier-space correlation. The interpretation is a clean physical division of labour: perturbative reconstruction efficiently recovers coherent large-scale displacements, while the CNN handles residual quasi-linear/non-linear small-scale evolution.

Relevant to the ML-for-cosmology methods interest (§3) and BAO reconstruction (§4): it is a concrete recipe for hybridizing analytic and data-driven reconstruction with a physically motivated scale separation — directly transferable to the density/initial-condition reconstruction problems the interest file flags (e.g. field-level and stochastic-interpolant reconstruction).

### [Searching for Folded Primordial Non-Gaussianity with Galaxy Surveys](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15337v1)

Si-Xiang Yang, Oliver H. E. Philcox (Philcox — LSS summary-statistics/field-level inference, an interest-file high-value author)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

The paper asks whether galaxy surveys can constrain *folded* primordial non-Gaussianity (PNG) — a shape enhanced near k₁+k₂−k₃ → 0 that arises in excited-initial-state, imaginary-sound-speed, and dissipative-inflation models — in addition to the usual local/equilateral/orthogonal shapes. They build a numerical pipeline within the EFTofLSS to compute folded-PNG contributions to the redshift-space galaxy power-spectrum multipoles and bispectrum monopole, and Fisher-forecast the constraints. Most of the constraining power comes from the bispectrum; for cutoff-regulated models, nuisance marginalization costs little but finite Fourier-space binning degrades constraints once the folded region is narrower than the bin width, while dissipation-regulated models have exponentially narrow enhancements that are hard to resolve.

Relevant to the LSS inference-methods interest (§4) and the EFT machinery the interest file tracks (the EFT of the Lyα forest is a live topic): it extends the EFTofLSS bispectrum program to a new PNG shape and provides a general, released pipeline for non-separable primordial bispectra.

*No figures extracted for 2607.15337 (a Fisher-forecast methods paper; the key figures are forecast contours that add little beyond the text).*

### [Constraints on the remote quadrupole field from the polarized Sunyaev Zel'dovich effect](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16071v1)

Jordan Krywonos, Matthew C. Johnson, Elena Pierpaoli et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The polarized SZ (pSZ) effect is a CMB polarization anisotropy induced by Thomson scattering of the remote CMB quadrupole off free electrons in non-linear structure. The authors show that a CMB-CMB-galaxy bispectrum compactly summarizes existing pSZ statistics, identify z ~ 1–2 as the optimal galaxy sample for detection, and evaluate the estimator on Planck+ACT CMB, unWISE galaxies, and Planck CIB maps. They do not make a significant detection (consistent with the expected O(1) S/N from this data combination), but place constraints on the optical-depth bias b_q = 1.02 ± 2.64, the optical depth to reionization τ_rei = −0.01 ± 0.14, and the tensor-to-scalar ratio; they forecast a factor ~3 improvement with future data, enough to independently check the low CMB quadrupole and τ_rei.

Relevant to the reionization (§2) and LSS cross-correlation (§4, expansion candidates) interests: the pSZ effect offers an independent handle on the optical depth to reionization and on large-scale-structure–CMB cross-correlations, and the CMB-CMB-galaxy bispectrum is a methodologically clean way to fold multiple pSZ statistics together.

*No figures extracted for 2607.16071 (a non-detection; the paper's main figures are a pSZ schematic and template maps rather than a headline result plot).*

### [Large deviations for halos and voids: beyond perturbative non-gaussianities](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16152v1)

Martin Teuscher, Ruth Durrer, Julien Grain et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

The authors extend the excursion-set formalism for halo and void abundances to primordial density fields with strongly non-Gaussian *tails* — the regime beyond the reach of perturbative moment-expansion treatments of PNG. Using the large deviation principle (suited to rare fluctuations), they derive new first-passage-time distributions for non-Gaussian random walks, yielding updated halo mass functions and — via the two-barrier problem — a new analytical void size function with improved large-scale accuracy. This provides an analytic bridge between inflationary models that generically produce non-Gaussian tails and late-Universe abundance observables.

Relevant to the void cosmology and LSS-methods interests (§4): the void size function is a well-developed secondary probe in the library, and an analytic, non-perturbative prediction that connects it to primordial physics is directly useful for void-based dark-energy/PNG constraints.

*No figures extracted for 2607.16152 (an analytic derivation; the void-size-function figures are technical first-passage-time comparisons that need the equations to read).*

### [Morphological Bias: How Ellipticals and Spirals Trace the Cosmic Web Differently](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15573v1)

Paula S. Ferreira, Carlos A. P. Bengaly, Renyue Cen
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

Using angular auto- and cross-power spectra from DES and DESI Legacy Imaging DR8, the authors measure galaxy bias and scale-dependent *relative* bias for morphologically-selected ellipticals versus spirals. They introduce the cross-tracer clustering ratio (CTCR = auto/cross power ratio) to isolate relative clustering as a function of multipole ℓ. Ellipticals are more strongly clustered than spirals; the relative bias is near unity on large scales (ℓ ≲ 50) but rises toward small scales, reaching a 2.6σ average separation at ℓ ~ 150–200. A complementary linear-bias analysis finds ellipticals positively biased relative to the matter field while spirals are consistent with weak bias or anti-bias, robust across two covariance estimators, contamination tests, luminosity splits, and two N-body mocks.

Relevant to the LSS/spectroscopic-survey interest (§4): galaxy bias and its scale dependence are central systematics for clustering cosmology, and this empirically ties morphology to both the amplitude and scale-dependence of bias — useful input for halo-occupation and assembly-bias modelling with imaging surveys.

### [Cosmological Evidence for Dark Axion-Dark Baryon Interactions from Apparent Phantom Crossing](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16191v1)

Justin Khoury, Meng-Xiang Lin, Mark Trodden
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

Following up on their earlier proposal, the authors implement a strongly-coupled dark sector (a dark axion coupled to dark baryons) in a Boltzmann code and confront it with CMB + DESI DR2 BAO + SNe Ia. The interaction produces an *apparent* phantom-crossing in the effective dark-energy equation of state (w_eff crosses −1 near z ≈ 0.3, reaching ≈ −0.7 today; a CPL fit gives w₀ = −0.87, w_a = −0.36), mimicking the DESI-preferred w₀–w_a expansion history without a genuinely phantom fluid. For CMB+DESI DR2+DES-Dovekie the best fit improves on ΛCDM by Δχ² = −14.48, driven by a non-monotonic dark-matter mass evolution; the same dynamics injects a small Early-Dark-Energy-like component near equality, but too little to meaningfully ease the H₀ tension.

Relevant to the DESI-BAO / dynamical-dark-energy secondary focus (§4): the DESI DR2 phantom-crossing signal is one of the newest additions to the interest file, and this is a concrete particle-physics mechanism that reproduces it from dark-sector interactions rather than a phenomenological w(z).

### [Tighter Dark Matter Constraints from the Projected Mass Method: A Neural Network Enhanced Method for Galaxy Groups and Clusters](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15339v1)

Yinbo Huang, David Benisty
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The classical Projected Mass Estimator (PME) for group/cluster masses is biased because it assumes satellite orbital geometries that rarely hold and is noisy when only a handful of tracers exist. The authors train residual neural networks on thousands of IllustrisTNG groups — separate networks per satellite-sample size (5 to 50 tracers) — to correct the PME. This cuts the bias from M_proj/M_true ≈ 1.30–1.46 (RMSE 0.29–0.32 dex) to ≈ 1.02 (RMSE 0.13 dex). Applied to the Milky Way it yields M_MW = 1.14⁺⁰·⁴⁰₋₀.₃₀ × 10¹² M_⊙, with the brightest-satellite subsets favouring a somewhat lower range.

Relevant to the ML-for-cosmology (§3) and small-scale-DM (§5) interests: it is a clean example of using a simulation-trained network to debias a classical dynamical estimator while explicitly conditioning on tracer count — a transferable pattern for low-tracer inference problems, and directly relevant to halo-mass measurements that anchor DM constraints.

*No figures extracted for 2607.15339 (the ML correction is well conveyed by the numbers in the summary; the figures are diagnostic scatter/importance plots).*

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Learning Standard Model structure from LHC data with Riemannian flow matching](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16144v1)

Midori Kato, Kevin A. Urquía-Calderón, Inar Timiryasov et al.
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: cs.LG

The authors build ShellFlow, a transformer-based Riemannian conditional flow-matching generative model that, given only the recorded event composition, generates each final-state particle on its on-shell manifold. Its sole physics priors are the on-shell condition and the invariant-mass formula. Trained on ~10⁹ real pp collision events from the ATLAS Open Data 13 TeV release — and told nothing else — a single model reproduces intra-particle kinematics, the J/ψ, Υ, and Z dilepton resonances at their PDG masses, the leptonic Weinberg angle, the W and top masses, and inter-particle correlations that enter no training objective, spanning five decades in invariant mass that no single Monte Carlo sample covers.

Notable as a striking demonstration that a substantial fraction of Standard Model structure is learnable directly from raw recorded data by a modern generative model — a genuine "foundation-model-for-physics" result. The methodology (Riemannian flow matching, a close cousin of the stochastic-interpolant/normalizing-flow machinery the interest file tracks in §3) is exactly the kind of generative-inference tool that could be repointed at spectroscopic or LSS data.
