## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [The local galaxy distribution does not violate the cosmological principle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01172)

Till Sawala
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

A recent analysis of DESI DR1 galaxy samples reported coherent anisotropic features in the local galaxy distribution extending to gigaparsec scales — a result that, if correct, would directly contradict the cosmological principle and motivate inhomogeneous cosmologies. Sawala re-analyses the same DESI data and compares them against galaxy distributions predicted by the FLAMINGO cosmological hydrodynamic simulation in standard ΛCDM. He shows that the apparent anomaly disappears once the correct comoving distance scale is applied, and that the observed structures are fully consistent with those expected in a ΛCDM universe. The claimed gigaparsec-scale anisotropy is therefore attributed to an analysis artifact rather than new physics.

Direct hit on the DESI large-scale-structure program that anchors the interest file (DESI is the top-priority data source): this is a targeted rebuttal of a would-be foundational anomaly in DESI DR1, exactly the kind of ΛCDM-vs-data adjudication that determines how one interprets DESI clustering and BAO measurements.

### [Intertwined Constraints in Extended Cosmologies: Dark Energy, Curvature, Neutrinos, and Inflation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01226)

William Giarè, Dong Ha Lee, Eleonora Di Valentino
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

A systematic reassessment of beyond-ΛCDM constraints using the latest CMB data together with DESI BAO and several SN catalogues, progressively relaxing assumptions on dark energy, curvature, neutrinos, and inflation. The preference for dynamical dark energy persists across every extended model considered, while curvature stays compatible with flatness (a mild 2.2σ hint for Ω_k>0 is degraded once dynamical DE is allowed). Crucially for forest science, the cosmological upper limit on the summed neutrino mass is shown to be strongly framework-dependent, ranging from ∑m_ν ≲ 0.06 eV to ≲ 0.2 eV depending on the DE/curvature assumptions, and the apparent tension with oscillation experiments shifts accordingly. None of the extensions resolves the H₀ tension; dynamical DE remains the only significant deviation from ΛCDM.

Direct hit on two interest-file threads simultaneously: DESI BAO cosmology (secondary-primary focus #4) and the neutrino-mass constraint that the Lyman-α forest also targets (§1). The headline lesson — that ∑m_ν bounds are not robust to the assumed background model — is exactly the caveat that matters when comparing forest-based and BAO-based neutrino limits.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Foreground Characterization and Mitigation in the Observations of the CD/EoR with the SKA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01048)

Jacob Burba, Philip Bull, Emilio Ceccotti et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

A review chapter on the central obstacle to 21-cm cosmology: Galactic synchrotron, free–free, and extragalactic foregrounds that exceed the redshifted 21-cm signal from the neutral IGM by 4–5 orders of magnitude. It surveys foreground modelling and removal for the SKA-Low AA* configuration, arguing that array design, wide field of view, and sub-0.01% calibration accuracy are what ultimately gate signal recovery. The chapter foregrounds the SKA Observatory Foreground Challenge — a community effort to develop and validate removal pipelines on realistic simulations — and reviews how statistical and machine-learning / simulation-based methods, tested against LOFAR/MWA/HERA experience, are being adapted for 21-cm signal extraction.

Relevant on two counts from the interest file: reionization/IGM is a primary focus (§2), and the paper's emphasis on simulation-based inference and ML pipelines for extracting a signal buried under huge systematics mirrors the inference toolkit (§3) the user wants to carry into forest/LSS analyses. Philip Bull is a recurring SKA/21-cm cosmology author.

### [TIME Commissioning Observations: II. On-sky Characterization and the 2D Map Data Processing Pipeline](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01220)

Benjamin J. Vaughan, Abigail T. Crites, Dongwoo T. Chung et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

The Tomographic Ionized-carbon Mapping Experiment (TIME) is a line-intensity-mapping (LIM) instrument designed to measure the [CII] 158 μm power spectrum during the Epoch of Reionization. This paper reports the 2022 commissioning run at the ARO 12-m, presenting on-sky observations of the Orion Molecular Cloud and the HII region G49.5 and a spectral-image pipeline that builds calibrated raster-scan maps using planet observations for gain calibration. Cross-checking G49.5 against the Bolocam Galactic Plane Survey, they achieve <3% calibration agreement, demonstrating preliminary on-sky performance and identifying the improvements needed for an actual LIM power-spectrum measurement.

Adjacent-but-useful: LIM of [CII] is a complementary probe of the same reionization epoch (§2) the forest constrains from z≳5, and the power-spectrum-estimation-under-foregrounds machinery overlaps with the estimator methodology the interest file cares about. A concrete look at how a young intensity-mapping experiment gets from raw scans to a calibrated map.

### [Tilting at the Turnover: Modeling the Faint-End of the UV Luminosity Function Behind Abell S1063 with JWST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01129)

Caio Moreira Goolsby, Christopher J. Conselice, Duncan Austin et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using ultra-deep JWST/NIRCam (GLIMPSE) plus HST/ACS imaging behind the lensing cluster Abell S1063, the authors push the rest-frame UV luminosity function to extremely faint, highly magnified galaxies over z≈6–11. Rather than imposing a hard faint-end cutoff, they model the turnover as a gradual quadratic suppression that still allows a suppressed-but-nonzero population beyond M_t. They find no direct evidence for a turnover down to M_UV=−13.5 at z=6, and infer that galaxies fainter than the conventional M_UV=−17 limit supply more than half the UV luminosity density and at least ~64% of the ionizing photons at z=6.

Relevant to the reionization/IGM primary focus (§2): the ionizing-photon budget of faint galaxies sets when and how patchily hydrogen reionizes, which directly controls the z≳5 Lyman-α forest opacity and the neutral-island signatures the interest file tracks. The turnover shape is the key uncertainty being folded into that budget.

### [Cosmology from HI galaxy surveys with the SKA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00827)

Ainulnabilah Nasirudin, Philip Bull, Ziad Sakr et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Forecasts for wide-field HI galaxy surveys with SKAO-MID, which will detect several million individually resolved HI-emitting galaxies in the late Universe and enable large-scale-structure measurements — baryon acoustic oscillations and redshift-space distortions — from a radio-selected sample. The chapter provides galaxy number-count-versus-redshift predictions and corresponding cosmological forecasts, bracketing the substantial uncertainty in the HI mass function with several modelling methods. It highlights radio-specific advantages: broader halo-mass sampling and Tully-Fisher luminosity distances not available to optical surveys.

Adjacent to the LSS/BAO secondary focus (§4): this is a forecast for a future BAO/RSD probe complementary to DESI, useful for thinking about how spectroscopic-survey cosmology extends into the radio and how HI number counts translate into BAO constraining power.

### [Variability in Supermassive Black-Hole Accretion Rates in Fuzzy Dark Matter Cores due to Black-Hole Wandering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00349)

Eric Ludwig, Philip Mocz, Victor Robles
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

A test of the proposal that fuzzy dark matter (FDM) soliton cores deepen nuclear potentials enough to boost Bondi accretion and help SMBHs grow rapidly at high redshift. Using live Schrödinger–Poisson FDM cores coupled to isothermal gas with a moving, mass-conserving black-hole sink, they measure accretion boosts as a function of seed mass. Low-mass seeds (<10⁶ M_⊙) sustain no large boost because BH wandering and soliton sloshing drive bursty, intermittent fueling; intermediate seeds (~10⁷ M_⊙) reach durable boosts of order 100; high-mass seeds (~10⁸ M_⊙) quickly become supply-limited. The conclusion is that soliton-enhanced accretion alone is unlikely to explain early SMBH growth.

On-topic for the small-scale dark-matter thread (§5): fuzzy/ultralight-axion DM is explicitly in the library (Rogers & Peiris-style constraints), and this is a direct dynamical study of soliton-core phenomenology — the same FDM physics that also imprints on the small-scale Lyman-α power spectrum, viewed here through black-hole fueling.

### [The age of the Universe from a large sample of the oldest Galactic stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00764)

Indranil Banik, Thenujaya Kudakolawa Kaluarachchige, Stephen Cookson et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

Using 247,103 LAMOST DR7 + Gaia eDR3 stars with high-resolution spectroscopy and YY isochrones (extended to 20 Gyr), the authors build a metal-poor, α-enriched old-star sample and apply an MCMC reconstruction of the latent age distribution to estimate the age of the oldest star, and hence a lower bound on the age of the Universe. They find A_⋆ = 13.73⁺⁰·¹⁸₋₀.₁₅ Gyr (varying quality cuts spans 13.31–14.02 Gyr), consistent with the 13.6 Gyr expected in CMB-calibrated ΛCDM. This agreement disfavors Hubble-tension solutions that inject new physics purely before recombination, which typically require a younger 12.9±0.2 Gyr universe.

Adjacent cosmological context (§4): an independent, low-redshift stellar-age constraint that bears directly on the H₀-tension model space — useful for situating DESI/BAO-based expansion-history results against a fully independent age probe.

### [Leveraging Multimodality for Real-Time Classification of Transients and Variables found by the Zwicky Transient Facility](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00228)

Ved G. Shah, Nabeel Rehemtulla, Adam A. Miller et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.HE, cs.LG

Building on the ORACLE hierarchical classifier, the ORACLE-2 models fuse light curves, tabular metadata, and images for real-time hierarchical classification of the ZTF alert stream. Adding modalities consistently improves early-time performance: ORACLE-2 Omni reaches macro F1 = 0.73 on ZTF's Bright Transient Survey (up to 40% over light-curve-only models), and a metadata+light-curve variant trained on the simulated ELAsTiCC dataset reaches F1 = 0.88 for LSST-scale volumes. The paper also quantifies the performance-versus-throughput trade-offs of multimodal inference on a real alert stream.

Relevant to the rising ML-for-astronomy focus (§3): multimodal fusion and hierarchical real-time inference on high-volume survey streams is the same class of methodology (foundation-model-adjacent, decision-under-latency) the interest file flags as increasingly central, transferable in spirit to spectroscopic-survey pipelines.

### [How can we finally see the first light? Status and perspective in the search for Population III stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00167)

Alessandra Venditti, Daniel Schaerer, Erik Zackrisson et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

A review of the theory and observational strategy for detecting the first (Population III) stars — the missing link in early star formation. It summarizes current expectations for the Pop III initial mass function, formation sites, and feedback, and the debated observability of their spectral signatures, then walks through the strategies now converging on candidates: JWST spectroscopy, time-domain and lensing searches, near-field stellar archaeology, and absorption-line studies, backed by improved simulations. The picture is a growing candidate list and a narrowing allowed parameter space, setting up a "golden era" of Pop III searches.

Adjacent context for the reionization/cosmic-dawn thread (§2, §7): the first stars set the initial ionizing sources and metal-enrichment topology that seed reionization and the early IGM, and the absorption-line/near-field techniques listed overlap directly with quasar-absorption methodology the user works in.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Prospects for Panspermia via Interstellar Objects like 3I/ATLAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00202)

Shokhruz Kakharov, Abraham Loeb
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP | also: astro-ph.GA

An order-of-magnitude feasibility study of natural and directed panspermia carried by interstellar objects (ISOs), anchored on the new interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS. It combines SPHEREx volatile/organic context (CO₂, H₂O, CO, a broad C–H feature) and the first JWST/MIRI CH₄ detection in an ISO with thermal, biological, and mission constraints. Natural panspermia is deemed plausible — microbes could survive at very low metabolic rates in ice films or veins — but matching the observed JWST CH₄ production by frozen survival metabolism would require ~10¹⁴–10¹⁵ kg of biomass, whereas active methanogens in warm liquid settings could do it with far less; directed panspermia is disfavored because a 60 km/s impact (1.8×10⁹ J/kg) would destroy any biological capsule. The authors frame 3I/ATLAS as a test case for panspermia diagnostics rather than evidence for life.

Off the research core, but a genuinely attention-worthy result: the first CH₄ detection in an interstellar object and an unusual volatile inventory are notable in their own right, and the panspermia framing (with Loeb) is the kind of high-visibility ISO science worth being aware of as a scientist.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Open Science in Astrophysics: Citation Benefits of Open Code, Open Data, and Open Access](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00546)

Parth Joshi, Rupert Croft
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: physics.soc-ph

An analysis of 53,194 peer-reviewed astrophysics papers (2021–2025) from NASA ADS, measuring eleven quantities per paper and isolating the citation contribution of openness via multivariate regression, partial correlations, and non-parametric tests. After controlling for author count, paper length, grants, code/data size, and date, all three forms of openness carry significant citation advantages: open data +32% (p<10⁻²⁴), open access +26% (p<10⁻⁶⁷), and open code +16% (p=0.003). The open-data advantage appears in all six sub-fields and is strongest in Galaxies+Cosmology and ISM, which also have the highest code/data sharing rates — reflecting their more developed community data infrastructure.

Meta-research on the practice of the field: a quantitative, incentive-level case that sharing code and data pays off in citations, directly relevant to how a data-and-pipeline-heavy cosmologist chooses to release forest/LSS analysis products. Notably co-authored by Rupert Croft, a foundational Lyman-α forest cosmologist.

*No figures extracted for 2607.00546 (bibliometric regression study; the result is a table of citation-advantage coefficients).*
