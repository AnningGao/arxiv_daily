## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Gravitational Lensing Predictions from Wave Simulations of Fuzzy Dark Matter](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20614)

Jiajun Zhou, Zhengxiang Li, Amruth Alfred et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

For the first time, the authors predict multiply-lensed image properties directly from 3D density fields obtained by evolving the Schrödinger–Poisson equations for fuzzy (wave) dark matter, rather than from the usual Gaussian-random-field (GRF) approximation to the interference pattern. Projecting the evolved halo along many axes gives the distribution of perturbations to lensed image positions. As an exacting test, particles of mass $10^{-22}$ eV reproduce the positions of the quadruply-lensed radio jets in HS 0810+2554 better than either the GRF approximation or an NFW best fit. The work argues that accurate FDM lensing predictions require full wave simulations, and that high-resolution lensed systems can serve as a direct probe of the nature of dark matter.

### [Hierarchical Bayesian inference with compositional score modeling for stellar streams](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20725)

Giuseppe Viterbo, Jonas Arruda, Tobias Buck
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors build an amortized simulation-based inference framework for the Milky Way potential from multiple stellar streams, using score-based diffusion neural posterior estimators trained on a library of simulated streams. The key methodological move is *compositional score modeling*: information from different streams (plus a circular-velocity constraint) is combined as a post-training step, so new streams or measurements can be folded in without re-running inference. Posteriors are shown to be well calibrated and accurate on held-out simulations, and combining streams tightens the global potential constraints relative to single-stream analyses. Applied to Gaia data, the model favors a mildly oblate dark-matter halo ($q_{\rm NFW}=0.77$, $a_{\rm NFW}=9.3$ kpc) with a local dark-matter density $\rho_{\odot}\simeq0.0115\,M_\odot\,{\rm pc}^{-3}$.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [A lower bound on primordial power spectrum from halo substructure](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20884)

Shin'ichiro Ando, Nagisa Hiroshima, Koji Ishiwata et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

The authors model a primordial curvature power spectrum that is nearly scale-invariant with a cutoff at $k=\mathcal{O}(1)\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ plus an additional log-normal bump at smaller scales, and compute the resulting host-halo evolution and subhalo mass function. Requiring consistency with observed satellite counts, gravitational lensing, and stellar-stream data sets a *lower* bound on the bump amplitude: a bump of amplitude $\gtrsim10^{-9}$ (comparable to the large-scale curvature amplitude) is allowed/required at wavenumbers $k\sim10$–$30\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$. The analysis inverts the usual small-scale-structure logic, turning subhalo observables into a constraint on the shape of the primordial spectrum at scales inaccessible to the CMB.

### [Probing the baryonic–dark matter connection in galaxy clusters using X-rays with gated recurrent unit neural networks](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20721)

Asif Iqbal, Subhabrata Majumdar, Weiguang Cui et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

A gated recurrent unit (GRU) network predicts full 3D cluster mass profiles from spherically-averaged intracluster-medium radial profiles, treating the ICM profile as a sequence so the model handles arbitrary radial sampling. Trained on The Three Hundred hydrodynamical simulations, it delivers unbiased masses with $\sim$5% $1\sigma$ scatter over most of the cluster, is robust to dynamical state and to observation-like radial binning, and generalizes when trained jointly on GIZMO-SIMBA and GADGET-X. SHAP attribution shows enclosed gas mass is the dominant predictor. Applied to REXCESS and X-COP, GRU masses agree with hydrostatic estimates for REXCESS but exceed them by up to $\sim$10% for the more massive X-COP sample.

### [A RELHIC twin candidate near the galaxy M51](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21034)

Qingze Chen, Jie Wang, Yingjie Jing et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using deep FAST observations, the authors find two compact H I clouds (Cloud S and Cloud N) at 70–90 kpc projected distance from M51, with no optical counterparts down to $g\sim27.5$ mag arcsec$^{-2}$ and stellar luminosities $<10^5\,L_\odot$. Each has $M_{\rm HI}\sim10^{6.5}\,M_\odot$ and velocity dispersion $\sim$20 km/s, consistent with Reionization-Limited H I Clouds (RELHICs) — hydrostatic H I confined in NFW dark-matter halos and kept starless by the UV background. Modeling gives host halo masses $M_{200}\simeq3.7\times10^9\,M_\odot$. A tidal-debris origin cannot be excluded with the unresolved FAST data, but the narrow line widths and high H I-to-light ratios favor the RELHIC interpretation.

### [The correlation between voids identified in 3D large-scale-structure and 2D weak-lensing maps](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20637)

Y. Fang, C. T. Davies, N. Hamaus et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using 108 full-sky lensing simulations with a DES-Y3-like source distribution, the authors quantify how strongly voids found in 2D weak-lensing (WL) convergence maps trace true 3D underdensities in the halo field. They measure a highly significant ($S/N\geq25$) excess of low-redshift 3D halo voids around WL-void centers at small angular separation, establishing that WL voids are genuine tracers of the 3D matter field. The correlation amplitude depends sensitively on WL-void selection (stringent peak thresholds wash it out), on source-redshift weighting, and mildly on void morphology (rounder, less LOS-aligned voids correlate more strongly). They lay out practical guidance for Rubin/Euclid void analyses.

### [Hubble tension: the shape wall](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21244)

Miguel A. Sabogal, Luis A. Escamilla, Davide Pedrotti et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

The authors sharpen the "no-go theorem" against late-time solutions to the Hubble tension. Beyond the familiar *normalization wall* (BAO constraining $H_0 r_d$), they show that at fixed sound horizon and acoustic scale $\theta_s$, any increase in $H_0$ must be matched by an equal fractional increase in the dimensionless distance integral $I\equiv\int dz/E(z)$, so the *shape* of $E(z)$ imposes a second constraint. Reconstructing $E(z)$ nonparametrically with Gaussian Processes from unanchored Pantheon+/DESY5/Union3 SNe and DESI DR2 BAO, they find the allowed late-time shift is $\lesssim2\%$ — far short of the $\gtrsim8\%$ needed — and this "shape wall" survives even alongside early-time new physics.

### [Web-Halo Model Peak-Background Split (WHM-PBS): halo bias as a distribution, not a number](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21334)

Samuel Brieden, Alexander Tipp
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Building on the Web-Halo Model with Shen et al. moving barriers for ellipsoidal collapse, the authors derive an analytic peak-background-split theory in which a halo's large-scale bias is inherited from its cosmic-web environment (filament inside sheet). This replaces the deterministic $b(M_h)$ relation with a strongly skewed *distribution* of biases. They use it three ways: as a physically motivated prior on higher-order bias relations (finding tight $b_2(b_1)$, $b_3(b_1)$ but scattered tidal bias $b_{s^2}(b_1)$, matching N-body), as a prediction of halo stochasticity that reproduces the super- to sub-Poisson shot-noise trend and yields a prior band on EFT stochasticity amplitudes, and as a framework for assembly bias.

### [Modeling Dependence Structures in Astronomical Multi-Band Time Series Data via Multi-Output Gaussian Processes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21431)

Samata Das, Lishan Shi, Yasaman Hamayouni et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: stat.AP

The authors present a unified framework for modeling dependence *among* photometric bands in irregularly sampled astronomical light curves using multi-output Gaussian processes. They contrast a covariance-based formulation (dependence specified directly through matrix-valued covariance functions and power spectral densities) with a latent-process formulation (observed curves as transformations of shared latent GPs, emphasizing the generating mechanism), deriving multi-output damped-random-walk models and their spectral representations for each. They demonstrate on multi-band AGN variability and continuum reverberation mapping, arguing the choice of dependence structure should follow the scientific objective rather than a universal default.

### [The Well-Tempered Likelihood: Honest Confidence Intervals for Misspecified Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20718)

Benjamin Nachman, Jesse Thaler
**Primary category:** stat.ME | also: hep-ph

Standard likelihood-based confidence intervals shrink to zero width as data accumulate, producing overconfident constraints when the model is misspecified. The authors propose dividing the likelihood-ratio test statistic by a goodness-of-fit statistic evaluated at the best-fit point: under correct specification the GOF is $\mathcal{O}(1)$ and standard inference is recovered, while under misspecification both scale as $\mathcal{O}(N)$, so their ratio stays $\mathcal{O}(1)$ and the interval self-limits at a floor set by the model's inadequacy (effectively reducing the sample size). They give binned and unbinned formulations — the latter using a classifier two-sample test — demonstrated on a Gaussian example and a strong-coupling measurement.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [What could an emerging Big Bang Nucleosynthesis discrepancy be hinting at?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20635)

Vivian Poulin, Julien Froustey, Cyril Pitrou et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

The latest primordial deuterium measurement is in $\sim2\sigma$ tension with standard BBN predictions when using the CMB-inferred baryon density, rising to $\sim3\sigma$ for early-dark-energy models that prefer a higher baryon density. Testing whether this points to a non-standard expansion history during BBN, the authors compute light-element abundances with PRIMAT for $\Lambda$CDM, a $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$ extension, and a "very early dark energy" (vEDE) component. A vEDE giving $\Delta H/H=0.087^{+0.036}_{-0.037}$ while deuterium is freezing out ($T_{\rm D}\simeq0.03$ MeV) removes the tension by preserving the deuterium abundance at the higher CMB baryon density, whereas extra radiation over-produces helium-4 and fails.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [How to Craft the Right Language AI Policy For Your Research Group (Some Assembly Required)](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20836)

Michelle Ntampaka, S. Burke-Spolaor, Ioana Ciucă et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: physics.soc-ph

This white paper (from the STScI/ESA/ADS "Language AI in the Space Sciences" workshop) argues there is no single correct AI policy for astronomy research groups. It introduces four laboratory archetypes with competing priorities — being first to a result, developing mentees, team cohesion, or maximal research quality — and uses them to show how a group's values shape decisions about AI's impact on productivity, scientist development, scientific integrity, and data governance. Rather than a universal set of rules, it offers a framework (and a self-assessment radar exercise plus an example policy) for aligning AI use with a group's mission, emphasizing the tension that every task delegated to a machine is one that may no longer build human expertise.

### [Toward Mechanistic Interpretability of an AI Foundation Model Fine-Tuned for Atmospheric Chemistry](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20778)

Jason Y. Hu, Ivan Higuera-Mendieta, Patrick Obin Sturm et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: physics.ao-ph

The authors present the first mechanistic-interpretability study of a scientific foundation model fine-tuned for a physical domain — Microsoft's Aurora, adapted for air-pollution forecasting — asking whether high forecast skill implies the model learned the governing chemistry or merely exploits statistical regularities. Imposing controlled chemical perturbations, they find Aurora captures a first-order ozone response to reactive nitrogen but violates basic chemical constraints (chemically inconsistent species combinations, relaxing wildfire plumes toward background). Internally, its representations remain organized around the meteorology inherited from pretraining, with little chemistry-specific structure; sparse autoencoders ("AuroraScope") find features that causally steer forecasts but don't map cleanly onto individual processes.
