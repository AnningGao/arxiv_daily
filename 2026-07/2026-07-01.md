## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Cosmological Concordance in an Especially Opaque Universe: A Tentative Cosmological Detection of Physical Neutrino Mass in $\Lambda$CDM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30903)

James M. Sullivan, Roger de Belsunce, Mikhail M. Ivanov
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors show that adopting a high prior on the reionization optical depth, $\tau = 0.11 \pm 0.006$ (i.e. dropping the large-scale *Planck* EE polarization constraint), simultaneously relaxes the Hubble tension, the "negative" neutrino-mass preference, and the apparent evolving-dark-energy signal, largely removing the need for physics beyond $\Lambda$CDM. Combining the CMB with DESI BAO and full-shape measurements in this high-$\tau$ Universe converts the lensing-driven "negative" mass preference into the first nominal $2\sigma$ cosmological detection of a positive neutrino mass, $\Sigma m_\nu = 0.10^{+0.04}_{-0.05}$ eV, robust to swapping ACT DR6 for SPT-3G DR1. The high-$\tau$ cosmology also predicts low-redshift distances in excellent agreement with DESI BAO (eliminating any need for dynamical dark energy) and reduces the Hubble tension from $\approx 6\sigma$ to $4.4\sigma$. They argue this motivates fresh large-angular-scale CMB polarization measurements (LiteBIRD, CLASS, PICO) to pin down $\tau$ directly.

This is a direct hit on the DESI-era large-scale-structure and fundamental-physics program at the core of the interest file: it ties together DESI BAO + full-shape inference, the reionization optical depth (the astrophysics half of the forest program), and the neutrino-mass measurement that Lyman-$\alpha$ P1D analyses also target.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Joint inference of weak lensing convergence map and cosmology with diffusion models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31988)

Benjamin Remy, Chihway Chang, Rebecca Willett
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

This paper performs field-level, simulation-based inference of cosmological parameters *and* the weak-lensing convergence map jointly, using a conditional diffusion model that learns the joint distribution from simulations without needing a differentiable forward model. A transformer operating in pixel space treats cosmological parameters as extra tokens alongside the map patches, and at inference time generates posterior samples of $(\theta,\kappa)$ conditioned on the observed noisy shear field. On log-normal $w$CDM mocks the recovered posteriors match traditional MCMC and stay well calibrated across the prior (MIRA score $0.635\pm0.017$ vs. $0.667$ optimal), while reproducing both two-point and non-Gaussian one-point statistics. The authors position diffusion-based implicit inference as a route to full field-level analysis with non-differentiable simulators.

Squarely in the interest file's rising-primary "ML/statistical inference for cosmology" thread — SBI, normalizing-flows/diffusion, and field-level inference — and the amortized, simulator-in-the-loop machinery here is exactly the kind of method transferable to forest/LSS field-level analysis.

### [Galaxy Power Spectrum at Two-Loop Order: Implications for Weak Lensing Surveys and New Physics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30713)

Mikhail M. Ivanov
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

Ivanov computes the galaxy power spectrum at two-loop order in the effective field theory of large-scale structure, deriving galaxy bias operators through fifth order with two-loop renormalization conditions consistent with CLASS-PT, plus higher-derivative/stochastic terms and IR resummation. The two-loop model requires 21 additional EFT parameters per sample but matches the PT Challenge N-body $P_{gg}$/$P_{gm}$ at the per-mille level to $k=0.85\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$. Even with conservative priors it yields an unbiased $\sigma_8$ with error bars three times narrower than linear theory ($\simeq40\%$ tighter than one-loop), promising large gains for $2\times2$pt analyses with Euclid, LSST, and Roman. The extra reach also probes new physics that modifies the matter power spectrum at $k\sim(0.4$–$0.8)\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$, e.g. ultralight axion sub-components at $m_a\sim10^{-24}$ eV.

Directly relevant to the interest file's EFT-of-LSS and precision full-shape inference interests (a natural sibling of the effective-field-theory treatment of the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest), with a bonus ultralight-axion dark-matter angle.

### [Constraining dark energy with complementary probes of large-scale structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31977)

Neel Shah, Kazuya Koyama, Johannes Noller, Lanyang Yi
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors constrain the Effective Field Theory of Dark Energy by combining expansion-history probes with several large-scale-structure probes: DESI DR1 redshift-space distortions, DES Y3 $3\times2$pt, and the ISW effect from CMB×galaxy/CMB-lensing cross-correlations. The complementarity of probes tightens the dark-energy perturbation parameters $\{c_B,c_M\}$ by a factor 2.69 in Figure of Merit (3.37 when mapped to the phenomenological $\{\mu(z),\Sigma(z)\}$). They find a strong interdependence between $\{w_0,w_a\}$ and $\{c_B,c_M\}$ imposed by the gradient-stability prior, and a $2.9\sigma$ deviation from $\Lambda$CDM for EFTofDE with CMB+BAO+SNe — nearly identical to the $3.1\sigma$ they find for $w_0w_a$CDM.

Relevant to the interest file's DESI/LSS secondary focus and the ongoing dynamical-dark-energy question raised by DESI DR2; a useful template for how RSD, lensing, and ISW jointly constrain perturbation-level dark-energy physics.

*No figures extracted for 2606.31977 (constraint-contour and FoM-comparison plots that are well summarized by the numbers above).*

### [Fuzzy Dark Matter Halo Mass Functions at Cosmic Dawn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30739)

Raghunath Ghara, Adam Lidz, Daniel Grin, Jackson Sipple
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

This work runs a new suite of N-body simulations for fuzzy dark matter (FDM) particle masses $10^{-22}$–$2\times10^{-21}$ eV and mixed FDM–CDM models with FDM fractions $0.3$–$1$, carefully identifying and removing spurious discreteness halos. They provide a new halo-mass-function fitting formula calibrated over $z=6$–$11$ for pure and mixed dark matter, finding $\sim30\%$ weaker small-scale suppression than earlier simulation-based formulas at $m c^2=10^{-21}$ eV (attributing the difference to prior extrapolation beyond the simulated mass range). Applied to the JWST UV luminosity function, they show upcoming lensed-field observations reaching $M_{\rm UV}\gtrsim-13$ at $z\gtrsim10$ will powerfully test FDM and mixed-DM scenarios.

Adjacent to the interest file's "dark matter from small-scale structure" focus (fuzzy/ultralight-axion DM and free-streaming suppression), providing an updated theory calibration that complements Lyman-$\alpha$-forest constraints on non-cold dark matter.

### [Possible chemical signatures of first-star enrichment in a very metal-poor galaxy overdensity near the end of reionization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31638)

Zihao Li, Koki Kakiichi, Lise Christensen et al. (S. E. I. Bosman, V. D'Odorico, F. B. Davies, X. Fan among co-authors)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Along the sightline to the $z\sim6$ quasar SDSS J0100+2802, a $z=5.945$ metal absorber with over-abundant carbon and silicon had been argued to carry a Population III enrichment signature. With JWST, the authors discover a 17-member galaxy overdensity (mean metallicity $\approx3\%$ solar, $\sim0.4$ dex more metal-poor than coeval galaxies in similar environments) coincident with the absorber, providing a chemically unevolved environment favorable for preserving Pop III absorption signatures. The absorber–galaxy cross-correlation implies a minimum halo mass $\log(M_{\rm h,min}/M_\odot)=10.68^{+0.93}_{-1.72}$, consistent with late-time Pop III formation in the outskirts of atomic-cooling halos.

Sits in the interest file's IGM/reionization and CGM (quasar-absorption) territory — end-of-reionization metal absorbers, high-$z$ quasar sightlines, and several high-value IGM/reionization co-authors (Bosman, D'Odorico, Davies, Fan).

*No figures extracted for 2606.31638 (the key content is the absorber–galaxy overdensity and cross-correlation, adequately conveyed in text; the source figures are a schematic cartoon and dense abundance-pattern panels).*

### [JWST spectroscopy of galaxies at $z>10$: Damped Ly$\alpha$ absorbers reveal efficient star formation and hidden redshift biases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30787)

Kasper E. Heintz, Clara L. Pollock, Joris Witstok et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The authors analyze 25 JWST/NIRSpec Prism galaxies at $z_{\rm spec}\geq10$, characterizing star-formation efficiency, burstiness, and rest-UV nebular lines against the local neutral-HI reservoirs traced by damped Ly$\alpha$ (DLA) absorption. They find rest-UV line prominence correlates with burstiness (very short depletion times $t_{\rm dep}\lesssim20$ Myr, requiring rapid pristine-gas replenishment) but no strong link between HI column density and $M_{\rm UV}$ or burstiness. Crucially, they quantify how strong DLAs bias photometric and Ly$\alpha$-break redshifts by $\langle z\rangle=0.39$ and $0.14$ respectively when unmodeled, though the net impact on the $z>10$ UV luminosity density is marginal.

Relevant to the interest file's neutral-hydrogen/DLA and reionization-era interests: it quantifies a DLA-driven systematic in high-$z$ redshift inference, the kind of contaminant modeling issue that also affects forest and continuum work.

*No figures extracted for 2606.30787 (multi-panel scaling relations and redshift-bias distributions; the quantitative biases are stated above).*

### [A Pixel-by-Pixel Path to Population III Discovery with JWST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30750)

Patricia Iglesias-Navarro, Thomas Harvey, Marc Huertas-Company et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.SR

This paper builds an end-to-end framework combining physically-motivated forward modeling (Yggdrasil primordial spectra) with simulation-based inference to test Population III detectability in JWST-like data, from isolated sources to realistic overlap with metal-enriched Pop II hosts. Spanning several IMF, nebular, and Ly$\alpha$-transmission scenarios and mocking JADES noise/filters, they show that unresolved/integrated analyses are strongly limited by host contamination, whereas spatially resolved, pixel-based model comparison reaches $\sim90\%$ recovery for young, massive Pop III clumps in nearly-quenched hosts at large projected separations. Applied to a literature candidate, a compact blue companion is preferentially fit by Pop III models while the host prefers Pop I/II.

Of interest as a concrete simulation-based-inference application with a forward-modeling + SBI pipeline (the ML-inference toolkit of the interest file) applied to the primordial-stars/early-Universe problem.

*No figures extracted for 2606.30750 ($\Delta\chi^2$ heatmaps and detectability-vs-parameter panels that mainly reinforce the recovery fractions quoted above).*

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [An Acceleration is Worth a Hundred Thousand Phase Space Measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31042)

Thomas Donlon II, Sukanya Chakrabarti, Elena D'Onghia
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Direct measurements of stellar accelerations (from e.g. pulsar timing and binary-orbit timing) can now constrain the local dark-matter density with fewer than 100 sources, rivaling kinematic Jeans analyses that use millions of stars. Using hydrodynamical N-body simulations of a Milky-Way-like galaxy, the authors show that in equilibrium a single direct acceleration measurement carries information comparable to $\sim10^5$ stars, and that Jeans modeling needs a thousand times more sources for equal precision. In a disk perturbed by a Sagittarius-like dwarf, the acceleration approach outperforms Jeans analysis regardless of sample size, because kinematic methods are systematically biased by disequilibrium (overestimating the local DM density).

Held to a high Tier-3 bar: a striking, quantitative statement about information content and the fragility of equilibrium-based inference — a cautionary methodological result of general interest to anyone estimating densities from statistical vs. direct observables.

*No figures extracted for 2606.31042 (the headline is the information-equivalence argument, well captured by the "$1$ acceleration $\approx 10^5$ stars" result).*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Deep Learning for Astrophysics: An Open Textbook from the NASA Cosmic Origins AI/ML Science and Technology Interest Group](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30855)

Yuan-Sen Ting, Digvijay Wadekar, Phill Cargile et al. (F. Villaescusa-Navarro, L. Verde among co-authors)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

Framing education (not tool availability) as the principal barrier to ML adoption in astronomy, the NASA Cosmic Origins AI/ML Interest Group presents *Deep Learning for Astrophysics*, a freely available textbook curated from its lecture series. It collects 23 chapters across six parts from 17 lecturers, spanning computational foundations, deep-learning architectures, generative modeling, simulation-based inference, reinforcement learning, and LLM agents, with many executable notebooks. The group also outlines a coming-year plan centered on agentic research and NASA's ASTRA mission-concept initiative.

Meta-research on how AI is reshaping the practice of astronomy — training, community uptake, and methodology — and a directly useful reference given the interest file's rising ML/SBI focus (co-authored by Villaescusa-Navarro and Verde, both central to the ML-for-cosmology community).
