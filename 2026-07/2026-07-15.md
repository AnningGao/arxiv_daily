### [Model-Independent Indication for a Localized Anomaly in the Late-Time Expansion History](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13009)

Shibendu Gupta Choudhury, Purba Mukherjee, Eleonora Di Valentino et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

A free-form, knot-based spline reconstruction of cosmological distances is applied to the latest DESI DR2 BAO measurements and the DES Dovekie Type Ia supernova compilation, and the inferred $H(z)$ is compared point-by-point against Planck 2018 ΛCDM. The analysis finds a localized deviation over $0.3\lesssim z\lesssim0.6$ reaching $\sim3.5\sigma$ at $z\simeq0.47$, persisting under variations of reconstruction method, dataset composition, and sound-horizon calibration. The authors introduce a physics-informed prior based on the Raychaudhuri equation (REIRA) to suppress unphysical derivative fluctuations, and mock analyses show the reconstruction is unbiased. The feature, if real, points to localized rather than global late-time new physics — or subtle early-vs-late probe inconsistencies.

### [Environmental Imprints on the Assembly of the Cool Gas around Bright Cluster Galaxies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12361)

Runyu Zhu, Hu Zou, Taotao Fang et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using Mg II absorption in stacked sight-line spectra from over a million DESI background quasars, this work produces the first statistical map of cool circumgalactic gas around brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) at $z\approx0.55$, tracing the radial profile from 40 kpc to 15 Mpc against a matched field-galaxy sample. It finds a dual environmental signature: the CGM within 200 kpc is significantly suppressed relative to field galaxies, while a pronounced excess of cool gas emerges from 200 kpc to 10 Mpc. A two-halo model is fit to the profiles, delineating gas stripping in the cluster core alongside accumulation in the outskirts.

### [Physically Consistent Parameter Inference: Transparent Machine Learning Emulation in High Energy Physics and Cosmology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12726)

Jorge Alda, Jacobo Asorey, Alejandro Mir et al.
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: cs.LG

An XGBoost gradient-boosted regression ensemble is trained to emulate expensive, generally non-Gaussian log-likelihood landscapes, with SHAP values providing an interpretability layer that recovers the physically expected hierarchy of observables. The framework is demonstrated on three global fits of increasing complexity — semileptonic $B$-meson flavour anomalies, axion-like particles addressing the Belle II $B^+\to K^+\nu\bar\nu$ excess, and $w_0w_a$CDM dynamical dark energy — reaching likelihood-evaluation speedups up to $10^5$. In the cosmological case, where the exact likelihood is cheap, the emulated posteriors are shown to be statistically indistinguishable from the exact ones, serving as a controlled validation.

### [Exploring Hu-Sawicki-like modified gravity with Genetic Algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12745)

Chiara De Leo, Elisa Fazzari, Matteo Martinelli et al. (Savvas Nesseris among co-authors)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

A genetic-algorithm reconstruction (GATO code interfaced with the CANDI cosmology code) generates analytic perturbations around the Hu-Sawicki class of $f(R)$ gravity, filters them through theoretical viability conditions (stability, matter-dominated recovery, GR limit, chameleon screening), and tests the survivors against DESI DR2 BAO and the Pantheon+ supernova catalogue. Current background data allow only mild departures from ΛCDM: the best-fit reconstruction stays close to $F(R)=-2\Lambda$, with the largest deviations at late times and an $Om(z)$ diagnostic showing only a very small departure. The GA fit yields a marginally lower $\chi^2$ than ΛCDM, but the difference is not statistically significant.

### [Mitigating galaxy systematics with gravitational wave clustering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12049)

Caterina Scarpel, Nicola Bellomo, Sarah Libanore et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This work develops a multi-tracer analysis in which gravitational-wave clustering — carrying a completely different set of observational systematics from galaxy surveys — is cross-correlated with large-scale structure to mitigate systematics that degrade constraints on local primordial non-Gaussianity ($f_{\rm NL}$) on cosmological ultra-large scales. Fisher forecasts for third-generation GW observatories quantify how the GW tracer both limits cosmic variance and preserves constraining power in the presence of galaxy-survey systematics. The authors also release an updated Multi_CLASS code with enhanced tracer customization.

### [Measuring Ultralight-Axion Coherence with Galaxy Polarization Correlations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12446)

Yasuo Doi
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: astro-ph.CO

Ultralight axion-like particles (ALPs) coupled to photons rotate the linear polarization of distant sources, and this paper proposes using the three-dimensional two-point correlation of galaxy polarization-rotation angles to measure not just the birefringence amplitude but its spatial coherence scale. For a nonrelativistic ALP with a Gaussian velocity distribution, the correlation has a characteristic $e^{-1}$ scale $L_{\rm G}^{\rm phys}=\sqrt6/(m_a v_a)$, so a detected turnover measures the momentum scale $m_a v_a$ while the amplitude constrains $g_{a\gamma}\sqrt{\Omega_a/\Omega_{\rm DM}}$. A fiducial survey of $10^6$ polarized galaxies over a quarter-sky to $z=2$ forecasts $5\sigma$ sensitivity to sub-degree correlated rotations over $m_a\sim10^{-29}$–$10^{-27}$ eV.

### [Quantifying Foreground Contamination in the Dark Ages 21 cm Power Spectrum Using an Electromagnetically Simulated Dipole Antenna Atop a Dielectric Surface](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11929)

Willow Smith, Jonathan C. Pober, Bang D. Nahn et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.CO

Targeting the highly-redshifted Dark Ages 21 cm signal from the lunar far side, this paper uses electromagnetically simulated dipole-antenna beams (including 1- and 4-layer lunar-regolith substrates) to quantify how instrumental beam structure spills bright synchrotron foreground power into the otherwise-clean observing window. With smooth analytic beams a window can be recovered, but finite bandwidth and high foreground power still cause substantial spillover; with the realistic FarView beam, regolith-induced spectral structure pushes the foreground floor well above the 21 cm signal. The authors conclude that high-fidelity per-antenna beam models and in-situ regolith characterization are prerequisites for a Dark Ages detection.

### [Overview: Cosmology with the SKAO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12887)

Marta Spinelli, Ian Harrison, Richard A. Battye et al. (Stefano Camera among co-authors)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This overview lays out the cosmology program for the SKA Observatory: HI intensity-mapping surveys (building on the MeerKLASS/MeerKAT precursor), continuum galaxy surveys, and — with the full AA4 configuration — weak lensing and its cross-correlations. Together these will map matter and velocity distributions on the largest scales, testing ΛCDM's foundational isotropy/homogeneity assumptions and probing evolving dark energy, primordial non-Gaussianity, and relativistic effects. The authors emphasize that radio surveys' radically different systematics make them valuable independent cross-checks as cosmology hits systematic-uncertainty limits, with combinations across wavelengths (and with GW and fast radio bursts) sharpening constraints.

### [The Limitations of Cosmological Collider Analyses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12010)

Oliver H. E. Philcox
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph, hep-th

Massive fields exchanged during inflation imprint "cosmological collider" oscillations on the primordial bispectrum, and this Letter combines precise CosmoFlow numerical predictions with Planck bispectrum reconstructions to assess their observability. In the weak-quadratic-mixing limit assumed by all previous searches, perturbativity forces the signals to be unobservably small except for double- and triple-exchange configurations at low mass — implying that many templates used in prior work should be read as effective, not theory-derived. Larger signals require boosting the mixing to non-perturbative (but still unitary) values, which damps and shifts the oscillations and breaks the factorization of the bispectrum into a fixed shape and free amplitude, complicating standard template searches. A full analysis therefore demands a non-perturbative joint treatment of all tree-level contributions.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Neutrino superradiance constraint on asteroid-mass PBH Dark Matter and beyond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12485)

Yi-Xuan Lin, Priyanka Sarmah, Martin Spinrath
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: astro-ph.CO

In the presence of a light scalar boson, rotating primordial black holes (PBHs) grow superradiant boson clouds that, if the boson couples to neutrinos, emit an approximately steady, nearly monochromatic ~MeV neutrino flux. Computing both Galactic and extragalactic fluxes and comparing them with existing low-energy antineutrino limits from Borexino, KamLAND, and Super-Kamiokande, the authors constrain PBH dark matter across much of the asteroid-mass window ($10^{17}$–$10^{23}$ g). For rapidly spinning PBHs the bound reaches $f_{\rm PBH}\sim10^{-7}$ near $2\times10^{22}$ g — significantly stronger than microlensing in that range — while the vector-boson case yields fluxes too small to constrain.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [AAAI-26 Dual Submissions: Novel Challenges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11918)

Kiri L. Wagstaff, Joydeep Biswas, Erich Merrill et al.
**Primary category:** cs.DL

The AAAI-26 organizers report a systematic effort to detect dual submissions — identical or substantially similar papers submitted to overlapping archival venues without disclosure. They compared main-track submissions against nine other venues with overlapping review windows and within the AAAI track itself, using a pipeline of title+abstract similarity screening, LLM-based overlap triage, and manual review of the highest-severity pairs, ultimately desk-rejecting 141 submissions. A central finding: easy-to-detect exact duplicates have been eclipsed by reworded duplicates that describe the same contribution in different words — extremely time-consuming to catch, and a phenomenon likely facilitated by generative-AI tools.

### [A genetic algorithm for peer-review panel composition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12757)

Ferdinando Patat
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: physics.hist-ph

Assigning a finite pool of expert reviewers across many panels while balancing scientific expertise and demographic diversity is a constrained combinatorial optimization whose configuration space grows explosively, making exhaustive search impractical. This paper presents a genetic algorithm — with a chromosome-based encoding of panel assignments and a fitness function combining scientific-expertise, gender, country, and seniority imbalance — developed for the European Southern Observatory (ESO) proposal-evaluation process and tested on real reviewer data. It rapidly finds panel configurations with substantially lower imbalance than random assignment and yields a set of high-quality realizations that can be further filtered against operational constraints; though built for ESO, the method generalizes to any panel-based peer-review system.
