## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Alleviating prior dependencies for DESI DR1 clustering fits through reparameterization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02498)

Marco Bonici, Simone Paradiso, Glenn McGee et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Bayesian full-shape analyses of DESI DR1 galaxy power spectra suffer prior-volume projection: weakly constrained EFTofLSS nuisance parameters drag the marginalized cosmological posteriors away from the posterior maximum. The authors reanalyze the DR1 power-spectrum multipoles with two mitigation strategies — nonlinear orthogonalization to decorrelate nuisance and cosmological priors, and a fully reparameterization-invariant Jeffreys prior over all EFT coefficients, evaluated on-the-fly via closed-form Fisher Jacobians. For the weakly anchored DESI+BBN+$n_s$ case the baseline priors project $H_0$, $w_0$, and $w_a$ by multiple $\sigma$; the Jeffreys prior recenters the posteriors to enclose the MAP within 68%, and a hybrid Jeffreys+Gaussian variant additionally tames over-broad $\omega_c$ tails (their fiducial choice). Once projection is controlled, the Bayesian credible regions agree with frequentist profile-likelihood confidence intervals in the $w_0$–$w_a$ plane.

Direct hit on the DESI large-scale-structure and statistical-inference focus: prior-projection control in EFTofLSS full-shape fits is exactly the class of methodology problem that also afflicts DESI cosmological-parameter extraction, and the Jeffreys/orthogonalization machinery is directly transferable to forest and full-shape analyses.

### [How I stop worrying about non-universality and $b_\phi$: Constraining local $f_{\rm NL}$ with $b_\phi$ priors from HOD posteriors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01314)

Jiaxi Yu, Nhat-Minh Nguyen
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Galaxy clustering constrains local primordial non-Gaussianity only through the product $b_\phi f_{\rm NL}$, and the usual universality relation fixing $b_\phi$ from the linear bias fails for the color- and flux-selected samples surveys actually target — the dominant obstacle to robust $f_{\rm NL}$ constraints. The authors propose a third route: sample the HOD posteriors fit to the small-scale clustering of DESI EDR LRGs, build mock pairs from the AbacusSummit simulations at a standard and a low $\sigma_8$, and measure $b_\phi$ with the separate-universe technique to construct a prior. Validated against mocks with injected local PNG, the method recovers unbiased $f_{\rm NL}$ even in the presence of halo assembly bias.

Direct hit on DESI large-scale-structure inference plus the statistical-methodology focus: a concrete recipe for turning galaxy–halo-connection knowledge into robust, defensible priors for a scale-dependent-bias clustering analysis, the kind of inference discipline the profile increasingly emphasizes.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [meer21cm: an Analysis Pipeline and Comprehensive Toolkit for HI Intensity Mapping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01864)

Zhaoting Chen, Steven Cunnington, Daniel Tassie et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

A modular Python pipeline for single-dish HI intensity-mapping cosmology, built around MeerKAT/MeerKLASS UHF-band data. It covers the full post-calibration chain — data read-in, foreground cleaning, power-spectrum estimation, mock simulation, transfer-function corrections, and parameter inference — and handles multi-tracer cross-correlation with overlapping optical galaxy catalogues. On a simulated survey of ten 750 deg² patches at $0.6<z<0.8$ it recovers the power spectrum to percent-level accuracy over $k\in[0.02,0.2]\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ with $\lesssim0.5\sigma$ mock-vs-model deviations. The package is public and documented.

Adjacent 21-cm cosmology, but the real value is borrowable machinery: survey-consistent power-spectrum estimation, transfer-function corrections, and galaxy cross-correlation are the same tools used in forest and LSS clustering analyses, packaged cleanly.

### [No Evidence for Superradiant Axions in LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA GWTC-5 Binary Black Hole Spins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01317)

Orion Ning, Benjamin R. Safdi, Catherine Welch
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: astro-ph.CO

Ultralight bosons can form superradiant clouds around spinning black holes, spinning them down and carving a forbidden region in the black-hole mass–spin ("Regge") plane. Using all 257 binary-black-hole mergers in the LVK GWTC-5 catalog in a hierarchical Bayesian population model (natal spin, age, and mass distributions plus the axion signal), the authors find no evidence for axions and exclude $1.7\times10^{-14}\,{\rm eV}\lesssim m_a\lesssim 3.3\times10^{-12}\,{\rm eV}$ at 95% confidence. Because GW spin inference comes from inspiral-merger-ringdown waveforms rather than accretion-disk modeling, this bound is more robust than prior X-ray-based superradiance constraints, and the larger catalog extends the excluded range by over an order of magnitude relative to GWTC-2.

A robust, independent constraint on ultralight/QCD-axion dark matter — the same fuzzy/axion DM candidates the profile probes through the small-scale Lyman-α forest — derived from an entirely different probe, useful as a cross-check on and complement to forest-based limits.

### [The Road to Identifying the Earliest Radio-Powerful AGN with the SKA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02366)

Jose Afonso, Stergios Amarantidis, Stas Shabala et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

A review chapter synthesizing hydrodynamical and semi-analytic simulations with pathfinder observations to argue that a substantial, still-undetected population of radio-powerful AGN exists in the Epoch of Reionization, currently hidden by selection biases and incomplete radio spectral information. It lays out a physically motivated identification strategy — broadband radio SEDs, spectral curvature, dynamical jet evolution, and radio-only redshift estimation — and highlights how the SKAO's 50 MHz–15 GHz coverage will find the earliest supermassive black holes and probe neutral hydrogen through 21-cm absorption.

Adjacent to the reionization and 21-cm threads: early radio AGN are candidate ionizing sources and act as backlights for 21-cm absorption during the EoR, both bearing on the IGM/reionization physics the profile treats as a primary focus.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Black Boxes in Black Hole Imaging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02110)

Juliusz Doboszewski, Jamee Elder
**Primary category:** physics.hist-ph | also: astro-ph.IM

A philosophy-of-science analysis of epistemic opacity in the computer simulations and machine-learning methods behind black-hole imaging. The authors argue that several forms of opacity — including opacity from ML — need not undermine the reliability of an inference when it sits inside a broader inferential framework, and they propose conditions under which opaque methods are trustworthy, with the next-generation Event Horizon Telescope as the setting. They also flag one genuinely problematic case: GRMHD models of Sagittarius A* are opaque in a way that signals the limits of current understanding and constrains how ML models can be used in future observations.

Meta-research on exactly when ML and simulation "black boxes" are epistemically acceptable in astronomy — directly relevant to judging the trustworthiness of the emulator, simulation-based-inference, and foundation-model machinery the profile is moving toward.

### [Development of a Retrieval-Augmented Generation Virtual Assistant for Enhanced Information Discovery at Rubin Observatory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01659)

Leanne P. Guy, Connor Yablonski, Aaron M. Meisner et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

A prototype retrieval-augmented-generation (RAG) assistant to help staff and scientists navigate Rubin Observatory / LSST's large, heterogeneous documentation — operational guides, technical notes, and papers scattered across platforms, much of it on internal systems that general-purpose LLMs cannot reach. The system integrates sources via Weaviate embeddings, LangChain query orchestration, and a GPT backend, grounding responses in domain-specific material to reduce hallucination and enable conversational semantic search over LSST data-product documentation.

A concrete example of LLM tooling entering large-survey workflows — the practice-of-astronomy side of the "AI is changing astronomy" trend, of interest as DESI/LSST-scale operations drive adoption of these assistants.
