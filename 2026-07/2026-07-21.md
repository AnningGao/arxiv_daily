### [Comparing explicit likelihood and likelihood-free simulation-based inference for weak lensing cosmic shear](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17942)

Simone Vinciguerra, Nicolas Martinet, Marco Gatti
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This is a controlled, side-by-side comparison of the two dominant simulation-based-inference paradigms for cosmological parameter estimation: explicit-likelihood inference (ELI — a GP emulator plus a simulation covariance and a Gaussian likelihood) versus likelihood-free inference (LFI — neural density estimators), applied to Euclid-like non-tomographic weak-lensing cosmic shear on GLASS Gaussian-random-field mocks constraining $\Omega_m$ and $S_8$. Using shear two-point functions under MOPED (linear) or neural (non-linear) compression plus a map-level CNN, they apply LFI calibration diagnostics — notably the test of accuracy with random points (TARP) — to both frameworks. They find ELI becomes badly miscalibrated once the emulator is inaccurate or the compressed likelihood is non-Gaussian, whereas LFI stays well calibrated; non-linear compression inflates ELI's $S_8$ error by ~2.2× while barely affecting LFI. Headline: ELI-vs-CNN constraints differ by up to a factor of two, versus ~30% for LFI.

Direct hit on the interest file's rising-primary focus #3 (ML/statistical inference for cosmology): SBI, neural density estimators, and posterior-calibration diagnostics are exactly the inference machinery flagged for application to forest/LSS data, and the ELI-vs-LFI calibration lesson transfers straight to a Lyman-α P1D or full-shape analysis.

### [Cosmological consequences of a dynamical dark matter in the light of DESI DR2 measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18234)

Abhijith Ajith, Utkarsh Kumar
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

The authors ask whether DESI's apparent preference for dynamical dark energy could instead be sourced by dark matter with a non-zero equation of state. Their phenomenological "dynamical dark matter" model lets $w_{\rm dm}$ interpolate (via a sigmoid in scale factor with transition $a_t$) between an early-time and a present-day value, implemented in modified CAMB + Cobaya and confronted with Planck+ACT lensing, DESI DR2 BAO, three SNeIa compilations, and $f\sigma_8$ growth data. The early-time EoS stays consistent with cold dark matter, while the present-day value is negative at 0.42σ–3.02σ depending on the dataset (strongest: $w_0^{\rm dm}=-0.060^{+0.013}_{-0.028}$, $a_t=0.41$ for CMB+DESI+DESY5), with lower $\sigma_8/S_8$ but unchanged $H_0$. The model beats $\Lambda$CDM ($\Delta{\rm DIC}=-7.8$) but is disfavored relative to CPL dark energy ($\Delta{\rm DIC}=+8.0$).

Relevant to the DESI-era LSS focus (#4) and the "DESI DR2 dynamical dark energy BAO" thread flagged as a recent library addition: it reframes the same DESI signal through the dark-matter sector, useful context for interpreting the BAO/full-shape results the owner tracks.

### [Breaking the $f_{\rm NL}$–$b_\phi$ degeneracy with the time evolution of tracer number counts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18229)

Caio B. de S. Nascimento, Neal Dalal
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Constraining local primordial non-Gaussianity via scale-dependent bias is limited by the PNG bias parameter $b_\phi$, which is fully degenerate with $f_{\rm NL}$ in the power spectrum and poorly predicted by the universality relation for realistic galaxies. This paper tests the "time evolution" estimator — which infers $b_\phi$ from the redshift evolution of tracer number counts — against the separate-universe estimator using the IllustrisTNG and SIMBA suites of CAMELS. Its main contribution is extending the estimator to realistic observer-frame photometric selections: correction factors derived from the galaxy SEDs handle luminosity-distance dimming and K-corrections, and an additional passive-stellar-fading correction restores agreement for apparent-magnitude cuts. After correction the estimator matches the separate-universe reference across both simulations and all redshifts, including DESI-like LRG selections.

Relevant to the LSS/PNG side of the primary program (#4) and built on the CAMELS suites already in the library; a cleaner handle on $b_\phi$ directly improves $f_{\rm NL}$ constraints from the spectroscopic surveys the owner works with.

### [Wide binaries in ultra-faint dwarf galaxies as a probe of extended dark objects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18185)

María Olalla Olea-Romacho
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: astro-ph.CO

This work generalizes the wide-binary disruption constraint on compact dark matter from point masses to spherically *extended* dark objects, replacing the point-mass impulse kick with a projected-enclosed-mass kick and computing diffusive weak-encounter heating for NFW, $\rho\propto r^{-3/2}$, and $\rho\propto r^{-9/4}$ profiles. Applied to the wide-binary population of the ultra-faint dwarf Boötes I, the resulting 95% limits on the dark-matter fraction stay within a factor of a few of the point-mass bound for compact perturbers ($R_{90}\lesssim10^5\,R_\odot$), reaching $f_{\rm DM}\sim10^{-3}$ at high mass and weakening only for the largest objects. Mapping the $r^{-3/2}$ case onto ultracompact minihalos yields an illustrative primordial-curvature-power-spectrum bound $\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}\lesssim$ few$\times10^{-6}$ over $k\approx4\times10^2$–$3\times10^3\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$.

Sits at the intersection of two interest threads: small-scale dark-matter structure (#5) and the small-scale primordial power spectrum — the same $k\gtrsim100\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ regime the Lyman-α forest constrains, so this is a complementary, purely gravitational bound worth cross-checking against forest limits.

### [Unveiling dark energy properties with high-sensitivity cross-correlations of neutral hydrogen intensity mapping and galaxy surveys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17141)

Alessandro Marins, Chang Feng, Filipe B. Abdalla
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors build an end-to-end simulation of low-redshift (0.1 < z < 0.5) HI 21 cm intensity mapping cross-correlated with an LSST-like narrow-band photometric galaxy survey sliced into 40 tomographic bins. Galactic synchrotron and free-free foregrounds are removed blindly with FastICA, and the resulting cross-power signal loss is corrected with an empirical transfer function calibrated on hundreds of Gaussian mocks using a robust Huber estimator. A Bayesian analysis of the recovered HI–galaxy cross-power spectra returns unbiased posteriors on the dynamical dark-energy parameters $(w_0, w_a)$ consistent with the fiducial values when the data sets are combined, establishing the pipeline as a viable route to dark-energy inference from 21 cm cross-correlations.

Relevant to the adjacent 21 cm / cosmic-dawn interest (#7) and the survey-inference toolkit: the foreground-mitigation-plus-transfer-function-plus-Bayesian-recovery workflow is a close methodological cousin of forest-contaminant and continuum-systematics handling.

### [Program Synthesis for Simulation-Based Inference: Joint Model Selection and Parameter Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17540)

Siddharth Mishra-Sharma
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: stat.ML

This paper treats a large language model as an implicit prior over scientific simulator *programs*, enabling joint model selection and parameter estimation rather than parameter inference within a fixed model. From a natural-language problem description the LLM proposes diverse candidate simulators, iteratively refined over rounds in an SMC-style loop where it acts as a feedback-driven mutation kernel, while neural ratio estimation scores the competing models and masked autoregressive flows give per-model parameter posteriors, yielding a joint posterior $p(M,\theta\,|\,x)$. Benchmarks span a damped harmonic oscillator, stochastic epidemic models, and — most relevantly — dark-matter subhalo population inference from gravitational-lensing images, where the method recovers plausible model families and its Occam-style penalty behaves as an honest diagnostic of data informativeness (a CDM subhalo-count bias at 4 lenses vanishes at 20).

A natural extension of the SBI toolkit the owner is building toward (#3): automating the simulator-model choice, not just parameter fitting, is directly transferable to forest/substructure inference where the generative model itself is uncertain.

### [Amortized Inference for Sampling Distributions Where the Bootstrap Fails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16666)

Akash Deep
**Primary category:** stat.ME | also: stat.ML

This paper trains a monotone quantile "root network" on datasets simulated from a prior over a distribution family, supervised by single draws of the root $T_n - T(F)$ scored with the pinball loss (a proper scoring rule whose minimizer is the posterior-predictive law of the root), so that one forward pass maps a dataset to its full sampling-distribution estimate and confidence intervals. It targets four problems where Efron's bootstrap is provably inconsistent — the maximum of a bounded-support distribution, the mean in the infinite-variance α-stable regime, the Pareto tail index, and 99% value-at-risk — and where the classical m-out-of-n and subsampling fixes need unknown rate corrections. The learned method attains 0.947–0.952 coverage on all four, beats every feasible classical method in Wasserstein-1 distance by 4–30×, and on real market returns averages 0.87 coverage versus 0.73 for the bootstrap.

A methods paper worth borrowing (#3): amortized, simulation-trained uncertainty quantification for statistics with pathological sampling distributions is exactly the situation for extreme-value or heavy-tailed summary statistics in forest/LSS analyses where the bootstrap quietly under-covers.

### [Cosmological simulations of the high-redshift galaxy population adopting a variable stellar initial mass function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16404)

Anna Durrant, Robert A. Crain, Cedric G. Lacey et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using a 100 cMpc cosmological simulation with a variant of the COLIBRE galaxy-formation model, the authors adopt a density-dependent stellar IMF that becomes top-heavy for stars born from dense gas, with heavy-element and dust yields and supernova-feedback energetics self-consistently adjusted to the changing IMF. Allowing a significant fraction of high-redshift star formation to proceed top-heavy raises rest-frame far-UV luminosities by up to a factor of ~4 relative to the fiducial Chabrier-IMF run, enabling galaxies as bright as $M_{\rm UV}\simeq-20$ at $z=15$ (versus $-18.5$ fiducially) and easing the tension with JWST's excess of UV-bright early galaxies. The far-UV boost is partly offset at later times by increased dust attenuation, and the run still reproduces the $z=5$ stellar mass function, optical luminosity function, and JWST-consistent UV slopes.

Relevant to the adjacent galaxy-evolution / reionization-source interest (#8, #2): the abundance and UV output of early galaxies set the ionizing-photon budget that drives reionization and shapes the high-z IGM the Lyman-α forest probes.

### [SpecFANN: Spectral Fitting via Artificial Neural Networks I. A deep learning based fastwind emulator and fitting suite](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17348)

M. Abdul-Masih, C. Hawcroft, T. Lechien et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.SR | also: astro-ph.IM

The authors build SpecFANN, a deep-learning emulator for the FASTWIND non-LTE radiative-transfer / spectral-synthesis code for OB-type massive stars, training a separate feed-forward network for each of 140 spectral lines on ~50,000 FASTWIND models spanning temperature, gravity, radius, and He/CNOSi abundances, plus an open-source Python fitting suite supporting Nelder-Mead, genetic-algorithm, MCMC, and nested-sampling fits. Most networks reach accuracies better than ~0.01–0.1% for photospheric lines and ~0.1–1% for wind lines, and the emulator delivers a fit in roughly 1/360,000 of the time of on-the-fly FASTWIND computation. It recovers literature-consistent parameters for 52 early-type stars, with detailed validation on the O-star standard 10 Lac.

A concrete instance of the emulation methodology at the heart of interest #3 (cf. ForestFlow, neural operators, DeepONet in the library): replacing an expensive radiative-transfer code with a per-observable neural surrogate to make full Bayesian inference tractable is precisely the pattern the owner applies to forest/LSS simulations.
