## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Selection effects in correlated observations with application to distance-ladder observations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22425v1)

Will J. Percival
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Percival derives the likelihood for truncated selection when objects are selected on one observable but measured on another *correlated* observable — a bias distinct from the classic Malmquist case and equivalent, in a Bayesian framing, to a misspecified generative model. He applies it to Cepheid brightness measurements underpinning the local distance-ladder H0, testing specifically for a selection correction that depends on photometric uncertainties. Only weak evidence (1.2σ–1.9σ) for a non-zero residual correction is found; adding a single cutoff parameter lowers H0 by 0.7–1.1 km/s/Mpc, and allowing a different selection per host galaxy can lower it further but becomes very sensitive to the distance prior. He concludes a new selection correction alone cannot explain the Hubble tension but may be one component of a multi-faceted solution.

This is exactly the Bayesian selection-effect / misspecified-likelihood methodology at the core of precision-cosmology inference, and it bears directly on the H0 tension and on correlated-observable selection biases that quietly pervade survey samples.

*No figures extracted for 2607.22425 (methodology paper; the argument is fully carried by the text and its H0-shift numbers).*

### [Probing dark energy evolution with Quaia quasars through the integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22461v1)

M. Ghodsi Yengejeh, J. R. Bermejo-Climent, A. Kovács et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors perform a tomographic cross-correlation of the *Quaia* (Gaia–unWISE) quasar catalogue with *Planck* CMB temperature maps to measure the ISW effect over a broad redshift range spanning ~(10 h⁻¹ Gpc)³ in comoving volume. They detect the ISW signal at 2.8σ with amplitude A_ISW ≈ 1.69 ± 0.61 relative to the *Planck* ΛCDM prediction — moderately stronger than expected. The measurement is stable against component-separation maps (SMICA/NILC/SEVEM), ℓmax and binning choices, North–South splits, and an alternative quasar bias model. The w₀wₐCDM models favoured by recent DESI BAO and DES Y6 constraints do not account for the excess.

A direct probe of late-time dark-energy evolution using DESI-era LSS tracers (a quasar catalogue × CMB), squarely inside the DESI/BAO/dark-energy program the profile tracks — and the mild excess over both ΛCDM and w₀wₐCDM is the kind of tension worth watching.

### [Dark matter-baryons elastic coupling](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22415v1)

David Figueruelo, Florencia Anabella Teppa Pannia
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

They construct a cosmological model in which dark matter and baryons interact via pure momentum transfer, deriving the modified Euler equations and connecting them to the tight-coupling approximation schemes used inside Boltzmann solvers. The model is implemented in CLASS, CAMB, and the approximation-free SymBoltz, with full consistency demonstrated across all three codes. The interaction suppresses small-scale clustering and thereby eases the σ8/S8 tension; fitting CMB + BAO + SNe Ia together with low-redshift SZ cluster counts and DES Y6, the interacting scenario is mildly preferred once the low-redshift data are included.

Hits the dark-matter-from-structure and LSS-cosmology interests at once, with a genuine methods bonus: an explicit, cross-validated treatment of how a DM–baryon coupling enters tight-coupling schemes in CLASS/CAMB versus an approximation-free solver.

![](figures/2607.22415/matter_power_spectrum_gamma.pdf)

### [An Introduction to Bayesian and Frequentist Simulation-Based Inference with Machine Learning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21702v1)

Maximilian Dax, Theo Heimel, Gilles Louppe
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: astro-ph.CO

A pedagogical overview of simulation-based inference (SBI) with machine learning, laid out across both the Bayesian and frequentist statistical frameworks. It describes how neural posterior estimation and neural likelihood estimation fit into each paradigm, and shows that the same networks apply to empirical-Bayes and unfolding / detector-inversion tasks. It also covers how to validate SBI results and where ML-based SBI breaks down. The paper is cross-listed to astro-ph.CO and astro-ph.GA, signalling its intended physics audience.

SBI / NPE / NLE is precisely the rising-primary methodological toolkit in the profile; a unified Bayesian-plus-frequentist treatment with explicit validation guidance is directly usable for forest and LSS inference on expensive simulations.

*No figures extracted for 2607.21702 (review/tutorial; its figures are worked pedagogical examples rather than a single headline result).*

### [Simulation-Based Empirical Bayes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21843v1)

Xinwei Shen, Diana Cai, Cheng Zhang et al. (David M. Blei)
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

The paper introduces simulation-based empirical Bayes (SBEB), extending nonparametric empirical Bayes to the common scientific situation where the likelihood p(x|z) is available only through a simulator. SBEB computes EB estimates without an explicit density, using the observed data, simulator samples, and an amortized inference network, and iteratively refines the fitted EB prior toward the population prior. Across several scientific simulators and real-world datasets, it improves accuracy over standard SBI run with a fixed prior.

Marries empirical Bayes with SBI for implicit-likelihood problems — exactly the expensive-simulator regime the profile targets — and the amortized prior-refinement idea is a borrowable technique for hierarchical forest/LSS inference.

*No figures extracted for 2607.21843 (methods paper; the learning-curve figures are illustrative rather than a central result plot).*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Where Do Quasars Live? DESI DR1 Constraints from PAC Measurements](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21749v1)

Shanquan Gui, Kun Xu, Donghai Zhao
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Applying the Photometric Objects Around Cosmic webs (PAC) method to DESI DR1 quasars at 0.8<z<1.0 and photometric galaxies from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys DR9, the authors measure the excess projected surface density of neighbouring galaxies around quasars down to M⋆=10¹⁰·⁸ M⊙ over 0.1–15 h⁻¹ Mpc. Jointly modelling this with the quasar and LRG auto-correlations and the quasar–LRG cross-correlation, via an N-body simulation plus a stellar-to-halo-mass relation and an explicit incompleteness model, they find a Gaussian quasar occupation peaking at log₁₀(M_acc/h⁻¹M⊙)=12.88 with width 0.51. The satellite-hosting parameter B=1.01±0.03 — quasars are equally likely to sit in central halos and satellite subhalos at fixed accretion mass.

The DESI DR1 small-scale clustering measurement and the halo-occupation machinery here are the same LSS/HOD tools used in galaxy and forest clustering analyses; a clean central-vs-satellite result is useful context for anyone modelling DESI quasar samples.

*No figures extracted for 2607.21749 (the headline is a single occupation-parameter result well summarized in text).*

### [Stacked Reverberation Mapping of High Redshift Quasars in DESI. I. Feasibility Analysis](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21869v1)

Rahma Alfarsy, R. E. A. Canning, Eva-Maria Mueller et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

This is a feasibility study for *stacked* (ensemble) reverberation mapping of high-redshift quasars in DESI. Using mock light curves emulating expected DESI quasar observations at 1.48<z<5.2, the JAVELIN pipeline recovers simulated C IV time lags within 1σ using only a few (2–10) irregularly-cadenced spectroscopic epochs paired with well-sampled photometry. The authors quantify how flux-error magnitude, number of stacked quasars, and epoch count affect recovery. The approach offers an economical route to extend the radius–luminosity relation to higher redshift and luminosity, feeding single-epoch black-hole masses and quasar cosmology.

A nice methods pattern — turning DESI's sparse, irregular spectroscopy into a black-hole-mass / quasar-cosmology tool via an ensemble estimator — worth knowing for anyone leaning on large spectroscopic quasar samples.

*No figures extracted for 2607.21869 (feasibility/forecast paper; figures are simulation-setup diagnostics rather than a measured result).*

### [Light Antinuclei Coalescence: Femtoscopic Constraints via Neural-Flow Surrogates](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21689v1)

M. Korwieser, L. Fabbietti, B. Hashemi et al. (L. Heinrich)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE | also: hep-ph

The authors build a fast, differentiable normalizing-flow surrogate for the CECA femtoscopic source model, fit to 49 ALICE proton–proton correlation functions and extrapolated via scaling laws to the low-multiplicity regime relevant for cosmic rays. The surrogate reproduces CECA at sub-percent emulation fidelity, yielding data-constrained source functions that remove the dominant uncertainty in coalescence-based antinuclei production. Uncertainties on the coalescence parameters B₂ and B₃ shrink from factors of 10–100 and ~1000 down to the few-percent and ~10% level, respectively (plus a ~15% wavefunction systematic for B₂).

Cosmic-ray antinuclei are a prime dark-matter signature, and this is a clean template for replacing an expensive physics model with a differentiable normalizing-flow emulator — exactly the surrogate/emulation pattern in the profile's ML toolkit.

### [Prior laundering: learned priors with inherited, undetectable overconfidence](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21721v1)

Ali Siahkoohi, Sina Alemohammad
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

A cautionary analysis of learned generative priors for ill-posed Bayesian inverse problems that are trained on archives of legacy reconstructions rather than ground truth — "prior laundering." Where the measurements are uninformative, the posterior reverts to the prior, so the reported uncertainty is the archive's, not the data's, and self-consistency checks such as simulation-based calibration pass regardless of the truth. The induced prior is shown to equal the old regularizer advanced by a single EM step — improved where the data resolve, frozen where they cannot — so it is overconfident wherever the inherited belief is tighter than reality; deployed diffusion and normalizing-flow priors under-cover the operator's blind subspace. They recommend reporting which directions the measurements actually resolve.

A direct, sobering warning about the SBI / flow / diffusion-prior methods the profile is adopting — and it explains *why* SBC-style checks can silently pass an overconfident learned prior, echoing the "trust crisis in SBI" already in the library.

*No figures extracted for 2607.21721 (the contribution is a conceptual/theoretical caution; the schematic figures add little beyond the text).*

### [The AGORA High-resolution Galaxy Simulations Comparison Project. IX - Part 1: Effects of a Major Galaxy Merger on Star Formation of a Milky Way-mass Galaxy Progenitor](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21709v1)

Thinh Huu Nguyen, Kirk S. S. Barrow, Minyong Jung et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Part 1 of AGORA CosmoRun's comparison of a major z≈4.5 galaxy merger across nine well-calibrated cosmological zoom-in codes, each with a different stellar-feedback scheme. The star-formation-rate response is strongly shaped by feedback type: kinetic feedback drives an early merger-induced starburst that subsides before coalescence; thermal-only feedback yields prolonged post-coalescence SFR growth; delayed-cooling / radiation-pressure feedback produces highly fluctuating SFR. Particle tracking shows kinetic feedback facilitates gas inflow from the secondary galaxy, and an inverse correlation between burst fraction and pre-merger gas fraction holds independent of feedback model.

A controlled code comparison that isolates how stellar-feedback prescriptions change simulation predictions — valuable calibration context for anyone relying on hydro simulations as the infrastructure layer beneath forest/LSS emulators.

*No figures extracted for 2607.21709 (code-comparison paper; no single figure captures the multi-code result without its full companion set).*

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [The diffraction-lensing interpretation of GW231123 with astrophysical priors](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21834v1)

Mark Ho-Yeuk Cheung, Digvijay Wadekar, Matias Zaldarriaga et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

GW231123, if unlensed, is a rare binary-black-hole merger with high masses and high spins for both progenitors; the authors show the signal is better fit by a lower-mass, lower-spin merger *diffraction-lensed* by an isolated ~1000 M⊙ (redshifted-mass) object, modelled as a point mass or a compact halo. Using GWTC-5 population priors and lensing optical depths for hypothesis testing, a point-mass lens is not excluded by BH-abundance bounds but is strongly disfavoured by predicted intermediate-mass-black-hole mass functions. A standard cold-dark-matter halo cannot be compact enough, but self-interacting dark matter undergoing gravothermal collapse could form the required lens — leaving the posterior odds inconclusive.

A striking cross-cutting result: gravitational-wave diffraction lensing as a probe of both intermediate-mass black holes and SIDM gravothermal collapse, connecting GW data to the nature-of-dark-matter question the profile cares about.

*No figures extracted for 2607.21834 (the key content is the hypothesis-test/odds argument; its corner plots need full context to read).*

### [Revealing Four Subpopulations of Binary Black-Hole Mergers with the Fifth Gravitational-Wave Transient Catalog](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22011v1)

Nir Guttman, Paul D. Lasky, Eric Thrane
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE

Using GWTC-5, a hybrid data-driven-plus-parametric discovery method finds the binary-black-hole population is naturally described by four subpopulations. A dominant low-mass component (~70% of the merger rate) centred near 10 M⊙ is separated by a depletion near 14 M⊙ (possibly failed-supernova black holes); above it lie an unequal-mass branch and a near-equal-mass branch peaking at 30–35 M⊙ (low spins, likely first-generation); and a percent-level high-mass, high-spin, broad-mass-ratio component consistent with hierarchical mergers. The four-component model is overwhelmingly preferred over the standard LVK population model, at ln(BF)=19.2.

A genuinely new observational result on black-hole formation channels, and its hybrid data-driven-plus-parametric mixture-discovery method is a transferable template for population inference.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Echoes of Star Stories: Integrating Indigenous narratives into modern stellar astrophysics](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21679v1)

Dionysios Gakis
**Primary category:** astro-ph.SR

A perspective piece integrating Indigenous celestial narratives — drawn from Aboriginal Australian, Pueblo, Inuit, and Polynesian traditions — with modern stellar astrophysics. It argues these oral records preserve observations of stellar variability, supernovae, eclipses, and planetary alignments that complement historical records and offer human-centric views of stellar life cycles paralleling modern models. It discusses their value for education, public engagement, cross-cultural collaboration, and even as a source of scientific hypotheses.

Meta-research on the practice and culture of astronomy — how non-Western knowledge systems and the field's own history feed into education, outreach, and how results get framed.

*No figures extracted for 2607.21679 (conceptual/historical perspective; the only figures are illustrative sky maps).*
