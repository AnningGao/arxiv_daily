## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Physical quasar pairs in DESI DR1 as tracers of protoclusters at $z\approx2$](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34350v1)

Martin G. Richiarte, Diego G. Lambas
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors select quasar pairs with rest-frame $|\Delta V|\leq900$ km/s at $1.2\leq z\leq2.8$ from DESI DR1, measure their purity against redshift-shuffled catalogues and correct for fibre-assignment incompleteness, which removes almost all close pairs in single-pass regions. This gives **40,626 physical pairs out to $r_p\leq2.5$ Mpc**, where purity falls to 50%. The sample is ten times the earlier SDSS DR16 sample, mostly because of DESI's fainter quasars. In the pair–quasar cross-correlation (projected $w_p$ and monopole $\xi_0$ fitted over 15–40 $h^{-1}$Mpc), pair members are **1.7–2.0 times more clustered than all DESI quasars**, while chance pairs show only $1.08\pm0.05$. That implies **haloes of $\sim1.4$–$2.2\times10^{13}\,h^{-1}M_\odot$**, which ten AbacusSummit DESI-quasar mocks reproduce within errors, so the pairs behave as ΛCDM predicts for protocluster tracers.

![](figures/2611.34350/fig_clustering.pdf)

*Members of physical pairs in every separation bin lie well above the all-quasar auto-correlation, while chance pairs sit close to it (panel c, blue band). The excess is genuine environment, not a selection artefact.*

### [The THESAN-ZOOM project: interpreting candidate Population III-enriched absorption line systems during reionization](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34789v1)

Giulia Pruto, Laura Keating, Rahul Kannan et al. (Ewald Puchwein co-author)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using the THESAN-ZOOM radiation-hydrodynamic zooms (14 regions), the authors select **DLA-like gas cells** (neutral, dense, cold) at $z\simeq6$–8 and compare their metal abundance ratios with the O I-bearing absorbers seen in high-$z$ quasar spectra. The fiducial Pop II-only model matches ordinary absorbers apart from **silicon, which it underpredicts by a factor of ~2**. About $10^{-5}$ of absorber-like gas volume falls in the **[Si/O]–[C/O] box of the claimed Pop III-enriched candidates**, but that same gas fails the other abundance ratios. One plane therefore cannot identify Pop III enrichment, and the larger scatter at higher $z$ comes from falling metallicity and dust, not from first stars. Resimulating one halo with explicit Pop III stars moves the ratios toward the candidates, which points to multi-ratio absorber statistics as the way to test Pop III.

![](figures/2611.34789/allgal_popIII_hist_z6.pdf)

*Pop II-only gas selected to match the Pop III candidates in [Si/O]–[C/O] lands in different, confined regions in the other abundance planes. The matching [Si/O]–[C/O] values can be produced without Pop III stars.*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Acoustic Priors from the Latest Cosmic Microwave Background Data](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33334v1)

Kushal Lodha, Jun-Qian Jiang, Purba Mukherjee et al. (Arman Shafieloo co-author)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors compress Planck PR4 + ACT DR6 + SPT-3G D1 plus the joint lensing reconstruction into a late-time-agnostic prior on $\{\theta_*,\omega_b,\omega_{bc}\}$. They do this by removing late-ISW sensitivity and replacing the model-predicted $C_L^{\phi\phi}$ with a free spline. The result, **$100\theta_*=1.04100\pm0.00021$, $\omega_b=0.02244\pm0.00010$, $\omega_{bc}=0.1410\pm0.0011$**, is invariant across ΛCDM, $\sum m_\nu$, $w_0w_a$, binned $w(z)$ and interacting dark energy. The reconstructed lensing spectrum exceeds ΛCDM near $L\simeq50$ and $L\sim10^3$. The key consequence for DESI is that once lensing-induced peak smoothing is marginalized rather than tied to the late-time model, **the CMB+DESI DR2 preference for $w_0w_a$CDM drops from 3.0σ to 1.9σ** (3.2σ → 2.4σ with Union3.1).

![](figures/2611.33334/w0wa_lensing.pdf)

*With lensing smoothing freed (via $A_{\rm lens}$ or the acoustic prior), the DESI $w_0$–$w_a$ contours move toward ΛCDM and $\omega_{bc}$ shifts. Part of the dynamical-dark-energy signal rides on the lensing that ΛCDM predicts for the CMB peaks.*

### [Signals from the early Universe: primordial features from the full-shape analysis of BOSS galaxies and eBOSS quasars in combination with Planck](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34672v1)

Mario Ballardini, Nicola Barbieri, Antonio Raffaelli et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This is a **one-loop EFTofLSS full-shape search** for four oscillatory primordial-feature templates (linear, log, localized bump, power-law decaying) in BOSS DR12 galaxies and eBOSS DR16 quasars, alone and with Planck PR4. IR resummation is applied separately to each oscillatory component, and window-convolution damping is validated on mocks. The best LSS features improve $\Delta\chi^2$ by only 6–10, which is **at most 1.6σ after the look-elsewhere effect**. A recurring low-frequency "feature" turns out to be degenerate with the BAO wiggle. LSS amplitude bounds are 1.4–3× weaker than Planck's, and adding BOSS+eBOSS leaves the CMB-preferred frequencies unchanged. This makes it a clean template for repeating the frequency-profile search on DESI quasars.

### [When precision exceeds fidelity: systematic error quantification for inference with misspecified simulators](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33282v1)

M. J. Yantovski-Barth, Hengyue Zhang, Nolan Smyth et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: stat.ME

When posterior widths shrink below the spread between candidate simulators, evidence-weighted model averaging collapses onto the least-wrong model and hides the systematics. The authors instead estimate each model's **influence map**, the derivative of the posterior mean with respect to the whitened data. They compute it as a **posterior covariance from ~512 forward evaluations at existing posterior samples**, with no gradients or Fisher inversion, so it also works for black-box and amortized posteriors. Applied to ALMA CO(2–1) black-hole-mass fits of NGC 4697, it shows that imaging-free stellar-mass models compensate for a shared **exponential-emissivity simplification** by biasing $M_{\rm BH}$. Propagating the emissivity residuals through the influence maps substantially restores cross-resolution consistency while keeping the honest between-model spread. The same tool transfers directly to emulator-based P1D or full-shape inference.

### [Black holes in little red and blue dots drive no superwinds in the first 1.5 billion years](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34743v1)

Mainak Singha, Yurina Nakazato, Wei Leong Tee
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

From 80,367 NIRSpec spectra in the DAWN JWST Archive, the authors select 67 little red and blue dots at $z\gtrsim4$ and search for [O III] outflows. They find **zero winds against ≥7 expected** from local sub- and super-Eddington AGN, giving incidence limits of 13% and 19% (95%) versus 31% and 49% locally. Broad Balmer lines are centred on the narrow lines, and **Balmer absorbers move below 8% of the escape speed**. Super-Eddington fountain, black-hole-star and quasi-star models each fail one measured spectral property. The black holes in these sources apparently grow without wind feedback, consistent with their offset above the local $M_{\rm BH}$–$M_\star$ relation.

### [Curvature Mismatch and Local Errors in Galaxy Property Estimation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29224v1)

Austin Lutterbach, Angus Hoy, Jasper Chong et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: astro-ph.IM

A linear probe on a curved embedding manifold has a nonzero intrinsic Hessian, **$\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$ (probe normal dotted with the second fundamental form)**, so it inherits a local bend that may or may not match the target's curvature. Using decoder-based curvature estimates on 86,471 AstroPT galaxies embedded by five pretrained encoders, the authors show the bend helps where it aligns with the label's curvature and hurts where it does not. A local **bend correction** raises held-out local $R^2$ by 0.006–0.010, more where the curvatures disagree. The practical lessons for probing a spectrum foundation model are to tune the ridge penalty, read probe accuracy by neighbourhood (dim objects fit worst) and correct locally where a decoder exists.

### [ChemoMAE: Self-Supervised Spectral Representation Learning Reveals Chemical Heterogeneity in Historical Wood](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34581v1)

Ryuji Yamaguchi, Miyuki Matsuo
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.CV

ChemoMAE is a **masked-denoising Transformer over the bands of individual NIR spectra**. Predicting masked bands from the rest exploits inter-band multicollinearity, and the learned per-pixel embeddings are clustered into label-free chemical-state maps of 49 hyperspectral wood sections. The authors evaluate with a hypothesis-driven metric, **Local Label Agreement** (spatial coherence of neighbouring labels), rather than cluster separation. On held-out specimens, masked reconstruction beats raw spectra, PCA and unmasked autoencoders at every cluster count. An unmasked denoising AE matches it on training specimens but generalizes worse, while PCA wins on cosine-silhouette. The setting is far from astronomy, but the ablation of masked vs. full reconstruction for 1D spectra is directly relevant to a quasar-spectrum SSL model.

### [Interpreting Speckle Effects Through Neuron Interventions](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33632v1)

Shubham K. Singh, Hare Krishna
**Primary category:** eess.IV | also: cs.LG

The authors run a mechanistic-interpretability study of how a pretrained masked-autoencoder foundation model (SARMAE) handles multiplicative noise, using synthetic scenes with known ground truth. They find 38 neurons that track the speckle statistics, but **restoring those neurons to clean values does not help**. Activation patching locates the effect in the first MLP, where **a different set of 64 gradient-ranked neurons recovers 44.1% of the lost correlation**. Holding the speckle-selective neurons noisy leaves 96.5% of that gain, while freezing three decoder heads' values removes 37%. The general lesson for interpreting noise handling in spectral transformers is to choose intervention targets by their causal effect on the task, not by how strongly they respond to the corruption.

### [Carrying Feature Ablations Across Sparse Autoencoder Seeds](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34637v1)

Karl Li
**Primary category:** cs.CV | also: cs.LG

SAE features matched one-to-one across training seeds by decoder cosine are routinely treated as properties of the encoder. This paper tests that directly: ablate a feature and, separately, its match in a sibling-seed SAE, then compare the change in the frozen encoder's output (DINOv2 and SigLIP layers). At the chosen tolerance, **the ablation carries over for only 12%–70% of 2,560 matched pairs** across three settings, against 0% for random-direction and shuffled-match controls. **Per-layer contribution error predicts which ablations carry better than decoder cosine does**, but one wrong prediction persists on independent images. The recommendation is to screen at the layer and confirm important edits at the output. This is a needed caution before editing or naming features of a spectral model through SAE directions.

### [Guidance as Variance Matching](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34801v1)

Ali Backour, Artem Lukoianov, Frank Permenter et al.
**Primary category:** cs.CV | also: cs.LG

At practical scales, classifier-free guidance and autoguidance **move the prediction further from the posterior mean**, the training target, even as samples improve. The authors explain this through variance: posterior-mean predictions are under-dispersed relative to the data, and guidance restores the missing variance. Their **closed-form variance-matched guidance** picks the scale so the guided prediction's variance matches the data's. It **recovers the hand-tuned scales of eleven image, video, audio, motion and world models** without tuning, and per noise level it gives a schedule users prefer on SD 3.5 Medium. The proposed mechanism is that the error difference between strong and weak models follows the posterior covariance. That predicts working negative scales and larger scales for self-checkpoint guidance, and both are confirmed. This matters for anyone using conditional diffusion as a calibrated posterior sampler.

![](figures/2611.34801/concept_story.pdf)

*In a 2D Gaussian-mixture toy with real trained models, the predictions of a weak and a strong model scatter as a shrunken copy of the posterior covariance. Guidance extrapolates their difference so the guided predictions span the full posterior spread.*

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [A magneto-convective origin for solar-wind Alfvenic fluctuations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34265v1)

R. J. Morton, Z. Huang, T. Schad et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.SR

This paper tests whether the young solar wind is powered by convectively launched Alfvén waves or by interchange reconnection. The authors compare coronal Doppler-velocity spectra (UCoMP, SDO/AIA) with **270 highly Alfvénic Parker Solar Probe intervals below the Alfvén surface**. Both carry the **non-WKB "fossil" spectrum predicted for waves driven by surface convection**, with energy peaking at 4–8 mHz in both weakly and rapidly expanding open fields, the sources of the fast and slow wind. DKIST Cryo-NIRSP splits the coronal power into convective waves (~60%), a ~4 mHz p-mode-conversion component (~30%) and an unexplained 6–8 mHz component, and the latter two also appear in PSP data. Reconnection jets disturb a given field line for ≲0.1% of the time, so **convection supplies a basal wave energy flux** while reconnection adds intermittency and composition.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Toward Automated Strong-Lens Modeling with Agentic Workflows: An Application to Euclid Q1 Lenses](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33363v1)

Xiaoyue Cao, Ran Li, Yunlong Yin et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.CO

Lens-Orchestra couples a team of multimodal LLM agents to explicit pixel-level forward modeling (power-law mass, shear, pixelized source). The agents do the masking, prior initialization, failure diagnosis and reconfiguration that normally take expert hand-tuning. Launched by natural-language instructions on **185 Euclid Q1 grade-A lenses with no human intervention**, it completed 149 fits. **Independent human review accepted 140** and endorsed all 36 halted runs. Einstein radii agree with the released models (+0.12% median offset), and the hierarchical mean slope is $2.002\pm0.009$. The cost was about **two billion tokens of frontier-model use**. This is a concrete data point on how agentic AI is replacing the human inspection step in survey-scale astrophysical modeling, and on what it costs.
