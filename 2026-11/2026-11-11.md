## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Stable cosmology under flexible covariances: Rescaling and cross-survey correlations in DESI BAO and supernova data](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13829v1)

Eleni-Anna Kolonia, Haoye Li, Hanyu Zhang et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors test whether the DESI DR2 BAO + CMB + SN preference for evolving dark energy could come from misestimated uncertainties. They rescale each published covariance (DESI DR2 BAO, Planck 2018, and a joint Pantheon+/Union3/DES-Dovekie likelihood on a common redshift basis) by **a free scale parameter**, and they let the SN compilations have effective cross-survey correlations. DESI, Union3 and DES-Dovekie agree with their published covariances, while Pantheon+ mildly prefers extra covariance. Pantheon+ and Union3 show a **positive effective correlation r ≃ 0.4–0.5**, plausibly from shared supernovae. The extra freedom widens the contours without moving them: the w₀wₐCDM improvement over ΛCDM changes only from **Δχ² = 12.8 to 12.3 (3.15σ to 3.07σ)**, so **the preference is robust to covariance misestimation of this form**.

![](figures/2611.13829/Fig9_cosmology_TableIV.pdf)

*(Ωm, w₀, wₐ) constraints from DESI DR2 BAO + Planck-lite + joint SN with fixed (blue) versus free (orange) covariance scales. The contours widen but stay centred in the same place, so the departure from w₀ = −1, wₐ = 0 remains.*

### [Connecting field-level and n-point function measurements of galaxy bias](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14456v1)

Hsiang-Ming Huang, Henrique Rubira, Fabian Schmidt
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Field-level inference measures bias coefficients at a finite cutoff Λ, while n-point analyses use renormalization conditions in the low-k limit. This paper uses **the renormalization group of galaxy bias** to connect the two. On mock galaxy fields from a 4LPT forward model with a bias expansion, the RG predictions describe how b_δ, b_δ² and b_G2 run with cutoff. In the **Λ → 0 limit they agree with tree-level n-point measurements**. On N-body halo catalogs the RG also describes the measured running within the predicted systematic errors. This makes the RG flow the natural way to compare bias values from field-level and power-spectrum/bispectrum EFT analyses.

### [Impact of Primordial Magnetic Field-induced Small-scale Power on Non-linear Structure Formation and Cosmic Reionization](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13806v1)

Hiten, Kanhaiya Lal Pandey, Rajesh Mondal et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors evolve PMF-enhanced initial power spectra (B₀ = 100–170 pG, n_B = −2.9) with N-body simulations and use the halo catalogues as sources in **semi-numerical reionization and 21-cm modelling**, keeping the ionizing efficiency fixed at the ΛCDM calibration. Extra small-scale power gives more early low-mass haloes. The reionization midpoint moves earlier by **Δz₅₀ ≃ 0.50–2.14**, and the Thomson optical depth rises from **τ_e ≃ 0.0547 to 0.0595–0.0786**, i.e. 0.66–3.28 times the Planck σ_τ. The authors stress that these are simulation-relative offsets, not exclusions. They do not model magnetic-field evolution or dissipative heating, which would also change the IGM thermal history.

![](figures/2611.13806/LCDM_100_130_150_170pG_volume_xHI_constraints.pdf)

*Volume-averaged neutral fraction against redshift for ΛCDM and the four PMF models, compared with observational x_HI inferences. Stronger fields shift the whole reionization history earlier, by up to Δz ≈ 2 at the midpoint.*

### [First constraints on axion-like particles with SPHEREx data](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13090v1)

Connor Hainje, Elena Pinetti, David W. Hogg
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: astro-ph.CO

The first search for **axion-like-particle dark matter decay lines in SPHEREx** spectra, using the first 16.5 months of Deep Field data over 0.496 < m_a < 3.32 eV. The analysis is deliberately conservative: an independent smooth continuum model at every sky point, power-constrained limits, and data-driven error calibration. All strong excursions match known features (atmospheric He I, the 3.3 μm PAH feature, Paα, Brα). The tightest limit is **g_aγγ < 2.10 × 10⁻¹¹ GeV⁻¹ at m_a = 0.606 eV**, with median τ > 6.5 × 10²⁵ s. The limits sit well above **the Cramér–Rao bound**, so better methods alone could improve them by more than an order of magnitude.

![](figures/2611.13090/constraint.pdf)

*The 95% upper limit on g_aγγ against axion mass (black) sits a large factor above the Cramér–Rao bound (green dashed), which marks the headroom for better methods. Bumps in the limit line up with the labelled detector band edges and known spectral lines.*

### [Dust Reverberation Mapping of Low-Mass Active Galactic Nuclei](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13181v1)

Chi-Zhuo Wang, Hengxiao Guo, Xue-Bing Wu et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

A systematic **dust reverberation mapping** study of low-mass AGNs (M_BH ~ 10⁶–10⁷ M☉), using ZTF optical and WISE W1/W2 light curves. It yields a core sample of 65 sources whose ICCF, JAVELIN and PYROA lags agree. W2 lags exceed W1 lags (median ratio 1.24). Combined with a high-luminosity sample, the unified R_dust–L_AGN relations span nearly seven decades in luminosity, with **slopes of 0.47 ± 0.01 (W1) and 0.44 ± 0.01 (W2)**, slightly shallower than the 0.5 expected from radiative equilibrium. Lag deviations anti-correlate with **Eddington ratio** (possibly slim-disk self-shadowing), and SPHEREx composites show luminosity-dependent SED changes; both may explain the shallow slope.

### [SHINE: Shear inference environment - Generative forward modelling for radio weak gravitational lensing](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14301v1)

Ezequiel Centofanti, Benjamin Remy, Emma Ayçoberry et al. (incl. F. Lanusse)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

Instead of measuring galaxy shapes, SHINE infers cosmic shear directly from radio interferometer visibilities. It uses **a differentiable generative forward model (galaxy model → shear → uv sampling) with the likelihood in Fourier space**, sampled by MCLMC in JAX. On parametric simulations it gives unbiased shear with sub-percent accuracy and **up to 3 times lower uncertainty** than state-of-the-art estimators. Coverage tests show well-calibrated posteriors. With realistic COSMOS morphologies the parametric galaxy model is biased by **over 10σ**. Replacing it with an autoencoder decoder plus normalizing-flow prior trained on real galaxies brings the bias **below 2σ**, a concrete case of learned priors fixing model misspecification in forward-model inference.

![](figures/2611.14301/cosmos_shear_estimates.pdf)

*Shear posteriors for 10,000 realistic COSMOS galaxies: the parametric galaxy model (violet) misses the true shear (black cross) because its morphology is wrong, while the autoencoder-based generative model (orange) recovers it.*

### [Massive Activations Confound Sparse Autoencoders](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13548v1)

Nathan Delisle, Chenhao Tan
**Primary category:** cs.LG

**Massive activations** put up to 99.9% of residual-stream variance on PC1. TopK sparse autoencoders tile that direction with features that differ from seed to seed, so the "most important" features are the least reproducible (47 of the top-50 are unmatched across runs). Variance explained also hides the failure: a Qwen-2.5-7B SAE reports 99.96% variance explained while leaving 33% of the non-PC1 content unexplained. The fix, **"PC1 bypass"** (remove PC1 before training the SAE and add it back afterwards), raises **top-50 cross-seed feature sharing from 4–39.5% to 73–99.5%** and cuts downstream model degradation by up to 44% at the same sparsity. This is a practical check for any SAE run on a spectral transformer.

![](figures/2611.13548/sec6_repair_sharing.pdf)

*Top-50 cross-seed feature sharing for standard TopK SAEs, the same SAEs with PC1-aligned features excluded after training, and SAEs trained with PC1 bypass. Only training with the bypass makes the important features reproducible.*

### [Linear Probes to Bayesian Manifolds: Geometry of Arithmetic in Large Language Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12998v1)

Anshul Kumar, Neha Prasad, Manoj Kumar et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

A **four-stage Bayesian pipeline** that splits a probe result into separate claims: decodability, shape (chosen among named manifolds by marginal-likelihood evidence), ownership (projecting out pre-registered related concepts) and causal use. Across two-digit arithmetic in GPT-J, Llama 3.1 8B and Pythia 6.9B, 89% of cells are decodable and every strongly supported shape is a **ribbon or torus**. However, **81% of shapes are inherited** from related concepts rather than owned. **Only 46 of 1,330 cells pass all four criteria**. The lesson for interpreting a foundation model's embeddings: a probe that decodes a quantity is weak evidence that the model represents and uses it.

### [AdjointFlow: Accelerating Scientific Inverse Problems with Physics-Based Transport](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13389v1)

Ethan Hanold, Nicholas M. Boffi, Qin Li et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

AdjointFlow replaces Gaussian-to-posterior diffusion with **stochastic-interpolant transport from a physics-based initialization (the adjoint F*y)**. Its noise-free velocity stays regular as the posterior concentrates, and the parameterization builds the initialization into the learned velocity. On InverseBench (inverse scattering, seismic full-waveform inversion, EHT-style black-hole imaging) it matches or beats conditional diffusion on 5 of 7 datasets with 8 Euler steps. It runs **up to five orders of magnitude faster than plug-and-play diffusion**. An optional refinement step that queries the forward model reaches state-of-the-art accuracy on every dataset where it was applied, without retraining.

![](figures/2611.13389/adjointflow_cartoon.pdf)

*Diffusion starts from uninformative noise and its transport becomes ill-conditioned as the posterior narrows. AdjointFlow starts from the physics-informed adjoint estimate, which shortens the path to the posterior.*

### [You Don't Need Feature Learning to Do In-Context Learning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13431v1)

Joseph Turnbull, Jamie Simon
**Primary category:** cs.LG

A solvable-model account of memorization versus generalization in the in-context linear-regression task. Decomposing rotation-invariant kernel regression in **the kernel eigenbasis**, the authors show that with more samples the kernel learns successively higher-degree pieces of the memorizing solution, which depend on successively higher-order statistics of the training tasks. **The "generalizing" ICL solution is a low-degree truncation, i.e. underfitting, of memorization**, and needs no feature learning. 12-layer GPT-2-style transformers follow the prediction: removing the **kurtosis of the training-task pool** delays or prevents memorization.

![](figures/2611.13431/kicl_left_summary.pdf)

*Theory and experiment learning curves: error on training tasks falls monotonically, while error on novel tasks reaches its minimum at the end of the generalizing phase and then rises as the model starts to memorize.*

### [Everything in its right place: attention is a radio head](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13146v1)

Lluís Arola-Fernández, Aissam Khadraoui, Alex Arenas
**Primary category:** physics.soc-ph | also: cs.LG

The paper derives an attention layer in closed form as **the locked state of a Kuramoto-type receiver synchronizing with oscillator-like context tokens**. Token frequency drifts with age, so position becomes a Lorentzian resonance. A **critical coupling K_c** separates a locked phase, where the weights sum to one, from a "silent" phase, where they sum to less than one. That lets the head abstain or count without sink tokens. In small trained networks (TinyStories, recall/counting/induction) the radio head matches softmax + RoPE in its widest heads. It fails where the phase diagram predicts, and it **keeps accuracy far beyond the training length**, where softmax + RoPE loses half or more. Large-scale behaviour is untested.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Extracting Published Discussions of 13,616 Galaxies with Language Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13118v1)

Nolan Koblischke, Jo Bovy
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

**LLM agents (GPT-5.5 in the Codex harness)** read 87,899 galaxy-mentioning papers out of 156,006 astro-ph papers. They extracted 44,332 genuine discussions of individual galaxies (85,314 quotes from 18,641 papers), with sky coordinates for 98.2%, grouped into **13,616 galaxies** in a browsable "GalaxyWiki". The run took ~71 h with 20 concurrent agents at ~$12,600 in API cost. 96.5% of quotes match the source text, and manual review found **118 of 120 records correct**. It is a concrete data point on what agentic literature mining now costs and how reliable it is.

### [Contrastive Discovery: Open-Ended Scientific Discovery over Competing Explanations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14514v1)

Ziang Liu, James J. Kim, Yijia Dai et al.
**Primary category:** cs.AI

AI "discovery agents" usually test one hypothesis against its complement, and the authors find that 89% of their highest-surprisal findings are mere rejections. They propose scoring each experiment against **a contrast set of mutually exclusive, exhaustive competing explanations**. On the same evidence this resolves 73% of those rejections to a specific alternative. Used to guide search across six datasets (including SDSS galaxies), it **resolves 67% more questions at the same experiment budget**. In a blinded study, 38 experts chose the same explanation on 78% of resolved questions.
