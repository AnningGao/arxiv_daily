## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Where the red rise stops: JWST/MIRI reveals a near-infrared turnover in the Little Red Dot Abell2744-QSO1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09397v1)

E. Iani, P. Rinaldi, A. Torralba et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

First MIRI F770W/F1000W imaging (rest-frame 0.96 and 1.24 μm) of the triply lensed LRD QSO1 at z = 7.04 shows that **the steep red optical rise stops near rest-frame 1 μm**: the slope goes from α = 1.83 ± 0.13 (F430M–F770W) to a flat α = −0.07 ± 0.36 between the MIRI bands. A **single T_BB = 4450 ± 60 K blackbody** describes 0.35–1.24 μm, with **no sign of the hot-dust rise seen in type-1 quasars**; a hotter (6874 K) blackbody behind A_V ≈ 1.5 fits equally well. The statistically preferred SED fits need (3–4)×10⁹ M☉ of stars, over 100× the dynamical limit, so they are disfavoured. A disc in a dense gas cocoon over-predicts the 1.24 μm flux by 5σ, and super-Eddington envelope models only work for a black hole ~2.5 dex below the dynamical mass. For a dust-reddened disc, hot (≥1200 K) dust can re-emit at most ~2% of the absorbed luminosity, so the data favour an accretion-powered source but cannot separate dust reddening from dense-gas processing; the authors predict F1800W/F2550W fluxes that would.

![](figures/2611.09397/qso1_bb_slopes.pdf)

*QSO1's rest-frame SED: the optical rise flattens between the two MIRI bands. One ~4450 K blackbody (or a reddened hotter one) fits 0.35–1.24 μm, and no extra hot-dust component is needed.*

### [A combined PRIMER+MINERVA study of the evolution of [OIII]+Hβ equivalent width from z=3 to z=8.5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08563v1)

Isaac J. B. Holst, Ross J. McLure, Ryan Begley et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Combining PRIMER broad-band and MINERVA medium-band NIRCam imaging in UDS cuts the photo-z outlier rate to 4.7% at m_F444W ≤ 28, which lets the authors measure [OIII]+Hβ equivalent widths for 1964 galaxies (M_UV ≤ −19.25) in five medium-band redshift slices. They fit them with BAGPIPES using non-parametric continuity star-formation histories. **The median EW rises monotonically, from log W = 2.54 ± 0.01 at z ≈ 3 to 3.05 ± 0.03 at z ≈ 8**, and the distribution grows a low-EW tail. The **extreme-emission-line fraction reaches 0.50 ± 0.10 at z = 8.3**, which they attribute to **increasingly bursty star formation**. The part most useful for reionization budgets: the **ionizing-photon production efficiency evolves only mildly**, with median log ξ_ion going from 25.14 ± 0.01 to 25.29 ± 0.03 across z ≈ 3–8.

### [New optimisations for exact, scalable 2D Gaussian process inference, with applications to astronomical time-series](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09198v1)

Mark Fortune, Neale P. Gibson, Benjamin Pope
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP | also: astro-ph.IM

The authors extend the JAX package luas with **exact GP likelihoods for data on a full 2D grid (e.g. wavelength × time)**. They do this by combining **Kronecker-product algebra with fast 1D solvers** (celerite/quasiseparable kernels, QR, arbitrary matrix square roots), with several new kernel families scaling linearly in one or both dimensions. For a 1024 × 16384 spectroscopic time-series, a quasiseparable measurement covariance gives **a 27 ms log-likelihood on CPU**, cheaper than the transit model for the same data. The motivating use is wavelength-correlated noise in JWST transit spectroscopy. The more general point is that **low-rank 2D GP kernels are a probabilistic, GP-smoothed generalisation of PCA**. The paper also gives exact optimisations for the Linear Mixing Model without OILMM's orthogonality constraint, which carries over to latent-factor models of noisy survey spectra.

### [Stable probabilistic emulation of partially observed dynamical systems](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09317v1)

Mario Massy, Sara Shamekh, Fabrizio Falasca et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The paper introduces **forced-dissipative stochastic interpolants**: an autoregressive generative emulator whose learned drift is hard-constrained to a forcing term plus skew-symmetric, dissipative (D ⪯ −γI) and energy-preserving parts. This bounds how much energy the sampler can inject without knowing the system's equations. From that energy bound they prove a **Foster–Lyapunov condition, giving geometric ergodicity and bounded energy under any bounded forcing**. On one- and two-layer Lorenz-96 (with only the 36 slow variables observed), unconstrained interpolants diverge and a conditional diffusion baseline is unstable at some model sizes, including its largest. **The constrained emulator stays stable at every capacity** and tracks the mean and variance response to large perturbations. The guarantees hold for the continuous-time sampler, and tests are limited to toy systems.

![](figures/2611.09317/two_layer_capacity.pdf)

*Emulation error against parameter count on the two-layer system (∞ marks a diverging rollout): diffusion-model stability depends erratically on size, while the forced-dissipative interpolant is stable at all capacities.*

### [Diffusion-free Score Matching for Exact Conditional Sampling](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09758v1)

Thomas Wedenig, Robert Peharz
**Primary category:** cs.LG

DISCO learns a **single time-independent score of the slightly-noised data distribution** instead of a family of noise-level scores. It is trained with a mixture of Fisher divergences under progressively noisier proposals, and its population minimiser is the true score. Because there is one joint score field, **fixing the observed coordinates gives the exact conditional score**, which can be sampled with MCMC (tempered SMC with HMC in low dimensions). Standard diffusion conditioning heuristics (replacement, guidance, DPS) do not have this property. On 2D benchmarks the conditional W1 error is 0.015–0.042, against 0.082–0.417 for those heuristics. It reaches **FID 3.18 on CIFAR-10 and 2.54 on FFHQ-64** (EDM: 1.97 / 2.39) and drifts least under repeated Gibbs-style inpainting. This bears directly on masked-spectrum inpainting and on SBI posteriors with arbitrary conditioning.

![](figures/2611.09758/gmm.pdf)

*Sampling x₂ given x₁ = 1.75 on a 2D Gaussian mixture: guidance, replacement and twisted-diffusion samplers deviate substantially from the true conditional, while DISCO's exact conditional score matches it.*

### [Cross-Layer TopK: Dynamically Allocating Sparsity Across Layers in Transcoders](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09233v1)

Anyuan Zhuo, Shaohua Zhang, Tianle Li et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

For cross-layer transcoders, **all layers' candidate features are pooled and a single global TopK keeps K = L·k per token**. Total sparsity stays exact, as in TopK, while the model decides how many features each layer gets, as in JumpReLU. On Qwen3-8B-Base, FVU drops from 27.64% (per-layer TopK) to **27.07%**, against 32.17% for JumpReLU, which leaves 41.2% of latents dead. The gain is concentrated in the hardest middle layers 15–19 (1.3–1.6 points), where the model **reallocates budget automatically**. Circuit-tracing replacement and completeness scores are the best of the architectures tested. The headline improvement is incremental, but the fixed per-token budget allows a 2.9× training-step speedup, and weights plus scaling laws are released.

### [Generative Latent Priors for Interpreting and Steering Protein Language Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09818v1)

Daniel Hinjos, Lucía Urcelay
**Primary category:** cs.AI | also: cs.LG

The authors train **flow-matching "generative latent priors" on frozen ESM-2 residual-stream activations** and use them both as an interpretability lens and as a steering prior, in a head-to-head comparison against SAEs on a scientific foundation model. As 1D probes of 110 Swiss-Prot concepts, single "meta-neurons" beat InterPLM's SAE features (**AUC 0.903 vs 0.845 at 8M parameters, 0.932 vs 0.872 at 650M**). However, their own falsification tests show **most of the advantage is concentration, not new information**: each winning meta-neuron is largely a linear read-out of the SAE dictionary (median R² ≈ 0.75), and a 20-feature SAE probe matches it on most concepts. Re-noising and denoising steered activations does pull edits back onto the activation manifold, though that benefit is lost after masked-Gibbs decoding.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [DarkMatterFM-Registry: A Machine-Readable Registry of Dark-Matter Models Extracted from the arXiv Literature](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08583v1)

Ioana A. Zelko, Jo Bovy, Nolan Koblischke
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

A staged LLM pipeline reads ~18,900 candidate astro-ph/hep-ph papers and turns them into **6,145 schema-validated dark-matter "model cards"**. The top ~2,000 are enriched with observables, constraints and theory-to-observable routes. Every step of those routes is then labelled available, partial or missing, and the gaps are aggregated into a priority-weighted roadmap of 25 community projects. The main finding is that **what blocks high-priority models is missing shared infrastructure, not missing theory**: a general non-cold Boltzmann framework, relic-solver model files, and **a Lyman-α forest likelihood for non-cold DM, the single largest structure-formation gap** (many models are constrained only through a thermal-WDM proxy their own cards flag as inapplicable). Validation is LLM-judged only: 81% of section verdicts correct, none hallucinated, and the enriched observables/constraints blocks only about 50% correct.

![](figures/2611.08583/fig_gaps.pdf)

*The capability-gap roadmap: gaps are dominated by missing likelihoods and software rather than theory (a), and among structure-formation projects the non-cold-DM Lyα forest likelihood unblocks the most models (b).*
