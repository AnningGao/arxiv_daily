## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Using fixed $w$ to reproduce evolving $w_0$-$w_a$ dark energy at the field level](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29597v1)

Yuxi Meng, Daniela Saadeh, Cheng-Zong Ruan et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Written in superconformal time, the N-body equations depend on the background only through $a(\tau)$, so a CPL cosmology can be mapped onto the **constant-$w$ model with the closest expansion history** at fixed σ₈, with growth-factor matching for other redshifts and a velocity rescaling. They validate this with **100 target/match mg-glam simulation pairs** spanning $0.1\le\Omega_m\le0.5$, $-1.4\le w_0\le-0.5$, $-1.8\le w_a\le-0.15$. For the DESI+CMB+DESY5 posterior-mean cosmology (matched to $w = -0.975$), the matter power spectrum agrees with the target to **0.24% for k ≲ 1 h/Mpc** and 0.8% to k ≲ 3 h/Mpc over 0 ≤ z ≤ 3, with density stochasticity below 0.02%. Displacements, velocities, redshift-space multipoles, bispectra and halo/void abundances are similarly well reproduced, so constant-$w$ simulation suites and field-level emulators can serve DESI-motivated dynamical dark energy without new runs.

![](figures/2611.29597/R2_ensemble.pdf)

*For most of the 100 CPL models, one constant-$w$ run reproduces P(k) to sub-percent and the density phases to ~10⁻⁴ stochasticity up to the Nyquist scale at z = 0–2; the residual errors grow with the background-matching mismatch ε.*

### [$w_0$ Is All You Need: Field-Level Matching of General Dark Energy Models to $w_0$CDM Simulations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29776v1)

Pavel Demidov, Carolina Cuesta-Lazaro, Peter L. Taylor
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

An independent companion to the paper above: **"spectral equivalence"** picks the $w_0$CDM predictor with the same comoving distance to last scattering, then rescales $A_s$ to match linear growth. With Gadget-III runs, a DESI-mean CPL model, a spline $w(z)$ and an adversarial spline built to maximise the $H(z)$ deviation are all matched to a single $w_0 = -0.882$ simulation. Their **power spectra agree within 0.2% to k = 0.5 and 0.6% to k = 1 h/Mpc**, bispectra within 0.26%/1.75% over the same ranges, wavelet-scattering coefficients within 0.05%, and decorrelation stays below 0.006%, whereas a naive same-$w_0$ guess misses P(k) by 6%. A NUTS toy analysis with a **$w_0$-only emulator reproduces the full $w_0$–$w_a$ posterior**.

### [Modelling of post-reconstruction galaxy clustering with a hybrid bias expansion](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29911v1)

Vicente Izzo Dominguez, Raul E. Angulo, Antonio D. Montero-Dorta et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors apply a single reconstruction displacement to each basis field of a **second-order hybrid Lagrangian bias expansion**, so one set of bias parameters describes clustering both before and after BAO reconstruction. Fitting configuration-space multipoles of SHAMe mocks (real and redshift space, RecIso and RecSym), the model reproduces ξ₀, ξ₂, ξ₄ for **r > 35 h⁻¹Mpc**. In a Fisher forecast for a 3 h⁻³Gpc³ volume at z ≈ 0, post-reconstruction clustering alone tightens Ωm by ~1.3×, while **combining pre- and post-reconstruction cuts σ₈ and Ωm errors by about a third and raises the figure of merit ~2.2×**, mostly by breaking bias–cosmology degeneracies. The framework is designed for emulation and joint full-shape + reconstruction analyses of DESI-like surveys.

### [A hybrid effective field theory description of diffuse baryons](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31069v1)

Ryan Raikman, Kiyoshi Masui, James M. Sullivan et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The diffuse free-electron field is modelled as the N-body matter field advected with **only a linear Lagrangian bias**, justified because ~95% of baryons are diffuse so higher-order terms are suppressed, plus a single-parameter **Lorentzian feedback suppression $1/(1+k^2c_f^2)$**. This two-parameter HEFT model fits the diffuse-gas power spectrum at **~1% accuracy to k = 1 h/Mpc at z = 0.5 (1.5 h/Mpc at z = 1)** across most of the nine FLAMINGO feedback variants, degrading only for the most extreme jet model. Free second-order fits confirm the b₂ and b_G₂ contributions are negligible. The target is FRB dispersion measures and kSZ, but the construction is a close cousin of the hybrid simulation+EFT models now used for the Lyα forest field.

![](figures/2611.31069/fig1_paper_Pdd_over_Pcc_allfeedback_resid_z0.5.pdf)

*Across nine FLAMINGO feedback variants at z = 0.5, the two-parameter fit tracks the feedback-driven suppression of diffuse-gas clustering (P_dd/P_cc) to percent level out to k ≈ 1 h/Mpc.*

### [A Cross-Domain Simulator and Benchmark for Uncertainty Quantification in Global 21-cm Inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30444v1)

Jacob Tutt, Harry Bevins, James Alvey et al. (Bevins is an interest-file high-value author)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors build a differentiable JAX forward model for global 21-cm experiments that joins a neural emulator of 21cmSPACE cosmic-dawn signals, a physics-informed regional foreground model, and a linear SVD surrogate of antenna beam chromaticity trained on electromagnetic simulations, for a **7.2×10⁷ speed-up** (0.14 ms per evaluation on one H100). The 100 beam coefficients are marginalised analytically, leaving 37 sampled parameters. With **GPU nested slice sampling** on 100 unseen injected signals, **94/100 profiles lie entirely within their 95% credible bands**, and 75 posterior means have RMSE below the 25 mK noise. The simulator plus reference chains and evidences are released as a benchmark for calibration, mode coverage and evidence accuracy of samplers, SBI and variational inference.

![](figures/2611.30444/Radio_Galaxy_Signal_Recovery.pdf)

*Representative recoveries ordered by credible level: injected 21-cm profiles fall inside the calibrated 68/95% bands even with foregrounds and beam left free.*

### [Radial mean free path bubble size distribution (RMFP-BSD) from the Epoch of Reionization](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30863v1)

Ivelin Georgiev, Robert Crawford, Saleem Zaroubi et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The paper introduces a **radial mean free path bubble-size statistic**: six axis-aligned rays from a random ionised point to the nearest neutral cell. Unlike the standard Mesinger mean-free-path method, its single-sphere distribution is analytic and bounded to [R/√3, R] (median 0.785R, σ = 0.113R, against σ ≈ 0.44R for mfp). Applied to (349 cMpc)³ radiative-transfer runs, both methods track ionised-region growth, ~15–20 cMpc with Lyman-limit-system opacity versus 50–65 cMpc without. Mapped onto the peak of the neutral-fraction power spectrum, the rmfp interval is **narrower (Δlog₁₀k = 0.54 vs 0.79)**, and the statistic is less fooled by tunnels between **neutral islands at the end of reionization**. It is simulation-only, with no observational forecasts yet.

### [Quasi-hostless LRDs with intrinsically red, faint UV continua](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28209v1)

Callum Witten, Andrea Weibel, Pascal A. Oesch et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

From 132 NIRSpec little red dots at 2 ≲ z ≲ 9, the authors select objects with **$L_{1500}/L_{5100} < 0.03$** and find three new "black hole star"-like sources, more than doubling the sample. As the UV-to-optical ratio drops, **β_UV reddens from −1.8 to −0.5** while [OIII] weakens and Hα/Hβ rises to ~10, which they read as the host fading and leaving the central engine exposed. The red NUV continuum is reproduced by a ~4600 K blackbody behind a **screen of n = 2 hydrogen with $N_{\rm H,n=2}\approx10^{17}$ cm⁻²** plus a weak host, with dense nebular emission needed for the FUV downturn. The model predicts a wavelength-dependent UV morphology as a test.

![](figures/2611.28209/BH_star_like_vs_BH_star_dominated_fnu.pdf)

*Median stacks: quasi-hostless LRDs have a distinctly redder UV slope and weaker forbidden lines than both ordinary LRDs and BH*-dominated objects, which look alike in UV and optical shape.*

### [LeJEPA Features Are Not Isotropic, and Should Not Be](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31216v1)

Richeek Das, Pratik Chaudhari
**Primary category:** cs.CV | also: cs.LG

The authors show, theoretically and empirically, that LeJEPA's SIGReg regulariser **cannot detect rank-collapsed embeddings**: it accepts 90% of batches confined to half the dimensions, and adding slices does not fix this. They argue the alignment term should instead couple embedding magnitude to ambiguity. Their replacement, **PolarReg**, analytically matches the isotropic-Gaussian marginals of embedding magnitude and pairwise directions, with one hyperparameter setting for all experiments. On ImageNet-1k it reaches LeJEPA/VISReg accuracy in **4.7× fewer epochs**, and embedding magnitude doubles as an out-of-domain/confidence score (AUROC 0.80–0.95), which is relevant to the choice of SSL objective for spectral transformers.

![](figures/2611.31216/fig1.pdf)

*LeJEPA embeddings are not isotropic: ambiguous images far from their class centre have smaller magnitudes (left), and building that coupling into the regulariser lets PolarReg reach LeJEPA's final accuracy in 86 epochs (right).*

### [Pretraining Representation Lottery Determines Fine-tuning Generalization](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28777v1)

Fuming Yang, Core Francisco Park
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Across 278 small transformers that differ only in seed and reach the same pretraining loss to within 5%, out-of-distribution fine-tuning generalisation varies **7-fold**. Causal interventions pin this on representation geometry: injecting the true latent coordinates lifts generalisation from 0.17 to 0.90, and **distilling a good seed's deep-layer representations** into a bad seed helps, while distilling its output behaviour does not. Representation regularisation during fine-tuning stops poor representations from fitting the task by reshaping themselves. The distillation effect reproduces in Pythia-160M/410M, but **only from deep layers**, so pretraining loss alone is a poor proxy for downstream usefulness.

### [Vitrify Exact Learning: exact differential equations, changes of variable and closed forms from numerical data](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30886v1)

Benedict Irwin, Alexander Mathiasen, Tom Etminan
**Primary category:** cs.SC | also: cs.LG

Instead of searching expression trees, this symbolic-regression method assumes the target is **holonomic**, i.e. annihilated by a linear ODE with polynomial coefficients, possibly after a change of variable. It finds that operator as a null vector of **weak-form measurements** (data integrated against test functions, never differentiated), makes the coefficients exact, validates on held-out data, and solves for a closed form. On noise-free benchmarks it recovers **108/120 AI Feynman equations** (best competitor 69) and 74% of SRBench Feynman problems, plus Meijer-G and hypergeometric forms such as Schwarzschild light deflection. It mostly fails with multivariate noise, sparse data or ODE order above four, and the full code stays on a commercial server.

![](figures/2611.30886/fresnel_fits.pdf)

*Six of seven SR codes fit the Fresnel integral C(x) to within a few percent on the training range but are 27–167% wrong just beyond it; Vitrify recovers C(x) itself via its ODE and extrapolates with no error.*

### [Statistical mechanics of linear classification on hierarchical tree data](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28824v1)

Jerome Garnier-Brun, Federica Kulka, Carlo Lucibello
**Primary category:** cond-mat.dis-nn | also: cs.LG

A linear classifier learns to infer the root of a binary-tree broadcast process from its 2^ℓ leaves. Because the data stay non-Gaussian along the signal direction even at infinite depth, Gaussian-equivalence theory fails, so the authors keep the exact census law there in a **finite-depth replica theory** that matches simulations down to sample ratios ~10⁻⁴. Between ε⋆ ≈ 0.080 and the Kesten–Stigum threshold ≈ 0.146 they find a **scale-separated double descent**: the first descent at P ~ N^ω is set by the hierarchical data statistics alone, while the overfitting peak at P ~ N depends on regularisation.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Continuum Pumping Revises Metallicity in the Orion Nebula](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30268v1)

Yuguang Chen, Tucker Jones, Ryan Sanders et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Deep Keck/KCWI spectroscopy of M42 detects 28 O II recombination lines, and the O⁺⁺/H⁺ inferred from each line correlates with its modelled **continuum-pumping (fluorescence) fraction**: pumping supplies ≳50% even of the V1 multiplet, at least twice previous estimates. Extrapolating to zero pumping gives **12 + log(O⁺⁺/H⁺) = 8.14 (+0.11/−0.15)**, consistent with the collisional-line value of 8.31 ± 0.06, so the long-standing abundance discrepancy factor in Orion is explained without temperature fluctuations. Reanalysing five other H II regions, the **mean ADF falls from 0.25 to 0.12 dex**, which means recombination-line metallicities, long considered the more reliable scale, are biased high.

![](figures/2611.30268/fig_corrected_m42_adf.pdf)

*After correcting for continuum pumping, the O II recombination-line abundances drop by ≃0.25 dex to agree with the collisional-line values, removing the ADF in M42.*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [HEP-Ex-Bench: Evaluating Agentic Performance in High Energy Physics Experiment Data Analyses with Sealed Worlds](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28194v1)

Andrzej Novák, Yiyang Zhao, Dolores Garcia et al.
**Primary category:** physics.data-an

This benchmark tests AI agents on real experimental-physics analysis without an LLM judge. Each agent builds a pipeline on a public world made from CMS open data, and the frozen pipeline is then rerun on many **sealed worlds with hidden injected truths**, scored against an expert reference analysis on accuracy, pull/coverage and quality. Across four tasks (H→ZZ signal strength, Higgs width, lepton and jet calibration), 11 models, 3 prompt tiers and 3 repeats, the **best agent reaches 60 ± 8% of the expert reference** and no open-weight model exceeds 10%. Agents systematically under-cover their uncertainties, and an expert plan in the prompt brings no gain. The blind-analysis/injection design transfers directly to evaluating agent-run cosmology pipelines.

![](figures/2611.28194/blueprint_schematic.pdf)

*An agent's pipeline is frozen and rerun on sealed worlds with hidden truths, so it is scored on recovering injected values within its own quoted uncertainties rather than on one result that could be recalled.*

### [MAASAI: A Tool-Augmented Multi-Agent System for Astronomical Data Analysis](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31088v1)

S. Riggi, A. Ingallinera, C. Bordiu et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

MAASAI is a graph-based multi-agent system that leaves orchestration to a locally served open-weight model and hands the science computation to established radio-astronomy tools (source finders, classifiers, archive queries) plus a RAG literature base. Against a commercial-model baseline it raises **macro-F1 from 0.53 to 0.72** for extended-emission classification and from 0.37 to 0.88 for morphology classification, although the end-to-end design does not isolate which component drives the gains. The more transferable finding is from its 94-question literature benchmark: **chance-corrected agreement between human experts, and between humans and the LLM judge, is near zero** (Krippendorff α −0.03 to 0.31), which undercuts LLM-as-judge evaluation of science assistants.
