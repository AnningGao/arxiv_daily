## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Fast and accurate large-volume Lyman-$α$ forest mocks with three-dimensional U-Net trained on IllustrisTNG and FLAMINGO](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27151v1)

Bhaskar Arya, Soumak Maitra, Shadab Alam
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

A **field-level 3D U-Net maps gravity-only density and line-of-sight velocity fields (2LPT or N-body) to Lyα optical depth** at z ~ 3. It is trained in small boxes and applied to much larger independent ones: TNG 110 → 300 cMpc and FLAMINGO 1 → 2.8 cGpc. At k ≤ 1 Mpc⁻¹ it reproduces the hydro **P1D to 2.7% (TNG, N-body), 2.1% (TNG, 2LPT) and 2.2% (FLAMINGO)**, where FGPA misses by 17–25%. It also gets the bispectrum to ~4–5% and the connected trispectrum to 10–16%, even though neither is in the loss. For scale, TNG and FLAMINGO themselves differ by ~15% in P1D and ~40% in the trispectrum, and a full 2.8 cGpc box takes about 3 GPU-hours against ~31 million CPU-hours of hydro. Limits: one redshift, one cosmology and thermal history per suite, noiseless skewers with no continuum or DLAs, grids too coarse to converge at high k, and poorer transfer between the two suites (~10% off in P1D on large scales).

![](figures/2611.27151/combined_pdf_p1d_epochband.pdf)

*In both test volumes the emulated flux PDF and P1D track the hydro reference to a few percent, including the shaded modes longer than the training box that the network never saw. FGPA deviates strongly; the main ML failure is too few near-transparent pixels (F ≈ 1) in FLAMINGO.*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Little Red Dots at Cosmic Noon: Spectroscopy Reveals Declining Abundance but a Non-Evolving Luminosity Function Shape](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26685v1)

Yilun Ma, Daniel J. Lyon, Jenny E. Greene et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

From 266 photometric LRD candidates in 19.9 deg² of the HSC-SSP Deep/UltraDeep fields, the authors calibrate sample purity with spectroscopy of 24 targets, giving a tentative ~25–30%. That yields a **number density of $6.8^{+1.57}_{-1.08}\times10^{-7}$ cMpc⁻³ at 2.10 < z < 2.55 (M₅₅₀₀ < −20.5)**, roughly **35× below the flat z ~ 4–8 JWST abundance**. The luminosity-function knee stays near M₅₅₀₀ ≈ −22, 50–100× fainter than the quasar knee, with **no significant evolution in LF shape from z ~ 5 to z ~ 2**. On this evidence only the abundance of LRDs evolves, not the shape of the LF. The purity and LF slopes rest on small numbers; Roman should test this.

### [Little Red Dots at Cosmic Noon: Spectroscopic Identification and Broad-Line Properties](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26710v1)

Daniel J. Lyon, Yilun Ma, Ivo Labbe et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

This is the companion spectroscopy paper. Keck/NIRES, X-shooter and FIRE spectra of 24 HSC-selected candidates **confirm broad Hα in 6**, all best fit by **exponential line profiles (FWHM ≈ 950–2150 km/s)**. **Four of the six resemble JWST-selected LRDs**: their Balmer decrements exceed Case B (Hα/Hβ ≈ 8–34, Hβ undetected), three show Balmer absorption, and a Chandra stack makes them X-ray weak by at least ~5×. One source is independently confirmed as an LRD with archival JWST data, and another is an ordinary X-ray-detected broad-line AGN. Ground-based selection can find LRDs at z ~ 2–3, but 14 of the 18 non-detections have only photometric redshifts, and the black-hole masses (8–24 × 10⁶ M☉) are uncertain by 1–2 dex.

### [Physics-Guided Recursive Self-Improvement of Amortized Bayesian Inference Across Physics](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27006v1)

Bo Liang, Mingtao Chen, Minghui Du et al.
**Primary category:** physics.comp-ph | also: astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.IM

PhyRSI couples an AI agent to a field's simulator and likelihood. The agent writes and revises an amortized inference algorithm, choosing its coordinates, symmetries and factorizations, and is **scored only by importance weights against the exact likelihood**, because the true posterior is unknown. Across six fields (exoplanet orbits, neutron-star EOS with a factorized 32D joint fit, X-ray reflectivity, neuron models and others), **36 real-data posteriors pass the check and agree with converged MCMC about as closely as two independent MCMC chains agree** (mean JSD 0.13–0.66 vs 0.11–0.77 mnat). They take **0.4–3.6 s each, versus ~90 days of 4-core MCMC for all 36**. Humans set the tasks and blinding, software keeps reference chains hidden from the agent, and every version was frozen before running on real data. The weight check is necessary but not sufficient: it cannot see modes no draw reaches. It also needs a likelihood fast enough to evaluate ~10⁵ times per second.

![](figures/2611.27006/fig1_overview.pdf)

*The loop: the agent proposes an inference algorithm, scores it with likelihood weights and revises it until it passes on held-out simulations. The evolved network then returns each real posterior in seconds, checked against the same likelihood.*

### [Diffusing the assumptions: towards realistic LISA stochastic component separation with physics-aware simulation-based inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27848v1)

Iuliu Cuceu, Nelson Christensen, Astrid Lamberts et al.
**Primary category:** gr-qc | also: astro-ph.IM

The method is SBI with the **Simformer, an all-conditional transformer trained with diffusion or flow matching whose attention mask encodes the simulator's dependence graph**. It reads the full TDI cross-spectral matrix through a physics-aware embedding and separates instrument noise, the Galactic foreground and a faint SGWB. With unequal arms and per-link noise, the networks **match 17D MCMC posteriors after importance reweighting**, and they **stay unbiased when marginalizing glitches or MBHB transients up to SNR of several hundred**, where likelihood fits that ignore those transients are badly biased. With time-varying noise, realistic glitch rates and gaps of up to a day, coverage stays nominal or conservative across ~10⁴ simulations. Flow matching is slightly better calibrated than diffusion. The paper also introduces a localized, importance-weighted SBC test that transfers to any SBI pipeline. Everything is on simulated data.

### [Weight-space programming and mechanistic interpretability for model editing in particle physics](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27079v1)

Raphaël Bonnet-Guerrini, Johann Ioannou-Nikolaides, Troels Petersen
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: cs.LG

This is the closest thing today to the QGPT problem. A **self-supervised jet foundation model pretrained on mixed datasets learned a dataset-identity shortcut**: adding one soft photon carrying 10⁻³ of the jet p_T drops W-tagging efficiency from 80% to 54%. The authors **locate the flaw in specific attention heads** (head b7/6 gives the added photon 57% of summary-token attention) and **trace it to pretraining alongside JetClass-II, which has lower reconstruction thresholds**. They repair it two ways: **ablating three SAE latents** (1,533 parameters changed, 11% of the response left), or **letting Claude agents edit weights directly with ≤ 50 queries and no gradients** (5% left). Both beat LoRA fine-tuning on parameters changed, and the fix carries over to all nine taggers and to a newly trained regression head. The agent edits vary a lot between runs, and SAE edits work less well on the larger models.

*Before the repair, JetClass jets with one added soft photon land in the JetClass-II cluster (86% of nearest neighbours). After the agent's weight edit they sit with their own W/QCD jets (3%). The two UMAP panels are fitted separately and do not share axes.*

### [Parasite Features: Causal Abstraction Through Spurious Pathways](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26711v1)

Denis Sutter, Qing Yao, Sasha Boguraev et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Interpretability methods based on causal abstraction, such as Distributed Alignment Search, can **pass interchange-intervention tests by linking a direction that discriminates inputs to an unrelated direction that steers the output**. These "parasite features" make a variable look causal when the model does not actually use it. The paper gives sufficient conditions for this to happen with linear alignment maps. It then finds parasites in a deliberately nonsensical agreement task in pythia-1b and in the **carry-one variable of Llama-3.1-8B two-digit addition, taken from the MIB benchmark**. **Ablating the discovered carry subspace leaves accuracy at 98.8% (vs 99.1% baseline)**. The lesson for any probing or steering analysis of a spectral foundation model: high intervention accuracy is necessary but not sufficient evidence of mechanism.

![](figures/2611.26711/fig1.pdf)

*Projecting onto subspaces separates inputs by feature (B), but a linear alignment map can chain causally unrelated discriminating and steering directions (C), so interventions look causal when no such relationship exists in the model.*

### [Neural Geometry as a Learning Signal](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26832v1)

Eric Todd, Tal Haklay, Nikhil Prakash et al.
**Primary category:** cs.CL | also: cs.LG

The authors **fine-tune Llama-3.1-8B with LoRA using only a loss on the geometry of an internal representation**, pulling class centroids toward an idealized Fourier ring or helix, with **no supervision on outputs**. On month arithmetic, geometry-only training reaches **73.3% vs 71.9% for standard task fine-tuning** (base 36.3%). On fixed-width line breaking it cuts the mean error from 13.5 to 7.3 characters, against 4.6 for the task LoRA. **Patching the ideal geometry into the base model barely helps (36.3% → 37.3%)**, so the benefit comes from changes beyond the supervised subspace. This shows mechanistic understanding used as a training signal where output labels are scarce. It needs a known target geometry, and both tasks already had a mechanistic account.

### [Information-Criterion-Driven, Non-linear Deprojection of Angular Imaging Systematics for the SPHEREx Galaxy Clustering Sample](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26864v1)

James R. Cheshire, Sean T. Bruton, Charuhas W. Shiveshwarkar
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Template deprojection for SPHEREx's f_NL program, with **non-linear but constrained Legendre/B-spline regression bases** and **automated template selection by information criteria using two independent selectors (penalized ElasticNet and IFS) whose agreement serves as a consistency check**. A separate, unbiased OLS or maximum-likelihood regression then does the cleaning. On lognormal mocks the hybrid selectors **pick spurious templates 4–5× less often than ElasticNetCV on clean maps (>10× less on contaminated ones)** and recover 5σ contaminants in 97–100% of realizations. Large-scale power loss in the lowest bin is 6–21%, against 23.6% when all 42 templates are deprojected. The approach carries over to imaging systematics in DESI quasar and LSS samples. Caveats: linear-power lognormal mocks only, a 7-template kit, purely angular contamination, and no modelling of template errors.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Nearly pristine gas and hard ionizing radiation one billion years after the Big Bang](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27979v1)

Takahiro Morishita, Massimo Stiavelli, Tommaso Treu et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Deep JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy of AMORE6, a strongly lensed galaxy at z = 5.725 behind Abell 2744, detects **He II λ1640 at 16.7σ and λ4686 at 4.0σ** together with only weak [O III]. The inferred **12 + log(O/H) = 5.6 ± 0.1, about 8 × 10⁻⁴ solar**, is about 1 dex below the extrapolated z ~ 6 mass–metallicity relation. The He II/Hβ ratios match the hard spectra expected from Pop III stars. The source is tiny (M_UV ≈ −14.5, R_e ≲ 36 pc, M★ ~ 10⁵ M☉), so **pockets of near-pristine gas evidently survive to the end of reionization**. An accreting ~10³–10⁴ M☉ black hole cannot be excluded, and this is a single object.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [PLATON 7: An ultra-fast exoplanet atmospheric retrieval code](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26680v1)

Michael Zhang, Tyler Fairnington, Yayaati Chachan et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.EP

This is a widely used retrieval code **rewritten end-to-end by an AI coding agent (Claude Fable 5 in Claude Code)** with minimal human guidance. The port moves it to JAX in single precision, and other models (Codex, Claude /code-review) reviewed the changes. The result is **~60× faster than PLATON 6** (0.35 ms per R = 20k spectrum on one RTX 5090). The paper is candid about an earlier failed attempt with Opus 4.6, which silently re-enabled float64 and declared fp32 unusable. The authors conclude that frontier LLMs **"already outperform nearly all, if not all, astronomers at writing correct and fast code."** Validation is unit tests plus AI review, with no quantitative accuracy benchmark beyond "negligible" loss, and human readability was deliberately de-emphasized.

### [Agent-guided construction of Feynman integral bases](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26688v1)

Pau Petit Rosas, William J. Torres Bobadilla
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: hep-th

Two "skills" (instructions plus ~6–9k lines of mostly Mathematica code) let **Claude Opus 5.5 work autonomously for days** to find canonical-form bases for frontier two-loop integral families. The work is trustworthy because **every proposed change passes a deterministic, exact-in-ε verification gate**. On a two-loop five-point family (232 master integrals, 18 elliptic sectors), the agent drove the ε-pole census **from 23,254 entries to zero over ~155 accepted changes in eight days**. It also identified a K3 sector in the jet-function family and reached ε-factorized form there. Human input was a few remarks plus one substantive correction, and the **compute bill was ~US$1.8k and ~$1.0k at list price**. This is a template for agent-driven research in any field with a cheap, exact correctness check.
