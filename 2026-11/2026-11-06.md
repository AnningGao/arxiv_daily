### [The box within the Universe: anisotropies in periodic cosmological simulations I. Dark-matter-only simulations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08223v1)

Gábor Rácz, Till Sawala
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The periodic Green function of a cubic $T^3$ box is not rotationally invariant, so the box itself acts as an **anisotropic tidal field that pushes structure to align with the Cartesian axes**. Using **498 DM-only N-body runs with box sizes from 6.25 to 100 Mpc** at fixed mass and force resolution, the authors measure this anisotropy in the matter power spectrum, the (sub)halo mass function, merger rates, halo shapes and alignments, and filament orientations. Through non-linear mode coupling, the box-scale preference **propagates to scales far below L**. Small boxes (L ≲ 25 Mpc) have biased halo concentrations and shapes at z = 0, and even L = 100 Mpc shows a percent-level power deficit. They recommend L ≥ 100 Mpc, stopping small boxes before the fundamental mode goes non-linear, or zoom-ins. The analysis is at late times and DM-only, so whether this matters for the z = 2–5 forest boxes still has to be checked; the "Paper I: dark-matter-only" title points to a hydrodynamical follow-up.

*Projected matter density at z = 0 in randomly chosen 12.5 Mpc periodic boxes, each centred on its most massive halo: most filaments line up with the faces of the box instead of being oriented at random.*

### [Latest $ν$s from the Earliest Galaxies: Probing neutrino self-interactions with JWST](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07013v1)

Ranjini Mondol, Priyank Parashari, Anirban Das et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

Self-interacting neutrinos ($G_{\rm eff}(\bar\nu\nu)(\bar\nu\nu)$) free-stream later, which leaves scale-dependent enhancement and suppression in P(k) instead of a WDM-like cutoff. The authors propagate this through the halo mass function and a galaxy–halo model into **the JWST UV luminosity function at z ≳ 9**. They fit it jointly with Planck 2018 and Pantheon in MontePython, marginalising over Σm_ν and the astrophysical parameters. **Planck + JWST gives Log₁₀(G_eff/MeV⁻²) < −2.75 (95%) for one interacting flavour**, against −2.00 from Planck alone. With all three flavours interacting the limit is < −3.41, only a marginal gain over Planck. This is the same small-scale-power logic as the forest WDM/ν constraints, applied to a much higher-redshift tracer whose galaxy–halo modelling is far less certain.

![](figures/2611.07013/All_constraints_first_plot.pdf)

*95% upper limits on the self-interaction strength from Planck alone (blue) and Planck + JWST UVLFs (red), for flavour-specific and flavour-universal coupling: adding the JWST UVLF tightens the flavour-specific bound by about 0.75 dex.*

### [Viable Phantom Crossing in Softly Broken Shift Symmetric Gravity](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07035v1)

Rodrigo Calderon, Eric V. Linder
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

A minimal Horndeski model, with only a kinetic term K and a braiding term G₃, **softly breaks shift symmetry through exponential field dependence**. It has no cosmological constant, no potential and no non-minimal coupling. Fitted to DESI DR2 BAO, supernovae and full Planck CMB spectra, it **crosses w = −1 at z ≈ 0.5** and reproduces the w₀w_a phenomenology. It fits better than w₀w_a CDM by **Δχ² ≈ −3 (Δln Z ≈ 3) with the same number of effective parameters**, and G_eff deviations stay modest but testable with growth data. It gives a physically motivated, stable alternative to the phenomenological w₀w_a for interpreting the DR2 dark-energy preference.

### [Joint analysis of the Macquart relation and the dispersion measure power spectrum: cross-covariance and redshift self-calibration](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08481v1)

Amy Wayland, Robert Reischke
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors derive, in the halo model, the cross-covariance between individual FRB dispersion measures (the Macquart relation) and the angular DM power spectrum. They show it is **a super-sample effect: only the DM monopole correlates with the C_ℓ**, which gives an exact, model-independent bound on the correlation. Validated on 5000 log-normal catalogues of 10⁴ FRBs, the correlation is at the per-cent level, and **ignoring it changes the χ² width by only 0.8%**. In a joint LSST-like weak lensing + FRB forecast, adding the Macquart relation **tightens Ω_b h² by 32% and the mean host DM by a factor 4.1**. The n(z) of FRBs without identified hosts can be self-calibrated by inverting their DMs. It is a clean example of a super-sample covariance bound for line-of-sight-integrated baryon tracers.

### [Detection of Differential Chromatic Refraction with LSST Camera data](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07161v1)

Matthew J. Temple, Phoebe N. Lister
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.GA

LSSTCam has no atmospheric dispersion corrector. The authors fit airmass-dependent astrometric offsets for **10,470 quasars and galaxies in the COSMOS field of Rubin Data Preview 2** and report the **first detection of differential chromatic refraction in LSST data**. They convert the offsets into a per-band characteristic wavelength λ_DCR that encodes where the flux sits inside each passband. For quasars, λ_DCR tracks broad Lyα, C IV and Mg II moving through the u, g and r bands with redshift, and it is **orthogonal to the u−g colour**. That makes it a free, extra SED measurement for quasar selection and photo-z degeneracy breaking. The signal survives only because DP2 astrometry is not chromatically corrected, and the authors warn that future releases should keep it.

*u−g colour against redshift for the quasar sample, coloured by the u-band λ_DCR: at fixed colour, λ_DCR still changes systematically as Lyα and C IV cross the band, so DCR adds information that the colours alone do not carry.*

### [Towards interpreting any residual stream direction by scaling activation inversion](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08465v1)

Celeste De Schamphelaere, Juan J. Vazquez, Ari Spiesberger et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The authors train a "maximally-activating example metamodel" (MAEM): a fine-tune of the frozen subject LM that takes a residual-stream direction v and writes text whose activations align with v. It is trained by SFT and then **RL that directly maximises the cosine between v and the subject model's own activation**, so the reward needs no learned judge and every exemplar is checkable with one forward pass. A Qwen3.6-27B MAEM beats corpus search and natural-language autoencoders at inverting held-out activations and **fires 89% of held-out SAE features**. It transfers to other languages and code, explains unseen steering vectors and recovers LoRA-backdoor payloads. Its failures concentrate on the rarest SAE features, which is the residual case where interpretation is hardest. It is a reusable recipe for the "what does this direction mean" question in any transformer.

### [Shaping & Monitoring Models through their Internal Representations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07071v1)

Thomas Fel, Matthew Kowal, Matt Feiszli et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The authors give a single framework for training against an internal concept direction: a difference-of-means direction at a chosen layer, penalised either through attributions ("reliance shaping") or through a per-token reward ("expression shaping"). Applied up to 32B parameters, it **removes spurious-feature reliance in DINOv2/Waterbirds and CheXpert, reduces sycophancy after DPO, and suppresses reward hacking under GRPO**. The core result is analytic and confirmed empirically. Under this pressure the concept is **either preserved along the monitored direction, re-encoded along a new linearly readable direction, or erased from the linear readout while staying available downstream**. Which outcome you get follows predictably from the objective, and refitted linear probes usually still detect the shaped concept. The mechanics of linear erasure (INLP, LEACE) versus attribution penalties carry over directly to null-space-style editing of scientific-model representations.

### [Localization Does Not Imply Steerability: A Mechanistic Study of Sign Errors in LLM Mathematics](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07478v1)

Shradha Agarwal, Tariq J., Deepak Rajbhar
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The paper studies single wrong sign tokens in determinant and integration-by-parts computations across five model families (14B–675B). Using logit lens and direct logit attribution, it shows that **the sign is committed late, by nameable MLP "habit" layers, along a single direction**. Subtracting that direction flips most held-out errors in some models (13 of 14 in Llama-70B). Yet **the same intervention at equally well-localized layers ranges from 4% to 93% steerability**, a spread predicted in a preregistration before measurement. Phi-4 resists because the sign is written redundantly by stamp/amplifier layers and opposed by rescue layers. The practical lesson for interpretability methods: a sharp localization map is not yet a working lever, and validating a circuit by editing it tests the network's cooperation as well as the localization.

### [Transport along Local Annealing Paths: Wasserstein Geometry and Multimodal Sampling](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05605v1)

Han Chen, Sifan Liu
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: stat.ML

The authors show that **geometric annealing paths between a reference and a multimodal target can take long detours in Wasserstein space**: mass has to travel between well-separated modes. This slows transport-map learning and drives mode collapse in flows trained along the path. Their **componentwise annealed transport (CAT)** splits the target into an exact mixture induced by a mixture base distribution. It learns a separate flow per component and estimates component weights separately, so the weights stay consistent even when the flows are imperfect. On IOHMM, LDA and skew-mixture posteriors, CAT gives higher importance-weight ESS and ELBO than geometric annealed transport and reverse-KL CNFs at equal wall-clock. It applies to any annealed or tempered flow sampler for multimodal cosmological posteriors.

### [Big Models, Small Gains: Frozen Forecasters as Classifiers on MONSTER](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06988v1)

Rishit Jain, Rishit Kar, Chetashri Bhadane et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Frozen embeddings from two pretrained time-series forecasters (Chronos-Bolt, Reverso) are tested as classification features on the large MONSTER datasets. The study includes **random-weight controls** and strong non-pretrained baselines (MiniRocket, Quant, MultiRocket) across label budgets. Pretraining clearly helps relative to the same network with random weights. But **the frozen features lose to the best non-pretrained baseline in 15 of 17 settings and only tie in the other two**, with the gap largest at low label budgets. Adding back the amplitude statistics removed by per-series normalisation recovers part of the gap. This is a cautionary benchmark design (random-weight control, strong classical baseline) and a warning about normalisation discarding information, both directly applicable to evaluating a quasar-spectrum foundation model.

### [MapHub: Daily Relevance Heatmaps of New arXiv Preprints for a Research Community](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07174v1)

Kaili Cao, Camille Avestruz, Feige Wang
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: cs.DL, cs.IR

MapHub has an LLM (on a Claude subscription) score every new arXiv paper 0–100 against editable portfolios for each member of a research community. It publishes the scores as a public heatmap, with one row per paper and one column per participant. Over 70 listings (11,932 papers), **about 6% of a day's astro-ph papers reach 30 for a given participant and 0.4% reach 60**. In inverse-probability-weighted blind ratings, **the top 10% by score held about 70% of the raters' own top 5%, against about a third for a text-matching baseline**. The validation is small: the first author built the tool and rated three of the five columns. It is a direct point of comparison for this digest's own design.

### [Scientist World: Grow a Research World for AI-Scientists](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08478v1)

Taiming Lu, Sachin Konan, Zhuang Liu
**Primary category:** cs.AI | also: cs.CL, cs.LG

An agent pipeline turns **1,000 ML papers (selected from 124,816 indexed) into executable, scored research environments**, each with a briefing, data, a reproduced baseline and a scoring script. They are used both to evaluate and to train AI-scientist agents, and the release includes 6,400 hours of research traces. Frontier agents **beat the reproduced method in 84% of "improve" and 74% of "innovate" attempts**. Note that the comparison is against the authors' reproduction in a scaled-down setting, not the published numbers. It is a concrete step toward literature-derived training worlds for automated research, currently ML-only.

### [Comment on the recent claims of H0 measurements with the ZTF DR2 SN Ia sample (arXiv:2610.36051)](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07794v1)

Mickael Rigault, Brodie Popovic, Ariel Goobar et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The ZTF SN Ia cosmology team responds to a recent H₀ measurement that combined the public ZTF DR2 sample with SH0ES calibrators. They point out that **the absolute photometric zero point of ZTF DR2 is not yet established** and cannot be derived from public data. **The SH0ES-to-ZTF zero-point offset is fully degenerate with H₀**, and the photometric corrections in the original paper do not address it. They conclude **the manuscript "is in error and does not represent a measurement of H₀"**, while pure-ZTF analyses remain internally consistent. A fully calibrated release is in preparation. It is a useful public example of how releasing data before calibration can lead to premature cosmology claims.
