## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [The Cool Circumgalactic Echo of the Stellar-to-Halo Mass Relatio](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12734v1)

Zeyu Chen, Enci Wang, Huiyuan Wang et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using DESI Y1 galaxy–quasar pairs (ELGs, BGS and LRGs), the authors stack quasar spectra to map **cool CGM Mg II λλ2796,2803 absorption** across nearly three decades in stellar mass. The radial profile $W_{\rm MgII}\propto D^k$ flattens steadily with mass, following **$k = 0.70\log(M_*/M_\odot) - 9.12$**. The absorption normalization peaks at **$M_*\sim3\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$, the same scale as the peak of the stellar-to-halo mass relation**, and at that mass the stacked line widths switch from super-virial to sub-virial kinematics. Massive halos show suppressed inner absorption, consistent with heating by a hot halo or AGN, but still carry substantial Mg II farther out. This is a DESI-scale, quasar-absorption measurement of the CGM, made with the same sightline machinery as forest work.

![](figures/2611.12734/forD.png)

*Mg II equivalent width falls more steeply with projected distance around low-mass galaxies than around massive ones; the inset traces the radial slope $k$ rising linearly with $\log M_*$ across all three DESI tracer classes.*

### [Search for a Dispersion Measure Excess Toward Nearby Galaxy Halos Using Fast Radio Bursts from the Second CHIME/FRB Baseband Catalog](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12860v1)

Affan Khadir, Ayush Pandhi, Jason Hessels et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The authors stack 1748 sub-arcminute-localized CHIME/FRB baseband bursts against foreground galaxy halos to measure **the ionized CGM through dispersion measure**. Within 50 Mpc they find a **mean DM excess of 119 ± 44 pc cm⁻³ inside one virial radius (~2.6σ)**, with a tentative decline with impact parameter driven by Milky Way-mass galaxies. Fitting an MCMC power-law electron-density profile gives **$\beta = 0.68^{+0.15}_{-0.16}$**, consistent with X-ray stacking. A DM deficit at very small impact parameter suggests that about 20 bursts come from these nearby galaxies themselves, so CHIME detects more low-z FRBs than assumed, and they forecast a 5σ CGM detection with 10⁴ FRBs. Together with the Mg II paper above, it gives a complementary, phase-independent view of the baryons around galaxies.

### [Coupled dark energy and apparent phantom crossing: a potential-independent lower bound](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12792v1)

Domenico Sapone, Esteban Arratia
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

If the DESI DR2 preference for a $w=-1$ crossing comes from a canonical quintessence field coupled to dark matter, the expansion history alone fixes **a minimum dark-matter mass loss since recombination and hence a minimum coupling $\beta_{\rm min}[H(z)]$, for any potential**. For the DESI DR2 $w_0w_a$ best fits, dark matter must lose 5–7% of its mass by $z\simeq0.8$, which requires **$\beta_{\rm min}=0.047$–$0.058$, or $|\beta|>0.025$–$0.038$ at 95% CL** from the public chains. If the field stays frozen until $z\simeq2.5$, the required coupling reaches the CMB upper limits. The bound also fixes a minimum size for two observables: a dark-matter fifth force of at least a few tenths of a percent of gravity, and **a lensing-to-clustering deficit of 1–2.5% at 95% CL**, which DESI × Euclid/Rubin can test.

![](figures/2611.12792/fig_background.pdf)

*For the DESI DR2 backgrounds, the minimum required mass loss peaks at $z\simeq0.8$, where the field comes to rest, not at the $w=-1$ crossing itself. That turnaround point is what sets the coupling bound.*

### [FAIR Universe Weak Lensing ML Uncertainty Challenge: Dataset and Cosmological Inference Competition](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10228v1)

Biwei Dai, Po-Wen Chang, Erwan Allys et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: cs.LG

This paper describes the NeurIPS 2025 benchmark for **cosmological inference from weak-lensing convergence maps under realistic systematics**. The maps come from N-body ray-tracing with baryonic feedback and photo-z biases injected, and only a modest number of cosmologies is available for training. Scoring rewards both accuracy and calibrated uncertainty. Ingredients that recur among the winners are **domain-specific data augmentation (including conditional optimal transport), ensembling, ViT backbones and physics-informed features**, plus one AI-agent-driven inference pipeline. A Phase 2 test set made entirely of held-out cosmologies is being kept private. It is a useful reference for anyone doing field-level or summary-based SBI with a small, expensive simulation suite, the same situation Lyα emulators are in.

### [Amortized Opacity Marginalization Improves C/O Interval Calibration for Brown-Dwarf Retrievals](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01665v3)

Kellen Heraty
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: cs.LG

The paper moves systematics marginalization into the simulator. Opacities are randomized while the training set is generated, using **smooth multiplicative Fourier fields in log ν plus discrete line-list swaps**, so that **a neural posterior estimator learns an already-marginalized posterior**. On perturbed held-out spectra, nominal 90% C/O intervals cover the truth **43% of the time for a standard NPE versus 82–89% for the marginalized network**, checked with SBC/TARP. An ablation shows the continuous field does most of the work. Training with real JWST bad-pixel masks is needed to keep coverage on deployed NIRSpec data. The recipe carries straight over to marginalizing continuum or thermal-nuisance errors in forest SBI.

### [Two-Phase Rapid Simulation-Based Inference with Differentiable Simulators](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12422v1)

Uros Zivanovic, Rahul Srinivasan, Roberto Trotta et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: stat.ML

RSBI first uses **a distance computed through a differentiable simulator as a one-shot surrogate likelihood**, which yields an efficient proposal without sequential rounds. A **Schrödinger-bridge diffusion sampler**, warm-started from that proposal, then moves it to the true posterior, with its energy corrected by a neural likelihood ratio trained only where the proposal puts mass. Because the method is variational, it avoids the prior leakage of sequential NPE. Optional well-tempered metadynamics improves mode coverage. Performance holds up when the prior is much larger than the posterior, degrading **only ~5% on Two Moons on a tight simulation budget**, and the method is demonstrated on black-hole ringdown posteriors.

### [Decomposing Earth Embeddings with Sparse Autoencoders](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11398v1)

Vitus Benson, Fanny Yang, Markus Reichstein
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The authors train **sparse autoencoders on the embeddings of two geospatial foundation models** (AlphaEarth, Tessera) with three SAE variants. They score them with a protocol suited to non-language data: reconstruction at matched sparsity, spatial scale, and concept alignment. The key control is **an SAE with a frozen random decoder**. It shows that **sparsity alone already makes features look specific and monosemantic, while only a learned dictionary reconstructs the embedding** (8× less unexplained variance at 16 active features). The authors therefore argue that interpretability scores should be reported against that baseline. The protocol transfers directly to interpreting a quasar-spectrum transformer's latent space.

### [Fast Accurate Quantum Monte Carlo without Metropolis Adjustment](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10187v1)

Reuben Cohn-Gordon, Gabriel Pescia, Sumner N. Hearth et al. (incl. Robnik, Seljak)
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The paper tests **unadjusted HMC, with no Metropolis–Hastings step,** on a hard, high-dimensional spin-fermion model of a quantum phase transition. It uses automated step-size tuning to keep asymptotic bias small relative to Monte Carlo error. **Unadjusted samplers beat adjusted HMC in effective sample size per gradient call while reproducing the physical observables**, and the bias can be estimated after sampling without a ground truth. The physics is condensed matter, but this is the Robnik–Seljak unadjusted/microcanonical sampling programme applied to a demanding target, and it bears on sampler choice for expensive cosmological posteriors.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [The Brown Dwarf Desert is a Mass-Ratio Desert: Astrometric Demographics of 63,254 Stars](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12881v1)

William Thompson, Alexandre Bouchard-Côté, Dori Blakely et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP | also: astro-ph.SR

A joint hierarchical model of Hipparcos plus Gaia DR2/DR3 astrometry for 63,254 stars measures companion occurrence on 3–94 yr orbits from M dwarfs to B stars. The sample is one to two orders of magnitude larger than earlier wide-orbit surveys. The brown dwarf desert sits at **a fixed companion-to-host mass ratio $q = 1.76^{+0.26}_{-0.19}\%$ independent of host mass**, and a desert at fixed companion mass is excluded. At the desert floor, occurrence drops by a factor of ~20–30 relative to either side. Occurrence rises with metallicity below the desert and falls above it, pointing to **two formation channels separated by mass ratio, not by the deuterium-burning limit**.

![](figures/2611.12881/fig_qprofiles.pdf)

*In every host-mass range the companion rate has its minimum at $q\approx2\%$, while in companion-mass units (panel b) the minimum shifts with host mass. The desert therefore tracks mass ratio, not a fixed mass.*

### [The deuterium-rich atmosphere of a mature super-Jupiter](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10129v1)

Jerry W. Xuan, Yayaati Chachan, Jean-Baptiste Ruffio et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP | also: astro-ph.SR

JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy of the ~275 K, ~4 Gyr imaged giant ε Ind Ab detects **CH₃D at 48σ** and gives **D/H = (4.4 ± 0.7) × 10⁻⁵**. That is ice-giant-like and about twice the Jupiter/protosolar value, even though C, O, N and S are enriched only to Jupiter-like levels. Matching both requires the planet to have **accreted solids with D/H ≳ 1%, at least 30× more deuterium-rich than typical Solar System comets**, comparable to the interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS and to cold-cloud organics. It is the first bulk D/H measured for an exoplanet in this regime, and it ties giant-planet formation to inherited interstellar ices.

![](figures/2611.10129/epsindi_dh.pdf)

*ε Ind Ab's bulk D/H sits with Uranus and Neptune, well above Jupiter, Saturn and the protosolar value. The deuterium-rich reservoirs needed to explain it are seen only in interstellar and protostellar material.*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [The Neptunian ridge depends on the choice of linear or logarithmic period](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12560v1)

Kaiming Cui, David J. Armstrong
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP

The authors show that the reported "Neptunian ridge" exoplanet overdensity at 3–6 d is **an artifact of expressing occurrence per unit period (dN/dP) instead of per unit log-period**. The comparison "savanna" bin is twice as wide in $P$, so a population uniform in ln P automatically shows a 2× contrast. Recast per ln P, the published TESS and Kepler ridge-to-savanna contrasts fall from 1.6–2.2 to **0.8–1.2, consistent with unity**, and a fresh TESS-SPOC reanalysis gives **$0.91^{+0.21}_{-0.16}$**. It is a short, clean case study of how a choice of measure can manufacture a "feature", with a recommendation that overdensity claims state the variable they are made in.
