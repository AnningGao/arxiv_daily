## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [The Lumina Project: Intergalactic Clumping and Recombination Sinks](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18382v1)

Ali Sadain, Aaron Smith, Oliver Zier et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The authors combine the high/medium-resolution Thesan-1/Thesan-2 (95.5 cMpc) runs with the much larger 500 cMpc Lumina simulation to measure IGM clumping factors and recombination rates consistently across resolution and box size during reionization. The recombination-weighted clumping factor $\mathcal{C}_{\rm rec}$ sits systematically below the standard density-only $\mathcal{C}_{\rm HII}$ because photoheated ionized gas recombines less efficiently, and — crucially — when plotted against the global ionized fraction $x_{\rm HII}$ rather than redshift, the three simulations follow an approximately universal relation at the 10–20% level. The density-only prescription overpredicts the instantaneous recombination rate by factors of 1.29 at $z\simeq8$ and 1.84 at $z\simeq5$, and the cumulative ionizing-photon-sink budget by 1.45 by $z\simeq5$. Recombinations are extremely concentrated (Gini $\gtrsim0.9$ at all redshifts): more than 80% occur in under 10% of the volume.

This lands directly on the IGM/reionization primary focus — opacity, recombination sinks, and the ionizing photon budget that set the late-reionization forest — and the paper hands over compact power-law $\mathcal{C}(x_{\rm HII})$ fits ready to drop into reduced reionization models.

### [Covariance shrinkage for cosmological inference with Sellentin-Heavens-type likelihoods](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18446v1)

Mattera Raffaele
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM, stat.ME

This paper studies how to regularise covariance matrices estimated from a finite number of simulations when doing cosmological parameter inference. Under the Gaussian plug-in likelihood, Hartlap covariance-side scaling is recovered as the likelihood-loss optimum, but under the covariance-marginalised Sellentin–Heavens likelihood the unscaled sample covariance is optimal — i.e. an extra Hartlap factor is *not* favoured once covariance uncertainty is marginalised. The author then introduces a rotation-equivariant linear shrinkage family that shrinks sample-covariance eigenvalues toward their grand mean, treating the shrinkage intensity as an auxiliary inferential parameter with a prior and marginalising over it so that regularisation uncertainty propagates into the final posterior. Monte Carlo experiments confirm shrinkage substantially improves covariance conditioning.

Covariance estimation, the Hartlap factor, and the Sellentin–Heavens likelihood are all explicitly on the precision-cosmology methods shelf in the interest profile; this is a directly usable prescription for the simulation-estimated covariances that show up in P1D and LSS likelihoods.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [The Via Project: Overview of the Science, Instrument, and Survey](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18332v1)

The Via Collaboration
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.GA

Via is a forthcoming all-sky multi-object spectroscopic survey deploying identical 576-fiber spectrographs ($R\approx15{,}000$) on the MMT and Magellan/Clay for a five-year dual-hemisphere survey of >2,000,000 faint stars beginning in 2027. Four key goals drive it: mapping velocity perturbations in cold stellar streams sensitive to $M\lesssim10^7$ subhalos (a stringent test of the particle nature of dark matter); a chemodynamical census of Milky Way satellites; the first 3D tomographic maps of cold CGM gas via Na I absorption; and rapid characterization of transients to LSST single-epoch depth. Ancillary science explicitly includes the Lyα forest at $z\approx3$–4 using spare fibers.

This single facility touches three separate interest threads at once — small-scale dark matter via stream perturbations, CGM tomography, and the $z\sim3$–4 Lyα forest — making it worth tracking even as a facility overview.

### [PHANTOM: A MATLAB and Octave Toolbox Connecting Linear Field Statistics to Dark Matter Halo Observables](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19104v1)

Mohammad Abu Thaher Chowdhury
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

PHANTOM is a public MATLAB/Octave toolbox that links the linear density field to dark-matter halo observables: linear power spectrum, variance, and correlation functions, plus halo mass functions, linear bias, density profiles, and concentration–mass relations. It covers cold, warm, and fuzzy dark matter scenarios and is validated against the Python packages colossus, hmf, and halomod with sub-percent agreement on shared models. A single cosmology structure is constructed once and threaded through the call graph so halo statistics and structure stay mutually consistent.

Small-scale-structure dark matter (WDM/fuzzy DM) is a secondary focus, and a validated native implementation of these statistics is a convenient utility — though the MATLAB/Octave target makes it more of a cross-check than a replacement for the existing Python stack.

*No figures extracted for 2606.19104 (code-release/validation paper; the WDM/FDM mass-function plots are capability demos rather than new results).*

### [Projecting the ultimate pulsar timing sensitivity to dark matter substructure in a stochastic gravitational wave background](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18329v1)

Joshua W. Foster, Tanner Trickle, Fabrizio Vassallo
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

The authors build a framework combining Monte Carlo signal modeling with machine-learned surrogate likelihoods to give a unified, likelihood-level analysis of pulsar-timing-array sensitivity to compact dark-matter substructure (primordial black holes, axion miniclusters) across the rare-encounter, dynamic-flyby, and stochastic regimes that were previously treated only in separate limits. They then assess the impact of the recently-detected stochastic gravitational-wave background, finding it substantially weakens the PTA sensitivity to compact DM. Even in the most optimistic observing scenario, only a Shapiro-delay search retains sensitivity to subdominant DM components under inferred SGWB parameters.

The link to the interest profile is methodological: machine-learned surrogate likelihoods are an SBI-adjacent inference tool the profile tracks, and this is a clean worked example of unifying disparate signal regimes under one learned likelihood — applied here to compact-DM constraints.

*No figures extracted for 2606.18329 (the key result is the sensitivity statement; its projection plots need substantial setup to read in isolation).*

### [DESI Data Release 2 ELGs: Property-dependent subsamples, imaging systematics, and clustering](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18581v1)

T. Hagen, K. S. Dawson, Z. Zheng et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

Using DESI DR2 emission-line galaxies, this paper evaluates a property-dependent alternative to the fiducial neural-network correction for imaging systematics, deriving linear-regression systematic weights separately on ELG subsamples. The deeper Dark Energy Survey imaging footprint is shown to yield a higher overall number density but a deficit of extreme-color targets and a distinct redshift distribution, motivating — in some cases requiring — a separate treatment of the DES region in the catalog pipeline. With DES handled separately, property-dependent weights further mitigate spurious clustering in roughly 10% of subsamples, while the fiducial scheme stays optimal for the full sample.

DESI clustering systematics fall squarely in the secondary DESI/large-scale-structure focus — directly relevant to anyone using DESI tracers for clustering measurements.

*No figures extracted for 2606.18581 (systematics-mitigation paper; clustering/weight diagnostics are incremental and don't need a figure to summarise).*

### [From Evidence to Evident: Decisive Cosmological Evidence for the Normal Neutrino Mass Hierarchy](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18987v1)

Raul Jimenez, Carlos Peña Garay, Fergus Simpson et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

Combining DESI DR2 clustering with NuFIT oscillation data, the authors compute the Bayesian evidence for the normal versus inverted neutrino mass ordering. In baseline ΛCDM, DESI DR2 + Planck CamSpec gives $\Sigma m_\nu < 0.0642$ eV (95%), close to the normal-ordering floor ($\sim0.059$ eV) and well below the inverted-ordering minimum ($\sim0.099$ eV), placing the inverted hierarchy in the tail of the likelihood. The Bayes factor favouring the normal ordering exceeds 460 for a conservative prior and stays above 40 in model extensions, robust to the choice of prior measure — marking a transition from prior-sensitive evidence to likelihood-dominated exclusion of the inverted hierarchy.

Neutrino mass from cosmology (and from DESI specifically) is an explicit interest; this is a headline late-time-cosmology constraint with direct bearing on the forest + CMB neutrino-mass program.

*No figures extracted for 2606.18987 (the result is the Bayes-factor/exclusion statement, fully captured in text).*

### [On the robustness of the angular homogeneity scale $\theta_H$: a comparative analysis of computational approaches](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18975v1)

Pedro Fanha, António da Silva, José Fonseca et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This study tests how robust the angular homogeneity scale $\theta_H$ — a probe of the transition to large-scale homogeneity underpinning the FLRW framework — is to analysis choices, using MICE Grand Challenge mock galaxy catalogues. The angular fractal dimension $D_2(\theta_H)$ is computed with the Landy–Szalay estimator and direct pair-counting, and modelled with several approaches including symbolic regression. The redshift evolution of $\theta_H$ is robust, but its absolute value is sensitive to survey area, redshift binning, numerical implementation, and fitting strategy, so methodological systematics — not just data — drive the determination.

This is a methods-systematics cautionary tale for testing the Cosmological Principle, and it leans on symbolic regression, a technique on the interest list, applied to LSS clustering estimators.

*No figures extracted for 2606.18975 (methods-comparison paper; the message is the systematic sensitivity itself).*

### [Reconstructing Galactic Gravitational Potentials from Stellar Kinematics with Physics-Informed Neural Networks](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18386v1)

Charlotte Myers, Nathaniel Starkman, Lina Necib
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

GalactoPINNs is a physics-informed neural network framework that reconstructs galactic gravitational potentials from stellar kinematics by combining data-driven learning with embedded physical constraints, treating an analytic model as a structured prior and learning corrections on top of it. Trained on acceleration measurements, it captures complex small-scale, non-axisymmetric structure while preserving global physical consistency, achieving sub-percent acceleration errors and consistently outperforming analytic baselines from controlled analytic halos to cosmological Milky-Way-like simulations. A Bayesian NN provides spatially calibrated uncertainties and a time-dependent extension captures smooth temporal evolution.

The science (Galactic dynamics) is off-focus, but the methodology — PINNs, the analytic-prior-plus-learned-correction design, and Bayesian-NN uncertainty quantification — is exactly the kind of ML-inference tooling the profile tracks and could transfer to differentiable forward-modelling of the forest or LSS.

*No figures extracted for 2606.18386 (the transferable content is the method design, not a specific reconstruction plot).*

### [Impact of lensing magnification on the power spectrum turnover](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18908v1)

Yolanda Dube, Roy Maartens, Bikash R. Dinda et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The turnover scale $k_0$ of the matter/galaxy power spectrum is a standard ruler set by matter–radiation equality and independent of the BAO; this paper forecasts how lensing magnification biases its recovery in high-redshift spectroscopic surveys (Euclid Hα, the proposed MegaMapper Lyman-break-galaxy survey). The scale-dependent magnification correction to the monopole grows with redshift, biasing the recovered turnover by up to $\sim0.4\sigma$ (Euclid-like) and $\sim3.6\sigma$ (MegaMapper-like). To keep the bias below $1\sigma$, a MegaMapper-like survey is limited to $z\approx2.9$, and the turnover effectively vanishes in the recovered signal for $z\gtrsim3.7$.

This is a systematics study for the high-redshift spectroscopic surveys (MegaMapper sits in the forest/LBG-era survey landscape) whose large-scale clustering the profile cares about — a reminder to model magnification before trusting a high-$z$ power-spectrum feature.

*No figures extracted for 2606.18908 (forecast paper; the bias numbers carry the message).*

### [CMBComp: A Simple and Accurate Compressed CMB Likelihood for Dark Energy, Curvature, and Massive Neutrinos](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18455v1)

Amogh Srivastav, Prakhar Bansal, Dragan Huterer
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

CMBComp is a compact, accurate compressed CMB likelihood capturing the dominant geometric information of the combined SPT-3G D1 + ACT DR6 + Planck PR3 primary + Planck PR4 lensing dataset, built and validated in five model spaces (ΛCDM, $w_0w_a$CDM, $\nu$ΛCDM, $o$ΛCDM, and the joint $\nu w_0w_a$CDM). Combined with DESI DR2 BAO, the compressed posteriors reproduce the full-CMB-chain posteriors to high precision while being fast to evaluate and trivial to drop into standard pipelines without a Boltzmann solver. The compressed likelihood files and example notebooks are released publicly.

Compression of cosmological information and fast inference are explicit interests (cf. the forest-EFT compression work in the profile); a drop-in compressed CMB likelihood is a practical tool for exploring late-time-expansion models against DESI BAO.

*No figures extracted for 2606.18455 (tool-release paper; the deliverable is the likelihood and its validation, conveyed in text).*

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [A Long Period Stellar-Mass Black Hole Binary in $\omega$ Centauri](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18350v1)

Matthew Whitaker, Evan Kerr, Anil Seth et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.SR

This is the first astrometric discovery of a stellar-mass black hole in a globular cluster — a black-hole + main-sequence-turnoff-star binary in $\omega$ Centauri, the most massive Galactic globular cluster, found using 23 years of HST (oMEGACat) plus JWST astrometry. The orbit has a period of $94^{+63}_{-42}$ yr, semi-major axis $31$ AU, and eccentricity $0.72$, and because the system is caught at periastron the black-hole mass is well constrained at $4.46^{+1.22}_{-1.01}\,M_\odot$ despite only a partial orbit being observed. The binary is almost certainly dynamically formed and dynamically "soft," with an expected disruption timescale of $\sim$800 Myr.

Off the cosmology focus, but a genuine first — the longest-period black-hole binary known and direct astrometric proof of a stellar-mass BH in a cluster — and it shows that low-mass black holes can form at metallicity $Z<10^{-3}$, which is worth knowing as an astronomer.

*No figures extracted for 2606.18350 (the headline is the discovery and orbital parameters, well summarised in text).*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Is RISC-V Ready for Massively Parallel Astrophysical Codes?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15490v2)

Jenny Lynn Almerol, Nitin Shukla, Federico Ficarelli et al.
**Primary category:** cs.DC | also: astro-ph.IM

The authors benchmark three established production astrophysics codes — iPIC3D, PLUTO, and OpenGadget3 (memory-bound, compute-bound, and hybrid workloads) — on a Sophgo SG2044 RISC-V processor against AMD EPYC (x86) and NVIDIA GH200 Grace (ARM) systems. Numerical correctness holds on all platforms, confirming portability, but RISC-V runs about 3–6× slower than x86 and 5–9× slower than ARM. The gap is dominated by limited memory bandwidth, shared-cache contention, narrower 128-bit vector units, lower clock frequency, and less-mature GNU auto-vectorization, with memory-bound kernels hit hardest.

This is meta-research on the computational infrastructure of the field — a sober readout on whether the emerging open RISC-V ecosystem can yet run the simulation codes cosmology depends on (functional and correct, but not yet performance-competitive).

*No figures extracted for 2606.15490 (the takeaway is the performance ratios and their causes).*

### [Embedding Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion in the WST Collaboration](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19013v1)

Laurane Fréour, Anna Puglisi, Maria Cristina Fortuna et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

This paper describes the creation and first year of activity of the Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion working group for the proposed Wide-field Spectroscopic Telescope, a next-generation 12-m facility with a community of more than 1000 members. It discusses how EDI considerations are being embedded into the early governance of the collaboration, ongoing and planned projects to broaden participation and accountability, and candid reflections on the challenges of sustaining this work — recognition of EDI contributions, uneven participation, and communication across heterogeneous groups, time zones, and career stages.

Meta-research on the sociology and organisation of building a large collaboration — relevant to how the next generation of spectroscopic facilities (the class that includes the DESI successors central to this profile) are assembled as social as well as technical projects.
