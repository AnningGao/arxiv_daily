## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [The 3D clustering of Lyman Alpha Emitters measured with DESI](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24852v1)

Haruki Ebina, Martin J. White, Rongpu Zhou et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This work measures the 3D clustering of medium-band-selected Lyman-α emitters (LAEs) over 2.26 < z < 3.41, using IBIS/DECam imaging selection followed up spectroscopically by the DESI `tertiary54` program at 98.6% fiber completeness. Fitting the monopole and quadrupole with a grid of AbacusUtils HOD mocks (configuration space) and the one-loop EFT power spectrum out to k_max = 0.3 h/Mpc (Fourier space, cross-correlated with DESI DR2 QSOs), they recover a linear bias b ≈ 2.2 at z=2.5 and ≈ 2.55 at z=3.0. They place first constraints on the Lyman-α radiative-transfer distortion (α = −0.64 ± 0.63 and −0.95 ± 0.55 in the two z-bins, consistent with no RT effect at <2σ) and show that Fingers-of-God suppression is weak, so small-scale cosmological information remains accessible. Fiber-assignment effects are shown to be negligible (χ² < 0.1 below the collision scale).

This is a direct hit on the Lyman-α / high-z spectroscopic-survey program: it is a DESI-II / Spec-S5 readiness study for LAEs as a cosmological tracer, touching RT modeling, RSD/FoG, and EFT analysis — exactly the forest-adjacent, DESI-era methodology at the core of the interest profile.

### [Cosmological constraints from the DESI DR1 Bispectrum Full-Shape and DR2 BAO](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23936v1)

D. Forero-Sánchez, S. Novell Masot, H. Gil-Marín et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors add the LRG bispectrum to DESI DR1 full-shape measurements, combine it with DR2 BAO (accounting for the cross-covariance via mocks), and use ShapeFit compression to avoid prior-volume effects in extended models. The bispectrum raises the amplitude parameters (σ8 up 1.1σ to 0.868 ± 0.025; S8 up 1.2σ) and tightens them by ~26–28%. For neutrinos, the power-spectrum-only DESI posterior is consistent with zero, whereas adding the bispectrum yields Σmν = 0.26 ± 0.17 eV (95% upper limit 0.57 eV), shifting the peak into the positive region; the bispectrum also consistently pulls w0–wa posteriors back toward ΛCDM (DESI+CMB still prefers evolving DE at 2.8σ, rising to 3.1σ with DES-Dovekie SNe).

Direct DESI large-scale-structure cosmology: full-shape + BAO with a higher-order statistic, delivering the neutrino-mass and dark-energy constraints that sit squarely in the secondary "LSS & spectroscopic surveys" and fundamental-physics priorities. The neutrino result is the headline for a forest cosmologist watching Σmν.

### [Measuring local primordial non-Gaussianity from the clustering of DESI DR1 LRGs and QSOs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24651v1)

Z. Brown, B. Levi, H. Randall et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This is the first measurement of local primordial non-Gaussianity (f_NL) from the configuration-space two-point correlation function, applied to DESI DR1 LRGs and QSOs. Using simulation-based modeling (Fast-PM mocks) and a new approach to mitigating imaging systematics, they find f_NL = −3 ± 22 (LRG) and 0 ± 17 (QSO), with a joint constraint f_NL = −3 ± 12 at 68% CL, imposing Gaussian priors on the bias-response parameter p (p_LRG = 1.0 ± 0.1, p_QSO = 1.6 ± 0.1). The constraining power exceeds previous LSS surveys and approaches CMB sensitivity.

Direct LSS / DESI primordial-physics result, and a clean example of simulation-based modeling applied to DESI clustering — both the survey and the SBI angle are central to the profile.

### [GalSBI: Forward Modelling Galaxy Clustering and Population](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23783v1)

Silvan Fischbacher, Luca Tortorelli, Tomasz Kacprzak et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

GalSBI is extended to jointly model the galaxy population and the galaxy-halo connection via an optimal-transport subhalo abundance matching (SHAM-OT) scheme, constrained with simulation-based inference by comparing UFig image simulations against DES Y3 imaging. The model reproduces luminosity functions, photometry, morphology, redshift distributions, and — for the first time in this framework — the angular power spectrum of galaxy positions across magnitude/color cuts, with mean redshifts matching HSC deep-field data to 0.2–1.6σ. A key practical finding is that treating blending as an averaged effect biases clustering, so they replace the detection classifier with image-simulation-based detection plus a normalizing flow; sample variance dominates the mean-redshift uncertainty for bright (<24 mag) samples.

Direct hit on the rising-primary "ML/statistical inference for cosmology" focus: SBI + normalizing flows + emulation applied to forward-modeled clustering for Stage-IV surveys (Rubin-LSST, Euclid), with an explicit path to spectroscopic surveys (DESI, 4MOST, PFS).

### [The Degeneracy Distillery](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23838v1)

T. Lucas Makinen, Deaglan J. Bartlett, Niall Jeffrey et al. (incl. B. D. Wandelt — high-value SBI/field-level-inference author from the interest profile)
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: stat.ML, physics.data-an

The "degeneracy distillery" automatically detects and symbolically resolves degenerate parameter combinations from parameter–data (or parameter–simulation) pairs alone, by estimating and globally flattening the Fisher information matrix. By working in the information geometry of the likelihood, degeneracies are characterized as an intrinsic property of the model, requiring no realized data; the method then discovers symbolic coordinate transformations that recombine parameters into independent effects on the data. Unlike posterior-based methods that flatten the Fisher matrix at a single point, this flattens in expectation globally and — in the authors' test cases — cuts the simulation budget for downstream neural posterior estimation by up to 10× at matched calibration.

Direct methods relevance: degeneracy diagnosis and simulation-efficient NPE are exactly the inference toolkit a forest/LSS cosmologist needs for expensive simulators, and the author list overlaps the SBI/field-level-inference community flagged as high-value.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Exploring the Cosmic Dawn through the 21 cm Forest and High-redshift Radio Sources with the SKA](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24665v1)

Junsong Cang, Benedetta Ciardi, Barun Maity et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This SKA chapter reviews the 21 cm forest — absorption features imprinted by intervening neutral hydrogen on the spectra of high-z radio sources — as a probe of the neutral IGM during reionization. Because it is sensitive to small-scale structure and gas temperature, it constrains early heating, the thermal history, exotic energy injection, dark-matter properties, neutrino mass, the running of the spectral index, and the baryon–dark-matter relative velocity. The authors highlight recent enablers: new statistical observables that improve sensitivity within realistic SKA-Low integration times, revised radio-source counts that update the supply of background quasars, deep-learning extraction of physical information, and methods to separate first-galaxy astrophysics from fundamental-physics effects on small scales.

Adjacent in the most useful way: the 21 cm forest is the radio sibling of the Lyman-α forest, hitting IGM/reionization, small-scale dark matter, and neutrino mass simultaneously — and the SBI/normalizing-flow inference machinery it adopts overlaps the user's methods interests.

### [Probing Baryonic Feedback Effect with CSST Weak Lensing and Future FRB Measurements](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24061v1)

Shuai Feng, Yan Gong, Qi Xiong et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using a baryonic halo model, the authors forecast joint constraints on the baryonic-feedback parameter log T_AGN from CSST weak-lensing tomography combined with fast radio burst dispersion-measure statistics from SKA / DSA-2000. CSST lensing alone constrains log T_AGN to 3.1% with Σmν < 0.53 eV; adding FRB DM statistics (which directly trace the electron power spectrum) sharpens log T_AGN to 0.4% and tightens Σmν < 0.47 eV by breaking the feedback–cosmology degeneracy. The analysis includes realistic noise plus intrinsic-alignment, photo-z, shear-calibration, and DM-noise nuisance parameters in a full MCMC.

Useful context for the LSS-systematics and neutrino-mass threads: baryonic feedback is a leading systematic for any P(k)-based probe, and FRB DM as a cross-probe to calibrate it (and to constrain Σmν) is a methodology worth tracking.

*No figures extracted for 2606.24061 (forecast contour plots; the headline numbers are in the summary above).*

### [Accurate modeling for 3×2pt analyses in Roman and Rubin: a study of model approximations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23781v1)

Junzhou Zhang, Chihway Chang, Jiachuan Xu et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This is a systematic study of three modeling approximations common in 3×2pt (galaxy density + weak lensing) analyses: the Limber approximation, neglecting redshift-space distortions, and using less-accurate nonlinear matter power spectra. Validating their CoCoA pipeline against CCL and running simulated likelihood analyses for the final Roman High-Latitude Imaging Survey and Rubin/LSST, they find all three effects matter — neglecting any one can bias cosmological constraints by ~1σ, and >2σ for Rubin in several configurations. The biases depend on the lens-sample configuration and scale cuts.

Adjacent methods relevance: a concrete catalogue of which modeling shortcuts are no longer safe at Stage-IV precision, directly applicable to anyone validating LSS likelihood pipelines.

### [First measurement of narrow-line flux ratios for a lensed quasar with JWST/NIRSpec IFS](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23808v1)

Hadrien Paugnat, Tommaso Treu, Anna M. Nierenberg et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

Flux-ratio anomalies in quadruply-imaged quasars are sensitive to low-mass dark-matter halos down to ~10⁶ M_⊙, but require flux measurements from a region insensitive to stellar microlensing. The authors present the first narrow-line flux ratios for a lensed quasar with JWST/NIRSpec IFS, using the [S III] 9071/9533 Å doublet (its first use for substructure) and a full lens-model reconstruction of the system RXJ1131−1231, achieving ~5% flux-ratio uncertainties. They detect a clear cusp-image anomaly relative to a smooth lens model, broadly consistent with JWST/MIRI warm-dust ratios, and release the `lensqso-specfit` fitting package.

Adjacent to the "dark matter from small-scale structure" secondary focus: this is a complementary, non-forest route to constraining the low-mass halo mass function, and the demonstrated JWST IFS technique is generalizable to a larger sample.

*No figures extracted for 2606.23808 (spectral-fit and lens-model figures; the key result is the ~5% flux ratios and detected anomaly).*

### [Direct Imaging Discovery of Giant Exoplanet β Pictoris d](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23801v1)

Ben J. Sutlieff, Markus J. Bonse, Valentin Christiaens et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP | also: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.SR

Two independent teams report the direct-imaging discovery of a third giant planet in the β Pictoris system in the same week — Sutlieff et al. (VLT/ERIS plus archival JWST/NIRCam and VLT/SPHERE, 11-year astrometric baseline) and Gibbs et al. ([Discovery of an Exterior Third Planet Orbiting β Pictoris](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23789v1), serendipitous JWST/NIRSpec IFU detection confirmed with MIRI/MRS). β Pic d is a ~2–4 M_Jup planet on a coplanar orbit at semi-major axis ~26–30 au, with CO₂, CO, CH₄, and H₂O detected and an effective temperature near 600 K, consistent with sculpting the inner edge of the debris disk. This makes β Pictoris only the second directly imaged system with more than two confirmed planets.

Off the user's research area, but a genuinely notable milestone in direct imaging — two concurrent independent discoveries of the same planet, and one of the lowest-mass planets imaged from the ground — worth knowing as a scientist.

### [Hydrogen airglow from an escaping ultrahot Jupiter atmosphere](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23899v1)

Yapeng Zhang, Chenliang Huang, Aaron Householder et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP | also: astro-ph.SR

Atmospheric escape from close-in gas giants is usually observed via transit absorption, which leaves the thermal structure and mass-loss rate degenerate. Using high-resolution spectroscopy of the ultrahot Jupiter KELT-9 b, the authors report the first detection of atomic-hydrogen emission (Hα airglow) from an escaping planetary atmosphere, originating on the dayside. The double-peaked line profile (±30 km/s peaks with central self-absorption) breaks the transmission degeneracies and points to a vigorous outflow with a mass-loss rate above 10¹³ g/s, among the highest measured.

Outside the user's area, but a genuine "first" — emission rather than absorption opens a qualitatively new diagnostic of atmospheric escape and upper-atmosphere wind dynamics.

### [Astrobiology in the Time of Artificial Intelligence](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23854v1)

Caleb Scharf
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.EP

This perspective examines how the present and future practice of space exploration and astrobiology is being reshaped by computing, using the Viking missions (digital line-scan imaging, onboard laboratories, software-controlled autonomy) as a historical anchor. It argues that machine learning is among the most transformative recent developments for the search for life beyond Earth, enabling integration of data across scales, increased sensitivity to complex features, and adaptive, autonomous strategies for sampling environments.

Meta-research on how AI is changing an adjacent observational field — relevant to the user's tracked theme of AI's growing role in astronomy and to anticipating how ML will alter data-driven discovery practice.
