## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Lyman-α forest constraints on pure and mixed fuzzy dark matter](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06969v1)

Jianxiang Liu, Yan Gong, Xingchen Zhou
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors constrain pure (PFDM) and mixed (MFDM) fuzzy dark matter using the 1D Lyman-α forest flux power spectrum at z = 5.0, 4.6, 4.2, running MP-Gadget hydrodynamical simulations and training a two-stage neural-network emulator: the first stage predicts the CDM P1D, the second predicts the MFDM suppression relative to that CDM baseline, which enforces the correct CDM limit and sharpens sensitivity to weak FDM effects. After marginalizing over the IGM thermal/ionization parameters they obtain m_FDM > 1.9 × 10⁻²¹ eV (95%) for PFDM and f_FDM < 0.07, 0.12, 0.65 at log₁₀(m_FDM/eV) = −23, −22, −21 for MFDM. The PFDM bound is ~1 order of magnitude weaker than Rogers & Peiris (2021), which they attribute to their offset-grid (vs. glass) gas initial conditions and a general-purpose NN emulator (vs. an optimized GP for the single PFDM axis); a frequentist profile-likelihood Neyman construction (m_FDM > 1.1 × 10⁻²¹ eV) confirms the result is not prior-volume driven. The emulator is explicitly advertised as extensible to WDM, primordial magnetic fields, and other small-scale-power modifications.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Halo mass functions in mixed cold and fuzzy dark matter models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06599v1)

Sarah C. Johnston, Simon May, Tibor Dome et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using the AxiREPO framework, the authors run mixed CDM + fuzzy-DM simulations (ultralight axion m = 10⁻²⁴·⁵ eV, fractions f ≤ 0.3) and build a grid-based halo finder that combines the particle-based CDM and wave-like FDM components on a common density grid, avoiding the usual hack of treating one species as the other. They confirm FDM traces CDM on large scales while wave interference suppresses low-mass halos, shifting the halo mass function (HMF) downward and flattening its slope with increasing f and decreasing redshift. The headline deliverable is a phenomenological transformation formula that maps a CDM HMF to its mixed-DM counterpart to ~0.1–0.2 dex over 1 ≤ z ≤ 4 and f ≤ 0.3, so a broad MDM parameter space can be predicted without a dedicated simulation per point.

### [Mass-Varying Neutrinos from an Inverse Symmetron](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07391v1)

Mainak Baidya, Øyvind Christiansen, Vitor da Fonseca et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Motivated by the low (verging on negative) neutrino-mass bounds from galaxy surveys, the authors build a mass-varying-neutrino (MaVaN) model in which neutrinos couple to a scalar dark-energy field. Standard symmetron screening still leaves linear-scale instabilities, so they propose an "inverse phase transition": a negative-exponential conformal coupling with a positive bare-potential quadratic term, so that the neutrinos' non-relativistic transition breaks the symmetry and switches on the fifth force, which then deactivates again as the neutrino density drops — taming the runaway growth of neutrino perturbations at the linear level. The coupling can also seed an early-dark-energy component near recombination, offering a possible route to easing the Hubble tension by shrinking the sound horizon.

### [Impact of fiducial cosmology in model-agnostic cosmological inference with the BAO feature](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06591v1)

Aseem Paranjape, Ravi K. Sheth
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This completes the "Zel'dovich smearing approximation," a model-agnostic BAO inference framework that, unlike template-based analyses, assumes no specific cosmology — built from the Zel'dovich approximation plus peaks theory, with a basis for the linear correlation function, a Gaussian BAO-smearing kernel, and a scale-dependent-bias/mode-coupling term. The new ingredient is the geometric (Alcock–Paczyński-like) distortion from the unavoidable fiducial cosmology used to convert angles and redshifts to comoving separations. Tested on AbacusSummit halo samples engineered to mimic DESI LRGs and Euclid ELGs, the framework returns unbiased constraints; the linear-point error (~1.7% DESI, ~2.0% Euclid) is larger than template analyses precisely because nonlinearities are modelled explicitly and primordial physics is decoupled from late-time geometry — a trade the authors argue is worth it, with interesting implications for isolating whether the Hubble tension lives in the primordial or late-time sector.

### [From Cosmic Web to Supernova Remnants: Modeling FRB DM to Trace Baryons across Multiple Scales](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06963v1)

Zhao Joseph Zhang
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This thesis decomposes the fast-radio-burst dispersion measure (DM) into its contributions across scales — IGM, circumgalactic gas, host galaxy, and source-local environment — using the CROCODILE cosmological suite, zoom-in galaxy simulations, and 1D supernova-remnant hydrodynamics. AGN feedback is shown to redistribute baryons from halo centres into the diffuse CGM/IGM, especially affecting DM from massive foreground halos; the simulated DM–z relation yields diffuse baryon fractions f_diff ≈ 0.86. Host-galaxy DM ranges from <100 pc cm⁻³ in dwarfs to >1300 pc cm⁻³ in clusters, and SNR modelling of young magnetars reproduces the time-variable DM/RM of FRB 20121102 and 20190520B.

### [The colour variability of low-z SNe Ia is entirely explained by dust](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06593v1)

Marco Giunta, Konstantin Karchev, Roberto Trotta (Karchev/Trotta — simulation-based-inference / Bayesian-cosmology group)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using the Simple-BayeSN Bayesian hierarchical model on the volume-limited ZTF DR2 and Foundation DR1 SN Ia samples, the authors revisit whether the colour–magnitude relation is driven by intrinsic colour variation or dust. They show that traditional colour cuts (removing highly-reddened SNe) induce a previously unrecognized selection effect that biased earlier conclusions. Once that is accounted for, the entire colour–magnitude correlation is explained by dust, with the intrinsic colour-slope β_int consistent with zero and intrinsic colour scatter ≪ the dust parameter τ. The practical upshot: the standard linear Tripp correction remains empirically valid, but for dust reasons rather than intrinsic colour.

### [Towards Bayesian Photometric Cosmic Chronometers: Application to VIPERS](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07298v1)

Swaraj Pradhan, Pablo Renard, Enrique Gaztanaga et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The cosmic-chronometer method measures H(z) from the differential ages of passively-evolving galaxies but normally needs high-resolution spectroscopy. The authors instead use the D4000 break as an age proxy on a photometry-selected, massive, passive VIPERS PDR2 sample (0.5 ≤ z ≤ 0.8), with a Bayesian framework that infers full (non-Gaussian) age posteriors against BC03 SPS grids while marginalizing over metallicity with a Gaussian prior to break the age–metallicity degeneracy, then propagates age-difference posteriors by direct convolution. They obtain H(z=0.65) = 93.7 ± 28.3 (stat) ± 10.7 (syst) km/s/Mpc, consistent with spectroscopic CC measurements and Planck ΛCDM, with the framework explicitly designed for future DESI/Euclid/J-PAS/PAUS samples.

### [Origin of monolithic high-z galaxies and UV luminosity of merging high-z galaxies in a non-standard density perturbation spectrum](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07357v1)

Yu. N. Eroshenko, V. N. Lukash, E. V. Mikheeva et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

To explain JWST's excess of bright z ≥ 10 galaxies without new z~10 physics, the authors test a modified primordial power spectrum: a bump at the ~10¹⁰ M⊙ scale plus a small-scale cutoff. The bump alone overproduces early galaxies but pushes reionization to unacceptably high redshift; adding the cutoff (from the primordial spectrum itself or from warm-DM free-streaming) suppresses the first low-mass star-forming halos, restoring a standard reionization history, and simultaneously eases the satellite-overabundance problem. Using an extended Press–Schechter merger-rate distribution validated against N-body, they reproduce the HST/JWST UV luminosity function and find an enhanced fraction of "monolithically" (single-collapse) formed massive galaxies plus a Population IV of pristine stars in those systems.

### [Covariance Shrinkage via Stochastic Interpolation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07382v1)

Mathieu Chalvidal, Florentin Coeurdoux, Eric Vanden-Eijnden (Vanden-Eijnden — co-originator of the stochastic-interpolants framework)
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: stat.ML

The authors recast classical shrinkage of high-dimensional covariance estimators as empirical risk minimization over a parametric stochastic interpolant between a source and target distribution, recovering known shrinkage estimators as special cases and exposing three distinct risk-reduction levers: the interpolant schedule (which class of covariances is admissible), the flow map/coupling (optimal-transport couplings and non-linear flows free the estimate from the empirical eigenbasis, enabling eigenvector regularization), and early stopping (an extra bias–variance trade-off from integrating a regressed vector field). They propose a neural interpolant estimator with a quadratic-risk bound and validate on synthetic data and neuroimaging.

### [Gaussian Process Latent Factor Regression for Low-Data, High-Dimensional Output Problems](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06576v1)

Edward T. Stevenson, Eric T. Wolf, Mei Ting Mak et al. (incl. Miles Cranmer)
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: astro-ph.EP, astro-ph.IM

GPLFR represents each high-dimensional output as a linear-Gaussian decoding of a low-dimensional latent state drawn from a Gaussian-process prior; by analytically marginalizing the decoder weights, compression and prediction are coupled in a single objective, so the learned representation is biased toward what is *predictable* from the inputs rather than (as in PCA-then-GP) what merely reconstructs. On benchmarks it matches PCA-GP with ~4× fewer training points and is used to build the first spatially-resolved emulator of global climate models for rocky exoplanets.

### [Reweighting Adversarial Networks for Unbinned Unfolding](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06603v1)

Umar Sohail Qureshi, Krish Desai, Jesse Thaler et al.
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: hep-ex, physics.data-an

The authors introduce the Reweighting Adversarial Network (RAN), a machine-learning method for unbinned, high-dimensional unfolding (deconvolving detector effects to recover the particle-level distribution). It generalizes Moment Unfolding to full phase space via a particle-level reweighting function steered by a Wasserstein critic at the detector level, and — unlike the iterative OmniFold — works in a single non-iterative adversarial pass and tolerates non-overlapping detector/particle support. On Gaussian and jet-substructure tests it matches or beats OmniFold, with a natural hook for profiling nuisance parameters and systematics.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Measuring a Black Hole's Area Immediately after Merger: A Direct-Wave Test of Hawking's Area Law](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06592v1)

Adrian Ka-Wai Chung, Kelvin Ka-Ho Lam, Anna Liu et al.
**Primary category:** gr-qc | also: astro-ph.HE

The authors propose a gravitational-wave method to infer a Kerr-equivalent horizon area from the *direct waves* in the near-merger signal — before quasinormal ringing dominates — by interpreting the fitted direct-wave frequency and damping rate as the horizon's angular velocity and surface gravity. Applied to GW250114, analyses started 3–4.5 M before peak amplitude yield a remnant area consistent with the Kerr value, giving a first area measurement from direct waves and a new near-merger test of Hawking's area law.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [A series of unfortunate events: CHIME/FRB misclassification of a Galactic pulsar as a periodic fast radio burst](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07087v1)

The CHIME/FRB Collaboration
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE

The collaboration documents how FRB 20191221A — reported in 2022 as a fast radio burst with a striking 217 ms periodicity — was actually a train of pulses from the known Galactic pulsar PSR J0248+6021. An unusual calibration fault produced a many-degree beam-pointing offset which, combined with the pulsar's atypical emission, drove the misidentification. They detail the calibration problem, verify that no other CHIME/FRB localizations (including the Second Catalog) were affected, and describe new checks to prevent recurrence.
