## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Learning the Universe: Posterior Reliability of Neural Generative Models in High-Dimensional Field-Level Inference of Cosmic Initial Conditions](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10023v1)

Ludvig Doeser, Jens Jasche (BORG / field-level inference group)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: cs.LG

The authors run a controlled head-to-head between two neural generative approaches to field-level inference of cosmic initial conditions — an implicit Stochastic Interpolant model and an explicit GLOW normalizing flow — using Hamiltonian Monte Carlo samples as ground-truth reference posteriors. Their central finding is a cautionary one: matching the posterior mean, the marginal distributions, or achieving high cross-correlation with the truth does *not* guarantee a correct uncertainty structure. The voxel-wise posterior variance fields reveal geometry failures that all the standard summary metrics miss. They argue this kind of HMC-referenced, sample-based validation should be standard before trusting amortized generative posteriors in high dimensions.

This is a direct hit on the rising-primary ML-inference focus (§3): stochastic interpolants and normalizing flows applied to initial-condition reconstruction are explicitly tracked in the interest profile, and the paper's message — that fast generative posteriors can look right while being wrong — bears directly on any plan to deploy SBI/flows on forest or LSS data.

### [The Manticore Project II: Bayesian digital twins of cosmic structure across the SDSS and BOSS volumes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10020v1)

Stuart McAlpine, Jens Jasche, Guilhem Lavaux et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

Manticore-Deep is a field-level Bayesian inference of large-scale structure spanning a $(4\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Gpc})^3$ volume out to $z\approx0.7$ at ~4 Mpc/h resolution, jointly constraining five galaxy redshift surveys (2M++, 6dFGS, 2dFGRS, SDSS, BOSS) within a single hierarchical BORG framework. A novel tiled inference strategy extends the reconstructed volume by more than an order of magnitude over the companion local analysis. The posterior density/velocity fields pass two independent template-free predictive tests — a 7.4σ cross-correlation with Planck CMB lensing and a 3.5σ kSZ detection via velocity-weighted cluster stacking — and recover the BOSS Great Wall as a ~3σ overdensity consistent with ΛCDM.

Field-level inference of the SDSS/BOSS volumes sits squarely at the intersection of the methods focus (§3, BORG/Alsing–Wandelt-style inference) and the spectroscopic-survey LSS focus (§4); the velocity-field and CMB-lensing/kSZ cross-checks are exactly the kind of forward-modelled, simulation-based machinery the profile is converging on.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Learning the Universe with cosmological rescaling of merger trees and semi-analytic galaxy formation models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10024v1)

Richard Stiskalek, Lucia A. Perez, Shy Genel et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

The paper extends cosmological rescaling to operate directly on halo merger trees in the $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$–$\sigma_8$ plane, then runs the Santa Cruz semi-analytic model on the rescaled trees to generate galaxy populations across new parameters at negligible cost. A single-parameter halo-profile correction keeps systematic mass bias below the percent level. For inferring $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ and $\sigma_8$ from the stellar mass function or two-point function, as few as 64 base N-body simulations rescaled to ~1000 samples match the accuracy of 750 dedicated runs — rescaling a CAMELS-SAM box costs ~0.1 CPUh versus thousands to run it.

This is a concrete emulation/training-set-generation trick for parameter inference (§3/§4): cheaply densifying cosmological parameter space from a handful of base simulations is directly transferable to building forest or LSS emulators where hydro runs are the bottleneck.

*No figures extracted for 2606.10024 (the contribution is a methodological cost/accuracy trade-off best read in the text).*

### [Learning the Universe with the 2nd Generation of CAMELS: Varying 35 parameters of the IllustrisTNG model in (50Mpc/h)^3 boxes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10038v1)

Shy Genel, Yongseok Jo, Boon Kiat Oh et al. (incl. F. Villaescusa-Navarro)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

This release presents 1,192 new CAMELS simulations exploring 35 cosmological, astrophysical, and numerical parameters around the fiducial IllustrisTNG model in (50 Mpc/h)³ boxes — eight times the volume of previous CAMELS runs. Using MLPs, CNNs, GNNs, and Gaussian processes on power spectra, maps, galaxy graphs, and halo thermodynamics, they show the larger volumes yield tighter parameter constraints, though the improvement scales more weakly than √volume, hinting at information loss from mode coupling or parameter degeneracies. Four new parameters control the amplitude and timing of the ionizing background, giving direct handles on the intergalactic-medium temperature.

CAMELS is named in the interest profile as core inference infrastructure (§3/§4), and the new IGM-temperature and reionization-timing parameters connect to the IGM thermal-state interest (§2) — a useful resource for anyone training inference models that must marginalize over astrophysical feedback.

### [Balancing bias, baryons, and scale cuts in LSST 3x2pt analysis](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10679v1)

Ottavia Truttero, Maria Tsedrik, Joe Zuntz et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors build an LSST Y1/Y10 3×2pt pipeline using the BACCO emulator for both hybrid-EFT nonlinear galaxy bias and baryonic feedback via baryonification, and study where to set scale cuts to balance model complexity against bias. Linear bias gives unbiased $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$, $\sigma_8$ only to $k_\mathrm{max}=0.1\,h/$Mpc; a minimal perturbative bias variant stays unbiased to $0.7\,h/$Mpc in ΛCDM. They find higher-order bias can mimic baryonic suppression, and that a neutrino-mass detection is possible for $k\gtrsim0.3\,h/$Mpc but the inferred $M_\nu$ value is not robust across plausible mocks if the bias model is too minimal.

The relevance is twofold: it is a worked example of emulator-based inference with explicit baryon/bias/scale-cut trade-offs (§3/§4), and the warning that neutrino-mass constraints are fragile to nuisance modelling speaks to the fundamental-physics-via-large-scale-structure interest (§1).

*No figures extracted for 2606.10679 (the message is a set of scale-cut trade-offs better summarized in text than in any one contour plot).*

### [Mixed Dark Matter: Limits from the Milky Way Satellite Galaxies](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10006v1)

Wendy Crumrine, Dominic Pang, Ethan O. Nadler et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

The authors derive new limits on two-component dark-matter mixtures — a CDM component plus a fraction of either fuzzy DM or interacting DM (coupled to photons, neutrinos, or baryons) — that suppress small-scale structure. Using the observed Milky Way satellite population, they require that allowed models' linear matter power spectra remain less suppressed than a constrained reference, deriving 95% bounds that weaken with decreasing beyond-CDM fraction along distinct power-law scalings. At 50% fraction, IDM cross-section bounds weaken by 2–6× and FDM mass bounds by ~1.5× relative to the pure case; future LSST-like satellite surveys could tighten the 100% bounds by up to ~14×.

This is the same small-scale-power-suppression physics the Lyman-α forest constrains (§5), approached through a complementary probe; the transfer-function framing and the fractional-DM degeneracies are directly comparable to forest-based WDM/FDM/axion limits.

### [On Cross-Correlating Line Intensity Maps from SPHEREx during Reionization](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10005v1)

Abigail E. Ambrose, Eli Visbal, Matthew McQuinn
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The authors simulate Lyα, Hα, Hβ, [OII], and [OIII] intensity maps observable by SPHEREx during reionization, including all significant emission sources, Lyα radiative transfer, and a simple dust model. Because interloping lines make auto-spectra hard, they focus on line cross-correlations, which cancel foregrounds, and find detectable cases up to S/N ~99 for Hα×[OIII] at z=5 (no dust). They show intensity mapping can statistically probe galaxies below the direct-detection limit ($M<4\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$), though the observable signal is dominated by bright, directly detectable galaxies, and clustering detections at z>6 will need more sensitive instruments.

Reionization-era line-intensity mapping with Lyα and a careful interloper/cross-correlation treatment is adjacent to the IGM/reionization focus (§2); co-author Matthew McQuinn is a high-value IGM/reionization theorist per the interest file.

### [Integral Field Unit Spectroscopy with One Fiber](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10197v1)

Zehao Peng, Biprateep Dey, Chris J. Maddison et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: cs.AI

The authors build a multi-modal, probabilistic masked-autoencoder foundation model that predicts high-resolution spectra with calibrated uncertainties at arbitrary spatial locations within a galaxy, directly from broadband images. Trained on 4.7M DESI single-fiber image+spectrum pairs, it injects fiber positional encodings and redshift-aware wavelength encodings to learn a rest-frame latent space, exploiting the natural variance of fiber placements and galaxies' morphological self-similarity. Without ever seeing IFU data, its predicted emission-line maps match independent MaNGA IFU observations, with performance comparable to a supervised baseline trained directly on IFU data.

This is a DESI-trained spectroscopic foundation model with calibrated uncertainties (§3, astronomical foundation models / spectroscopic transformers) — methodologically close to the SpecPT / universal-spectral-tokenization line the profile flags as the newest direction, and a template for self-supervised models on survey spectra.

### [Amortized Simulation-Based Inference of Colliding-Wind Binaries from Short, Noisy Image Time Series](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10762v1)

Niklas Knöll, Tobias Buck, Lorenzo Branca et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.SR | also: astro-ph.IM

The authors present a complete amortized SBI pipeline that infers seven physical parameters of colliding-wind binaries from 10-frame Hα photon-count image time series, where the forward hydrodynamic simulator is expensive and the likelihood intractable. They use a factorized spatio-temporal architecture that separates spatial encoding from temporal aggregation — inducing time-translation equivariance and improving identifiability in low-signal regimes — coupled with a neural spline flow. Posteriors are verified well-calibrated via TARP and SBC diagnostics, and the method correctly widens its uncertainties in information-poor (low photon count) regimes.

The science target is far from the forest, but the *machinery* is directly borrowable (§3): amortized SBI with neural spline flows, a physics-motivated equivariant encoder, and rigorous TARP/SBC calibration are exactly the tools the profile wants applied to expensive cosmological simulators.

### [Formation of Parallel Stellar Streams through Encounters with Dark Matter Subhalos and Intermediate-Mass Black Holes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10859v1)

Yuka Kaneda, Masao Mori, Yohei Miki et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors propose that a single stellar stream can split into two nearly parallel structures after an encounter with a dark perturber — a starless DM subhalo or a wandering intermediate-mass black hole. Backed by an analytic model of density perturbations projected perpendicular to the stream and by N-body simulations, they show the encounter generates density depletions across (rather than along) the stream, producing parallel morphologies distinct from conventional gap-like signatures. They note this qualitatively resembles the observed parallel Streams C and D in the Andromeda halo and discuss observables to distinguish it from other formation channels.

Stellar streams as a probe of dark substructure is a peripheral but genuine thread in the library (§5, small-scale dark matter); a new, qualitatively different perturbation signature is worth filing alongside forest-based small-scale-power constraints.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [A Multi-Wavelength View of the First Type Ic-BL Supernova with an Einstein Probe X-ray Shock Breakout](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10011v1)

Jillian C. Rastinejad, Gokul Srinivasaragavan, Nikhil Sarin et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE

The Einstein Probe detected EP260321a, its most nearby fast X-ray transient (z=0.0343) and the first to strongly match expectations for X-ray "shock breakout" emission, accompanied by the broad-lined Type Ic supernova SN 2026gzf. Radio follow-up over 5.8–54.5 days rules out an on-axis jet of $E_K>10^{49}$ erg for circumburst densities $n>10^{-2}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$, while well-sampled optical data place SN 2026gzf within the 90% range of optically discovered Ic-BL SNe. Light-curve modelling favours combined circumstellar-interaction plus $^{56}$Ni decay, and the inferred SBO rate is in mild tension with EP detection rates, suggesting most Ic-BL SNe produce fainter X-ray breakouts.

Held to a high bar: this is a genuine observational first — the first definitive X-ray shock breakout caught for a stripped-envelope SN — opening real-time core-collapse timing for coordinated neutrino/gravitational-wave searches, which is worth knowing as a notable result even outside cosmology.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [DarkAgents](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11157v1)

Michele Lucente, Silvia Pascoli, Filippo Sala et al.
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: astro-ph.CO

DarkAgents is a multi-agent system that pairs the reasoning and code-generation of large language models with deterministic, tested human-written code to build orchestrated pipelines for theoretical astroparticle-physics research, targeting model building, multi-constraint computation, and explicit assumption auditing. Its first application takes a classically scale-invariant particle model through a cosmological first-order phase transition all the way to a fit of the NANOGrav nanohertz gravitational-wave spectrum, returning best-fit parameters, existing constraints, and an audit report of the priors and assumptions entering each. In test runs it flagged inconsistencies in some published fits and produced new ones; the framework runs on several agentic CLI tools (including local LLMs via Ollama) and is publicly released.

This is a concrete look at how LLM agents are starting to do — and audit — physics research pipelines, the kind of "AI is changing how the field works" development worth tracking, especially its emphasis on assumption-auditing as a guard against the opaque-automation failure mode.
