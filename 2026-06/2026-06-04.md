### [2606.04089] A multi-eigenbasis approach to covariance matrix denoising for DESI 3D Lyα forest analysis
**Wynne Turner** | astro-ph.CO  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04089>

The DESI 3D Lyα forest covariance matrix is ~7500×7500; estimating it reliably from a finite mock ensemble is an unsolved noise problem that directly limits parameter constraints. Turner introduces a **multi-eigenbasis denoising** method: project the noisy sample covariance onto the eigenbasis of a noiseless reference (LyαCoLoRe or Saclay DR1 mocks), reconstruct via pseudo-eigenvalues, then apply a weighted residual correction whose weights are derived by classifying the noisy covariance against at least two mock suites. The initial reconstruction alone already outperforms the current smoothing-based approach used in the DESI 3D Lyα analysis, with a factor ~30 improvement in Frobenius norm distance to the truth on synthetic tests. Applied to the actual DESI DR1 Lyα data, the denoised covariance shifts cosmological posterior contours measurably and reduces their area. The method is general: it applies whenever the data vector is very large or the sample covariance is near-singular.

**Figures:** `figures/2606.04089/correlation_matrices.pdf` — comparison of noisy, smoothed, and denoised correlation matrices at several Δz separations. `figures/2606.04089/dr1_chains.pdf` — DESI DR1 cosmological posteriors under the three covariance treatments.

### [2606.05006] A Measurement of the Thermal and Ionization State of the IGM at z < 0.5
**Teng Hu, Vikram Khaire, Joseph F. Hennawi et al.** | astro-ph.CO  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05006>

Using 82 archival HST COS G130/G160 quasar spectra, the authors measure the IGM thermal and ionization state in the low-redshift universe for the first time via a **machine-learning-based inference framework** (Hu et al. 2022) that jointly constrains T₀, γ, and Γ_HI from the b–N_HI column density distribution and line density dN/dz. Results are striking: at z = 0.1, they find **T₀ = 28183⁺⁵⁷⁰⁰₋₆₈₀₄ K and γ = 1.06⁺⁰·¹³₋₀·⁰⁹** — roughly a factor of seven (~7σ in log-space) above the canonical expectation of T₀ ~ 4000 K with γ ~ 1.6 at that epoch. The IGM is far hotter and close to isothermal. The photoionization rate log(Γ_HI/s⁻¹) = −13.70 is ~4σ below current UV-background synthesis model predictions. Two candidate explanations explored: new low-z IGM heating mechanisms (shocks? unresolved structure?), or unresolved small-scale turbulence of v_tur ≃ 15 km s⁻¹ that broadens lines mimicking thermal widths. Comparison with high-resolution STIS data makes instrumental resolution effects unlikely. This is a significant anomaly for the standard IGM thermal evolution picture post-He II reionization.

**Figures:** `figures/2606.05006/IGM_evol_lowz_revised.pdf` — T₀ and γ redshift evolution measurements vs. standard cooling curve. `figures/2606.05006/tri_rho_T_rescaled_Nyx_z01_weightedfit.pdf` — T₀–γ–Γ_HI posterior triangle at z = 0.1.

### [2606.04895] Posterior sampling in the Age of Emulators (BEST package)
**Andreas Nygaard, Luca Janken, Steen Hannestad et al.** | astro-ph.IM  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04895>

Releases **BEST** (Bayesian Emulator Sampling Tool), a Python package that benchmarks five MCMC samplers — Metropolis–Hastings (MH), MALA, HMC, NUTS, and AIES — against neural-network emulator likelihoods implemented in TensorFlow with GPU/JIT acceleration. The key finding: when likelihood evaluations are cheap (emulators), sampling *wall time* changes the ranking dramatically compared to *samples-to-convergence*. NUTS is the most sample-efficient, but its per-step overhead often makes MALA competitive on wall time. MH and AIES remain viable via massive chain parallelisation. Running thousands of chains simultaneously on a GPU converges faster with only modest wall-time penalty. This is directly relevant to emulator-based Lyα forest inference (SBI, flow-based posteriors) where the advice "use NUTS" may not be optimal.

**Figures:** `figures/2606.04895/convergence_walltimes_lcdm.pdf` — wall-time convergence comparison across all five samplers for a ΛCDM likelihood.

### [2606.04083] HI Lyα absorption from the warm-hot CGM: coronal broad Lyα absorbers in TNG50
**P. Aparicio Marcos, P. Richter, M. Sparre et al.** | astro-ph.GA  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04083>

Using TNG50 mock sightlines, the authors study HI Lyα absorption from the **warm-hot (T ~ 10⁵–10⁶ K) circumgalactic medium**. They introduce a new absorber class, **CBLAs** (Coronal Broad Lyα Absorbers), characterised by very large Doppler parameters (b ≳ 80 km s⁻¹) arising from collisionally ionised gas rather than IGM photoionisation. The covering fraction of CBLAs is f_c ≈ 0.8 for halos of M_halo ~ 10¹²M☉ at z ~ 0. They further identify a "strong CBLA" subclass with log N_HI > 13.5 and connect it to the innermost halo. This work establishes a simulation-based framework for disentangling cool-photoionised from warm-coronal CGM absorption in the Lyα forest, directly relevant to interpreting low-z COS surveys and the T₀ anomaly found in 2606.05006.

**Figures:** `figures/2606.04083/figure_1.pdf` — CBLA population overview in TNG50. `figures/2606.04083/logNHI_and_EW_vs_radius_CBLA_N_75.pdf` — N_HI and EW vs. impact parameter for the strong CBLA sample.

### [2606.04793] Cooler Phases of the CGM Are More Centrally Concentrated: DESI Y1 CaII/MgII/CIV stacking
**Weiwen Kong, Zeyu Chen, Enci Wang et al.** | astro-ph.GA  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04793>

First large statistical measurement of **multiphase CGM radial structure** using DESI Y1 galaxy–quasar pairs: ~150 kpc impact parameter, 0.1 < z < 4. By stacking CaII (T ~ 10³·⁵ K), MgII (T ~ 10⁴ K), and CIV (T ~ 10⁵ K) absorption around BGS, ELG, and QSO host halos, they show definitively that **progressively colder phases are more centrally concentrated**, reproducing the phase stratification seen in TNG50 and FIRE. For ELGs, the W_CIV/W_MgII ratio rises steeply with impact parameter, signalling a cool-dominated core transitioning to a warm-gas envelope. QSO halos show a flat ratio, indicating AGN feedback redistributes phases. The CGM phase structure dependence on stellar mass follows W_i ∝ D^α with α varying across tracers. Provides new benchmark for CGM feedback models.

**Figures:** `figures/2606.04793/fig_ratio_schematic_r.pdf` — W_CIV/W_MgII ratio vs. impact parameter schematic. `figures/2606.04793/fig_z_mass_dependence_figure.pdf` — EW scaling with mass and redshift.

### [2606.04830] Cosmic variance or galaxy bias? Disentangling finite-volume effects and galaxy formation for BAO in DESI/Euclid
**Francesco Sinigaglia, Francisco-Shu Kitaura** | astro-ph.CO  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04830>

Develops a new **Eulerian perturbation theory formalism** that mathematically separates cosmic variance (finite-volume realization noise) from galaxy biasing in the estimation of BAO peak position. The key insight: bias parameters are typically estimated assuming the ensemble-average matter power spectrum, implicitly folding in cosmic variance; this inflates uncertainty on the BAO scale. By introducing a new set of "cosmic-variance bias parameters," they show that variance-suppressed N-body simulations validate the analytical framework and confirm the expected reduction in BAO peak position uncertainty. Practical strategies for applying the formalism to DESI and Euclid are proposed. Directly relevant to any analysis using galaxy/quasar/Lyα BAO from DESI DR2+.

**Figures:** `figures/2606.04830/BAO_hist.pdf` — distribution of BAO peak positions in standard vs. variance-suppressed simulations.

### [2606.04827] Steep Redshift Evolution of the Ionizing Escape Fraction at z = 5–12: Empirical Constraints on f_esc(M_h, z)
**Zihan Wang, Huanyuan Shan** | astro-ph.CO  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04827>

First **empirical constraints on f_esc(M_h, z)** — the halo-mass and redshift dependence of the LyC escape fraction — across z = 5–12, using a three-parameter power-law model conditioned on HST+JWST UV luminosity functions, Planck τ_e, seven neutral-fraction measurements, and reionization ODE constraints. Profile-likelihood result: f₀ = 0.061⁺⁰·⁰¹⁸₋₀·⁰²³, α_M = 0.18⁺⁰·²²₋₀·³⁰, α_z = 1.98⁺⁰·⁴⁸₋₀·⁴². The population-averaged ⟨f_esc⟩(z) rises from ~2% at z = 5 to ~9% at z = 12; sub-threshold halos (M_h < 10¹⁰ M☉) contribute >80% of the ionizing budget at z ≥ 10. THESAN tension: the steep α_z ~ 2 profile preference is in ~2σ tension with THESAN's luminosity-weighted α_z ~ 0.13. Sets empirical calibration targets for reionization simulations.

### [2606.05047] Full Nonlinear Velocity Reconstruction with Transformer and Ensemble Tree Machine Learning
**Yulin Gong, Rachel Bean** | astro-ph.CO  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05047>

Applies a **gradient-boosting decision tree (GBDT) and a Transformer** to reconstruct galaxy peculiar velocities beyond linear theory, using multi-scale features from AbacusSummit mocks tailored to DESI LRG and ELG catalogs. Both models learn the residual between the true velocity and the linear-theory estimate. The Transformer outperforms GBDT and both substantially outperform linear theory across a wider range of spatial scales, better recovering the velocity power spectrum and maintaining cross-correlation with truth. Tested in both periodic box and lightcone geometries; photometric redshift uncertainty (Rubin LSST) is also assessed. Two immediate applications: cluster pairwise velocity correlations and stacked cluster density profiles for kSZ analysis with CMB-S4/Simons Observatory. Useful ML toolset for DESI-era velocity field science.

### [2606.05005] Neutrino mass constraints in interacting dark energy models after DESI DR2
**Hui Li, Guo-Hong Du, Tian-Nuo Li et al.** | astro-ph.CO  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05005>

Investigates how four interacting dark energy (IDE) phenomenologies — coupling Q ∝ Hρ_de, Hρ_c, H₀ρ_de, H₀ρ_c — affect cosmological **neutrino mass upper bounds** using DESI DR2 BAO + Planck + ACT + PantheonPlus/DESY5/DES-Dovekie supernovae. The choice of IDE model matters dramatically: IΛCDM2 (Q ∝ Hρ_c) relaxes the bound to Σm_ν < 0.129 eV, while IΛCDM3 (Q ∝ H₀ρ_de) tightens it to Σm_ν < 0.051 eV — below the normal-hierarchy lower bound of ~0.06 eV from oscillation experiments. This exacerbates the tension between cosmological and laboratory neutrino physics. DIC and Δχ²_MAP favor the mass-suppressing IDE models. Timely given the ongoing DESI DR2 dark-energy discussion.

### [2606.05166] Compressed Gaussian likelihood for Planck low-ℓ CMB EE data (planck-gaussian-lowl)
**Nanoom Lee** | astro-ph.CO  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05166>

Releases **planck-gaussian-lowl**, a lightweight Python package compressing the Sroll2 likelihood for Planck CMB low-ℓ (ℓ = 2–29) E-mode polarization into piecewise offset log-normal functions whose χ² takes a Gaussian form. This makes the tightest existing τ constraint (τ = 0.0566⁺⁰·⁰⁰⁵³₋₀·⁰⁰⁶²) directly usable in Fisher-matrix forecasts and bias analyses without the usual Gaussian-prior approximation. Validated against full MCMC with Planck + ACT DR6. Relevant for any forecasting that couples τ (i.e., the reionization epoch) to other parameters including Σm_ν and A_s.

### [2606.04044] The Cosmological Hart-Tipler Conjecture
**David Kipping** | astro-ph.CO  
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04044>

Curiosity paper. Extends the Fermi-paradox/Hart-Tipler argument (absence of von Neumann probes implies no aggressive spacefaring civilisations in the Milky Way) to **cosmological volumes**. A three-parameter model (spawn rate, propagation speed u, start time) accounting for cosmic expansion finds that half the observable universe would be "infected" by today if u = 0.1c and the spawn rate exceeds ~1 per million galaxies. The "cosmological Hart-Tipler problem" places a remarkably sharp minimal-model bound on the prevalence of aggressive self-propagating technological behavior. Keywords: SETI · Cosmology.
