## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Lyman-α Forest and its Cross-Correlation with High-Redshift Galaxies in Effective Field Theory at the Field Level](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26234v1)

Roger de Belsunce, Mikhail M. Ivanov, James M. Sullivan et al. (LSS/EFT group at MIT–IAIFI)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

The authors build a field-level perturbative forward model for the Lyman-α forest flux decrement and validate it against both large-volume AbacusSummit N-body mocks (forest painted onto the dark matter field) and the Sherwood hydrodynamic simulations. A cubic EFT bias expansion with IR resummation reproduces the simulated 3D and 1D power spectra at the 1% (5%) level out to k ≈ 0.3 (1.0) h/Mpc — vastly outperforming the linear/phenomenological fitting functions used in current DESI analyses, which already break down by k ≈ 0.1 h/Mpc — and matches the one-point PDF down to ~2 h⁻¹Mpc cells and the cross-correlation with massive halos. Leveraging cosmic-variance cancellation, they robustly detect the full set of quadratic line-of-sight bias operators (consistent with EFT naturalness) and characterize the forest stochasticity, finding it white on large scales. They release large-scale clustering mocks of the forest plus DESI-II Lyman-break-galaxy and Lyα-emitter samples for validating joint forest × high-z-galaxy inference pipelines.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Dark Matter in Draco and Boötes I: Hints of a Core in an Ultra-Faint Dwarf from Simulation-Based Inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26218v1)

Tri Nguyen, Lina Necib, Ting S. Li et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.HE

The authors present GraphNPE, a simulation-based inference (neural posterior estimation) method for dynamical mass modeling of dwarf spheroidals that folds measurement uncertainties and spectroscopic selection functions directly into the forward model. Using mock data they show that line-of-sight-dispersion-only methods are biased toward cuspy profiles even absent the mass–anisotropy degeneracy, whereas accessing higher-order velocity moments — particularly line-of-sight kurtosis — breaks the key degeneracies. Applied to Draco (MMT/Hectochelle and DESI) and Boötes I (S5), GraphNPE recovers consistent profiles across datasets where second-order Jeans modeling fits the dispersion but fails the kurtosis, and finds Boötes I significantly lower density and consistent with a cored inner slope.

### [Clustering of high-redshift quasars with DESI DR2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27115v1)

M. Charles, P. Martini, A. J. Ross et al. (DESI Collaboration)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using ~715,000 DESI DR2 quasars at 2.0 < z < 3.5, the authors measure a mean quasar bias b_Q ≈ 3.61 and trace its strong redshift evolution, well fit by a simple quadratic-in-(1+z) form that also matches lower-redshift literature. The bias evolution is consistent with a roughly redshift-independent characteristic halo mass of ~10¹² M_⊙ and duty cycles of order 10⁻², and the sample size enables the first statistically significant measurement of the luminosity dependence of quasar bias at these redshifts — weak, but inconsistent with no dependence and weaker than tight luminosity–halo-mass models predict.

### [Spherical Collapse and Halo Formation in a Cosmology with Decaying Dark Matter and a Semi-Cosmographic Dark Energy](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27076v1)

Mohit Yadav, Tapomoy Guha Sarkar
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

The authors study nonlinear structure formation in a model combining one-body decaying dark matter (decaying to dark radiation) with a dark-energy sector reconstructed directly from the expansion history rather than fixed to Λ. They constrain the background using DESI DR1 BAO and compressed ShapeFit measurements and propagate the posterior into spherical-collapse and halo-abundance calculations. They find the reconstructed dark-energy equation of state can depart from w = −1 while the collapse threshold stays near standard, with the most pronounced signatures in the abundance of massive halos; combining DESI DR1 clustering with DESI Legacy DR9 halo-mass-function measurements yields joint constraints on the decay lifetime and dark-energy parameters.

### [First full-shape joint analysis of the two- and three-point correlation functions on real data: ΛCDM constraints from BOSS DR12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26237v1)

Massimo Guidi, Michele Moresco, Alfonso Veropalumbo et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors present what they describe as the first full-shape, redshift-space joint analysis of the galaxy 2-point and 3-point correlation functions on real data, using BOSS DR12. They model both statistics in the velocity-difference-generating-function framework — with non-perturbative Fingers-of-God damping, a complete Eulerian bias expansion, and IR resummation — using dedicated emulators for fast likelihood evaluation, a MultiDark-Patchy covariance from 2048 mocks, and optimal data-vector compression. Adding the 3PCF to the 2PCF tightens constraints on σ(h), σ(ω_cdm), and σ(A_s) by roughly 29%, 10%, and 24%, with the gains driven mainly by extra BAO information in the triangle configurations.

### [Cross-correlation of SPT-3G D1 CMB lensing and DES Y3 galaxy lensing](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26223v1)

A. Ouellette, Y. Omori, E. Anderes et al. (SPT-3G & DES Collaborations)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors cross-correlate SPT-3G D1 CMB-lensing maps with DES Y3 cosmic-shear catalogs over ~1,300 deg², achieving a ~14σ detection using a polarization-only CMB-lensing reconstruction expected to be robust against extragalactic foreground biases (with several temperature-based estimators tested for the foreground/noise trade-off). Marginalizing over intrinsic alignments and baryonic feedback, they obtain S₈ = 0.833⁺⁰·⁰⁴⁷₋₀.₀₆₁, consistent with both Planck primary CMB and DES Y3 shear-only results. Combining with Planck yields competitive constraints on the DES intrinsic-alignment amplitude and a lower limit on baryonic-feedback strength.

### [Methodological Frontiers in 21-cm Intensity Mapping: the Treatment of Systematics and Foreground Contamination](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27244v1)

Marta Spinelli, Matilde Barberi-Squarotti, José Luis Bernal et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This SKA science-book chapter reviews the state of the art in single-dish 21-cm intensity mapping for post-reionization cosmology, where neutral hydrogen traces large-scale structure up to z ~ 3 with SKA-Mid. It surveys recent advances in map-making and component separation for isolating the faint cosmological signal from astrophysical foregrounds that are orders of magnitude brighter, with emphasis on telescope-specific systematics such as beam response and correlated (1/f) noise, and assesses methods in controlled simulation environments.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Interpreting "Interpretability" and Explaining "Explainability" in Machine Learning in Physics](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26228v1)

Rikab Gambhir, Luisa Lucie-Smith, Jesse Thaler
**Primary category:** physics.data-an | also: astro-ph.GA, cs.LG, hep-ph

The authors disentangle two often-conflated notions for ML in the physical sciences: interpretability (structural transparency — whether a model's inner workings can be understood) versus explainability (scientific content — whether the model maps onto domain knowledge). They lay out the trade-offs each entails (interpretability vs. expressivity; explainability vs. adaptability), the contexts where each is needed, and the intrinsic vs. post-hoc tools for achieving them, arguing throughout that machine-learned models face the same scientific scrutiny as classical ones and that interpretability/explainability are deliberate modeling choices rather than inherent properties, with task specification and intervention plans as core design concerns.
