## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [The impact of source and survey modelling on the connection between [O III] emitters and Ly α forest transmission at z ~ 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03753)

Luke Conaboy, James S. Bolton, Laura C. Keating et al.  
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO (also astro-ph.GA)

JWST is now measuring the cross-correlation between [OIII]-emitting galaxies and Ly α forest transmission at z~6, and recent measurements present a challenge to numerical models. This paper builds an empirical model connecting halos to the observed [OIII] population, constructs mock JWST survey catalogs from the Sherwood-Relics simulation suite, and compares mock 1D and 2D galaxy–Ly α cross-correlations directly to observations. The key finding is that large scatter in mock survey realizations allows statistical consistency with the data, but the peak of the 1D correlation in mocks falls ~10 cMpc below observed. The scatter is large enough that current data cannot yet rule out a broad range of ionizing source models; progress requires larger observational samples and simulation boxes >250 cMpc.

This is a direct hit: Lyman-alpha forest + late reionization + galaxy–IGM cross-correlation using Sherwood-Relics, and the lead authors (Bolton, Keating, Haehnelt) are central figures in the field. The tension between models and observations in the 1D peak position is exactly the kind of open question worth tracking.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Lyman-alpha Pressure Strongly Enhances Pre-Supernova Feedback at Cosmic Dawn: The First Multi-Dimensional Lyman-alpha Radiation Hydrodynamics Simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02711)

Olof Nebrin, Aaron Smith, Garrelt Mellema et al.  
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA (also astro-ph.CO)

Presents Lydion, a new 2D Lyman-alpha radiation hydrodynamics (RHD) code using a novel M1 moment method for Ly α transfer with self-consistent dust dynamics, achieving ~100× speedup over Monte Carlo transfer. Simulations of star clusters and isolated stars in dense metal-poor clouds (Z ≤ 0.01 Z☉) show Ly α feedback dramatically boosts outflows and dominates over direct and infrared radiation pressure. The implied enhanced early feedback has significant implications for Pop III star formation efficiency and ionizing photon escape fractions at cosmic dawn.

The connection to the user's interests runs through the escape fraction: Ly α radiation pressure in metal-poor clouds at cosmic dawn directly controls how efficiently ionizing photons escape to drive reionization. The Lydion code opens the door to multi-dimensional simulations of early-galaxy feedback that have been missing from reionization models.

### [21cmEMUv3: a hybrid diffusion-LSTM emulator of 21cmFAST summary observables](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00219)

Daniela Breitman, Andrei Mesinger, Steven G. Murray et al.  
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO (also astro-ph.GA, cs.LG)

21cmEMUv3 emulates 21cmFASTv3 over 11 astrophysical and cosmological parameters using a hybrid architecture: a score-based diffusion model for the cylindrical 21-cm power spectrum, LSTM networks for five time-evolving summary observables (global signal, optical depth, neutral fraction, UV luminosity function, stellar mass function), and a feed-forward network for the CMB optical depth. The emulator enables combined EoR inference over diverse datasets at negligible computational cost relative to running the full simulator.

This paper sits at the intersection of two rising interests: 21cm EoR cosmology and ML emulation for inference. The use of a diffusion model for the power spectrum is a direct application of the stochastic-interpolant/flow methodology to a cosmological observable. Mesinger's group is the leading EoR inference team and 21cmFASTv3 is the standard EoR simulator.

### [Generative Diffusion Priors for 3D Mapping of the Dark Universe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00803)

Brandon Zhao, Diana Scognamiglio, Olivier Doré et al.  
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO (also cs.CV, cs.LG)

Introduces Conicus3D — a dataset of paired 3D dark matter light cones and 2D lens-plane projections from N-body simulations — and trains a score-based diffusion model as a data-driven prior for 3D dark matter field reconstruction from weak lensing. The diffusion prior replaces handcrafted smoothness priors and learns the full non-Gaussian statistics of cosmic structure. Tested against noisy weak-lensing realizations, it recovers filamentary structure and produces well-calibrated posteriors that standard methods miss.

This is field-level inference using generative models — exactly the "field-level / differentiable forward modelling" expansion direction flagged in the interest file. The approach directly parallels what one would want to do for the Ly α forest (replacing standard power-spectrum summaries with a full-field posterior given a generative simulation prior).

### [Optimal Transport Reconstruction of Biased Tracers in Primordial Non-Gaussian Fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01529)

Farnik Nikakhtar, Ravi K. Sheth, Nikhil Padmanabhan et al.  
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Extends the optimal transport (OT) reconstruction framework — which maps present-day tracer positions back to their Lagrangian initial positions — to handle local-type primordial non-Gaussianity (f_NL). The key advance is a modified "dust" model (the unobserved portion of the full matter field) that accounts for non-Gaussian biasing. Demonstrated on bispectrum statistics, the method accurately recovers f_NL signatures that would otherwise be washed out by non-linear structure formation, opening a new path to constraining primordial non-Gaussianity with next-generation spectroscopic surveys.

Methods for inferring initial conditions from biased tracers directly intersect with the inference methodology interest. OT reconstruction is a competitor/complement to the flow- and SBI-based approaches already in the library, and the extension to non-Gaussian fields makes it relevant to future DESI full-shape analyses.

### [The Murchison Widefield Array Phase III upgrade: Sensitivity Doubled, Number of Baselines Quadrupled, Flexibility Enhanced, and EoR Observations Optimised](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01644)

S. J. Tingay, M. Johnston-Hollitt, R. B. Wayth et al.  
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM (also astro-ph.CO)

Reports completion of the MWA Phase III upgrade: new digital receivers enable full correlation of all 256 antenna tiles simultaneously, doubling instantaneous sensitivity and quadrupling interferometric baselines relative to the previous Phase II configuration (which was limited to 128 tiles). The upgrade is specifically optimized for 21-cm Epoch of Reionization power spectrum measurements and is enabled by the new MWAX correlator. The MWA is the SKA-Low precursor at the same site as the currently-under-construction Square Kilometre Array.

Included as a capability milestone: the MWA is one of the primary instruments for EoR 21-cm observations alongside HERA and CHIME. Doubling sensitivity with the same telescope is a significant step for constraining the neutral fraction and reionization power spectrum, directly complementary to Lyman-alpha forest and optical depth probes.

### [Constraints on the gravitational potential from DESI DR2 BAO and its implications for the local void scenario](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02712)

Indranil Banik, José Antonio Nájera, Harry Desmond  
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO (also astro-ph.GA)

Tests the hypothesis that the Hubble tension arises from a local underdensity (void) by constraining the gravitational redshift offset z₀ between our location and sources at z ≥ 0.3 using DESI DR2 BAO, Planck CMB, and GWTC-4 standard sirens. DESI DR2 data rules out the local void scenario with high confidence unless z₀ is extremely small (consistent with zero and with ΛCDM). The paper carefully accounts for subtle effects: a local void also produces a hotter CMB and a reduced BAO sound horizon r_d, both of which compound against the void interpretation.

Relevant because it uses DESI DR2 BAO — the same dataset as the recently-added DESI DR2 paper in the library — and explicitly tests a non-standard cosmological interpretation. Useful context for understanding the current state of the Hubble tension using DESI data.

### [The sound of dynamical dark energy and modified gravity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00411)

João Rebouças, Guilherme Brando, Felipe T. Falciano et al.  
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Tests whether the dark energy effective sound speed c_s²(k,a) is constrained by current data and what correlations it predicts with the growth rate of structure in modified gravity (MG) scenarios. Uses DESI DR2 BAO + Planck PR4 CMB + Pantheon+ SN + DES-Y3 weak lensing. Current data cannot rule out a wide range of c_s² values, but MG scenarios produce distinctive correlations between sound speed and the growth rate that can in principle be tested with the σ₈/S₈ parameter.

Another DESI DR2 BAO paper — this one characterizing the dark energy perturbation sector rather than just the background expansion. Useful context for the DESI dynamical dark energy signal, which is a topic the user is currently tracking.

### [CSST large-scale structure analysis pipeline: IV. Cosmic Voids Identified from Galaxy Group Samples as Probes of the Large-scale Structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01907)

Yingxiao Song, Xiaohu Yang, Yan Gong et al.  
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO (also astro-ph.GA)

Proposes "group-voids" — voids identified in galaxy group catalogs rather than individual galaxy catalogs — as cleaner LSS probes because groups track halos directly, simplifying theoretical modeling. Using mock galaxy surveys for the Chinese Space-station Survey Telescope (CSST), demonstrates that group-void size functions and density profiles recover those of halo-voids accurately when spectroscopic completeness exceeds ~40%. The approach is also applicable to DESI. Opens a path to building group-void emulators for cosmological parameter inference.

Void cosmology is a secondary interest, and the emulator approach (replacing void statistics computations with a fast neural surrogate) directly parallels the emulation strategy in the library. The DESI applicability makes it near-term relevant.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Detection of a four-carbon sugar in interstellar space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03313)

Izaskun Jimenez-Serra, Juan Garcia de la Concepcion et al.  
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA (also astro-ph.EP, astro-ph.SR)

Reports the first detection of a sugar — erythrulose, a four-carbon ketose — in the interstellar medium, using ultrasensitive broadband spectral surveys toward the Galactic Center molecular cloud G+0.693-0.027 with the Yebes 40m and IRAM 30m telescopes. Erythrulose is at least 8× more abundant than its 3-carbon analogs, which were not detected in the same survey. Quantum chemical and astrochemical models indicate it forms efficiently on interstellar dust grain surfaces from two-carbon species. As a sugar that readily isomerizes into aldoses in aqueous environments, it could have contributed to the prebiotic sugar inventory on the early Earth.

First detection of any sugar in the ISM — a scientific first in astrochemistry and astrobiology. Completely outside the cosmology interest area but a notable result by any measure: 14 atoms, the largest non-cyclic molecule and the first four-oxygen molecule detected in the ISM, detected via radio spectroscopy.
