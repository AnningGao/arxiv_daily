### [Probing Direct Contributions of Galaxies and AGN to Cosmic Reionization in a Quasar Field J0226+0302 with JWST NIRCam and NIRSpec](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14959v1)

Xiangyu Jin, Jinyi Yang, Feige Wang et al. (incl. high-value co-authors George D. Becker, Yongda Zhu)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

Using JWST NIRCam WFSS/imaging and NIRSpec MSA in the z=6.54 quasar field J0226+0302, the authors identify 65 new line emitters at 5.3<z<6.4 and measure the IGM–galaxy cross-correlation against the quasar Lyman-α forest. They find a ~2σ excess in IGM transmission at ~10–40 cMpc from star-forming galaxies, consistent with THESAN at a neutral fraction of 5–7% and an average escape fraction f_esc~6%. NIRSpec Hα detections reveal four broad-line AGN (AGN fraction 8±4%); excess transmission within ~5 h⁻¹ cMpc of AGN implies very high AGN escape fractions (~50–100%) via the transverse proximity effect. The work establishes the IGM–AGN cross-correlation as a future probe of the AGN contribution to reionization.

A direct hit on the primary IGM/reionization focus: forest effective optical depth, IGM transmission, the z>5 neutral fraction, and the sources of the UV background, with explicit THESAN comparison.

### [Benchmarking Exact, GP-Emulated, and Simulation-Based Inference for Late-Time Cosmology](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16248v1)

Sai Swagat Mishra
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

A controlled, like-for-like comparison of three inference paradigms — exact MCMC, GP-emulated MCMC, and neural simulation-based inference (SBI via neural posterior estimation) — for late-time ΛCDM using Cosmic Chronometers, DESI DR2 BAO, and Pantheon+. For CC+DESI all three agree on (H₀, Ω_m) to better than 0.3σ; for the tighter CC+DESI+Pantheon+, method-dependent shifts reach ~1.5σ in a single parameter even though reconstructed expansion histories agree at the percent level. SBI delivers order-of-magnitude runtime savings through amortization while GP emulation stays bottlenecked by MCMC sampling, and the paper maps SBI posterior stability against simulation budget.

Squarely in the rising-primary ML/statistical-inference focus (SBI, NPE, emulators) and the secondary DESI BAO interest — a practical reference on the fidelity-versus-cost trade-offs of SBI against exact likelihoods.

### [Probing the fate of large primordial perturbations with exoplanets](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14827v1)

Théo Paré, Julien Lavalle
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

The authors propose ultra-wide-orbit exoplanets and brown-dwarf companions as a dynamical probe of compact dark-matter substructure: impulsive gravitational heating from passing dark objects accumulates over the (well-measured) stellar age. Specializing to ultra-compact minihalos (UCMHs) seeded by enhanced small-scale primordial perturbations, they build a Galactic UCMH population from standard structure-formation theory and derive limits on the injection scale and amplitude of the primordial power spectrum. The resulting bounds are competitive with and complementary to CMB and pulsar-timing-array constraints, with sensitivity mainly to UCMHs above ~10⁵ M_⊙, and they flag orbital-misalignment signatures in multiplanet systems as an additional diagnostic.

A direct hit on the secondary focus (dark matter from small-scale structure and the primordial power spectrum): the headline constraint plot is benchmarked explicitly against Lyman-α forest limits on the small-scale PPS, the researcher's core territory.

### [Tomography of the gamma-ray sky from cross-correlation with DESI DR2 and unWISE galaxies](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16297v1)

Alex Krolewski, Neal Dalal, Will J. Percival et al. (incl. DESI co-authors A. Cuceu, A. Font-Ribera)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.HE

The authors cross-correlate the Fermi-LAT gamma-ray sky (resolved point sources and the unresolved gamma-ray background, UGRB) with 3D LSS maps from DESI DR2 and unWISE to recover bias-weighted redshift distributions tomographically. Point sources cluster strongly (bias b~2, massive AGN-hosting halos ~10¹³ M_⊙), while the UGRB is detected at ~10σ total but with weaker amplitude, implying a substantial UGRB fraction not arising from z<2 LSS. They note tension with prior shear-based UGRB analyses and argue z>2 tracers or CMB lensing are needed to pin down high-redshift emission.

Adjacent: the science target (gamma-ray sources) is off-focus, but the tomographic cross-correlation methodology with DESI/unWISE and the familiar DESI estimator machinery is exactly the secondary-interest LSS toolkit worth tracking.

### [Joint Modeling of GD-1 and C-19 as Old Streams](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15863v1)

Raymond G. Carlberg, Ting S. Li, Emma Jarvis et al. (incl. DESI co-author A. Font-Ribera)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

DESI spectroscopy of the GD-1 and C-19 stellar streams is compared to streams evolved 13 Gyr in a common cosmological multi-halo Milky-Way potential populated with CDM or WDM subhalos. Using the (geometry-insensitive) stream velocity width and the stream-density power spectrum, the authors find CDM best reproduces the observed velocity widths and rule out streams younger than ~10 Gyr as too cold. The GD-1 density power spectrum carries 1.7–1.9× more power than 7 keV and 5.5 keV WDM simulations, favoring CDM-like small-scale substructure; inferred progenitor masses are ~8×10⁴ M_⊙ (GD-1) and ~4×10⁴ M_⊙ (C-19).

Secondary interest: a warm-vs-cold dark-matter free-streaming constraint at the same keV scales the researcher probes with the forest, here delivered via stream density power spectra from DESI data.

### [Constraints with CMB lensing on dark matter decays to massive decay products](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14849v1)

Anna Bencke, Nanoom Lee, Marc Kamionkowski
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

Motivated by DESI DR2 BAO preferring a ~5% lower late-time matter density than Planck, the authors test decaying-dark-matter models where DM decays into two warm massive daughters (parameterized by decay fraction, rate, and mass retention) using the integral-equation Boltzmann solver CLASSIER-DDM. Combining DESI DR2 BAO with Planck 2018 CMB+lensing, DDM is not favored over ΛCDM because CMB lensing tightly constrains the daughters' velocity kicks. Free-streaming lengths at the 1σ boundary are ~30–200 h⁻¹ Mpc, so models suppressing sub-galactic power remain unconstrained, and DESI explanations via decay to a single massless particle will be lensing-limited.

Secondary interest: DM free-streaming and small-scale power suppression — the WDM-like physics the forest constrains — set against the DESI low-Ω_m / dynamical-dark-energy context, here probed via CMB lensing rather than the forest.

### [Neural Bayesian Anomaly Mitigation: A Robust Loss that Doubles as an Unsupervised Contamination Classifier](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16524v1)

S. A. K. Leeney, W. J. Handley, H. T. J. Bevins et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: astro-ph.CO

The authors derive a drop-in supervised loss (NBAM) from a Bayesian latent-switch mixture model: each sample carries a latent contamination flag that is analytically marginalized, yielding a negative-log-marginal-likelihood objective that is simultaneously robust and produces a per-sample contamination posterior. Two learned heads — an input-dependent contamination prior and a structured anomaly likelihood — let the model recover the structure of the corruption process, with a normalization-induced "Occam penalty" regularizing over-flagging without a tunable coefficient. On CIFAR-10 under asymmetric label flips it beats CE/Student-t/Huber/GCE, and the posterior cleanly separates clean from corrupted samples.

Adjacent methods value: a clean Bayesian-marginalization recipe for contamination-aware, uncertainty-propagating inference that maps onto the researcher's ML-inference interest — and the same formalism the group already uses for RFI excision and SN Ia light-curve curation, both relevant to forest/IGM data pipelines.

*No figure extracted for 2606.16524 (the three-head architecture is conveyed adequately in text for a methods-transfer read).*

### [Scalable Bayesian data curation for next-generation radio experiments](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16525v1)

S. A. K. Leeney, E. de Lera Acedo, W. J. Handley et al. (incl. high-value co-authors A. Fialkov, G. Kulkarni)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.CO

This is the astronomy-application companion to the NBAM paper above: the same Bayesian anomaly-detection formalism (a latent anomaly indicator marginalized inside the likelihood, giving each datum a posterior anomaly probability) is used for fully automatic curation of 4655 first-year REACH global-21cm drift-scan spectra. A two-stage JAX/BlackJAX GPU nested-sampling pipeline fits a chromatic foreground model with a per-channel anomaly mixture, compresses each observation to a reference-frequency posterior, then fits a population-level Fourier model with a second anomaly mixture, the order chosen automatically by Bayesian evidence. The anomaly scores recover persistent RFI, weather-driven systematics, and instrument drifts without thresholds, propagating curation uncertainty into the inference.

Adjacent: a concrete, evidence-driven, threshold-free automated curation framework for a 21cm/cosmic-dawn experiment (adjacent reionization probe), authored by high-value reionization names — a directly transferable approach to systematics-aware inference.

*No figure extracted for 2606.16525 (the antenna-temperature/anomaly waterfalls are technical instrument diagnostics that do not aid a quick read).*

### [Impact of CMB low-ℓ EE polarization data on dark energy parameterizations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15603v1)

Shubham Barua, Shantanu Desai
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors test how removing the Planck low-ℓ EE (sroll2) likelihood — which dominates the τ_reio measurement — affects three dynamical dark-energy parameterizations (CPL, JBP, BA) plus ΛCDM, using Planck or ACT DR6 with DESI DR2 BAO and Pantheon+. Excluding low-ℓ EE shifts τ_reio and A_s upward through the A_s e^(−2τ) degeneracy (1.4–1.8σ in A_s) and pushes the equation of state toward quintessence (w>−1), most strongly for JBP. AIC/DIC comparison favors CPL and BA, with the SNe likelihood the main driver, and they argue τ_reio is a common thread across the DDE preference, negative-neutrino-mass preference, and Hubble tension.

Adjacent: secondary-interest DESI BAO + dynamical dark energy, but the central lever is the optical depth to reionization — sitting at the edge of the primary IGM/reionization focus — driving the dark-energy conclusions.

*No figure extracted for 2606.15603 (results are a table-and-heatmap study with no single decisive plot).*

### [The cosmic tetrarchy: four estimators breaking the assumption degeneracy in cosmological distance tensions](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16844v1)

Matteo Martinelli, Domenico Sapone
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The paper recasts distance-tension consistency as a single null test — that the dimensionless sound-horizon ratio r_d/r_d^fid be one redshift-independent number — and shows it admits exactly four reconstructions by combining BAO (α_perp, α_par) with either SNIa or cosmic chronometers, transverse or radial. Each channel depends on a distinct subset of assumptions (distance-ladder calibration, distance duality, flatness, standard ruler), so the pattern of (dis)agreement localizes which assumption fails; the radial CC-anchored channel requires none. Evaluated on DESI DR2 BAO + Pantheon+ + cosmic chronometers with both a binned full-covariance analysis and a Bayesian GP reconstruction, the data are compatible with a constant ratio, the only robust feature being a coherent SNIa-vs-CC normalization offset interpreted as a model-independent Hubble tension.

Adjacent: secondary-interest BAO/DESI plus model-independent estimator design, with GP reconstruction methodology overlapping the statistical-inference interest.

*No figure extracted for 2606.16844 (the four-reconstruction plot is informative but secondary to the digest; text suffices).*

### [The Peculiar Growth of Structure: Validating fσ8 Measurements from TITAN Type Ia Supernovae and the Uchuu Simulations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14962v1)

Mitchell Dixon, David O. Jones, Nicole E. Drakos et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This is an end-to-end, simulation-based validation and systematic-error budget for measuring the growth rate fσ8 from SN Ia peculiar velocities via the reconstruction-and-scaling method, tailored to the ~2000 low-z SNe of the TITAN/ATLAS survey. Using eight Uchuu N-body subvolumes with UniverseMachine galaxies and SNANA SN Ia simulations with correlated peculiar velocities, they recover ⟨fσ8⟩ = 0.429 ± 0.038 (σ_stat=0.030, σ_sys=0.023), matching the Uchuu input 0.428 to 0.1%, with mock-to-mock scatter confirming the error estimates. SN and reconstruction systematics contribute roughly equally, and the assumed SN Ia intrinsic-scatter model is the single largest systematic.

Adjacent: secondary-interest growth-of-structure/LSS, valued for its rigorous forward-likelihood-plus-N-body-mock systematics treatment — the kind of simulation-based validation methodology that transfers to data-driven inference.

*No figure extracted for 2606.14962 (the headline is a single validated number plus a systematics breakdown).*

### [HYPERION. The cold ISM of rapidly growing z>6 quasars: diverse gas reservoirs, dust enrichment, and feedback signatures](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16012v1)

Claudia Maria Pierro, Roberta Tripodi, Laura Pentericci et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using new ALMA Band 3 (CO(6-5) and ~100 GHz continuum) plus archival ALMA/NOEMA data, the authors characterize the cold ISM of ten z>6 HYPERION quasars. They detect continuum in eight hosts and CO(6-5) in four, inferring molecular gas masses ~10¹⁰ M_⊙ with non-detections at a few ×10⁹ M_⊙, indicating a broad range of reservoirs. Well-sampled FIR SEDs for two QSOs yield unusually low dust temperatures (36 and 32 K) and one of the lowest measured high-z gas-to-dust ratios (~16), with a tentative link between dust temperature, X-ray photon index, and CIV velocity shift suggesting winds redistribute dust — overall a heterogeneous population with unsynchronized SMBH growth, star formation, and feedback.

Adjacent: these are the same luminous z>6 quasars whose sightlines probe the Lyman-α forest and reionization, so their host ISM/feedback properties are useful context for the adjacent galaxy-evolution interest, even though the science here is ISM rather than IGM.

*No figure extracted for 2606.16012 (an observational continuum-map and SED study with no single cosmology-at-a-glance plot).*

### [MA-SBI: Misspecification-Aware Simulation-Based Inference via Side-Channel Guidance](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16923v1)

Arunkumar V, Manoranjan Gandhudi, Gangadharan G. R. et al.
**Primary category:** cs.AI | also: stat.ML

The paper tackles simulator misspecification in SBI/NPE without requiring ground-truth calibration pairs (which prior state-of-the-art RoPE needs). It learns a corrector mapping unstructured side-channel text (regime labels, instructions, bulletins) to an observation-space shift applied before any pre-trained amortized posterior, with no retraining; a theorem bounds the achievable bias reduction by the mutual information between misspecification and side-channel. On "hide-the-calibration" SLCP benchmarks, MA-SBI with text alone is statistically equivalent to the oracle and beats RoPE variants, and a three-way decomposition separates a constant correction from a regime-conditional residual.

Adjacent methods value: this is the same amortized NPE/SBI machinery the researcher uses, and robustness to misspecification is a real concern for forest emulator/SBI pipelines — though the text-side-channel mechanism has no clean analogue in forest cosmology, limiting practical transfer.

### [Bridging data-driven priors via the score function for posterior sampling — Comparative review and experimental study](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14800v1)

Elhadji Cisse Faye, Mame Diarra Fall, Sylvain Delchini et al.
**Primary category:** stat.ME | also: cs.LG, stat.ML

This review unifies a diverse set of data-driven priors for Bayesian inverse problems — regularization-by-denoising, score-based generative models, normalizing-flow priors, and convex-ridge regularizers — through their respective score functions, and shows they all plug into a single Langevin-within-Split-Gibbs sampler built on asymptotically exact data augmentation. The split separates data-fitting from regularization into two conditionals, the prior conditional sampled via a Langevin step exploiting the explicit prior score. Experiments on inpainting, super-resolution, and geological-image restoration show the sampler compares favorably to ULA-based schemes across all four prior families.

Adjacent methods value: score-based priors, normalizing flows, and score-based generative models as plug-in Bayesian priors are exactly the toolkit the researcher tracks; the unifying score-function framing and Split-Gibbs sampler could transfer to cosmological inverse problems, though the demos are image-restoration only.

*No figure extracted for 2606.14800 (the contribution is a unifying framework and sampler, not a single decisive plot; demos are image-restoration examples).*

### [Unlocking Latent Dimensions: Exploring Representations of Large-Scale X-ray Scattering Data using Variational Autoencoders](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14999v1)

Monika Choudhary, Xiaoya Chong, Runbo Jiang et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The authors train a domain-specific attention-based convolutional VAE on 1.5 million X-ray scattering images, learning low-dimensional latent representations that organize scattering regimes into interpretable clusters and smooth trajectories and support controlled synthetic generation via UMAP-guided latent sampling. Deployed without retraining on live experiments at two synchrotrons, it organizes unseen time-resolved data in real time (~0.05 s/image). Benchmarked head-to-head against the general-purpose DINOv3 (ViT-7B) foundation model, the bespoke C-VAE yields cleaner cluster separation and smoother trajectories.

Off-area scientifically (materials X-ray scattering), but the "domain-specific model beats a 7B-parameter general-purpose vision foundation model" result is a genuinely useful data point for the rising-primary interest in scientific foundation models — it speaks directly to whether to build bespoke representations/emulators or fine-tune a large foundation model for forest/IGM/spectroscopic data.
