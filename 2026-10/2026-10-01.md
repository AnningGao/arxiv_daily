## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Probing the Reionization History and Bubble Sizes with JWST Lyman-$α$ Fraction Measurements](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40363v1)

Aritra Kundu, Adam Lidz, Guochao Sun
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The paper models the JWST Lyα-fraction evolution at $z\sim6$–14 by **forward-modelling IGM damping-wing transmission through the Thesan radiation-hydrodynamic reionization simulations**. ISM/CGM escape is handled by a Gaussian line profile with optional outflows, calibrated to the observed $z\approx6$ EW distribution (Tang et al.). With no further tuning, **Thesan's reionization history and bubble sizes match the Kageura et al. fractions ($\chi^2_\nu = 0.4$–1.2)**. A semi-analytic model (bubble size plus global exterior $\langle x_{\rm HI}\rangle$) reproduces the exact patchy sightline calculation well enough for current data. The authors then vary the history and obtain **$\langle x_{\rm HI}\rangle = 0.38^{+0.14}_{-0.38}$, $0.57^{+0.23}_{-0.57}$, $0.93^{+0.05}_{-0.17}$ at $z\sim7$, 8, 11** (EW > 25 Å). The main caveat is the assumption that the intrinsic EW distribution does not evolve. Lower dust at high $z$ would push toward a more neutral IGM, while dense ISM/CGM HI columns would push the other way.

![](figures/2609.40363/X_Lya_fiducial.pdf)

*Calibrated only at $z=6$, both the exact (patchy) and semi-analytic models follow the observed decline in Lyα fraction out to $z\sim14$, for both EW > 25 Å and > 10 Å. Including outflows barely changes the curves once the $z=6$ EW calibration is redone.*

### [Raising the Optical Depth to Reionization with Dark Matter Decay](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39923v1)

Yu-Ning Wang, Paulo Montero-Camacho, Ziwei Wang et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The DESI DR2 vs. CMB discrepancy can be recast as a preference for **$\tau_{\rm reio}\simeq0.09$**, above the ≈0.06 measured from large-scale polarization. This paper tests whether **DM decaying through $\chi\to e^+e^-$** can supply the extra free electrons. Energy injection is computed with DarkHistory and added to a Gompertzian astrophysical reionization history, and the fit uses ACT+Planck, CMB lensing, BAO and **quasar damping-wing $x_{\rm HI}$ constraints**. For $m_\chi = 1$ GeV, the marginalized optical depth moves from **$\tau = 0.064$ to $0.072$**, but the fit improves by only $\Delta\chi^2=-0.66$. $P(\sum m_\nu \ge 0.06\,{\rm eV})$ rises from 8.5% to 13.1%. Across 2 MeV–7 GeV the 68% intervals stay below the $\tau\simeq0.08$ needed to relieve the tension, so **decay moves things the right way but not far enough**. The extra $\tau$ comes from a broad dark-ages ionization tail rather than earlier astrophysical reionization, which keeps the late-reionization damping-wing constraints intact.

![](figures/2609.39923/ionization_history.pdf)

*DM decay adds a long, low-level $x_e$ tail across the dark ages (left), while the end of reionization (right) stays where the quasar damping-wing data require. The extra optical depth therefore comes from high redshift, not from changing the reionization midpoint.*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Is DESI Seeing Dynamical Dark Energy, or a Cosmic Glitch in Gravity?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40176v1)

Robin Y. Wen, Lukas Tobias Hergt, Niayesh Afshordi et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The paper proposes a **one-parameter alternative to $w_0w_a$CDM: a cosmological Newton constant different from the local one**, $\Omega_g = 1 - G_N/G_{\rm cosmo}$. It is motivated by low-energy Hořava/khronometric gravity in the incompressible-khronon limit, which evades Solar-System and GW170817 bounds. Using Planck PR4 + ACT DR6 + SPT-3G with DESI DR2 BAO, they find **$G_{\rm cosmo}/G_N = 0.9920\pm0.0025$, a 3.3σ preference for weaker cosmological gravity**, robust to adding CMB lensing and DES Dovekie SNe. With one parameter instead of two, it fits at least as well as $w_0w_a$CDM. Their sharpest point is that **fitted to the CMB alone, the glitch model predicts the DESI distances, while $w_0w_a$CDM does not**. That matters for anyone interpreting DR2 Lyα BAO in an extended-cosmology context.

![](figures/2609.40176/glitch_bao_residuals.pdf)

*Without any BAO input, the CMB-only glitch posterior (teal) passes through the DESI DR2 points, including the Lyα point. The CMB-only ΛCDM and $w_0w_a$CDM predictions sit off them. This is the paper's case that a single gravitational-coupling offset accounts for the CMB–BAO mismatch.*

### [Two bits about lossy compression: On the limits of compression in cosmology](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40239v1)

Hurum Maksora Tohfa, Matthew McQuinn
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

The paper asks how many bits per pixel cosmological simulation outputs actually need at percent-level pixel accuracy. It compares three coders: **a rate-distortion-optimal Gaussian (reverse water-filling) Fourier coder**, the SZ3 scientific compressor, and **a neural coder that fixes the quantizer and learns bin probabilities with an autoregressive transformer**. The test fields are weak-lensing convergence maps, Bolshoi densities, and N-body displacements and velocities. Because the quantizer sets the error, a poorly trained network can only waste bits, never accuracy. The transformer wins on every field, **9–27% below the Gaussian coder**, with the biggest gain where fields are most non-Gaussian. Eulerian grids need only **1–4 bpp**, particle data 5–6 bpp, and power spectra stay well within 1%. A model trained only on lensing maps transfers to N-body densities, and the authors tie compression limits to where cosmological information lives. That is directly relevant to storing large hydro/forest simulation suites.

### [Gestalt: a meta-foundation model for astronomy](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38312v1)

Michael J. Smith, Shashwat Sourav
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: cs.LG

Motivated by the Platonic Representation Hypothesis, the authors **embed galaxy images with a basket of 22 frozen foundation models from eight families**. They whiten each embedding and take a randomized SVD of the concatenation, giving a 1024-d "meta" representation with no training beyond that one projection. Using linear probes on HSC and JWST (COSMOS-Web), DESI Legacy Survey and GZ10, **Gestalt beats every basket member on 19/21 metrics** (photo-z, stellar mass, sSFR, morphology). Performance grows with basket size and architectural diversity, and transfers across surveys. Building it takes minutes on one machine, versus $\mathcal{O}(10^4$–$10^5)$ A100-hours to pre-train a comparable domain model. For a spectral foundation-model project, the useful result is that **whitened ensembles of off-the-shelf encoders can be a strong, cheap baseline**.

### [PTED: A multi-dimensional two-sample test for scientific inference and generative machine learning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38388v1)

Connor Stone
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: physics.data-an, stat.ML

The paper packages **an energy-distance permutation test (Székely & Rizzo) as an exact multi-dimensional two-sample test**. Because it uses only pairwise distances, it works in high dimensions, on learned feature spaces, and with unbalanced samples, and a landmark approximation makes it scale linearly. It is benchmarked against KS, MMD, FID, FLD and PQM on Gaussian, two-moons over-fitting, MNIST and CIFAR-10 perturbations. **PTED is the only method sensitive to every test**, and MMD matches it everywhere except replication detection. The author also shows it as a **posterior-coverage test for SBI**, flagging over- and under-confident posteriors, and compares it with KS and HPD-region coverage tests. It is a practical drop-in for validating emulators, generative spectral models or neural posteriors.

### [Mitigating Representation Gaps in Amortized Bayesian Inference with Auxiliary Supervision](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39525v1)

Hans Olischläger, Svenja Jedhoff, Šimon Kucharský et al.
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG, stat.CO, stat.ME

Amortized neural posterior estimators often settle on summary networks that are not maximally informative. The authors frame this as **a local optimum at the information bottleneck between summary and inference networks**. Their fix adds **auxiliary posterior-mean losses on the summary space** (directly, or through an ensemble of point-estimate heads), weighted to stay comparable to the main loss. They also add a **canonical-correlation diagnostic that separates summary failures from inference failures**. On toy location-scale problems and four real case studies (BayesFlow, coupling flows and flow matching), guidance speeds convergence and recovers poorly estimated parameters without hurting calibration. In one case it does not help, and the diagnostic correctly blames the architecture instead. This applies directly to SBI pipelines that learn summaries of P1D or forest fields.

### [When Streams Curve Away: a Test of Dark Matter from Extragalactic Stellar Stream Populations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40057v1)

Nathaniel Starkman, Jacob Nibauer, Sarah Pearson et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

This paper proposes a purely geometric halo-shape test. **If any segment of a projected stellar stream curves away from the host's projected centre of mass, it rules out all LoS-axisymmetric, centrally concentrated (including spherical) potentials.** The test needs no kinematics, distances, progenitor model or potential fit. Halo triaxiality sets the population-level rate of such "convex" segments, so the rate becomes an observable of the halo-shape distribution. CDM halos are triaxial, while self-interactions round SIDM halos, so the rate differs between the two. Current Local-Group samples (N≈30–100) fall well short of 1σ. However, **Euclid/Rubin/Roman-scale extragalactic stream catalogs could separate CDM from SIDM at 3σ, and up to 5σ** with the largest Roman catalogs. Converting a measured rate into $\sigma/m$ still requires SIDM simulations of halo shapes at large radii.

### [An LSST-DESC Precursor Project: Hyper Suprime-Cam Year 1 $3\times2$pt in Harmonic Space](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39603v1)

D. Sanchez-Cid, J. Sanchez, I. Sevilla-Noarbe et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This is the first fully photometric **HSC Y1 3×2pt analysis in harmonic space run end to end with the LSST-DESC stack** (TXPipe, TJPCov, Firecrown), as a dress rehearsal for LSST Y1. A key step is **mode deprojection of spin-2 survey-property maps** from the shear field; cross-correlations with CMB maps confirm it removes no cosmological signal. Null tests pass, and the official HSC Y1 cosmic-shear constraints are reproduced. The joint result is **$S_8 = 0.821^{+0.018}_{-0.021}$, $\Omega_m = 0.249^{+0.061}_{-0.051}$**, consistent with DES Y6, with no preference for $w\neq-1$ or massive neutrinos. It is mostly a validation paper, useful as a reference point for the $S_8$ landscape and harmonic-space systematics handling.

### [Right Answer, Wrong Mechanism: Detecting Pernicious Divergence in Causal Interventions](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39243v1)

Beiming Liu, Minjie Chen
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Activation patching and DAS can push representations off-distribution and produce the "right" output through **hidden pathways the model never uses on natural inputs**. To make this testable, the authors **plant silent pathways in GPT-2 small** and run 100,800 interventions across 72 configurations. **Nearest-neighbour and local-PCA off-manifold distances score below chance (AUROC 0.35–0.47)** at flagging pathway-driven successes. Their label-free Hidden-Pathway Contribution test, which clamps downstream units to their natural-run regime, reaches **AUROC ≥ 0.99**, but only when the pathway shows up as out-of-range unit activity. It fails when everything stays in range, which they leave open. Optimized interventions actively exploit hidden pathways: on a gender task, DAS routes 90–95% of its successes through planted pathways. This is a cautionary result for attention- or patching-based interpretability of a quasar-spectra transformer.

### [Which Tasks Survive Self-Supervised Learning?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38393v1)

Achleshwar Luthra, Lucas Bryant, Tracy Zhu et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.AI

The paper asks which downstream tasks remain recoverable from a same-instance (two-view) SSL representation. The authors define **semantic recoverability: the fraction of a task's posterior score captured by the representation's function space**. For centred, whitened features it exactly sets directional neural collapse (CDNV) and few-shot nearest-centroid accuracy, and makes the linear probe coincide with the centroid axis. For a canonical two-view objective, the population optimum spans **the leading cross-view-stable modes of a two-view operator**. A task therefore survives SSL **to the extent that its posterior lies in that spectral subspace**. Predictions are checked on synthetic and real data across several SSL methods. In practice, choosing augmentations (e.g. continuum or noise perturbations of spectra) decides which physical labels a pretrained spectral encoder can still linearly decode.

### [Learning Chaos Without Seeing Chaos: Extrapolation of Global Dynamics in Autoregressive Transformers](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38814v1)

Yilun Liu, Yi Zhang, Ganyu Wu et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: nlin.CD

Small autoregressive transformers are trained from scratch on **continuous-token trajectories from restricted parameter windows** of the logistic and sine maps, Lorenz, and generalized Hopf systems. Run in closed loop far outside training, they **recover period-doubling cascades, chaos and multistable attractors never seen in training**. For the logistic map the model reproduces doublings up to period 128, with a scaling ratio of **4.6687, within $5\times10^{-4}$ of the Feigenbaum constant**. Causal interventions show how the control-parameter token is routed through attention into state prediction. The global structure appears early in training and does not track one-step OOD accuracy. Relevant to transformer pretraining on continuous scientific sequences: **local next-token training can imprint global dynamical structure**, and one-step loss is a poor proxy for it.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Removing Timing Shortcuts Improves Non-Invasive Brain-to-Text](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40359v1)

Dulhan Jayalath, Oiwi Parker Jones
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: q-bio.NC

The authors show that **most of the headline gain in a high-profile MEG/EEG brain-to-text result (d'Ascoli et al. 2025) is reproducible with no brain data at all**. Overlapping word-locked windows leak inter-word intervals, so the network learns word durations instead of neural signal: **22.0% balanced accuracy on synthetic signals vs. 22.3% on real recordings**. Decoding each window independently removes the shortcut. After that, averaging repeated observations and adding an LLM prior become genuinely useful. The resulting SimpleB2T reaches a 36.6% word error rate with five observations per word. As a methodology lesson for ML in science, **an architecture can quietly exploit how the data are windowed, and a no-signal control is what exposes it**.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [The AI crisis for teaching: "Train your own neural network!"](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39433v1)

Douglas Scott
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

An essay arguing that while the astronomy community debates how LLMs and agents change research, **the real crisis is in teaching**, where near-instant AI solutions to any problem set have made graded homework meaningless. Scott describes what his department is trying: dropping homework grades, weighting exams more heavily, device-free quizzes and oral exams. He also notes that AI devices are now showing up even in formal exams. His core argument is that **students must "train their own neural networks"**, using AI to support learning rather than replace it, so that graduates can still think like astrophysicists. It is short and opinionated, a useful counterpoint to the many essays focused on research.
