## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Shared Geometry Is Not Shared Physics: A Layerwise Test of the Platonic Representation Hypothesis in Astronomy](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04130v1)

Kshitij Duraphe, Aravind Kannappan, Dun Li Chan et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: cs.LG

The authors extract every layer's representation from **36 pretrained models** (including astronomy foundation models) on cross-matched objects: HSC images, DESI spectra, Legacy Survey, JWST and COSMOS-Web. They then separate geometric alignment (mutual-kNN, CKA, Procrustes, retrieval) from scientific transfer, meaning whether a linear probe for a physical property carries across surveys. All 139 model–survey comparisons show significant local alignment, but alignment does not grow with depth. Choosing a layer by cross-survey geometry instead of labels always costs held-out R². On HSC–COSMOS-Web, within-survey redshift probes work (mean R² = 0.50) while **every cross-survey redshift transfer is negative** (median R² ≈ −1.4). Models therefore **share geometry without a survey-invariant linear encoding of physics**, which matters directly for any multimodal spectral foundation model.

![](figures/2610.04130/04_scientific_transfer.pdf)

*Picking a representation stage by geometric alignment instead of labels always loses R² (a). For 31 aligned HSC–COSMOS-Web model pairs, within-survey redshift R² is always positive and cross-survey R² always negative (b): aligned does not mean physically interchangeable.*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Testing $Λ$LTB as an alternative to dynamical dark energy](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05729v1)

Yukang Zhang, Pengjie Zhang
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors build a **first-order perturbative ΛLTB framework**: a cosmological constant in a weakly radially inhomogeneous universe, with analytic distance–redshift relations and a kSZ prediction that needs no ray-tracing. They fit DESI DR2 BAO + Planck with each of Union3, Pantheon+, DES SN5YR and DES Dovekie. All four prefer an observer inside a shallow overdensity, and the fit matches w0waCDM (|Δχ²| ≲ 1.7) with the same number of parameters. Adding a **kSZ bound at ℓ = 3000** removes most large-amplitude, large-radius profiles, but **a ~300 Mpc/h region with ~10% matter excess remains allowed**. That keeps local inhomogeneity alive as an alternative reading of the DESI dynamical-dark-energy signal.

![](figures/2610.05729/SN_kSZ_limit.pdf)

*Posteriors on profile amplitude vs. scale radius for the four SN+BAO+CMB combinations, with lines of constant kSZ power. The kSZ limit cuts the kSZ-bright part of each posterior (most strongly for Pantheon+ and Union3), but a compact ~10% overdensity survives.*

### [Limits on the size of electron density fluctuations in the early universe and implications for the Hubble tension](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05147v1)

Gabriel P. Lynch, Jens Chluba, Richard Battye et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors model kpc–Mpc electron-density fluctuations at recombination in a modified Boltzmann code, **classito**. Besides accelerated recombination, they include a new photon-transport effect: line-of-sight correlations lower the effective Thomson rate and enhance diffusion damping. Planck+ACT DR6+SPT-3G show no detection, with **⟨δ_e²⟩ < 0.21 (95%), tightening to < 0.033 at a 2 Mpc coherence length**, mapped onto the electron power spectrum at 1 ≲ k ≲ 100 Mpc⁻¹. Planck + SH0ES alone allows H0 = 71.1 ± 0.7, but **ACT and SPT-3G break the degeneracy through the TE damping tail** and leave only H0 = 69.3 ± 0.5 with no preference over ΛCDM.

![](figures/2610.05147/power_spectrum_constraints.pdf)

*95% upper limits on the electron-density variance vs. coherence length. The limit is flat where the recombination channel dominates and falls roughly as 1/Δ_c once photon transport takes over; adding ACT+SPT tightens it well beyond Planck PR3.*

### [Weak lensing, Big Magnification: Recovering hidden lensing information from galaxy positions](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03913v1)

Alex Alarcon, Carles Sánchez
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

In deep z ≈ 0.7–2.5 photometric samples, galaxies with positive and negative magnification response cancel each other's foreground–background cross-correlation signal. Using DES Y6 photometry and **Balrog injections to split background galaxies by the sign of their magnification response**, the authors define "BigMag": foreground clustering combined with response-selected background positions. In optimistic DES-like forecasts this raises cross-correlation S/N from ~64 to ~194. Combined with inverse galaxy–galaxy lensing it reaches σ(S8) ≈ 0.012, close to 3×2pt, and **adding it to 3×2pt cuts σ(S8) by 16–29%**. The gains depend on calibrating responses to ~0.01–0.02, which current Balrog statistics cannot yet deliver.

### [Forecasting cosmological constraints from the nonlinear halo density field with Zel'dovich-inspired transform](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04486v1)

Yu Zhou, Yun Wang, Haozhe Zhang et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors apply a **local Gaussianizing "Zel'dovich-inspired" transform**, δ_ZI = η[1 − (1+δ)^(−1/η)], which becomes a log transform as η → ∞, to redshift-space Quijote and Quijote-PNG halo fields. They run Fisher forecasts on the combined η = 1, 10, ∞ monopole power spectra to k_max = 0.5 h/Mpc, with a calibrated scale-dependent shot-noise correction. The joint ZI spectrum beats the standard halo P(k) by **factors of 5.6, 5.5, 5.2 and 5.8 on f_NL^equil, f_NL^ortho, Mν and n_s**, and keeps gaining information at high k where P(k) saturates. Caveats: Fisher-only, z = 0, sparse halos, and no theory model yet for the noise term.

### [Forming Molecular Hydrogen in a Galaxy Halo Through Star Formation Wind Interactions](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06662v1)

Brad Koplitz, Sanchayeeta Borthakur, Timothy Heckman et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

HST/COS spectra of QSO J0057+1446 show 19 H₂ lines (J = 0–3, log N(H₂) = 16.52 ± 0.05) in the CGM of a starbursting LIRG at z ≈ 0.17, **139 kpc from the galaxy**. The authors call this the farthest H₂ yet detected from a host. The cloud has an unusually low HI column (log N(HI) = 18.52, very uncertain because Lyα is saturated) and super-solar metallicity, and it fits a **single excitation temperature of 245 K** with no cold-core/warm-envelope structure. Comparing formation scenarios with Cloudy cooling models, the authors favour **in-situ H₂ formation in cooling wind–CGM interface gas**, giving a short-lived but direct probe of wind–halo microphysics.

![](figures/2610.06662/totalH_fracH2.pdf)

*Molecular fraction vs. neutral-H column: this absorber sits apart from Milky Way, IVC/HVC, Magellanic and DLA/sub-DLA H₂ systems at all redshifts. It is the lowest-N(HI) H₂-bearing cloud known, consistent with a non-ISM formation channel.*

### [Measuring Effective Data Resolution in Guided Diffusion Posteriors](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04422v1)

Ridham Patel, Defu Cao, Jiacheng Pang et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The paper asks how much of a guided-diffusion reconstruction is actually fixed by the data. It defines an **effective data resolution**: the sampler-realized degrees of freedom, estimated with a black-box perturbation/Hutchinson estimator (bias −0.009 ± 0.028), compared with the classical degrees of freedom for signal that the inverse problem warrants. In a 1D backward-heat problem with an exact prior, the DPS guidance weight moves the fidelity ratio **from 0.15 to 1.27**, and no single weight gets the mean, spread and resolution right together. In a nonlinear 2D Darcy problem, projection guidance, which looked calibrated in 1D, over-resolves by 50%. **How principled a guidance rule looks does not predict its resolution fidelity**, which is a useful check for diffusion-prior field reconstructions such as Lyα tomography or initial-condition recovery.

![](figures/2610.04422/localization.pdf)

*The exact posterior spreads data influence almost uniformly across the field. Strongly guided DPS piles it into a narrow band and is unstable across realizations, and its realized degrees of freedom overshoot and then return to the warranted value as guidance grows, so matching resolution cannot be used as a tuning rule.*

### [A Testable Theory of Atomic Features](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05794v1)

Kenny Peng, Jon Kleinberg, Nikhil Garg
**Primary category:** cs.AI | also: cs.LG

The authors formalise the linear representation hypothesis and prove that the resulting **decomposition into "atomic features" is unique**. They then derive a **recovery principle**: SAEs of growing width, trained at the right sparsity, recover a growing prefix of the most prevalent atoms. They test three predictions with k-sparse SAEs from 512 to 131,072 features on embeddings of 90M texts. About **70% of 4K-SAE directions persist in every larger SAE**, and 26–59% of features are shared across SAEs trained on different data distributions, against mostly 0% for PCA. Parent and child features in hierarchies are both best recovered at the largest widths. This directly challenges the usual "SAE features are unstable and split arbitrarily" picture.

![](figures/2610.05794/persistent_stability_cosine.pdf)

*About 70% of the directions in a 4K SAE are matched (cosine ≥ 0.7) in every larger SAE up to 131K: SAE dictionaries are far more stable across width than the feature-splitting narrative suggests.*

### [Efficient Mass Matrix Estimation with Gaussian Cooling](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04806v1)

Jake Hofgard, Michael Lindsey
**Primary category:** stat.CO | also: stat.ML

The paper replaces ad hoc HMC mass-matrix warm-up with **Gaussian cooling**: it samples a sequence of well-conditioned tempered distributions, and the sample covariance at each stage preconditions the next. Any log-concave sampler can be plugged in. The authors prove first-order query complexity Õ(d^{4/3} log κ) for affinely ill-conditioned log-concave targets, and **Õ(d^{1/3} log κ) for translation-invariant targets**. That is the first theory behind Stan-style staged warm-up, with only logarithmic dependence on the condition number. On lattice φ⁴ fields up to 512² sites, the method reaches a constant relative condition number within a few stages. It matches empirical warm-up rather than beating it, but the translation-invariant result applies directly to field-level HMC inference.

### [Two-Sample Testing via Path-based Inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05684v1)

Eshant English, Wei-Cheng Lai, Yanfeng Yang et al.
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

Two datasets are joined by a **stochastic-interpolant path through a shared Gaussian bottleneck**. The authors prove the distributions are equal if and only if the path is reflection-symmetric, which is equivalent to the two populations' learned denoisers or velocity fields matching at any single noise level. Held-out regression risks of those networks, aggregated along the path, give test statistics. One weighting equals the Jeffreys divergence between the noise-smoothed distributions, and **permutation calibration makes the tests valid in finite samples** for any trained network. On Gaussian-mixture and MNIST-family benchmarks they beat MMD-D, C2ST, AutoTST and MMDAgg by **up to 33 points of power**. This is a candidate tool for checking simulations or mocks against data.

### [How to scale your HEP ML models: A recipe for robust architecture comparisons at scale](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06784v1)

Matthias Vigl, Nikita Pond, Jackson Barr et al.
**Primary category:** hep-ex | also: cs.LG, hep-ph, physics.data-an

The authors give a careful recipe for compute- and data-constrained scaling laws. It includes joint transfer of the optimal learning rate and batch size across training horizons, applied to multi-task transformers on the **~11-billion-jet ATLAS JetSet2 dataset**. At compute-optimal scaling, model and data size both grow as **N*, D* ∝ C^~0.5**, consistently across three fitting methods. Auxiliary tasks and lower-level inputs lower the loss without changing the exponent. The key warning is that **the power law only begins after a few hundred million examples**, so scaling exponents fitted on smaller scientific datasets can be biased. That caution transfers directly to sizing a spectral foundation model.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Priced Guidance: Can Language Models Generate Future Research Ideas?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04976v1)

Kaiyue Wen, Tengyu Ma, Percy Liang
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The paper frames research-idea generation as compression. The generator model asks multiple-choice questions and assigns probabilities to the answers, and a guide that has seen the hidden future paper picks answers, charging −log₂p bits each. A theorem shows that **K bits of guidance implies an unguided success probability of at least 2^−K**. On 87 recent highly-upvoted ML papers, the best frontier model needs a **median ~70 bits** to reach a paper's core idea, against ~5,700 bits for gzip on the idea summary. An ensemble of models lowers this to ~56 bits. Difficulty shows no trend with publication date over four months, and technical reports are the hardest to "predict."
