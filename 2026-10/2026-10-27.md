## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [A Composite Rest-Frame UV Spectrum of Galaxies at $5.7 < z < 6.9$: Ionization, Outflows, and Implications for Intergalactic Metal Enrichment](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33154v1)

David DePalma, Robert A. Simcoe, Anna-Christina Eilers et al. (Eilers)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors stack JWST/NIRSpec G140M spectra of **104 [O III]-selected galaxies at mean z = 6.50** (MASQUERADE/EIGER quasar fields). The composite looks remarkably like the z~3 Lyman-break-galaxy stack, apart from stronger O III], C III] and He II emission. Interstellar absorption lines are **blueshifted by ~170–200 km/s**, evidence of outflows. A kinematic toy model (NFW halo plus Hubble drag, integrated over each galaxy's stellar age) gives **maximum wind radii of only ~3–9 pkpc**. Bubbles this size could account for **less than a percent of the metal-absorber incidence dN/dX seen in quasar spectra**, which points instead to **undetected M★ ~ 10⁵–10⁶ M☉ galaxies clustered around the observed emitters** as the main source of z~6 intergalactic metals.

![](figures/2610.33154/NFWhalomass.pdf)

*Modelled wind trajectories for the sample: most 200 km/s winds reach only a few pkpc (limited by travel time, or turning around and falling back), well inside the ~34 pkpc mean R₂₀₀ and short of the tens to hundreds of pkpc separating observed absorber–galaxy pairs.*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [FastPM: Fast Pretrained Model for Cosmological Simulation-Based Inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33955v1)

Masaya Yamamoto, Adrian E. Bayer
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

A neural posterior estimator for Ω_m and σ₈ from the matter power spectrum is **pretrained on cheap FastPM particle-mesh runs and fine-tuned on a few CAMELS N-body/hydro simulations**. Pretraining on 2,000 20-step FastPM boxes, which together cost less than one CAMELS hydro run, **cuts the number of high-fidelity simulations needed by 80–90% and total training CPU cost by 4.7–8.8×**. The gain survives for the hydrodynamical total-matter P(k) even though FastPM has no baryons, and coverage stays calibrated. With 10⁴ more accurate 200-step pretraining runs, the model **beats anything trained on CAMELS alone at any simulation budget tested**. This is a direct template for emulator/SBI pipelines where high-fidelity hydro boxes (e.g. for the forest) are the bottleneck.

![](figures/2610.33955/nltp_camels_tng_kmax5_ncpuh.pdf)

*Posterior accuracy (NLTP) against total training-simulation CPU hours: cheap FastPM pretraining wins at low budgets, and more accurate (200-step) pretraining overtakes at larger budgets, both beating CAMELS-only training.*

### [Intrinsic Information Rates of Summaries in Simulation-Based Inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34703v1)

Vansh Bansal, Pradip Sharma Poudel, James G. Scott
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

This paper treats an SBI summary statistic as a **lossy channel from data to summary with an information budget I(Y;S)**. It proves that for parametric models **the required rate grows without bound as the posterior concentrates**, even when a fixed-dimensional sufficient statistic exists, and bounds that rate through posterior quantization. Its main theorem shows that at any budget too small for exact preservation, **a stochastic summary achieves strictly lower distortion than every deterministic summary** or time-shared mixture of them. Inserting a binary bottleneck into neural posterior estimators gives rate–loss curves on four simulator benchmarks: stochastic bottlenecks come out favourably, and **architecture rankings change with the information budget**.

### [AllBRICQS: the All-sky BRIght, Complete Quasar Survey - Quasar Selection and Final Southern $G_{\rm RP}<16$ Sample](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34069v1)

Christian Wolf, Christopher A. Onken, Wei Jeat Hon et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The final southern release of a **Gaia+WISE colour selection of optically bright quasars** (low parallax and proper motion, BpRp-excess cut against extended hosts) adds 119 spectroscopically confirmed quasars at z = 0.03–4.35 from ANU 2.3m/WiFeS (R~3000). This brings the sample to **745 quasars with G_RP < 16, z > 0.1 at Dec < +20°, |b| > 10°**, a regime large spectroscopic surveys avoid because of saturation. The sample is **argued to be complete for z > 0.12** apart from host-dominated Seyferts and some lensed or dual quasars (at least one lensed quasar is known to be missed). It is a bright, complete reference set for continuum, variability and reverberation-mapping work.

### [Exploring the UV Fe III Emission along the Quasar Main Sequence](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33339v1)

Diego Martínez Collipal, Mary Loli Martínez-Aldama, Ashwani Pandey et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors fit the **1700–2200 Å Fe III+Fe II "λ1900 blend"** in 69 low-z HST AGN spectra and compare it with optical diagnostics, using PCA and CLOUDY grids. **W(Fe III+Fe II) correlates with optical R_FeII**, and FWHM(Al III λ1860) tracks FWHM(Hβ). Together these define a **UV analogue of the quasar main sequence** (Pop. B / A / xA split at W ≈ 17 Å), usable where Hβ has redshifted out of reach. Object-to-object variation in the Fe III multiplets shows that **a single UV iron template cannot represent all quasars**. That matters for continuum and pseudo-continuum modelling of high-z quasar spectra.

![](figures/2610.33339/A3_F3_OPT_MS.pdf)

*The optical main sequence (FWHM Hβ vs R_FeII, top) and its proposed UV counterpart (FWHM Al III vs W(Fe III+Fe II), bottom) divide the sample into the same Pop. B, A and xA groups.*

### [Multiphase Gas Structure in JWST Little Red Dots](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34552v1)

Seok-Jun Chang, Max Gronke, Jorryt Matthee et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

In LRD spectra, **Hβ absorption is systematically deeper than Hα, which is impossible in any homogeneous gas**. The authors fit both line profiles in 11 LRDs with Monte Carlo resonant-scattering radiative transfer and independently constrain the n=2 hydrogen column from the Balmer break. In a homogeneous medium, Hα needs N_HI(n=2) ~ 10¹⁴ cm⁻², which under-predicts Hβ and is **typically two orders of magnitude below the column required by the Balmer break**. A **clumpy medium of optically thick clumps in diffuse ionized gas** (about 20 clumps per sightline in the median) resolves both tensions at once. The authors argue that **multiphase gas is required by any viable LRD model**, whatever the central engine.

![](figures/2610.34552/NHI_comparison_object.pdf)

*For each LRD, the n=2 column inferred from Hα, Hβ and the Balmer break agrees in the clumpy model (a), but the homogeneous model (b) cannot bring the line-based columns up to what the Balmer break demands.*

### [Emission line stacking on cosmological scales: equivalence to two-point statistics and modelling of the observational effects](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33603v1)

Zhaoting Chen, José Luis Bernal
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

The authors prove that **3D emission-line stacking of an intensity map on a galaxy catalogue is exactly a quadratic estimator of the map–galaxy cross-correlation function**. In the shot-noise limit it reduces to the mean line profile of the stacked sources. Any linear operation (**foreground cleaning, weighting, selection function, aperture tapering**) enters through the estimator's window function. Stacked signals and their covariances can therefore be modelled analytically, without forward simulations, and computed much faster. Lognormal HI intensity-mapping mocks with beam, noise and foreground removal validate this to within 1σ in both the angular and spectral directions.

### [The biases caused by interpolated table models and their impact on X-ray spectroscopy](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35174v1)

Matteo Lucchini, Benjamin Ricketts, Phil Uttley et al. (Huppenkothen)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE | also: astro-ph.IM

Starting from a pure power law and moving to real soft- and hard-state observations of a black-hole X-ray binary, the paper shows that **linearly interpolating precomputed non-linear model grids biases posteriors toward or away from grid points**. It can also make them **artificially multimodal, with a peak or valley at every grid node**, because the likelihood gradient becomes discontinuous. With standard settings, samplers including Dynesty converge confidently to a single solution; the multimodality appears only with non-default, much more expensive sampler settings. Frequentist Levenberg–Marquardt fits are hit just as hard. The lesson carries over to **any inference that interpolates a coarse emulator or table**, such as thermal-parameter grids for P1D.

![](figures/2610.35174/posteriors_powerlaw.pdf)

*Recovered power-law posteriors when the true parameter sits on a grid node (left, unbiased) versus midway between nodes (right), where interpolation error biases and distorts the posterior.*

### [Cracking into RACS - I. Measuring the cosmic dipole in the RACS-low3 and RACS-mid1 radio surveys with simulation-based inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35207v1)

Oliver T. Oayda, Mali Land-Strykowski, Stefan W. Duchesne et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors measure the number-count dipole of ASKAP RACS radio sources with an SBI pipeline. Its forward model includes a newly found systematic: **reported flux densities fall as the phased-array-feed temperature rises**. Without that term, the "no temperature response" model is disfavoured at ln B ≈ −38 for RACS-low3. RACS-low3 gives a dipole **aligned with the CMB direction but ~4× the kinematic amplitude**, in line with the 3–4× excess reported for joint radio dipoles. RACS-mid1 is offset in direction (pulled toward the north equatorial pole), with declination-striped residuals that point to an unmodelled systematic. This is a worked example of **folding instrument systematics into SBI forward models** in the cosmic-dipole tension debate.

### [Distilling neural networks into equations to predict planetary instability](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35617v1)

Simon Alford, Edward Gu, Omar Costilla-Reyes et al. (Tamayo, Cranmer)
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: astro-ph.EP

A two-stage network first learns **an interpretable variable basis from short N-body integrations of multi-planet systems**. Its latent knowledge is then **distilled by symbolic regression into closed-form equations for the time to instability**. The resulting equations **outperform existing analytic instability estimates** across wide ranges of mass ratio, spacing and eccentricity, and generalize beyond the training data better than the original network. The authors present this as **interpretability by design**: the architecture is built to decompose into pieces that substitute for the black box rather than only explain it. The approach transfers to any emulator one wants to read physically.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Indication of Electron Antineutrinos from the Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35619v1)

The Super-Kamiokande Collaboration, K. Abe, Y. Asaoka et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE | also: hep-ex

Super-Kamiokande combines 5003 days of pure-water and gadolinium-loaded phases. With neutron tagging and better rejection of atmospheric-neutrino backgrounds, it fits a **DSNB ν̄_e flux of 3.6 (+1.6/−1.5) cm⁻² s⁻¹ above 13.3 MeV**. This is a **2.6σ tension with the background-only hypothesis**, stable across DSNB spectral models. It is the first hint of the relic neutrino background from all past core-collapse supernovae, an integral probe of the cosmic star-formation and supernova history.

### [An Intermediate Mass Stripped Star in a 4.2-hour Orbit around a Compact Object](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35539v1)

Bethany Ludwig, Maria R. Drout, Ylva Götberg et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.SR | also: astro-ph.HE

The authors report a **~3.4 M☉ helium star stripped of its hydrogen envelope, orbiting an unseen companion every 4.2 hours**. Spectroscopy and orbit rule out a main-sequence or second stripped-star companion, and high-cadence photometry gives a **99.4% probability that the companion is too massive to be a white dwarf**, so it is most likely a neutron star. Binary modelling predicts another mass-transfer episode, a hydrogen-free supernova, and then a **double neutron star that merges in ~220 Myr**. This is direct observation of the long-missing post-common-envelope stage that sets double-neutron-star merger rates.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Automated, auditable dating of the Babylonian Astronomical Diaries with language models and astronomical tools](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34297v1)

Lucas Carrit Delgado Pinheiro, Yuan-Sen Ting, Jon Clindaniel et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: cs.AI, cs.CL, physics.hist-ph

A rule-based parser turns the recorded observations in each cuneiform diary into structured clauses, and **a language model only checks and adds clauses**. Ephemeris tools then score **every year from 626 BCE to AD 75** against them, with published dates withheld until the results are final. On 120 dated tablets, **the published year is among the top-ranked years in 96% of cases**, and the full benchmark run cost about $3. The system also gives ranked candidate years, with their evidence, for 56 undated ADART IV entries. It illustrates a pattern for AI-in-science that **confines the LLM to a narrow, auditable role and leaves the physics to deterministic tools**.
