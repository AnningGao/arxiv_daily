## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Improving cosmological distance measurements at high redshift using Lyman-Alpha forest BAO reconstruction](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37664v1)

Boryana Hadzhiyska, Roger de Belsunce (de Belsunce)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors adapt **standard Zel'dovich BAO reconstruction to the Lyα forest**: they solve for displacements from δF/b_F and move flux pixels, testing this on six Lyα mocks painted onto AbacusSummit, with linear control variates cutting the error bars by about √50. In fully sampled boxes, reconstruction **halves the radial BAO damping scale**, tightens α∥ by about 25% and α⊥ by about 15%, and **reduces the non-linear radial shift from +0.3% to +0.10 ± 0.07%**. With a **DESI-like sightline density of 50 deg⁻²**, the gains survive (α∥ and α⊥ errors down about 27% and 10%), whether displacements come from the forest itself or from an external LBG/LAE tracer at n̄ ≈ 10⁻³ h³ Mpc⁻³, and they also carry over to the Lyα × quasar cross-correlation. The authors are explicit that the test is idealized: there are no continuum fitting, metals, DLAs or instrument noise.

![](figures/2610.37664/fig06_density.pdf)

*Precision gain σ_pre/σ_post versus sightline density: self-reconstruction from the forest already gives ~1.3–1.4× on α∥ and ~1.1–1.2× on α⊥ at DESI-like densities. An external halo tracer gives comparable gains only once its density reaches ~10⁻³ h³ Mpc⁻³.*

### [Does the Escape Fraction Prescription Shape Reionization History and Morphology?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36046v1)

Pulkit Sheoran, Ankita Bera, Sultan Hassan
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

The authors fit three power-law **f_esc(z), f_esc(M_h) and f_esc(z, M_h)** models with MCMC to JWST UV luminosity functions plus the ionizing emissivity, x_HI from quasar sightlines and Planck τ. Priors allow either sign of each dependence. The data require an escape fraction that **falls with redshift, from ≈0.8 at z = 5 to ≈0.35 at z = 15**. When both dependences are free, the halo-mass slope is consistent with zero, and steeply mass-decreasing f_esc (as in feedback-regulated dwarf simulations) is disfavored. Propagated through SimFast21, all prescriptions end reionization at z ≈ 6, and at matched x_HI they give **21 cm power spectra within ≈10%**. The f_esc details therefore largely average out, and the authors argue that **sightline statistics of the Lyα and 21 cm forests** are needed to tell them apart.

![](figures/2610.36046/fesc_evolution.pdf)

*All three variable-f_esc fits share a declining escape fraction with redshift (~0.9 → 0.3 for the z-dependent models). In the mass-only model the decline comes entirely from the evolving halo population, not from an explicit z term.*

### [Detection of star formation in a near-pristine pocket of the Universe](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36042v1)

Louise Welsh, Ryan Cooke, Michele Fumagalli et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

A five-pointing Keck/KCWI mosaic around the **extremely metal-poor DLA at z_abs = 3.078 toward J0903+2628 ([Fe/H] < −3.66)** finds two Lyα emitters at Δv = −1200 and −200 km/s and impact parameters of 66 and 137 physical kpc. This is **the first detection of star formation in the environment of a chemically near-pristine DLA**. The Lyα lines are weak and narrow (F ≈ 4 × 10⁻¹⁸ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻²), and KCRM limits on He II 1640 are below about 4 × 10⁻¹⁹. Together with the expected position on the mass–metallicity relation, this suggests metal-poor galaxies. Both sources lie beyond the expected dwarf virial radius, so the DLA may sit in the outskirts of a larger halo rather than hosting them. Rest-frame optical follow-up is needed to test whether such DLAs signpost primitive galaxies about 2 Gyr after the Big Bang.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Field-level modeling of halo and galaxy alignments](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36027v1)

Nickolas Kokron, Mikhail M. Ivanov, James M. Sullivan (Ivanov)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

The paper builds the **Lagrangian spin-2 EFT of intrinsic alignments at the field level**, keeping the non-linear Zel'dovich displacement and all shape operators through third order. It is fit directly to the 3D helicity fields of halo shapes in Quijote and galaxy shapes in FLAMINGO. The field-level EFT **correlates better with simulated halo shapes than NLA or TATT** at every scale, and the reduced χ² per box has a median near one. Recasting the fit as **generalized least squares gives an optimal, cosmic-variance-cancelling estimator with analytic covariances** for linear, quadratic and cubic shape biases. These are measured versus halo mass and for LRG, ELG, spiral and elliptical samples, the basis the authors propose for simulation-based IA priors. The same transfer-function machinery is directly portable to field-level EFT work on other biased tracers such as the forest.

![](figures/2610.36027/eulerrcc_gain.pdf)

*Cross-correlation coefficient between model and measured halo-shape helicity fields: the 10-parameter field-level EFT sits above NLA and TATT at all k, so no standard Eulerian IA model fits the field as well.*

### [Little Red Dots with Broad Hβ Lines Reveal the Black Hole Mass Function at 7<z<9.5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37564v1)

Anthony J. Taylor, Steven L. Finkelstein, Hollis B. Akins et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

A systematic search of **21,770 JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra** (four blank fields, six lensing clusters) finds **20 significant broad-Hβ emitters at 7 < z < 9.5**, 13 of them new. **All have LRD colors and morphologies**, compared with only 37% of broad-line AGN at 3.5 < z < 6. With completeness corrections the authors build the **z ~ 8 black hole mass function**: its normalization rises by ≈1 dex to z ~ 5, consistent with average growth at f_Edd ≈ 0.1, and matching it requires heavy (~10⁵ M☉) seeds or super-Eddington growth of light seeds. The inferred BHAD of 4.8 (+5.8/−2.6) × 10⁻⁷ M☉ yr⁻¹ Mpc⁻³ agrees with photometric estimates but not with X-ray models. A discussion section shows how much of this rests on LRD broad lines tracing BLR kinematics.

![](figures/2610.37564/bhmf.pdf)

*The z ~ 8 BHMF (red) lies well below the JWST z ~ 5 BHMFs, with >3.5σ evolution in all but the two heaviest mass bins, where small numbers limit significance to 1–2σ.*

### [Cosmological Constraints on Interacting Dark Matter with SPT-3G D1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37604v1)

Cynthia Trendafilova, Fei Ge, Ali Rida Khalife et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The paper updates CMB+BAO bounds on DM–baryon scattering, DM–dark radiation interactions and DM annihilation, using Planck + ACT DR6 + **SPT-3G D1 primary and MUSE lensing** together with DESI DR2. The headline 95% limits are **σ_DM-b < 4.6 × 10⁻²⁶ cm²** (velocity-independent), Γ₀,nadm < 5.2 × 10⁻⁸ Mpc⁻¹ and p_ann < 3.7 × 10⁻²⁸ cm³ s⁻¹ GeV⁻¹. The scattering limits tighten by ~30–50% over Planck+ACT, driven by **small-scale CMB lensing**. The authors caution that adding DESI tightens some bounds partly because extended models absorb the **known CMB–DESI Ω_m discrepancy**, so the apparent gains are not purely constraining power. This complements forest-based small-scale DM limits from the CMB side.

### [Late reheating in the era of precision cosmology](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36062v1)

Kensuke Akita, Oleksii Ihnatenko, Maksym Ovchynnikov
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: astro-ph.CO

Using full neutrino-transport calculations against current BBN abundances and CMB data, the authors find the minimum reheating temperature. If a light scalar decays into both electrons and neutrinos, reheating at **T_RH ≈ 0.4 MeV** is still allowed, **an order of magnitude below the bound for purely electromagnetic decays**. In this scenario nearly standard N_eff can coexist with a **neutrino mass density up to five times smaller** at unchanged physical masses (normal ordering), a way to ease cosmology's low-Σm_ν preference without exotic dark energy. The weaker free-streaming suppression shifts matter power at the percent level, within reach of upcoming LSS surveys. It is a concrete alternative to keep in mind when interpreting DESI-era neutrino-mass constraints, including those from the forest P1D.

### [The Cosmic Ultraviolet Baryon Survey X: Density and Pressure Profiles of Clumpy Circumgalactic Gas at z=0.4-0.7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36088v1)

Suyash Kumar, Hsiao-Wen Chen, Gwen C. Rudie et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

For 23 CUBS galaxies and groups with background QSOs inside the virial radius, the authors decompose **HST COS/STIS absorption into individual cool clumps**. Photoionization modeling and line widths give n_H ≈ 10⁻⁴–10⁻² cm⁻³, T ≈ (1–5) × 10⁴ K and b_NT ≈ 2–30 km/s. Combined with literature samples, the cool CGM is **consistent with photoionization equilibrium and predominantly subsonic turbulence**, and at 10¹²–10¹³ M☉ its pressure matches stacked X-ray hot-gas pressures. The total cool-gas mass is **nearly constant across halo mass**, so the **cool baryon fraction falls from ≈6–19% at 10¹² M☉ to ≈0.6–1.5% at 10¹³ M☉**. Kinematically aligned low- and high-ions spanning two decades in density indicate local pressure fluctuations.

![](figures/2610.36088/Baryon_budget.pdf)

*Baryon census for 10¹² and 10¹³ M☉ halos: the cool CGM is a substantial reservoir in L* halos but a minor one in group-mass halos. Stars, ISM, cool and hot gas together remain a lower limit, because the warm-hot phase is not counted.*

### [ASTROLABE: A 3D Particle-Level Surrogate for Cosmology Simulation with Physical Flow Matching](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27068v1)

Ibrahim Elsharkawy, David Curtin, Yonatan Kahn et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: cs.AI, hep-ph

Astrolabe is a **flow-matching emulator that acts directly on the 6N phase-space coordinates of N-body particles**, so there is no voxel-resolution ceiling. A **field–particle hybrid transformer** lets each particle cross-attend to multiscale field tokens, keeping compute linear in N. The flow's prior is **approximate COLA coordinates rather than noise ("physical flow matching")**, with momentum and mass conservation enforced along flow time. In ΛCDM tests it reproduces power spectra, halo mass functions and NFW/Jeans-consistent halo profiles. It **generalizes out of distribution to dark-acoustic-oscillation initial conditions** and fine-tunes to emulate TNG50-3-dark (150M particles), at one or more orders of magnitude less compute than PKDGRAV3. The target is the expensive scans over dark-matter microphysics that small-scale structure probes need.

### [A self-supervised machine learning approach to identify instrumental systematics in 21cm Cosmology](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37596v1)

Ajith Sampath
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The method exploits two drift-scan facts: the instrument is fixed as the sky rotates, and the same sky is seen under different instrument states. A convolutional autoencoder trained on simulated visibilities uses these to **split the latent into instrument-only and sky-only vectors** without a forward model or instrument labels. A linear probe on the 2D instrument latent recovers a held-out beam-ripple amplitude with **R² = 0.9975** (versus −3.55 for a random encoder), and that latent carries no detectable sky signal. The reverse fails: **instrument information still leaks into the sky latent**, so the "clean" visibilities are not yet usable for cosmology. It is a clean small-scale demonstration of disentangling representation learning for instrument systematics, transferable in spirit to continuum/instrument separation in spectra.

### [The Great Chat in the Sky: Aligning Exotic Astronomical Data to Language with AstroBridge](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36375v1)

Ibrahim Habib, Kevin Li, Trung Nguyen et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

To get training pairs without manual captioning, AstroBridge matches catalog objects to literature passages and has a VLM generate **observation–caption pairs** for images, spectra and light curves. **Frozen modality-specific encoders feed a shared trainable fusion network aligned to an LLM's embedding space**, trained to caption raw observations. On spectra, caption-based evaluation reaches **91.5% accuracy separating quasars from galaxies**, versus 69.0% for the base model before fine-tuning. The authors report clear weaknesses in galaxy morphology, minority supernova classes and the factual quality of generated captions. It is a data-pipeline template for attaching language to spectral foundation-model embeddings, with the usual caveat that the LLM-generated captions are the supervision signal.

### [Data-driven Discovery of Lie Symmetries and First Integrals with Latent ODEs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35967v1)

Matt L. Wiemman, Peter Melchior (Melchior)
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: stat.ML

LatentLieODE maps states or video frames through an autoencoder into a **decorrelated latent chart**. There it fits a latent ODE jointly with a bank of **linear Lie generators via a differentiable bracket residual J_f(z)Az − Af(z)**, then fits a network for the conserved quantity. On six systems (oscillator, pendulums, Lotka–Volterra, pixel pendulum, sphere geodesics) it recovers the relevant symmetries, and **invariants match true conserved quantities at |ρ| ≥ 0.978**. On the sphere it recovers a **non-abelian so(3)**, certified by a negative-definite Killing form, with over-complete generator banks collapsing to the right dimension. Stated limits: only symmetries linearizable in some chart, quasi-periodic orbits only, and single runs rather than seed ensembles.

### [Structure, Not Sequence: Tracking and Validating Folding Mechanisms in ESMFold's Trunk with Sparse Autoencoders](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36226v1)

Bohan Liu, Xiaohan Fan, Wenqian Ye et al.
**Primary category:** q-bio.QM | also: cs.LG

The authors train **sparse autoencoders on both the single and pair representations of ESMFold's folding trunk**, label the features at scale with a reasoning LLM, and compose them into a circuit with integrated gradients. They find a clean division of labor: the **pair track is ~91% geometric**, while the single track preserves the ESM-2 sequence semantics. On top of the language-model embedding, the trunk code adds **up to +0.32 AUROC** for residue relational properties. **About 73% of the circuit's edges are supported by known biochemistry**, and the interpreted features also forecast the model's own fold quality. It is a worked example of the SAE → auto-interpretation → attribution → external validation pipeline on a scientific (not language) transformer.

### [Symmetry Breaking Is the Price of Finite Capacity](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36138v1)

Maggie Chlon, Leon Chlon
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

A symmetry groups a model's directions into blocks of equal strength, and a symmetric model must use whole blocks. When a capacity budget cuts a block, **every optimal model must break the symmetry**. For a Gaussian problem with a k-unit bilinear predictor, the **log-loss price of staying symmetric is exactly (U_k − I_k)/(2σ²)**: the signal of the best k directions minus that of the best whole blocks. Because an attention head is a similarity of rank at most its head dimension, the same result governs heads. Trained heads **break rotation symmetry at exactly the predicted head dimensions** (rotation-sensitivity RMSE 0.004), and flatter softmax attention restores the symmetric solution. The paper gives a procedure to separate capacity-driven symmetry breaking from other causes, relevant when interpreting attention in models of rotation- or shift-symmetric scientific data.

![](figures/2610.36138/fig_blocks_col.pdf)

*Directions coloured by symmetry block (sizes 1, 3, 5, 7): with a budget of 6 the unrestricted model keeps the six strongest directions and splits a block. The symmetric model must drop that block and leave capacity unused, and the signal it gives up is the exact price of symmetry.*

### [An end-to-end framework for uncertainty-aware validation of generative models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37398v1)

Giada Badaracco, Sean Benevedes, Christina Reissel et al.
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG, hep-ex, hep-ph

The core point is that any generative model fit to N_train samples is inexact, so **a point-null goodness-of-fit test will always reject it once N_test ≳ N_train**, even when the model family contains the truth. SIGMA uses **Gaussian mixture models, whose parameter covariance is available analytically from score functions and Fisher information**. It **profiles these fit uncertainties as nuisance parameters**, turning the test into a convex composite-null problem calibrated with a parametric bootstrap. On a 2D toy and a 4D embedding of QCD jets, the same model can be trusted on **test sets an order of magnitude larger** than the point-null test allows. The authors note that SGD-trained neural flows lack a well-defined optimum and invertible Fisher matrix, so they cannot be validated this way, a relevant caveat for emulator/generative-model validation in cosmology.

![](figures/2610.37398/multiseed_1d_paper.pdf)

*Even for a Gaussian fit to Gaussian data, point-null p-values (gray) collapse once the test set outgrows the training set. The composite null that includes fit uncertainty (blue) stays calibrated near the median of 0.5.*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [ASTRA: An Autonomous Framework for Physics-Validated Analysis and Hypothesis Generation in Astrophysics](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36144v1)

Glenn J. White
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

ASTRA chains **dimensional analysis, structural causal models, Bayesian evidence and multi-wavelength fusion** into one automated workflow. It is validated by recovering known results on Herschel filaments, CDF-S, SDSS DR16, Gaia and virial model selection, and on sealed synthetic outlier datasets (purity > 0.99). The most useful part for the AI-in-astronomy debate is the head-to-head: **agentic-LLM and pure-ML baselines match the statistical fitting but not the physics-validation stage**. They split evenly on the physical verdict and **adopt a deliberately supplied erroneous benchmark in all six "trap" runs**, and an astrophysics-fine-tuned model completes zero of six. The author frames everything as recovery validation rather than discovery, with hypothesis generation shown only qualitatively.
