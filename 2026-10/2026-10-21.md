## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [The Future of Gamma-Ray Bursts as Probes of the Epoch of Reionization in the Era of JWST and the Extremely Large Telescope](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27282v1)

H. M. Fausey, A. J. van der Horst, N. R. Tanvir et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors simulate Lyα damping-wing spectra of high-z GRB afterglows for JWST NIRSpec and ELT HARMONI, using both the Miralda-Escudé and McQuinn damping-wing models, and fit them jointly for the host H I column and the **IGM neutral fraction $x_{\rm HI}$**. Host columns are recovered very precisely (>97% of ELT and >90% of JWST posteriors within ±0.2 dex down to H = 24 mag). The neutral fraction is much harder: only ~50% of posteriors land within ±0.1 of the input for **bright (H ≈ 20 mag) bursts with ~2 hr exposures**, and telluric absorption leaves ELT blind at z ≈ 6.5–7 where JWST must fill in. A mock 500-GRB population on a linear reionization history shows that samples dominated by late-EoR bursts **bias the end of reionization low ($z_{\rm end} = 5.55 \pm 0.18$ for an input of 6 with 50 GRBs)**, so early-EoR bursts should be the follow-up priority.

![](figures/2610.27282/compare_all.pdf)

*Fraction of neutral-fraction posteriors within tolerance versus redshift for H = 20 mag bursts. JWST NIRSpec stays usable across the range, while ELT HARMONI loses accuracy wherever the damping wing falls on telluric bands.*

### [Are Dynamical Dark-Energy Cosmologies Old Enough?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27209v1)

Agripino Sousa-Neto, Susana J. Landau, Jailson Alcaniz
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors rerun the **DESI DR2 BAO + CMB + Pantheon+ $w_0w_a$ fit** while imposing hard lower bounds on the cosmic age, $t_0 \geq 13.6$–$13.85$ Gyr, motivated by recent globular-cluster and oldest-star ages. Because the DESI-preferred dynamical-dark-energy models predict comparatively young universes, raising the age floor steadily pushes the posterior toward $(w_0, w_a) = (-1, 0)$. For $t_0 \geq 13.85$ Gyr the **Mahalanobis separation from ΛCDM drops from 2.56σ to 1.72σ**, placing ΛCDM inside the 95% region. The age bound enters as a step-function prior rather than a likelihood, but the test shows **stellar chronometry already erodes much of the DR2 dynamical-dark-energy preference**.

![](figures/2610.27209/w0wa_age_priors_3panels.pdf)

*As the minimum cosmic age is raised from none to 13.85 Gyr, the $(w_0, w_a)$ contours migrate toward the ΛCDM point: the dynamical-dark-energy preference lives in young-universe expansion histories.*

### [ASTROLABE: A 3D Particle-Level Surrogate for Cosmology Simulation with Physical Flow Matching](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27068v1)

Ibrahim Elsharkawy, David Curtin, Yonatan Kahn et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Astrolabe is a **flow-matching generative emulator acting directly on the 6N phase-space coordinates of N-body particles**, so there is no voxel-resolution ceiling. The flow starts from cheap COLA particle fields rather than noise and enforces momentum and mass conservation along flow time; a field–particle hybrid transformer, in which every particle cross-attends to multiscale grid tokens, keeps cost linear in N. Trained on 1500 GIZMO simulations, it sharply improves on COLA at small scales (**halo-concentration error 1.80 → 0.42** in the 10 Mpc/h box) and generalizes out of distribution to dark-acoustic-oscillation initial conditions. Fine-tuned, it emulates the 150M-particle TNG50-3-Dark run at a **~10–250× speedup over GPU PKDGRAV3**, which makes systematic scans over dark-sector microphysics plausible.

![](figures/2610.27068/CDM-2M-10Mpc-zoom-z0.pdf)

*Zoom on the largest halo of a 10 Mpc/h ΛCDM box at z = 0: COLA gets the large scales but smears the halo, while Astrolabe, starting from the same COLA field, restores the substructure of the N-body truth.*

### [Searching for Dense Perturbers in Cold Dark Matter Simulations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27236v1)

Xingyu Zhang, Hai-Bo Yu, Demao Kong et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA, hep-ph

The authors ask whether three inferred compact perturbers — the GD-1 stream gap, the B1938+666 gravitational-imaging perturber, and a proposed Fornax 6 perturber — can be ordinary CDM subhalos. Using >5,000 subhalos down to $10^6\,M_\odot$ from a CDM-only Milky Way zoom-in, with inner profiles extrapolated by an anchored tidal-truncation NFW model, they find that **no simulated CDM subhalo is dense enough** to match any of the three in density profile, inner enclosed mass, concentration, or projected mass. The lensing perturber sits **~8σ above the median concentration** at its mass, and the three perturbers share similar properties, which the authors read as a hint of a common mechanism such as SIDM gravothermal collapse. They also show the standard subhalo concentration–redshift fit needs **b = −3.52 rather than −5.52**, tracing the old value to a comoving/physical unit slip.

![](figures/2610.27236/cV-z0.pdf)

*All three inferred perturbers sit far above the concentration–mass relation of the simulated CDM subhalos; none of the >5,000 subhalos approaches their central densities.*

### [Probing the Optical/UV Continuum Variability of Active Galactic Nuclei with the Damped Random Walk Model](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27806v1)

Xiang-Wei Ning, Wen-Jie Hu, Zi-Han Yuan et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Fitting damped-random-walk models to up-to-20-yr optical light curves of 1214 AGNs (largely SDSS-RM), the authors find that **only 115 yield unbiased DRW parameters** once timescales longer than a fifth of the baseline are excluded — a useful warning for survey-scale variability work. For the subset with virial masses, the rest-frame 2500 Å damping timescale scales as **$\tau \propto M_{\rm BH}^{0.50\pm0.07}\,\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{0.27\pm0.04}$**, with no residual dependence on luminosity or redshift, and $\tau \propto \dot M^{0.41\pm0.04}$ is consistent with the thin-disk thermal timescale. Combining this with the microlensing $R_{2500}$–$M_{\rm BH}$ relation gives a **disk viscosity $\alpha = 0.04 \pm 0.01$**.

### [The Explainability Illusion: Must ViTs Exhibit Patch-Decoding Interpretability?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25289v1)

Ronen Schaffer, Hila Manor, Tomer Michaeli
**Primary category:** cs.CV | also: cs.LG

The authors test whether **logit-lens-style decoding of patch tokens** reliably localizes class evidence in vision transformers, across dozens of pretrained models. Most supervised ViTs are "aligned", but SAM-trained ViTs and small DeiTs are inverted, and **every contrastive vision–language model tested (CLIP, OpenCLIP, MetaCLIP, SigLIP) shows inverted explainability**, scoring object patches below background. Controlled training shows that task and data only shift the probability of each regime, and a one-layer toy transformer provably implements the same optimal classifier in aligned, unexplainable, or inverted ways. The takeaway: **token-decoding explainability must be verified per model, not assumed** — a direct caution for attention/token attribution on spectral transformers.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Evidence for Normal Neutrino Ordering with Combined Atmospheric Neutrino Experiments](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12208)

Miaochen Jin, Carlos A. Argüelles, Pablo Fernández-Menéndez et al.
**Primary category:** hep-ex | also: hep-ph

The authors jointly fit public atmospheric-neutrino data from Super-Kamiokande, IceCube-DeepCore, and KM3NeT/ORCA6 bin by bin, **treating flux and cross-section systematics as fully correlated across experiments** instead of combining published likelihoods. They measure Δm²₃₁ = 2.44 (+0.06/−0.04) × 10⁻³ eV² and sin²θ₂₃ = 0.52 (+0.03/−0.04), competitive with accelerator experiments, and prefer the normal ordering with Δχ² = 11.0, which pseudoexperiments calibrate to **excluding the inverted ordering at 99.95% CL (3.3σ)**. If it holds, it sets the minimum Σmν ≈ 0.06 eV floor that cosmological neutrino-mass bounds (including forest + DESI) must be confronted with.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [AI and The Enclosure of the Scientific Commons](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27251v1)

P. K. G. Williams
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

Williams frames the scientific literature as an **information commons sustained by commons-based peer production**, with peer review as its integration mechanism. He argues that the right historical analogy for AI is not the "tragedy of the commons" but the **English enclosure movement (~1600–1800)**, which privatised shared land while dismantling the social systems that maintained it. Frontier LLMs, whose value derives largely from freely scraped common material, combined with "slop spam" that overwhelms peer review, make a **previously infeasible enclosure of the scientific commons** possible; the essay argues the urgent AI questions are about ownership and social structure, not capability.
