## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [A Burst of Reionization in the Dark Ages](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12552v1)

Neal Weiner, Soubhik Kumar, Wenzer Qin et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors add a **redshift-localized burst of ionization at z ~ 10–300** to the standard CLASS/HyRec recombination history. After the source switches off, the electrons recombine, so the burst contributes optical depth Δτ without changing late reionization. Fitting SPT-3G, ACT DR6, DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon+ and three different Planck reprocessings, they find **a ~2–2.5σ preference (Δχ² ≈ 7–8) for Δτ ≈ 0.012**, with total τ ≈ 0.073. All of the extra τ is deposited above z ≈ 30. The preference weakens when DESI BAO or ACT+SPT are dropped. The burst also eases the pull toward negative neutrino mass: the fraction of the posterior with Σm_ν > 0 rises from 18% to 30%. The burst redshift is poorly pinned and differs by likelihood (best fits z ≈ 48, 106 or 165). LiteBIRD should detect such a source at 40 ≲ z ≲ 150. It is relevant to forest work because low-z τ probes, the Lyα forest among them, cannot see this extra τ.

![](figures/2610.12552/prod_triangle_tau_dtau_zx_plik_camspec_hillipop.pdf)

*Posteriors for total τ, the burst Δτ and its redshift z_x under the plik, CamSpec and HiLLiPoP Planck likelihoods, compared with ΛCDM. Δτ is consistent across likelihoods, but the preferred burst epoch shifts between them.*

### [Two-way Guidance: Simultaneous Learning of Summary Statistics for Generative Bayes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12850v1)

Percy S. Zhai, Max Pachman, Peverel D. Shipley et al.
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: stat.ME

The paper targets SBI in the regime where the number of observations grows while the number of parameters stays small. It learns a **summary statistic jointly with the posterior, constraining the posterior learner to depend affinely on the summary**. The authors prove what they call "two-way guidance": a sufficient summary simplifies posterior learning, and the posterior objective pushes the summary toward sufficiency. This comes with bounds on both the posterior error and the summary's departure from sufficiency. They implement it for generative quantile Bayes, a block-triangular summary flow-matching sampler and an affine GAN. On a Gaussian benchmark at n = 4096, the **sliced-W2 error drops from 0.170 to 0.012** (quantile Bayes) and from 0.210 to 0.017 (flow matching) compared with raw-data inputs. The same joint training also helps NPE and NRE. The test problems are toy and financial time-series models, but the structure is the same as learning compressed summaries of many forest skewers.

![](figures/2610.12850/gaussian-swd.pdf)

*Posterior error against observation size for each sampler, with learned summaries (solid) and raw data (dashed). The gain from joint summary learning grows with n and is largest for the quantile and flow-matching samplers.*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [The Redshift-Space Galaxy Power Spectrum at Two-Loop Order](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13867v1)

Oliver H. E. Philcox
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

This is the first complete **two-loop EFTofLSS model of the redshift-space galaxy power spectrum**. It includes fifth-order bias, redshift-space counterterms, IR resummation, stochasticity and AP projection, for 49 free coefficients against 10 at one loop. The loop integrals are precomputed with VEGAS into σ8-interpolated templates, so a likelihood call takes under a millisecond. Validation uses 25 AbacusSummit boxes with DESI-like HOD samples. The **unbiased k_max rises from 0.20 to 0.34 h/Mpc for z = 0.5 LRGs** and to at least 0.40 h/Mpc for z = 1.1 ELGs. **The σ8 errors do not shrink**, however: the extra small-scale modes are mostly absorbed by the new nuisance parameters. Full cosmology fits and the bispectrum are left to future work.

![](figures/2610.13867/s8_boxes_DESIfid.pdf)

*σ8 constraints against k_max for LRGs and ELGs at tree level, one loop and two loops. The one-loop model biases σ8 beyond ~0.2 h/Mpc, while the two-loop model stays unbiased to 0.34 (LRG) and ≥0.4 h/Mpc (ELG), with error bars about the same size.*

### [A dust-reddened quasar in a [C II]-luminous host at z=6.55](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13758v1)

Daming Yang, Joseph F. Hennawi, Rychard Bouwens et al. (Hennawi is a co-author)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The target is J1440+0019, a z = 6.5488 SHELLQs quasar observed with JWST/NIRSpec high-resolution spectroscopy and ALMA band 6. The JWST data come from a GO program designed to measure damping wings in faint z > 6.5 quasars. The continuum matches the Selsing blue composite only after **SMC-like reddening with E(B−V) = 0.20 ± 0.03**. Three blueshifted N V/Si IV/C IV absorbers at −900 to −3600 km/s probably trace a nuclear outflow. From Mg II, log M_BH = 9.04 ± 0.42 and λ_Edd ≈ 0.8. The host is among the most [C II]-luminous known at z > 6 (L_[CII] = 7.2 × 10⁹ L☉), with M_dust ≈ 4 × 10⁸ M☉. For damping-wing work, the point is that **intrinsic continuum templates built on blue quasars will mis-model reddened sources** like this one.

![](figures/2610.13758/fig1_overview.pdf)

*NIRSpec spectrum compared with the Selsing blue-quasar composite and the same composite reddened with E(B−V) = 0.20. Only the reddened template matches the continuum, and broad lines and blueshifted high-ionization absorption are still visible.*

### [Spatial Prediction with Guided Diffusion](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12967v1)

Andrew Zammit-Mangion, Lachlan Astfalck, Raphaël Huser et al.
**Primary category:** stat.ME | also: stat.ML

The authors **train one unconditional score model of a spatial field and condition on observations only at sampling time**, using DPS or pseudoinverse guidance (ΠGDM) with any differentiable data model. This separates the process prior from the observation operator. They test partial Gaussian observations, Poisson counts, areal aggregates, censoring and exceedance functionals. On a Matérn GP, **ΠGDM matches exact conditional sampling** (90% coverage 0.89, CRPS 0.214), while DPS is slightly under-dispersed. In a UK/Ireland methane-flux inversion through a transport operator, the guided samplers match HMC-within-Gibbs on RMSE, CRPS and the domain total. At 1948 cells they are **about 11× cheaper per effective sample** than MCMC. The setup (linear projection plus noise, a learned non-Gaussian prior, coverage checked against MCMC) maps closely onto forest tomography and field-level reconstruction.

![](figures/2610.12967/methane_scaling.pdf)

*Time per effective sample of the domain-total flux against the number of flux cells (122, 487, 1948), comparing guided diffusion on a GPU with ten-chain MCMC on CPUs. MCMC cost grows much faster, so guided diffusion wins at high resolution.*

### [SLICE: Sequential Likelihood-Free Inference via Conformal E-Values](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13855v1)

Stefano Cortinovis, Laura Battaglia, François Caron
**Primary category:** stat.ME | also: stat.ML

SLICE builds **exact, anytime-valid frequentist confidence sequences for simulator models**. At each new observation it runs fresh simulations under every candidate parameter. These conformalize a score into a conditional e-value, and the running product of e-values is a test martingale that is inverted over a parameter grid. Coverage holds for any score. **The likelihood-ratio score maximizes power**, so the authors plug in an amortized NRE-C classifier. The finite-simulation penalty is bounded by log(1 + χ²/(K+1)). The intervals stay valid under continuous monitoring, where Wilks-type intervals fail, and the NRE score is nearly as efficient as the exact likelihood ratio. On **real OGLE-IV microlensing timescales with a Galactic simulator**, the IMF-slope sequence recovers the published value in 97% of data orderings. The main costs are a parameter grid and the assumption that the simulator is correct.

![](figures/2610.13855/ogle_microlensing.pdf)

*OGLE-IV microlensing test. Left: miscoverage on semi-synthetic runs stays below α = 0.1. Centre and right: confidence sequences for the two IMF slopes on real data, compared with published values. The first slope is pinned down; the second is bounded only from below.*

### [Precision Tokenization for Multi-Modal Data in High Energy Physics](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13876v1)

Jeffrey Krupa, Samuel Klein, Nilotpal Kakati et al.
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: physics.data-an

The paper treats **tokenizing continuous detector data for foundation models as a rate–distortion problem**, using residual-VQ VAEs with transformer encoder–decoders on four modalities (tracks, topoclusters, calorimeter cells). Systematic scans over codebook size, code dimension and number of residual stages give **four design rules**:
- use many residual stages, because a single stage collapses;
- use large codebooks;
- scale code dimension with the bit budget;
- bin global coordinates separately.

Three of the four tokenizers keep **jet p_T resolution below 1% at 4–11× compression**. Learned tokenizers need up to 2.4× fewer bits than per-feature binning. Downstream particle-flow reconstruction and flavour tagging lose little. The rules transfer directly to designing a spectral tokenizer for a quasar-spectrum transformer. The open-source heptokens library is released.

![](figures/2610.13876/rate_distortion_pareto_with_cells_binning.pdf)

*Jet p_T resolution against bits per feature for each tokenizer. Learned tokenizers reach the 1% target at 4–11× compression and beat uniform per-feature binning at every bit budget.*

### [How Well Do Prior-Data Fitted Networks Amortize Bayesian Inference?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13764v1)

Manuel Hülskamp, Julius Kobialka, Emanuel Sommer et al.
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

The authors decompose a PFN's deployment error into **prior, context and amortization error**. They measure the amortization part against a large MCMC reference on a BNN-likelihood prior with realistic covariates. PFNs recover the target posterior predictive closely. The **residual error concentrates on inputs that are rare under the pretraining prior**, and independently pretrained PFNs disagree there even when model size grows. That disagreement is a label-free diagnostic. Among Bayesian model averages, deep ensembles help most (+1.7–3.1% LPPD), while IVON and SG-MCMC add little. An 8-member ensemble of shortened TabICLv2 runs matches the official checkpoint on TabArena. The lessons carry over to amortized posterior estimators in SBI pipelines, whose weak spots are likewise inputs that are rare under the training prior.

### [Sparse Dictionary Learning for Measure-Valued Data via Linearized Optimal Transport: Efficient Algorithms and Sample Complexity](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12924v1)

Brendan Mallery, Shuchin Aeron, James Murphy
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

The method **embeds probability measures into a Hilbert space with linearized optimal transport**: exact 1D maps, entropic maps, or Kantorovich potentials in higher dimensions. It then learns sparse nonnegative dictionaries with SAE/LISTA-style encoders. The authors prove generalization bounds via new covering-number estimates for OT and entropic-map atom classes. Under a fixed time budget, reconstruction error is **4–100× lower than full Wasserstein dictionary learning**. On hyperspectral pixel spectra treated as 1D distributions over wavelength, it is the **most robust method under random band dropout** (best at 13 of 15 corruption levels), though NMF has lower plain L² error. It also gives interpretable atoms for LLM activation distributions. This is an early-stage but natural sparse-coding tool for spectra with masked pixels.

### [The Fast & Fourier: Using neural operators to speed up radiative modeling of protoplanetary disks](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12632v1)

Lukas Welzel, Melissa K. McClure
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP | also: astro-ph.IM

The authors train a **Fourier Neural Operator on 41,658 RADMC-3D spectra** of ice-bearing edge-on disks on mixed-resolution wavelength grids. The trained surrogate is the forward model in an explicit-likelihood nested sampler, with emulator error folded into the likelihood. The median spectral error is 4.84%, with a heavy tail (Q95 ≈ 19%). Synthetic recoveries show no detectable bias, and **TARP coverage is nominal** (ATC ≈ 0.01). Applied to ESO-Hα 569, it gives M_gas = 0.028 (+0.015/−0.011) M☉ and i = 79.8 (+5.2/−2.4)°, in line with earlier image-plus-SED modelling. Each spectrum evaluation is **~10⁹–10¹⁰× faster** than the radiative-transfer code. The science is far from the IGM, but the neural-operator spectral emulator with a coverage-validated inference loop is a template worth copying.

### [The Predictability Bias of Joint-Embedding Predictive Learning in Medical Imaging](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13237v1)

Fabian Drexel, Bernhard Kainz, Jakob N. Kather et al.
**Primary category:** eess.IV | also: cs.LG

A JEPA predictor outputs a point summary of masked-target features given the context. **Variation the context cannot resolve gets no conditional-mean training signal** — the authors call this "predictability bias". They train TAP-JEPA, a ViT on 113,611 CT volumes that is competitive with current CT encoders, and replay its exact training masks. Prediction lowers pathology discriminability (mean Skill **0.388 → 0.271**) far more than anatomy (0.921 → 0.873). It pulls pathology 34.7% of the way toward matched controls along teacher directions, and the shrinkage grows with distance from the visible context. For masked/predictive pretraining on spectra, the analogue is that rare, locally unpredictable features — DLAs, BALs, metal absorbers — risk being averaged away by the objective itself.

### [Defence against the dark arts of distillation: Unsupervised discovery of subliminal learning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13536v1)

Joseph Egan, Ciarán M. Gilligan-Lee, Michael O'Riordan et al.
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

A student distilled from a teacher can inherit a hidden trait through unrelated data, a known effect called subliminal learning. STIFLE recovers that trait **without labels, from LoRA weight updates alone**. The key assumption is that the trait is written consistently across many teacher-generated datasets, while task-specific updates vary. The estimator is a **scatter-whitened mean update direction**, computed cheaply with Woodbury, with a bound on the off-trait energy. Projecting this direction out of a held-out adapter removes the trait while keeping task performance on all tasks shown (Qwen2.5-7B, animal-preference traits). Amplifying it instead exposes the trait for interpretation. It is a clean unsupervised member of the null-space-projection / representation-editing family (INLP, AlphaEdit) in the interest file.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [On the estimation and validity of AI time horizons---a statistical look at the METR plot](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12466v1)

Drew T. Nguyen, William Fithian
**Primary category:** cs.AI | also: stat.ML

This is a statistical re-analysis of METR's 50% time-horizon benchmark, with 228 tasks and 26 AI models. The authors replace the assumed log-linear link between human task time and AI difficulty with **a monotone spline and an item-response-theory model** that includes task-family effects and over-dispersion across runs. On cross-validated proper scoring rules, both beat METR's method. **The fitted conversion is nearly flat between 2 and 30 min**, so a 3 → 30 min horizon jump is much easier than 30 min → 5 h. Some re-estimated horizons fall outside METR's confidence intervals. The authors propose construct-validity diagnostics to report alongside any new time-horizon benchmark.

### [PPG-Bench: A Unified Benchmark for PPG Foundation Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13435v1)

Arvasu Kulkarni, Mahir Jain, Aditya Ray Mishra et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The benchmark evaluates five public photoplethysmography foundation models on 9 datasets and 17 primary tasks, under four downstream regimes with subject-disjoint CV. Each is compared against **a constant baseline, hand-crafted pulse-rate-variability features, and a generic time-series foundation model**. In **35.3% of task–regime combinations the foundation models show no meaningful advantage over a constant predictor**, and in 83.8% no PPG-specific model beats both simple controls. The authors conclude that domain foundation-model papers need non-learned controls. The same check applies to claims about spectral foundation models.
