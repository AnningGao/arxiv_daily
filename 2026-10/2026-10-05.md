## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Probing Early Phases of the Epoch of Reionization with the kSZ²×21cm² Cross-Correlation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03527v1)

Wen-Qing Guo, Yin-Zhe Ma, Adam Lidz et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using 21cmFASTv4 lightcones over z ≈ 9–16, the authors model the **cross-correlation of the squared kSZ field with the squared 21-cm field**. Squaring both fields avoids the line-of-sight cancellation that kills the linear kSZ×21cm signal and keeps pairs of high-k∥ modes that lie outside the foreground wedge. The main physics result is that **spatial fluctuations in the HI spin temperature reshape the signal's redshift evolution**: there is a dip where T_s approaches T_CMB and a rise in the absorption regime, which the saturated-heating approximation misses. That makes the statistic a handle on the **early IGM thermal history**, not just the neutral fraction. Forecasts give **S/N ≈ 5.9 for SKA-Low AA\*×CMB-HD and ≈ 3.6 for HERA×CMB-HD** with optimistic foreground avoidance, falling to about 2.9 if the wedge extends to the horizon. Simons Observatory sensitivity is not enough for a detection.

![](figures/2610.03527/Redshift_Evolution_Ts_vs_noTs.pdf)

*With spin-temperature fluctuations included (solid), the cross-power dips where T_s ≈ T_CMB and then rises again at z ≳ 12, whereas the saturated-heating approximation (dashed) predicts a signal that simply fades toward high z. The gap between the two is the thermal-history information the statistic carries.*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [A novel, fast, and accurate code for cosmological loop calculations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03466v1)

Tjark Harder, Luca Amendola, Thiago S. Pereira et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

The paper presents **neckar**, a public C code (with a Python wrapper) for the redshift-space one-loop EFTofLSS power spectrum and correlation function of biased tracers. It is built on FFTLog and uses a semi-analytic, FFTLog-compatible treatment of the Alcock–Paczynski effect for the multipoles. The authors find and fix an IR-resummation inaccuracy in existing implementations, which had caused **~1% errors in the quadrupole and ~10% in the hexadecapole at k ≳ 0.1 h/Mpc**. They also add an asymptotic scheme that extends predictions to k ≈ 0.4 h/Mpc and smooths the correlation function around the BAO peak. The headline is a speed-up of **more than 60× (up to ~two orders of magnitude) over PyBird-class codes without emulators**, aimed at high-dimensional DESI/Euclid full-shape inference.

### [Dark Energy Survey Year 6 Results: fast and interpretable posterior predictive checks for correlated cosmic probes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03447v1)

C. Doux, J. Muir, A. Ferté et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

DES introduces **Δ_PPD, the fraction of a Gaussian-mixture approximation to the posterior predictive distribution that has lower density than the observed data**. It is a scalar internal-consistency metric that takes **minutes per test** from existing chains. Applied to DES Y3 3×2pt data, it recovers the same inter-probe tensions as the published, expensively calibrated PPD p-values. On simulated Y6 analyses it responds coherently to injected inconsistencies. The paper also documents two pitfalls that carry over to any multi-probe unblinding: **tensions that nuisance parameters can absorb become hard to detect, and poorly constrained parameter directions produce spurious "tensions" between disjoint observables**. Δ_PPD is explicitly a diagnostic rather than a calibrated p-value.

### [Shining LIMelight into the dark: Probing Dark matter - baryon interactions with Line Intensity Mapping](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02311v1)

Durba Ghosh, Ranjini Mondol, Ranjan Laha
**Primary category:** hep-ph

DM–baryon elastic scattering, with momentum-transfer cross sections σ_n v^n for n = 0, −2, −4, suppresses small-scale linear power and therefore the abundance of low-mass, CO-emitting halos. The authors propagate this into **CO line-intensity-mapping observables (power spectrum and voxel intensity distribution)** for COMAP-EoR and COMAP-ERA and run Fisher forecasts that marginalize over line-emission astrophysics. They find that the **VID alone, combining the 16 and 30 GHz bands, would beat existing cosmological limits for dipole-like (n = −2) and Coulomb-like (n = −4) interactions with sub-GeV DM**. The physics is the same small-scale-suppression lever used in forest-based DM constraints, applied here to a different tracer. Unmodeled foreground residuals remain the main caveat.

### [Do Astronomical Foundation Models Know When They Will Fail?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02613v1)

Vaidehi Bulusu, Mehul Goyal, Md Rabius Sany Apu et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

The authors **audit cross-survey transfer of foundation-model embeddings with conformal prediction**. They learn translators between embeddings of the same objects in cross-matched Legacy–HSC, HSC–JWST, and SDSS–HSC pairs and calibrate regions around the predicted target embedding. **AstroPT embeddings transfer more precisely (tighter conformal regions at equal coverage) than general-purpose vision backbones**, and different model families fail on different galaxies. A ridge "difficulty model" fit on source embeddings shows that **some representations carry an advance warning of their own transfer failure** (consistently for Legacy→HSC, backbone-dependent for HSC→JWST, almost absent for SDSS→HSC). Matched-control checks find silent failures that magnitude, size, or nearest-neighbour distance do not explain. This is directly relevant to deciding whether a spectral foundation model trained on one instrument can be trusted on another.

### [A Missing Latent, Not a Missing Simulator: Radius-Augmented Inference for Real JWST Retrieval](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02245v1)

Angshuman Chakravertty, P V V Raj
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.EP, cs.LG

An amortized flow posterior for exoplanet transmission spectra collapses on real WASP-39b data (**importance-sampling ESS = 1, χ²/N = 301**). Nested-sampling tests rule out the usual "missing physics" fixes. The actual cause is that **the planet radius, which the simulator encodes but the inference omitted, could not absorb a ~3% transit-baseline offset**. Adding radius as an inferred latent, training on domain-randomized spectra, and calibrating against a nested-sampling reference brings the fit to χ²/N ≈ 0.06 across three real JWST targets. The general lesson for SBI on real survey data is that **an omitted nuisance latent can masquerade as simulator misspecification**. The authors note that the calibration still needs one nested-sampling run per target.

### [Flow Matching for Fast Posterior Sampling in Bayesian Inverse Problems](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02377v1)

Jan Blechschmidt, Oliver G. Ernst, Moritz Poguntke et al.
**Primary category:** math.NA | also: cs.LG, stat.CO

This is an MCMC-literate assessment of conditional flow matching as an amortized posterior sampler for PDE inverse problems with function-valued parameters. Because the flow has a tractable density, the authors **derive computable TV and KL accuracy estimates from the acceptance rate of an independence Metropolis–Hastings sampler that uses the flow as its proposal**, which also makes the hybrid sampler asymptotically exact. Tested against DRAM and pCN references, including electrical impedance tomography, flow matching reproduces posterior means and 95% bands to ~1%. Yet **acceptance can be only 5–11% for concentrated posteriors**, because the flow fits the tightly constrained directions poorly. This is a useful, honest benchmark for anyone using flows as SBI posteriors.

### [Drive vs. Decay: On the Training Dynamics of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02344v1)

José Lucas De Melo Costa, Seong Woo Ahn, Fabrice Popineau et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The authors linearize the coupled JEPA gradient flow around the collapsed fixed point. They find **a competition between an alignment-driven "drive" γ and a predictor-induced "decay" σ**, which gives a per-mode stability ratio μ = γ/σ whose count of unstable modes tracks the achievable representation rank. The predicted **μ = 1 phase boundary is confirmed across more than 800 Tabular-JEPA configurations**, and the paper re-derives seven published anti-collapse tricks (EMA, predictor scaling, masking ratio, DirectPred, and others) as different ways of shifting μ. On that basis they propose **ResidualPred**, an identity-biased transformer predictor that improves effective rank and linear-probe accuracy on tabular data and in I-JEPA pretraining up to ImageNet. The theory is linear and relies on a spectral-decoupling approximation.

### [Neural Data Needs Semantic Tokenization: Behavioral Events as Boundaries of Session-Transferable Tokens](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03001v1)

Sangyoon Bae, Jiook Cha
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Neural foundation models embed neuron and session identity into their tokens, so every new recording session is out of distribution. That is the same problem as a spectral model meeting a new instrument. The proposed tokenizer, **TWS, segments each trial at task events and turns each inter-event "state" into tokens of population geometry, with no neuron or session embedding**. On held-out International Brain Laboratory sessions, **TWS decodes movement where a per-neuron foundation model pretrained on those sessions decodes at chance**. Frozen after training on mice alone, it transfers to macaques and Utah arrays with only a linear probe (reach-direction MCC 0.23 vs 0.01 from event timing alone). The main conclusion is that **for cross-session generalization the token design matters more than the backbone**.

### [Amortized Structured Stochastic Variational Inference for Gaussian Process Latent Variable Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03647v1)

Maksym Tretiakov, Sarah Lucie Filipp, Vincent Fortuin et al.
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

All existing GPLVM inference uses a mean-field factorization between the latent variables and the GP inducing points, which limits how well the model expresses epistemic uncertainty about the learned manifold. The authors **apply structured stochastic VI so that the amortized latent posterior is conditioned on the inducing-point values**. They derive the corresponding ELBO and an amortized encoder. Across Oilflow, qPCR, MNIST, and FMNIST, the structured model gives **statistically significant gains in train/test CRPS and NLPD over the mean-field GPLVM**, and uncertainty grows faster away from the data. Coverage, however, does not improve uniformly. This matters for GP-LVM-style generative models of quasar continua, where calibrated manifold uncertainty is the point.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [A two-stage settling of the Milky Way disk revealed by precise ChronoGal ages](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03164v1)

David Mirabal, Carme Gallart, Tomás Ruiz-Lara et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors run **CMD fitting of complete Gaia samples in the solar neighbourhood** to get age–metallicity distributions with **better than 5% age resolution (~0.5 Gyr at old ages)**, then measure absolute vertical density profiles for quasi-mono-age-metallicity populations. Scale height falls with age throughout, but the slope **changes abruptly 10.85 (+0.13/−0.12) Gyr ago**. Before that, h_Z drops from >1.26 kpc to ~0.5 kpc in only ~2 Gyr; afterwards the thinning is gradual, down to ~0.1 kpc for the youngest stars. This supports **an early, turbulent upside-down formation of the thick disk followed by quiescent secular settling into the thin disk**, timed roughly after the Gaia-Enceladus merger, rather than a single continuous heating sequence.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Comment on: "The Preprint Evolution: The Rise of Unreviewed Drafts on arXiv and its Implications for Astronomy"](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02273v1)

Noam Soker
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

Soker rebuts the recent proposal (arXiv:2609.35787) to **split astro-ph into a peer-reviewed stream and a "pre-review discussion draft" stream**. He argues it would delay posting rather than reduce volume and would start a slippery slope toward impact-factor or mainstream-versus-non-mainstream tiers. He points out that a reader who wants only refereed work can filter with AI tools. Against single-referee review, he cites community feedback on missing references and errors, and transparent priority records. His counter-proposal is that **journals should require posting to arXiv for at least a week before acceptance**.

### [BootLoops: an LLM-driven toolkit for exact quantitative science](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02286v1)

Matthew D. Schwartz
**Primary category:** hep-ph

Schwartz packages exact-computation tools for agentic LLMs to operate and extend: **integration-by-parts reduction, differential equations and high-precision evaluation from collider physics, PSLQ-style integer-relation fitting, exhaustive enumeration, and ball arithmetic**. The thesis is that **an LLM's breadth lets it carry exact methods across fields that few humans span**. Examples include bootstrapping multi-loop Feynman integrals (twelve of fifteen showcased integrals have no prior computation the author could find), evaluating Bayesian evidence for phylogenetic trees as exact rationals, and redoing floating-point calculations with certified digits. It is a concrete statement of how AI agents might change the practice of quantitative science.
