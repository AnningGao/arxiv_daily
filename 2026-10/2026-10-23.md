## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [A Robust Estimator for the Lyman-$α$ Forest 1D Power Spectrum at High Redshift](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31626v1)

Molly Wolfson, Joseph F. Hennawi, Frederick B. Davies et al. (Wolfson, Hennawi, Davies: high-value forest/IGM authors)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

The authors validate a **maximum-likelihood estimator for the $z \geq 5$ Lyα forest $P(k)$**. It builds the pixel-level flux covariance from precomputed $k$-space integrals that include spectral resolution and pixelization, so window functions and missing data are handled directly rather than corrected after an FFT. They test it on three mock sets of increasing realism: Gaussian skewers drawn from a known $P(k)$, forward-modeled simulation skewers post-processed with fluctuating UV backgrounds ($\lambda_{\rm mfp} = 5$ and 150 cMpc, $R = 8800$), and XQR-30-like masked spectra at $z = 5.7$. Across 100 mock datasets the **recovered power matches the reference within the expected errors over the full $k$ range, and realistic XQR-30 masking introduces no significant bias**, only the extra scatter expected from losing pixels. The estimator is aimed at measuring the large-scale power excess from UVB fluctuations at the end of reionization. The XQR-30 measurement itself is left to future work, and the authors note that the UVB fluctuations in their mocks are not coupled to temperature fluctuations.

![](figures/2610.31626/p1d_residuals_summary.pdf)

*Residuals of the likelihood (red) and FFT (blue) estimates against reference models over 100 forward-modeled mock datasets, for λ_mfp = 5 cMpc (left) and 150 cMpc (right). The 1σ bands of both methods are consistent with zero at all k and sit inside the expected error (yellow) from a 500,000-mock covariance.*

### [A Catalog of Spectral Properties for DESI DR1 Quasars](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31416v1)

Qiaoya Wu, Yue Shen (Shen: high-value quasar-spectra author)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

This is a value-added catalog of uniform PyQSOFit spectral decompositions (power-law plus polynomial continuum, Fe II, host galaxy at $z < 1$) for **1,717,774 unique DESI DR1 quasars (1,774,723 spectra)**. It gives line properties from Lyα to Hα, single-epoch Hβ/Mg II/C IV black-hole masses, bolometric luminosities and Eddington ratios. The part most relevant to forest work is the **improved systemic redshifts, which account for luminosity-dependent blueshifts of high-ionization lines**. They agree with the DESI pipeline redshifts to within 500 km/s for 88.6% of the sample. The remaining 11.4% are concentrated at $z > 1$, where C IV blueshifts and BAL troughs bias the pipeline redshifts. The catalog also fits optical coronal lines ([Ne V] λ3426 is detected in 9.5% of spectra with coverage) and shows that several coronal/narrow/broad line ratios vary systematically with Eddington ratio. Results agree with SDSS DR16Q for matched objects.

![](figures/2610.31416/coadd_spec.pdf)

*Median composites of 10k-quasar z > 1.5 subsets binned by pipeline-versus-systemic velocity offset. As the offset grows, stacks built on the systemic redshifts give sharper, better-aligned emission lines (notably C IV), and the large-offset bins show BAL absorption that confuses the pipeline.*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [The THESAN-ZOOM project: the delayed rise of Lyman continuum escape fractions in bursty high-redshift galaxies](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30265v1)

Parth Mehrotra, Aaron Smith, Yuan Bian et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The authors post-process 13 THESAN-ZOOM radiation-hydrodynamic zoom haloes at $z > 3$ with COLT radiative transfer at ~10 Myr cadence and measure the timing between star-formation bursts and LyC escape. **$f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}$ lags the burstiness tracer SFR$_{10}$/SFR$_{100}$ by typically ~16 Myr**, with peak correlations of 0.4–0.6 in massive haloes and longer, less coherent delays at low mass. The delays scale as **$M_\star^{-0.19}$ and $M_{\rm halo}^{-0.27}$**, though the shortest lags approach the snapshot spacing. Because photon production and transparency peak at different times, a galaxy can be highly transparent while its fading stars emit little, so **the overlap of the two sets the escaping ionizing output**. The observable Hα/UV ratio keeps a delayed link to escape, which matters for interpreting low-z leakers and the galaxy contribution to the reionization photon budget behind forest opacity and mean-free-path constraints.

![](figures/2610.30265/pub_timelag_10SFR100_vs_f_esc_LyC.pdf)

*Lag-correlation function between burstiness (SFR₁₀/SFR₁₀₀) and LyC escape fraction for halo m12.6. The nearest positive peak sits at a 15.2 Myr delay with r = 0.47, and repeated peaks reflect recurrent burst–feedback cycles.*

### [Same Events, Weaker Bound: Model Dependence in Magellanic Microlensing Limits on Primordial Black Holes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30510v1)

David Morales-Zapien, Lucas Brown, Stefano Profumo
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.HE, hep-ph

The authors reanalyze OGLE's 20-year LMC survey (13 events) and the high-cadence campaign with the field-level data, efficiencies and timescale likelihood held fixed. They change only the event-rate model: a flattened, truncated Milky Way halo, updated stellar lens populations, and a cored, truncated LMC halo. **The bound on compact-object dark matter weakens by ~1.2–1.6 for a benchmark motivated by recent data and up to ~3.4 in a stress test** relative to OGLE's relaxed limit (up to 6.2 and 13.2 relative to the strict limit). LMC-induced halo disequilibrium, often cited as a reason to distrust the bound, actually raises the lensing-weighted density. If PBHs are primordially clustered, **the planetary-mass bound weakens by 3.4–6 once a Lyα-forest bound on cluster shot noise is imposed**, while the stellar-mass bound moves by at most 16%. The authors recommend quoting microlensing PBH limits together with their halo and stellar-population assumptions. Halo parameters are fixed rather than marginalized, so these factors measure sensitivity, not a calibrated envelope.

### [Self-Supervised Representation Learning for JWST Galaxy Images and Low Resolution Spectra](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31078v1)

Yang Cheng, Morgan Fouesneau, Ivelina G. Momcheva et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

A DINOv2 ViT trained on single-band F150W cutouts is paired with a **1D masked autoencoder that tokenizes PRISM spectra into overlapping flux patches**. The autoencoder uses a noise-weighted reconstruction loss and excludes invalid tokens end to end. The two are then **CLIP-aligned after attention pooling, with the flux scale re-injected**. A linear probe on the joint embedding recovers **stellar mass at $1 < z \leq 3$ with $R^2 \sim 0.9$ and $\sigma_{\rm NMAD} = 0.24$ dex**, using only the observed-frame 1–2 μm spectral window (chosen to emulate Euclid/Roman). Cross-modal alignment raises single-modality performance by $\Delta R^2 = +0.04$ for imaging and +0.09 for spectra. Performance degrades at $z > 3.5$, where the window covers only featureless rest-frame UV. This short workshop paper is a compact template for patch tokenization plus contrastive alignment on low-resolution survey spectra.

### [Wavenumber-Aware Deep Learning for Robust Analysis of Raman Spectra](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30746v1)

Jihwan Kim, Jaeseong Heo, Yujin Kim et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: physics.chem-ph, physics.optics

Each spectral point is fed as an (intensity, wavenumber) pair, and **Transformer positional encodings are computed from the physical wavenumber rather than the array index**. Tests use bacterial Raman spectra and synthetic mixtures with noise, downsampling and partial-coverage augmentations. In a controlled comparison where only the positional encoding changes, **wavenumber-based encoding matches index encoding on clean spectra (93.8% vs 93.4% F1) but wins clearly under degradation**. On the 30-isolate Ho et al. benchmark with noise plus slicing, it reaches 73.9% top-1 versus 42.8% for index encoding and 24.8% for learned encoding. Beyond the training downsampling range, the Transformer stays above 90% F1 at 8× downsampling while CNN/ResNet drop to 75–80%, because each token carries its own coordinate instead of relying on a fixed-pixel kernel. The result transfers directly to tokenizing spectra on varying wavelength grids, masks and resolutions, as in quasar spectra.

### [Random Initialization as a Baseline for Time Series Foundation Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30950v1)

Pinar Sungu Isiacik, Georgiana Ifrim
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Seven released instances from four time-series foundation-model families (MOMENT, Mantis, UniTS, NuTime) are compared against **architecture-matched randomly initialized copies** on the UEA multivariate classification archive. Frozen representations go into four downstream classifiers, with five random seeds. Across 183 paired comparisons, **pretraining adds only 1.43–5.19 percentage points per instance (2.58 on average)**, and after Holm correction only NuTime's per-instance gain remains significant. **Non-pretrained ROCKET, Hydra and Quant still win on aggregate** (mean accuracy 0.754/0.752/0.752 against 0.731 for the best foundation model, Mantis, on the common 24 datasets). Much of the apparent value of these encoders therefore comes from the architecture and aggregation pipeline. The paper argues for random-init and strong non-pretrained baselines as standard controls when claiming pretraining benefits, which applies equally to self-supervised spectral models.

### [Rethinking SAE Evaluation: An Atomic Interpretable Unit Framework Reveals Hidden Polysemanticity and Redundancy](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30474v1)

Riteng Zhang, Xiaoqian Wang
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.AI, cs.CL

The authors argue that SAE dictionaries should be judged as **an "atomic interpretable unit" basis for the whole representation, not as a set of individually monosemantic features**. They organize existing metrics as proxies for that objective and benchmark hundreds of SAEs across seven architectures. Their dominance-aware monosemanticity test (DAME) finds that **the features contributing most to computation are the least interpretable**: top-quartile features score 61.8% vs 64.1% (Wilcoxon $p = 6.8\times10^{-8}$), with the largest deficits for BatchTopK, TopK and Matryoshka. **Dominant features are also 1.64× enriched in redundant pairs** (decoder cosine > 0.7) despite dissimilar encoders, a pattern the authors call "feature sharding". Sparsity can therefore be satisfied while the most important structure stays entangled. The authors recommend stratifying monosemanticity and decoder similarity by dominance before using SAE features for attribution or circuit tracing.

### [AI Mindmeld: A Latent Interconnect Protocol](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30215v1)

Siavash Golkar, Jake Kovalic, Shirley Ho (Polymathic AI)
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: q-bio.BM, stat.ML

The **Latent Interconnect Protocol (LIP)** replaces text-mediated tool output with direct representation exchange. **Small connectors map frozen specialist-model embeddings into frozen LLM hidden states** at chosen layers and prompt slots, and each connector is trained independently. Three protein and three small-molecule specialists are connected to Gemma 3 (4B, 12B) and Kimi K2.6. **Connectors answer questions about unseen objects, transfer zero-shot to new question types, and compose without joint training**, including protein–compound relation queries. With Kimi K2.6, combining three protein connectors beats every single connector, while with Gemma 3 4B the combination does worse than any one alone. Four-token MLP connectors match supervised linear probes on trained tasks. The authors' own caveat is that current LLMs are not trained to use such inputs, so access does not guarantee use in reasoning. The paper is relevant as a template for plugging a spectral foundation model into an LLM agent.

### [2Flow2SN: Dusty Supernova Cosmology with Photometric Redshifts and Contamination using Neural Simulation-Based Inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30244v1)

Benjamin M. Boyd, Maximilian Autenrieth, Kaisey S. Mandel et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: stat.AP

FlowSN2 recasts photometric SN Ia cosmology as **joint hierarchical inference over light-curve summaries and photometric redshifts**. Two normalizing flows provide neural likelihood estimates: one for observed summaries and redshifts under selection, given latent $z$ and $\mu$, and one for the selected latent Hubble diagram. Both are **amortized over cosmology, so a single training supports $w_0w_a$CDM and other models away from the fiducial cosmology**. The framework separates intrinsic SN variation from host dust with a Simple-BayeSN standardization and handles non-Ia contamination by Bayesian resampling, with no explicit contaminant likelihood. Across five simulated cosmologies it recovers true cosmological and population parameters, matching analytic solutions and pure-Ia baselines. It **scales to >10⁵ SNe without retraining**, and Bayesian model comparison comes at negligible extra cost. Tests use simplified simulated surveys, and the application to the DES 5YR sample is still to come.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Early panchromatic spectra reveal helium in the thermonuclear supernova 2024kce](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30674v1)

Abigail Polin, Dillon Hasenour, Sahana Kumar et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE | also: astro-ph.SR

The team took simultaneous optical and NIR spectra of the faint SN 2024kce in the M49 halo, starting ~11 days before maximum light. They identify **He I absorption at three wavelengths, all at ~16,400 km/s, above the ~12,500 km/s photosphere**: positive detections at 2.058 μm and 7065 Å, and a tentative one at 7281 Å. This breaks the usual degeneracy between He I 1.083 μm and C I. It is **the first detection of helium before maximum light in a thermonuclear supernova**. Helium at those velocities needs nearby radioactive material for non-thermal excitation, which points to ash from a detonated surface helium shell. A **double-detonation model** reproduces the optical evolution, the suppressed blue flux and the He I 1.083 μm feature. The object sits between subluminous SNe Ia and Ia-like Ca-rich transients, suggesting the same explosion mechanism operates across both classes.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Astro2Method: Benchmarking LLMs on Machine Learning Method Selection in Astronomy](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30282v1)

Xingzhong Fan, Hangfeng He, Fatemeh Nargesian
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

The authors build **500 expert-reviewed binary questions from 346 astronomy papers (2024/01–2026/06)**, each asking which of two ML methods performed better on a stated problem. Questions are split by whether the source paper gives a complete (Easy) or partial (Hard) explanation for the difference. Across five LLMs, direct prompting reaches 87.5–92.6% on Easy but only **58.9–64.5% on Hard questions, barely above chance**. Neither RAG over historical papers nor agent schemes (ReAct, Self-Ask, CRITIC) help consistently. Web-search agents improve mainly when they find the source paper itself. **Supplying the source paper's pipeline description with method names masked helps as much as or more than web search.** The takeaway is that LLMs cannot yet infer from domain knowledge which experimental conditions make one ML method beat another in astronomy.
