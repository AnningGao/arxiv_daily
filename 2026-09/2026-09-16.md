## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Lyman-α forest 1D flux power spectrum constraints on QSO-assisted reionization models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16992v1)

Vid Iršič, Avery Meiksin
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Iršič & Meiksin post-process the Sherwood-Relics IGM simulations with QSO-driven He II reionization — seeding He III regions whose temperature is boosted by ΔT_b = 1–2×10⁴ K — and compare the resulting Lyα forest 1D flux power spectrum against the Boera et al. (2019) high-resolution P1D at z = 4.2, 4.6, 5.0 in a full MCMC likelihood with the Iršič (2024) covariance. Adding He III thermal inhomogeneity modestly improves the fit over a homogeneous-reionization baseline (Δχ² ≈ −5 to −6 for one extra parameter). Framing the QSO luminosity-function faint-end cutoff M_UV^max as the model parameter, they find that a contribution from the faint JWST QSO population with M_UV > −21.6 is excluded at 2σ if those spectra are hard enough to raise the IGM temperature by ≥ 2×10⁴ K, and translate this into a ceiling on the QSO ionizing emissivity, ε₉₁₂ < 1–3×10²⁴ erg s⁻¹ Hz⁻¹ cMpc⁻³ (2σ), consistent with independent Lyα-opacity results.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Cosmological information from field-level analysis in redshift space](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16719v1)

Julia Stadler
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Stadler quantifies how much more cosmological information a field-level EFT analysis extracts over the power spectrum + bispectrum for redshift-space samples resembling DESI LRGs. Using the LEFTfield forward model both to generate mocks and to run the field-level inference, and a matched Fisher forecast for the power/bispectrum, she finds the primordial fluctuation amplitude is measured about twice as precisely at field level for ΛCDM. For extended cosmologies with a more flexible shape–growth relation the gains grow to a factor of ~3 in the amplitude and ~2 in the growth parameters (γ, f), with similar improvements on the noise amplitude. The advantage is conservative given the Fisher comparison is generous to the two-point/three-point combination.

### [The Equivalence Principle in the Dark Sector in light of DESI](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17423v1)

Andrea Clini, Pierre Zhang, Emanuele Castorina et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

The authors address the mild DESI–CMB tension by postulating that dark matter's inertial and gravitational masses differ, which introduces a new long-range force in the dark sector (a violation of the equivalence principle for DM). Fitting CMB with either DESI DR1 BAO + full shape or DR2 BAO alone prefers a dark-sector force of strength β ≈ 0.5% relative to gravity at the ~2.5σ level. A preliminary combination of DR1 full shape with DR2 BAO raises the evidence for dark forces to ~3σ (modulo the not-yet-available cross-covariance).

### [Probing multi-state dark matter via optical absorption lines in DESI spectra](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16547v1)

Anoma Ganguly, Shadab Alam
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

Ganguly & Alam introduce a new spectroscopic dark-matter probe: in a two-state DM model with a magnetic-dipole transition, a photon traversing a foreground DM halo can be resonantly absorbed, imprinting a narrow line that falls in the optical band for an eV-scale mass splitting. Stacking 145,087 DESI Early Data Release spectra paired with GAMA galaxy groups, they measure a stacked optical depth consistent with zero and set the first 2σ upper limits on the magnetic-dipole cross-section for few-MeV DM at transition energies ΔE = 2.5–4 eV — a region inaccessible to both BBN/CMB and direct detection. They forecast that the full DESI Year-5 and Stage-5 surveys will tighten these limits by more than an order of magnitude.

### [The WEFT project: I. The emergence of turbulence in a cosmic filament](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17205v1)

Théo Lebeau, Saleem Zaroubi
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The first paper of the Web Evolution in Filament Targeted (WEFT) project applies cosmological zoom-in to a single filament, evolving it from z = 63 to z = 0 with AREPO at ~8 kpc median gas-cell size to follow how turbulence emerges in the warm-hot intergalactic medium (WHIM). The filament assembles by hierarchical merging of proto-filaments rather than laminar accretion; vorticity is seeded baroclinically where rotating strands meet accretion shocks obliquely; and the flow transitions from a supersonic, shock-dominated (Burgers-like) state to a developed, mildly supersonic, intermittent cascade with She-Lévêque-like longitudinal exponents by z = 0. This provides the turbulent input needed for forecasts of WHIM observability.

### [B-sure. Part II. Scattering transforms as robustness test for tensor-to-scalar ratio detection from CMB observations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17531v1)

Claudio Ranucci, Sébastien Pierre, Léo Vacher et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

This work builds a robustness test for a would-be primordial B-mode (tensor-to-scalar ratio r) detection by a LiteBIRD-like experiment. After blind component separation, residual Galactic foregrounds can bias r; the authors use scattering transforms as summary statistics sensitive to the non-Gaussian structure of those residuals and to their correlation with foreground tracer maps. On realistic simulations with varied foreground complexity, the test flags a bias on r ≳ 10⁻³ in ~90% of cases (comparable to LiteBIRD's target accuracy) while correctly staying silent when the bias is below the r sensitivity.

### [ΛCDM in a courtroom](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16097v1)

Ofer Lahav, Paul Shah
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Lahav & Shah offer a Perspective that "puts ΛCDM on trial," weighing the evidence for a genuinely constant Λ against dynamical dark energy in light of recent galaxy-clustering (DESI), Type Ia supernova, and CMB results. They assess the strength of the case on each side and discuss what the near-future data — and the eventual fate of the Universe — hold for the field's most mysterious component.

### [Noise2Noise Revisited: Training Pair Distributions Dominate Loss Choice in Self-Supervised Denoising](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16788v1)

Dingyan Shang, Zhenyu Xu, Youting Wang et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: eess.IV

This paper stress-tests why the L1 loss outperforms L2 in Noise2Noise self-supervised denoising (training on pairs of independently corrupted observations, with no clean references). It debunks the "L1 confers robustness via sparsity" story — L1- and L2-trained weight distributions are indistinguishable and an explicit Lasso penalty fails to reproduce L1's behavior — and shows the two losses' population optima nearly coincide, so measured differences are dominated by optimization dynamics (bounded-influence gradients). The headline lesson is that the *training-pair distribution*, not the loss, carries the inductive bias: retraining on a sensor's own noisy pairs gains 9.4–11 dB versus 0.8–3.7 dB for mismatched synthetic-noise training.

### [Generative models for simulation based filtering: Formulations and Empirical Comparisons](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16317v1)

Mohammad Al-Jarrah, Wei Deng, Bamdad Hosseini et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The authors give a unified formulation of generative-model approaches to nonlinear/Bayesian filtering in which the analysis step is a learned transport of the forecast distribution to the posterior, with methods differing only in how that transport is chosen. They derive three new filters — based on stochastic interpolants, their deterministic flow-matching limit, and Schrödinger bridges via forward–backward SDEs — and benchmark them against optimal-transport, Knothe–Rosenblatt, particle (SIR), and ensemble-Kalman filters. Every generative filter resolves multimodal posteriors that EnKF and SIR miss, though no single framework dominates (the best choice depends on the online compute budget and ensemble size).

### [Large Language Models Develop Belief State Geometry In-Context](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17376v1)

Daniel Balcells, Andrew Jun Lee, Chirag Rastogi et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.CL

To understand the representations that support in-context learning, the authors prompt six open LLMs with sequences emitted by hidden Markov models and probe for the belief state — the Bayesian posterior over the HMM's hidden states given the observed history. Belief states are linearly decodable from residual-stream activations with probe R² = 0.83–0.99 across model/HMM combinations, and patching/steering interventions on the probe-identified subspace preserve downstream prediction while controls degrade it. This is functional, representation-level evidence that ICL approximates optimal Bayesian prediction over a context-inferred generative model.

### [ResLRP: The Role of Residual Cancellation in Attribution Instability in Vision Transformers](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17152v1)

Jim Berend, Reduan Achtibat, Daniel Schäffer et al.
**Primary category:** cs.CV | also: cs.LG

The authors diagnose why Layer-wise Relevance Propagation produces noisy, unfaithful attributions in Vision Transformers: cancellation effects in residual pathways cause "attribution explosion," and these cancellations are substantially stronger in ViTs than in language transformers. They introduce ResLRP, a residual-aware LRP whose rules are exactly conservative and provably bound relevance explosion, improving faithfulness and localization across supervised, self-supervised, contrastive, hierarchical, and multimodal ViTs (largest gains in vision-language models), with a residual-amplification measure that predicts where attribution degrades. ResLRP can also localize sparse-autoencoder features in input space.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Discovery of radio emission from the exoplanet β Pictoris b](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16720v1)

Kevin N. Ortiz Ceballos, Edo Berger, Yvette Cendes
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP

Using MeerKAT, the authors report the first direct detection of auroral radio emission from an exoplanet, the giant planet β Pictoris b: rapid, recurring, highly circularly polarized bursts plus persistent emission at 0.85–3.5 GHz. They identify it as electron-cyclotron-maser instability radiation, whose highest frequency implies a magnetic field ≳ 1.25 kG at the planet — the first direct field-strength measurement for a world beyond the Solar System, and consistent with the magnetic-dynamo energy scaling for low-mass stars and Solar System planets.

### [A Dark-matter Origin of Little Red Dots: Early Seeding and Super-Bondi Accretion](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16122v1)

Hua-Peng Gu, Fangzhou Jiang, Xian Chen et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.HE

The authors argue that Little Red Dots — accreting supermassive black holes at high z with tiny or undetectable stellar hosts and extreme black-hole-to-stellar mass ratios — arise naturally if dark matter is self-interacting. Using a relativistic, non-equilibrium halo-evolution model, they show that gravothermal collapse of highly concentrated SIDM halos assembled before reionization produces stellar-mass BH seeds within a few hundred Myr, after which heat conduction sustains a prolonged super-Bondi dark-matter inflow that grows the seeds to supermassive scale without baryonic assistance. The resulting population matches the observed LRD abundance and redshift distribution.

### [Lower central dark matter densities in nearby galaxies than predicted by simulations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16740v1)

Yu Lei, Ling Zhu, Meng Yang et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors consistently measure dark-matter distributions from galaxy centres out to 20–50 kpc for 136 nearby galaxies spanning stellar masses 10⁹–10¹¹·⁵ M⊙ along the local mass–size relation. They find central DM densities systematically lower than ΛCDM (hydrodynamical) simulation expectations, with the low-density region growing from ~10 kpc at 10¹⁰ M⊙ to > 50 kpc at 10¹¹·⁵ M⊙. While the physical origin is left open, the trend is a clear observational benchmark that stresses baryonic-contraction and feedback prescriptions in current simulations.
