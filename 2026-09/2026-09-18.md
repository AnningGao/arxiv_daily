## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Where the Forest Goes Dark: Halo-centered Characterization of High Column Density Systems with IllustrisTNG](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19235v1)

Victor R. Soares da Silva, Daniel López-Cano, L. Raul Abramo et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using the TNG50 hydrodynamical simulation, the authors characterize the high-column-density systems (LLSs, sub-DLAs, DLAs) that contaminate both the 1D and 3D Lyα forest statistics, tracing every absorption feature back to the gas that produces it. They identify HCDs on the gas cells, deblend them in velocity space, split each sightline into HCD-only and forest-only spectra, and measure each absorber class's covering fraction against impact parameter and halo mass out to 50 virial radii at z≈3. The radial sequence — DLAs give way to sub-DLAs at b≈0.11 R200c, sub-DLAs to LLSs at 0.3, and LLSs to the forest at 0.5 (the forest covering ≈78% of sightlines at R200c) — is nearly mass-independent over three decades in halo mass, so the virial radius sets the scale of the neutral-gas distribution. A single Voigt component recovers essentially every damped system's column density but fails for LLSs, whose absorption often arises from several distinct structures along the sightline.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Reconstructing the Projected Dark Matter Field across 0.1-100 Mpc Scales from the SDSS Survey](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19295v1)

Kunyao Zhao, Yingjie Peng, Dingyi Zhao et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors build a conditional diffusion model that reconstructs the projected dark-matter density field from the galaxy stellar-mass density field, trained on CAMELS and validated on the independent IllustrisTNG300-1 box. Halo masses inferred from the reconstructed projected-aperture measurements agree with truth to below 0.2 dex scatter, and 100-kpc surface densities scatter ~0.3 dex in the observationally relevant regime. They apply it to SDSS galaxies with M⋆≥10⁹ M⊙ over a 90×90 (h⁻¹Mpc)² region at z~0.03 (averaging 100 stochastic realizations), release the resulting projected DM map, and show projected-aperture masses consistent with SDSS group-catalog masses and a halo mass function matching mock expectations.

### [Distinguishing Coupled Dark Matter Dark Energy from Kinematic Phantom Crossing](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19602v1)

Samit Ganguly, Koushik Dutta, Goutam Manna
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

In coupled dark-energy–dark-matter (CDEDM) models, energy exchange between a canonical scalar field and cold dark matter can reproduce the same effective equation of state as w0waCDM — including apparent phantom crossing — without any fundamental phantom field. The authors show this microscopic coupling leaves testable signatures in structure growth (only CDM feels the scalar fifth force and its drag, while baryons stay uncoupled), and that combining it with neutrino free-streaming produces a coupling–neutrino-mass degeneracy scaling as Σmν ∝ f_c² α², which they recover analytically from the two-fluid growth equations. Fitting DESI DR2 BAO, CMB distance priors, ACT DR6 lensing, RSD, and three supernova compilations, the scaling holds for both exponential and inverse-power-law potentials, and the interaction induces a running dark-matter mass that changes ω_c at recombination by 4.3–5.5%.

### [Generalised Visibility ILC: a new foreground mitigation strategy for interferometric HI intensity mapping in the low-redshift Universe](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19276v1)

M. Ruiz-Granda, A. Mazumder, L. Wolz et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This paper introduces GVILC, a non-parametric foreground-cleaning method that works in visibility space for low-redshift interferometric HI intensity mapping, tested on MeerKAT- and SKA-Mid-like simulations. It combines PCA for foreground-subspace identification — informed by a prior HI signal-plus-noise covariance — with a multi-dimensional internal linear combination in visibility space, and picks the number of PCA modes to remove via a data-driven statistic. Combined with foreground avoidance it prevents most signal loss while recovering modes that were initially foreground-contaminated, and the mean-squared-error statistic shows GVILC outperforming foreground-avoidance-only for both instruments; adding a realistic HI model with line broadening increases post-cleaning signal loss without changing the number of modes removed.

### [On the Probability Distribution and Null-hypothesis Testing of Cross-correlation for Light Curves in Active Galactic Nuclei](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19672v1)

Yan-Rong Li, Jian-Min Wang
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.IM

The authors supply a framework for assessing the significance of cross-correlations between AGN light curves — a longstanding gap in reverberation mapping, where the interpolated cross-correlation function is used to measure time lags but a principled significance test has been missing. They leverage the result that, for independent stochastic light curves, the distribution of cross-correlation coefficients is asymptotically normal, with a variance analytically estimable from the light curves' auto-correlation functions, and validate this with Monte Carlo simulations of irregularly sampled red-noise AGN light curves to build a fast null-hypothesis test. The procedure requires the ACFs to be determined beforehand by model fitting, and the authors flag the still-unresolved bias in recovering the ACF when a light curve's duration is comparable to its variability timescale.

### [Topological Signatures of AGN Feedback in the Simba Simulations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19993v1)

Ryusei R. Kano, Romeel Davé, Tsutomu T. Takeuchi et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Applying topological data analysis (persistent homology) to Simba runs with varying AGN feedback at z=0, the authors ask which galaxy populations, environments, and scales are most feedback-sensitive. The two-point correlation function responds most at r<1 h⁻¹Mpc; using Betti curves, persistence diagrams, and the 2-Wasserstein distance, they find persistence diagrams reveal clearer feedback differences than Betti curves, most strongly for satellite galaxies in quenched halos with M_h≳10¹² M⊙. Loop-like (H1) features differ significantly at α~0.1–0.4 h⁻¹Mpc, with a weaker cumulative signal at 1–3 h⁻¹Mpc, and the differences are driven by shifts in the birth/death scales of features rather than their appearance or disappearance.

### [PosteriorBench: From Point Estimates to Posterior Matching in Evaluating Generative Inverse Solvers](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20794v1)

Jiachen Yao, Zi-Siang Hsu, Xi Deng et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.CE

This work introduces a benchmark for the distributional — not merely pointwise — accuracy of generative inverse solvers, motivated by ill-posed problems where a method can be pointwise-accurate yet miss the true posterior through mode collapse, overconfident uncertainty, or averaging incompatible solutions. For four physics-based inverse problems the authors build high-fidelity reference posteriors (rejection sampling, MCMC) and pair them with a five-metric suite: posterior-mean error, posterior-standard-deviation error, maximum mean discrepancy, sliced Wasserstein distance, and radially-averaged power-spectrum error. They find substantial distribution-matching gaps across current solvers, that neural operators improve resolution robustness, and that guidance weights and generation noise are the levers for posterior-variance calibration.
