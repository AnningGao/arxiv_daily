### [Reassessing Evidence for Dark-Sector Interactions with Dynamical Dark Energy and DESI DR2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03062v1)

Jincheng Wang, Hongwei Yu, Puxun Wu
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

Combining Planck+ACT CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and DES-Dovekie supernovae, the authors test whether a dark-sector coupling ($Q=\beta H\rho_{\rm de}$) is still favored once the dark-energy equation of state is allowed to evolve. They find that promoting the background to interacting $w_0w_a$CDM substantially weakens the preference for a nonzero coupling, while the preference for dynamical dark energy itself persists — i.e., much of the apparent "coupling" in more restricted models is a degeneracy with dark-energy dynamics. Extending to three one-parameter trajectories (thawing, mirage, GEDE), they show the role of the interaction is trajectory-dependent, and that the models predict markedly different growth signatures (enhanced clustering for interacting thawing vs. strong suppression for interacting GEDE) despite near-identical background expansion.

This is squarely on the DESI DR2 / dynamical-dark-energy thread (focus rank 3): it is a cautionary systematics result on how degeneracies between background and dark-sector physics can be broken by late-time large-scale-structure growth — exactly the kind of full-shape/clustering leverage the forest program contributes to.

### [Euclid preparation. The shape of halo profiles in ΛCDM and non-standard cosmologies](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04192v1)

Euclid Collaboration: L. Pizzuti, G. Y. Ferron et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

Using the DUSTGRAIN-pathfinder and DEMNUni N-body suites, the authors quantify how massive neutrinos, $f(\mathcal{R})$ gravity, and dynamical dark energy imprint on 3D and projected dark-matter halo profiles (density, mass, velocity dispersion, excess surface density) out to $5\,r_{500c}$, in view of Euclid cluster weak-lensing. In low-mass haloes, $f(\mathcal{R})$ produces ~10% deviations in the outskirts where screening is weak, partially counteracted by neutrino free-streaming; dynamical dark energy and neutrinos alone give few-percent effects. Detecting these profile differences at $z=1.1$ needs stacks of ~$10^5$ haloes, dropping to a few thousand at $z\lesssim0.5$, and the concentration–mass relation is a weaker (~5%) discriminant.

Useful context on the LSS/neutrinos/modified-gravity secondary thread: a concrete forecast of how non-standard physics perturbs halo structure and what stacking is required to see it — relevant background for interpreting cosmological constraints (including neutrino mass) that the forest also targets.

*No figures extracted for 2609.04192 (the key content is percent-level profile-deviation curves across many model/redshift panels; not reducible to one informative figure).*

### [The moving lens effect: analytical modelling and foreground suppression](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04133v1)

Amy Wayland, David Alonso, William Coulton
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The moving-lens (ML) effect — a CMB secondary from the transverse motion of gravitational potentials — probes the large-scale cosmic velocity field via cross-correlation of a CMB map with a reconstructed transverse galaxy-momentum field. The authors build an analytic framework for the ML estimator and its foreground contamination, showing that signal and foregrounds have distinct dependence on the direction of the reconstructed long-wavelength velocity mode. In the squeezed limit enforced by the velocity-reconstruction filter, the leading foreground bispectrum contribution becomes direction-independent and vanishes after angular averaging, while the ML signal survives; halo-model corrections show the residual contamination stays parametrically suppressed. A curved-sky treatment shows the cross-correlation is sourced solely by the longitudinal galaxy-momentum component as a spin-1 E-mode.

Adjacent LSS methodology: the estimator design and the squeezed-limit foreground-cancellation argument are directly borrowable for velocity-field and cross-correlation analyses, and the reconstruction-filter reasoning parallels bias/foreground handling in forest and quasar cross-correlations.

*No figures extracted for 2609.04133 (analytic/estimator paper; the argument is formula-driven rather than visual).*

### [CLAS+: A large catalog of Changing-Look AGN candidates selected through S-PLUS narrow-band photometry](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03232v1)

L. Nakazono, L. L. Oliveira, R. R. Valença et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

CLAS+ selects changing-look AGN (CLAGN) candidates by comparing synthetic photometry from DESI DR1 and SDSS DR17 spectra against contemporaneous S-PLUS narrow-band imaging, flagging spectral–photometric mismatches ($\chi^2_r>15$) across a parent sample of 98,139 quasars. They identify 3,554 strong candidates (3.6% of the parent sample, 3,481 previously unreported), with temporal classes split ~40% photometric turn-on / 41% intermediate / 19% turn-off and an apparent redshift trend toward more turn-ons at higher $z$. The method offers a scalable, imaging-driven alternative to purely spectroscopic CLAGN searches and a target list for Rubin/LSST follow-up.

Relevant to the rising quasar-spectra/AGN thread (§4): it is a survey-scale demonstration built on DESI/SDSS quasar spectra and quasar spectral variability, and the synthetic-photometry-vs-spectrum comparison is methodologically adjacent to continuum/variability modelling of quasars used in forest work.

*No figures extracted for 2609.03232 (understandable from the text; example-spectra and demographic plots are illustrative rather than essential).*

### [Same galaxy, different CGM: how the metal loading of galactic winds regulates the baryon cycle in Milky Way-mass galaxies](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03083v1)

Prachi Khatri, Freeke van de Voort, Rebekka Bieri et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

A controlled suite of five Auriga cosmological MHD simulations of a Milky-Way-mass halo varies only the metal content and energy loading of stellar-feedback-driven winds, tuned so all five reach nearly identical ($\lesssim$10%) $z=0$ stellar masses and morphologies. Despite producing similar total metals, the runs differ dramatically in where those metals end up: metal loading sets the halo-gas cooling efficiency, regulating the CGM cool-gas fraction and accretion, with up to an order-of-magnitude spread in median gas metallicity at $|z|\gtrsim30$ kpc between extreme models. Current absorption-line metallicity constraints on Milky Way halo clouds at $|z|\lesssim15$ kpc are broadly consistent with the simulations.

Adjacent context on the CGM thread (§7), the absorption-line sibling of the IGM work: it shows how feedback parameters that are near-degenerate in stellar mass leave a strong imprint on circumgalactic gas metallicity — a caution for interpreting quasar-absorption metal diagnostics.

*No figures extracted for 2609.03083 (the takeaway is a metallicity contrast summarized clearly in text).*

### [Prospects for probing dark matter with filamentary 21cm emission](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03649v1)

Yizhou Liu, Yingjie Jing, Haoran Shi et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

Warm dark matter (WDM) suppresses low-mass halos and smooths cosmic filaments relative to CDM; the authors test whether filamentary 21cm emission at $z=4$ and $2.5$ could distinguish the two. From cosmological hydrodynamical zoom simulations they build intrinsic 21cm brightness-temperature maps, then convolve with Gaussian beams and compare with SKA1-Low/SKA2-Low sensitivities. The intrinsic maps clearly show the smoother WDM filament morphology (especially at $z=4$), but beams larger than ~6″ erase the small-scale structures that carry the DM information; although the WDM filament stays systematically brighter, neither SKA1-Low nor SKA2-Low can resolve individual filaments with enough sensitivity to separate the models. They conclude direct morphological DM discrimination via filament imaging is not feasible with current/planned SKA-Low.

On the DM-from-small-scale-structure (§5) and 21cm/reionization (§8) threads: a clean statement of where a WDM signature lives (filament morphology at high $z$) and why an angular-resolution vs. sensitivity trade-off defeats it — the same small-scale-suppression physics the forest constrains, seen through a complementary probe.

### [Morphology of Radio Sources in Representation Space](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03779v1)

Nicolas Baron Perez, Marcus Brüggen, Luisa Lucie-Smith
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.GA

Building on a self-supervised deep-clustering model trained on LoTSS-DR2, the authors apply a fine-tuned classifier ensemble to >13M LoTSS-DR3 radio sources to discover rare morphologies beyond the 12 known classes. Among low-class-probability sources they search representation space for new clusters and use them as centroids to re-classify the tail: 88% of sources fall into the DR2 clusters, and within the rest they identify six additional morphological classes (including winged and FR-ambiguous sources), yielding a strongly skewed 18-class distribution. They frame this as open-set recognition via representation learning — finding representatives of novel classes rather than flagging individual anomalies.

A strong §6 match: self-supervised/contrastive representation learning applied to scientific data, with an explicit open-set/novel-class-discovery workflow in a learned latent space — directly transferable machinery for the self-supervised quasar-spectra transformer project (clustering, confidence intervals in representation space, handling the long tail).

### [Benchmarking Machine Learning Emulators of Stellar Evolution for Precision Asteroseismology](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03004v1)

Naomi Gluck, Earl P. Bellinger, Yan Liang et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.SR

The authors study the grid density required for reliable ML emulation of stellar-evolution outputs ($T_{\rm eff}$, $L$, $\Delta\nu$, $\nu_{\max}$) for main-sequence models $M=[0.7,1.2]\,M_\odot$, a regime made hard by the radiative-to-convective core transition and Kepler's part-per-thousand seismic precision. Comparing linear interpolation, k-NN, random forests, and neural networks across analytical, MESA, YREC, MIST, and ASTEC grids, they find NNs win overall but sparse grids induce localized failures at the core transition — unstable derivatives, ensemble disagreement, and fragmented inference posteriors. Performance gains from denser grids are non-uniform, arguing for adaptive rather than uniform refinement, and NN ensembles provide localized uncertainty propagation that global error estimates miss.

Relevant to the §6 emulation and uncertainty-quantification threads: a careful, method-focused study of emulator failure modes and ensemble-based localized UQ that generalizes to any surrogate-modelling / SBI pipeline (including flux-power-spectrum emulators), independent of the stellar application.

*No figures extracted for 2609.03004 (the message — localized emulator failure at the core transition and ensemble UQ — is fully conveyed in text).*

### [High-Dimensional Learning Dynamics of Attention-Indexed Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03858v1)

Yizhou Xu, Margarita Sagitova, Lenka Zdeborová et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: stat.ML

The authors introduce "attention-indexed models," a framework representing multi-layer/multi-head attention, and solve its high-dimensional learning dynamics. In a suitable limit the population loss is characterized by a finite set of trace order parameters, while online SGD follows an infinite hierarchy of matrix moments that they show can be exponentially well approximated by a finite truncated system. The analysis reveals that attention parameterization is itself an implicit bias: directly optimizing $S\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ can stay trapped in an uninformative state, whereas tied attention ($S=WW^\top$) induces automatic symmetry breaking with weak recovery in $\Theta(d^2\log d)$ samples, and untied attention exhibits a fast–slow mechanism governing when symmetry breaks.

A §6 deep-learning-theory / transformer-mechanics paper (from a group known for exact ML dynamics): it gives principled insight into how attention actually learns and why parameterization matters — the kind of foundational understanding that informs training and interpretability of the quasar-transformer, with no astronomy hook required.

*No figures extracted for 2609.03858 (theory paper; results are analytic scaling laws and phase behavior).*

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Universal CMB Phase Coherence from Single-Field Inflation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03012v1)

Siméon Vareilles, Thomas Colas, Julien Grain et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc, hep-th

Phase coherence of primordial perturbations shapes the CMB acoustic peaks and is often treated as a discriminating signature of inflation, with the expectation that inflationary scenarios at different energy scales (hence different durations) leave different coherence. This short letter argues that expectation is wrong: because the curvature perturbation is conserved on super-Hubble scales, the coherence at recombination is fixed by the observed primordial spectrum alone, independent of the inflationary energy scale and the subsequent expansion history.

Outside the primary program but a genuinely clarifying negative result: it removes phase coherence as a lever on the inflationary energy scale, which is worth knowing when reasoning about what CMB acoustic-peak structure can and cannot constrain.

*No figures extracted for 2609.03012 (concise conceptual letter; the argument is analytic).*

### [Beyond isolated curvature peaks: collective collapse and multiple Primordial Black Hole formation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03051v1)

Albert Escrivà
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc, hep-ph

Primordial-black-hole (PBH) formation is usually modelled peak-by-peak, one collapsing region at a time. Using fully nonlinear $3+1$ numerical-relativity simulations in a radiation-dominated universe, this work shows neighbouring curvature perturbations need not map one-to-one onto PBHs: they can disperse, collapse collectively into a single PBH, or collapse locally into two (identified by two disconnected apparent horizons on a time slice). The author introduces a nonspherical quasi-local compaction diagnostic based on the Hawking mass, with a formation threshold $\mathcal{K}_{\rm form,c}\approx0.56$, and shows the maximum curvature amplitude alone does not determine the outcome — spatial extent, characteristic scales, and geometry of the surrounding curvature field matter.

A surprising, methodologically clean result relevant to the dark-matter landscape: PBH abundance calculations that assume isolated peaks may misestimate outcomes, which matters for any PBH-as-dark-matter accounting.

*No figures extracted for 2609.03051 (numerical-relativity snapshots and horizon diagnostics are technical; the headline — collective vs. multiple collapse and a compaction threshold — is stated in text).*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [The Impact of Artificial Space Objects on Optical Astronomy as Determined from 32 Million Photometric Observations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02951v1)

Anthony Mallama, Sergey Karpov, Richard E. Cole
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

From 32 million photometric observations, the authors characterize the sky density of satellites, rockets, and debris brighter than the IAU interference thresholds (mag 6/7/8), finding densities of 0.38/0.76/1.10 ×10⁻³ objects per square degree. They translate these into contamination probabilities: for objects brighter than mag 7, a 10-second exposure over a 10°-square field has a 15% chance of a streak, and 59% of such objects are naked-eye visible under dark skies. Extrapolating operators' plans for ~10⁶ spacecraft, they warn that nearly every wide, few-second astronomical image could be contaminated during several hours of each night.

Meta-research on the practice of the field: a quantitative, IAU-reviewed assessment of how satellite megaconstellations are degrading ground-based optical astronomy — directly relevant to the observational infrastructure (Rubin/LSST, wide-field surveys) that feeds survey cosmology.

*No figures extracted for 2609.02951 (source is PDF-only; the quantitative densities and probabilities are summarized in text).*
