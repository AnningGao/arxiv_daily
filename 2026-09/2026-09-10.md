## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [A Finely-Binned Measurement of the Connected Even-Parity Galaxy 4-Point Correlation Function of DESI Year 1 Luminous Red Galaxies](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09340v1)

William Ortolá Leonard, Alex Krolewski, Zachary Slepian et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors present one of the first on-data measurements of the *connected* even-parity galaxy 4-point correlation function (4PCF), using DESI Year 1 Luminous Red Galaxies in 18 finely-spaced radial bins from 20 to 160 h⁻¹Mpc. They report a clear detection at ~12–17σ in the full-sky analysis, robustly tested against hemisphere splits, redshift-range choices, and completeness cuts, and validated with EZmocks and Abacus mocks. A cross-correlation of spatially well-separated NGC and SGC patches — which have largely independent noise — still yields ~15σ evidence and, at leading order, removes mismatch between the data and mock covariance matrices. The measurement is framed as the groundwork for fitting a 4PCF model to constrain cosmological parameters, galaxy bias, and BAO features.

This is core DESI large-scale-structure working material (focus #3): a new, finely-binned higher-order-statistic estimator on the flagship survey, exactly the kind of non-Gaussian-information and covariance-robustness tool that feeds the same estimator/systematics concerns as the forest P1D/P3D program.

*No figures extracted for 2609.09340 (the headline is the ~12–17σ detection significance stated above; the paper's 4PCF and T² plots are dense multi-panel mode-by-mode comparisons that add little at a glance).*

### [Inductive Biases in Field-Level Cosmological Inference from Galaxy Catalogs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09504v1)

James O. Baldwin, Shy Genel, Francisco Villaescusa-Navarro (high-value co-author per interest file: Villaescusa-Navarro)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: cs.LG

Using CAMELS hydrodynamic simulations, the authors perform field-level likelihood-free inference of Ω_m from simulated galaxy catalogs and systematically vary the model's inductive bias, comparing permutation-invariant Deep Sets (built on MLPs or Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks) against graph neural networks that explicitly encode spatial relations. They find that line-of-sight peculiar velocities carry the dominant Ω_m information for set-based models (~18% error in-distribution, ~25% out-of-distribution), while adding positions helps only architectures that encode galaxy–galaxy relations: GNNs reach ~10% in-distribution and 10–17% OOD. The same set-based approach fails to constrain σ₈ at all, and KANs and MLPs perform comparably as Deep Sets backbones. They are explicit that the velocity inputs are exact simulated values, so survey application will require validation under realistic velocity noise, selection, and geometry.

A clean, controlled study of how architecture and observable choice govern information extraction in simulation-based inference for cosmology — squarely on the §6 SBI/representation thread and the LSS focus, and a direct methodological cousin of deploying the same neural-posterior machinery on forest/LSS simulations.

*No figures extracted for 2609.09504 (the contribution is the architecture-vs-observable error comparison summarized above; the figures are per-suite scatter and coverage panels that need the text to read).*

### [Learning cosmic web environments with diffusion models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09950v1)

Mehdi Noor, Tony Bonnaire, Nabila Aghanim et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors train a diffusion model on the Quijote N-body suite and ask what semantic structure its self-attention maps encode, using Dice-coefficient overlap and cross-power spectra to compare attention maps against cosmic-web environments (voids, walls, filaments, nodes) defined by the T-Web classifier. They find that attention maps at different spatial resolutions across layers capture overdense and underdense structures in distinct ways, with strong positive and negative correlations to the matter distribution and to individual cosmic-web classes. The model predominantly encodes cosmological information on intermediate-to-large scales, indicating attention primarily captures globally coherent structure. Beyond reproducing two-point statistics, the diffusion model is shown to learn a multi-scale, non-Gaussian representation of the cosmic web through self-attention.

A rare two-for-one on the §6 method threads — generative diffusion modelling *and* attention/mechanistic interpretability — applied directly to cosmological fields; this is exactly the "interpretability of scientific foundation/generative models" direction flagged as an expansion candidate, and the attention-vs-structure decoding transfers conceptually to interpreting a quasar-spectrum transformer.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [JZ-FMM: GPU-native differentiable N-body simulations with the Fast Multipole Method](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09307v1)

Jens Stücker, Oskar Foldal
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

The authors present JZ-FMM, a GPU-native, differentiable implementation of the fast multipole method for N-body simulations, with a GPU-oriented tree structure, dual-tree traversal, and multipole-translation operators that improve performance over established CPU and hybrid codes by more than an order of magnitude. Crucially, gradients can be evaluated efficiently and accurately, positioning the code as a building block for field-level reconstruction where small-scale forces matter. They demonstrate it on reconstructing the initial conditions of a tidally stripped satellite, showing that deeply non-linear problems can be solved with simulation gradients — though care is needed to navigate the complicated optimization landscape.

Differentiable simulation with accurate small-scale forces is the enabling infrastructure for field-level and gradient-based inference (focus #3 techniques/simulations); the same machinery underlies differentiable forward models one would want for forest/LSS reconstruction.

*No figures extracted for 2609.09307 (the contribution is the >10× GPU speedup and differentiability, both fully conveyed in text; figures are performance-scaling and reconstruction-landscape plots).*

### [From quantum fluctuations to galaxy power spectrum multipoles](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09272v1)

Nhat-Minh Nguyen
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

These are self-contained lecture notes tracing large-scale structure from primordial curvature perturbations to galaxy power-spectrum multipoles. Three core lectures develop the linear matter power spectrum, spherical and anisotropic collapse, galaxy bias, redshift-space distortions, the Kaiser model, and multipole estimators with Gaussian covariance; an extension develops nonlinear bias and the one-loop effective field theory model used in full-shape analyses. Derivations are explicit, with appendices collecting longer calculations and solutions. The core assumes undergraduate cosmology while the extension assumes perturbation-theory familiarity.

Not new research, but a genuinely useful, explicit reference on the one-loop EFT full-shape machinery that sits directly beside the EFT-of-the-forest and full-shape threads (focus #1) — handy for onboarding or for checking derivations of the multipole/estimator formalism.

*No figures extracted for 2609.09272 (a pedagogical derivation document; there is no single result plot that stands alone).*

### [SHELLQs: Black Hole Mass and Eddington Ratio Distributions of Intermediate-Luminosity Quasars at 6<z<7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09266v1)

Masafusa Onoue, John D. Silverman, Yoshiki Matsuoka et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using JWST/NIRSpec and Subaru/MOIRCS near-infrared spectroscopy of 21 quasars at 6.07 < z < 6.90 with −25 < M₁₄₅₀ < −22, the authors measure broad Balmer lines and MgII to fill the intermediate-luminosity gap between luminous quasars and the faint JWST AGN. They derive virial black hole masses spanning 7.2 < log M_BH/M_⊙ < 9.4 and Eddington ratios −1.3 < log L_bol/L_Edd < 0.4, and, combining with earlier SHELLQs measurements, build a 27-quasar sample to derive the M_BH and Eddington-ratio distributions at 6 < z < 7. Relative to luminosity-matched z ~ 1.3 quasars they find median offsets of Δlog M_BH = −0.4 and Δlog(L/L_Edd) = +0.2, with 11–22% of the high-z sample accreting near or above Eddington. The results point to a representative population undergoing rapid but not extreme accretion, consistent with anti-hierarchical SMBH growth.

Survey-scale quasar demographics and black-hole-mass/Eddington-ratio distributions (focus #4) — the kind of DESI/SDSS-adjacent quasar-population work that contextualizes the objects behind the forest and constrains the high-z quasar samples used as background sources.

*No figures extracted for 2609.09266 (the mass and Eddington-ratio distributions and the z~1.3 offsets are the headline numbers, fully stated above).*

### [Searching for Type Ia Supernovae in the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09661v1)

Xiaoyu Zhuang, Song Ran, Dezheng Meng et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE

The authors mine 1,757,303 DESI DR1 galaxy spectra for supernovae by combining Principal Component Analysis, the Local Outlier Factor algorithm, and the SNID classification tool, recovering 247 Type Ia and 17 other supernovae. Of these, 202 have no Transient Name Server record and represent newly identified SNe found as spectral outliers superimposed on host-galaxy spectra. The work demonstrates that archival multi-object spectroscopic surveys can supplement supernova samples, particularly for transients missed by photometric surveys. The pipeline is a straightforward PCA + outlier-detection + template-matching stack applied at survey scale.

A borrowable spectral-outlier/anomaly-detection recipe on the top-priority DESI dataset (focus #3 surveys, with a methods flavour) — the same PCA-plus-outlier machinery is directly relevant to mining DESI/SDSS spectra for contaminants and rare objects in the quasar/forest samples.

*No figures extracted for 2609.09661 (the result is the 264-SN yield including 202 new detections; the figures are PCA/LOF diagnostics and example spectra).*

### [RIGEL: Ultra-faint dwarf galaxy diversity shaped by inhomogeneous cosmic reionization](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09267v1)

Yunwei Deng, Hui Li, Ewald Puchwein et al. (high-value co-author per interest file: Puchwein)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using eight radiation-magnetohydrodynamic zoom-in simulations of isolated ultra-faint dwarfs (present-day halo mass ~10⁹ M_⊙) coupled to realistic large-scale radiation fields extracted from the THESAN reionization simulation, the authors study how inhomogeneous reionization quenches star formation. Despite similar z=0 halos, the galaxies span nearly two orders of magnitude in stellar mass and reproduce observed Local Group UFD luminosities, sizes, metallicities, and kinematics. Quenching proceeds in two stages: the arrival of the ionization front photoionizes diffuse circumgalactic/intergalactic gas and suppresses accretion, after which star formation continues for several hundred Myr on self-shielded gas until it is consumed or dispersed, with photoevaporation the dominant loss channel. The halo mass *at the time of reionization* emerges as the key parameter setting final stellar mass, age spread, and chemical evolution.

Reionization astrophysics from the galaxy side (focus #2): it couples a realistic patchy-reionization radiation field to galaxy formation, and the emphasis on photoheating, self-shielding, and the local timing of the ionization front is the same physics that governs the IGM opacity and thermal relics the forest program cares about.

*No figures extracted for 2609.09267 (the two-stage quenching mechanism and the halo-mass-at-reionization dependence are the takeaways, captured in text; figures are simulation snapshots and SFH/mass-relation panels).*

### [A Dominant Diffuse Phase in the Sparse Autoencoder Phase Diagram](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10299v1)

Alexis D. Plascencia
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are widely used to recover interpretable features from neural-network activations, but systematic feature co-occurrence can cause features to be absorbed or merged. Following the MAIS-O43 open-problem protocol, the author varies the nesting fraction γ, sparsity penalty λ, and dictionary size M, running 200 independently-initialized fits across ten grid cells (and 3,300 more with minibatch Adam over the full 165-cell grid). The striking result is a reproducible "diffuse phase": reconstruction is nearly perfect, yet zero runs recover the full synthetic dictionary and zero runs show the predicted feature merging — learned atoms typically sit far from the true features (median best cosine 0.5–0.7 versus a 0.95 criterion) and learned codes are an order of magnitude denser than ground truth. Since the exact sparse-coding optimum is known to merge nested features, this suggests trained SAEs simply do not reach those minima, and the phase diagram of trained models differs fundamentally from that of objective minimizers.

Directly on the §6 sparse-autoencoder/mechanistic-interpretability thread and a cautionary result for the QGPT interpretability project: it warns that SAE feature-recovery on real (quasar-spectrum) activations may land in a diffuse regime where atoms neither cleanly recover nor cleanly merge the true features.

*No figures extracted for 2609.10299 (the zero-recovery/diffuse-phase result and the 0.5–0.7 median cosine are the headline; figures are convergence curves and cosine histograms).*

### [Mode Coverage in Normalizing Flow Boltzmann Generators via Log-Ratio Variation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09473v1)

Qi Feng, Rongjie Lai, Di Qi et al.
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

Normalizing-flow Boltzmann generators keep a tractable pushforward density, but training with forward KL depends on target samples that may be biased or miss modes, so a flow can drop target mass while its importance weights still report a high effective sample size. The authors introduce the log-ratio variation — the mean absolute pairwise difference of the target-to-pushforward log-density ratio — and use it to build a new loss, KLXX, adding two such variations to forward KL (one weighted by the target for accuracy, one by a quench/temper/pushforward mixture to search for missing modes). They derive its Fisher–Rao gradient flow (both variations contribute non-positive dissipation), a fixed-surrogate error bound, and prove asymptotic unbiasedness of the associated adaptive-staging sampler. Numerically, KLXX improves mode coverage over forward KL and recovers observables close to independent references.

Squarely on the §6 normalizing-flows/generative-sampling thread: mode-collapse-robust flow training with importance-reweighting diagnostics is exactly the toolkit for flow/diffusion-based emulation and simulation-based inference where missing a mode silently biases the posterior.

*No figures extracted for 2609.09473 (the contribution is the KLXX loss and its coverage/unbiasedness guarantees, summarized above; figures are mode-coverage diagnostics on benchmark Boltzmann targets).*

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [The Ashes of Supermassive Stars: Globular Cluster-like Aluminum Enhancement in Little Red Dots](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09271v1)

V. Kokorev, J. Chisholm, R. P. Naidu et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.SR

Using deep JWST spectroscopy from the SPURS program, the authors report that the gas around Little Red Dots is magnesium-depleted and aluminum-enhanced at only ~1% solar metallicity — the same abundance anomaly seen in some globular-cluster stars, whose polluting engine has never been identified. They argue this pattern cannot be produced by ordinary massive stars or mimicked by ionization, geometry, or dust, but is reproduced by hot hydrogen burning in fully convective supermassive stars of ≥10,000 M_⊙ (~100× any present-day star). If correct, LRDs would simultaneously reveal the long-sought source of globular-cluster abundance anomalies and a formation pathway for massive black-hole seeds via direct collapse.

Well outside the forest program, but a genuinely surprising, high-bar result: it ties together two open puzzles (globular-cluster chemical anomalies and heavy black-hole seed formation) through a single exotic stellar engine, and would reframe how the earliest luminous sources of reionization are understood.

*No figures extracted for 2609.09271 (the Mg-depleted/Al-enhanced signature and the supermassive-star interpretation are fully described in text; the key figure is an abundance-pattern model fit).*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Enabling New Discoveries with Machine Learning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10109v1)

Michelle Lochner
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.GA

This review asks whether machine learning can automate scientific discovery itself, not just data reduction, in the era of the Square Kilometre Array and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory. The author argues that automated discovery becomes feasible only when ML is combined with expert human input through active learning, and surveys discoveries made with Astronomaly, a publicly available framework for active anomaly detection that recommends interesting sources to a scientist. The paper reviews recent deep-learning-based anomaly-detection approaches and emphasizes the central role of the cyber-human interface in surfacing rare and new classes of objects in massive datasets.

Meta-research on how ML is changing the practice of astronomy — specifically the human-in-the-loop discovery workflow — which speaks directly to how the field will mine next-generation survey data, and complements the anomaly-detection/active-learning angle relevant to finding rare objects in DESI-scale spectroscopy.

*No figures extracted for 2609.10109 (a perspective/review on ML-driven discovery; its figures are example Astronomaly discovery vignettes rather than a single standalone result).*
