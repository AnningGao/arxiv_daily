## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Field-Level Baryon Acoustic Oscillation Reconstruction of the DESI DR1 Luminous Red Galaxies with Linear Field Transformer (LiFT)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14862)

Liam Parker, Uroš Seljak, Adrian E. Bayer et al. (high-value author: Seljak)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

The authors present the first application of neural field-level BAO reconstruction to real spectroscopic survey data. LiFT is a 3D vision transformer that ingests the observed galaxy field, its standard (RecSym) reconstruction, and context channels (line of sight, survey coverage, redshift), and learns local non-linear corrections toward the linear density field; it is trained on forward-modeled DESI DR1 LRG lightcones and validated on held-out mocks that vary gravity solver, halo finder, HOD, cosmology, and fiber-assignment history. Applied to DESI DR1 LRGs it tightens α_iso by 13/24/30% and α_AP by 5/22/30% across the three LRG bins relative to the standard DR1 reconstruction, equivalent to a 1.2–2.0× Figure-of-Merit (effective-volume) gain, with central values consistent with DR1/DR2.

### [MUSHY: Multimodal Flow-Based Amortized Bayesian Inference for Spectroscopic Data Fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15945)

Jeff Shen
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

MUSHY is an amortized Bayesian inference method that fuses heterogeneous spectroscopy from different surveys (APOGEE, GALAH, DESI) to infer stellar physical parameters, using a rectified-flow model with multimodal inputs to learn the posterior. Trained on synthetic ATLAS12/SYNTHE spectra with realistic instrumental effects and noise, it produces well-calibrated posteriors from any combination of surveys, and multimodal inputs consistently tighten constraints beyond any single survey alone. The held-out information-gain and calibration tests show the multimodal posterior always beats single-survey inference while staying calibrated.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [NERV: Neural-network Enhanced Reconstruction of the UniVerse with Application to Baryon Acoustic Oscillations in the BOSS DR12 Galaxy Sample](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15943)

Shi-Hui Zang, Hong-Ming Zhu, Tian-Xiang Mao et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

NERV augments standard BAO reconstruction with a convolutional neural network trained on cubic N-body simulations, explicitly handling curved-sky geometry, the redshift-dependent selection function, and finite survey boundaries by tessellating the survey volume into local patches. Validated on MultiDark-Patchy mocks with unbiased BAO dilation parameters, it is then applied to the real BOSS DR12 galaxy sample, where it significantly improves the precision of the BAO distance measurements. The authors position it as a practical component for ongoing surveys such as DESI.

### [Toward Precision Kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich Cosmology — 1. The Matter–Momentum Bispectrum at One-Loop Order](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15750)

Ryan Raikman, Jamie Sullivan, Mikhail M. Ivanov (high-value author: Ivanov)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors reformulate kSZ extraction as a measurement of the one-loop galaxy–galaxy–electron-momentum bispectrum and compute a pure-dark-matter version in the Effective Field Theory framework, comparing to simulations. They derive the EFT counterterms that capture small-scale backreaction, finding that two acquire a transverse direction (one sourced entirely by velocity-field vorticity), and make the first detection of these transverse counterterms from simulations. The one-loop EFT bispectrum dipole matches simulations to 5% up to k_max ≈ 0.23 (projected momentum field similar), far beyond tree-level's k_max ≈ 0.07 at z = 0.5.

### [Nitrogen-Loud Quasars from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument. II. Broad-Line Region Metallicity and Relative Nitrogen Enrichment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14460)

Shuo Zhai, Wei-jian Guo, Yong-Jie Chen et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Analyzing 121 nitrogen-loud quasars at 2.13 ≤ z ≤ 3.90 from DESI DR1 against a control sample matched in redshift, luminosity, and virial black-hole mass, the authors measure broad-line-region metallicities from several diagnostics. N V/C IV implies ~3–50 Z_⊙ and runs systematically higher than the nitrogen-independent (Si IV+O IV])/C IV and Al III/C IV diagnostics (~1–20 Z_⊙, which agree with each other). The widening N V/C IV vs. (Si IV+O IV])/C IV gap toward the high-metallicity end indicates genuine nitrogen enrichment on top of overall metal enrichment, partially decoupled from total metallicity.

### [The Multiphase CGM in the Epoch of Reionization: CII and CIV absorbers around [OIII] Emitters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15547)

C. Piscitelli, V. D'Odorico, M. Galbiati et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Cross-correlating cool (C II) and warm-ionized (C IV) absorbers with star-forming [O III] emitters, the authors combine JWST/NIRCam slitless spectroscopy (EIGER) with medium/high-resolution optical–NIR spectra of six background quasars (including new VLT/X-Shooter data). They find a statistically significant excess of both ions around galaxies, with C IV covering fractions enhanced out to ~1 pMpc while C II drops to background beyond ~0.5 pMpc, showing the warm-ionized phase is more extended than the cool gas. The weak galaxy–absorber clustering relative to the galaxy auto-correlation argues that early metals permeate the diffuse IGM or come from widespread faint dwarfs, with an evolving CGM ionization structure toward high z.

### [Using the Circumgalactic Medium of Dwarf Galaxies as a Calorimetric Dark Matter Detector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15748)

Abby Mintz, Digvijay Wadekar, Romain Teyssier
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

Many DM scenarios (dark-photon DM, axion-like particles, sterile neutrinos, primordial black holes) can deposit energy into ordinary gas, and the authors implement a model-independent DM-heating term (an effective local rate Γ_heat) in the RAMSES code. In simulations of an isolated WLM-like gas-rich dwarf, DM heating strongly alters the circumgalactic medium's thermal structure while leaving the star-formation rate and dense ISM nearly unchanged: for Γ_heat = 10⁻²⁷–10⁻²⁵ s⁻¹ the neutral-hydrogen column at 10 kpc drops by 0.6–3.9 dex and C II/Si II columns fall by 4–5.5 dex. These signatures are accessible to quasar absorption-line spectroscopy of nearby field dwarfs.

### [Less isn't more: Cosmological bounds on the neutrino masses are robust to changes in the neutrino abundance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15970)

Sebastian J. Figueroa, Alexander C. Sobotka, Adrienne L. Erickcek
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

The authors test whether the cosmological Σm_ν bound depends on the assumed thermal history, since post-decoupling photon injection would lower the neutrino abundance inferred from the CMB temperature. Using MCMC with Planck primary CMB, Planck+ACT lensing, and DESI BAO, they find the degenerate-ordering 95% limit moves only slightly — from Σm_ν < 0.0691 eV (standard) to 0.0652 eV (decay into photons) or 0.0710 eV (with added dark radiation), and 0.0724 eV under model-agnostic changes to the neutrino-to-photon ratio. Because neutrino temperature and Σm_ν are positively correlated, lowering the pre-recombination radiation density only worsens the tension between cosmological mass bounds and the measured mass splittings.

### [Sharp Rates and a One-Line Correction for Spectral Representation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15825)

Dier Tang, Jing Yee Tan, Guangyue Han
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: stat.ML

Canonical correlation analysis, HGR maximal correlation, and the population optimum of the spectral contrastive loss all return the top-k singular subspace of a cross-view dependence operator, justified by an isotropy assumption on the task prior. The authors show isotropy is the wrong hypothesis: transfer risk enters only through the task covariance compressed onto the operator's leading singular directions, so what matters is alignment between the prior's preferred directions and the operator spectrum, not symmetry. They prove matching two-sided rates (worst-case regret exactly 1 − 1/κ(Λ), refining under a spectral gap), show alignment is generic when tasks are diverse, and give an O(kd²) diagnostic plus a one-line positive-pair reweighting that restores optimality (regret 0.86 → 0.003 on controlled data).

### [The Misery of Mechanistic Interpretability: A Formal Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15533)

Tobias Ladner, Matthias Althoff
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.AI

Mechanistic interpretability increasingly relies on interpretable replacement networks (IRNs) that expose features through sparsely activated neurons, but their faithfulness is usually only checked empirically on clean data. The authors show that semantically minor input perturbations flip the dominant IRN features — and thus the human interpretation — across five open-weight model families (GPT-2 small, Gemma 2/3, Llama 3.2, R1-Distill-Qwen). They propose the first formal verification framework for IRN faithfulness, using reachability analysis to certify a sound upper bound on the adversarial faithfulness gap, and show verification-aware training substantially tightens that certified bound.

### [Quenched Ensemble Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15894)

David Yallup
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG, stat.CO

Sampling near phase transitions is hard because the density of states changes abruptly; nested sampling is robust to this but limited in high dimension by the difficulty of sampling under a hard energy constraint. Quenched Ensemble Sampling generalizes the hard constraint to a family of repulsive boundary potentials, preserving the monotonically-decreasing-energy quenched path while making the constrained target amenable to scalable gradient-based kernels. On synthetic phase-transition models it estimates the marginal likelihood and draws posterior samples across a first-order transition where tempering fails, and it is applied to Bayesian-neural-network model comparison and to partition-function estimation in a high-dimensional lattice field theory.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Astrophysics in the Era of Artificial Intelligence powered by Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13301)

A. R. Rao
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

Responding to a white paper by Hogg on how AI powered by large language models is used to do astrophysics, Rao examines the field's future under two deliberately extreme assumptions: that astrophysicists have become preoccupied with the administrative act of publication over physical discovery, and that AI can synthesize literature and generate manuscripts but cannot perform original astrophysical inquiry. He argues these assumptions, if granted, imply a positive outcome — situating AI as an agent of publication rather than discovery frees the human researcher to refocus on fundamental astrophysics. The piece is a perspective on the division of labor between AI and astronomers.
