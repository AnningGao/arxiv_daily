## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [A consistent, physical, and analytical model for CMB observables of reionisation I. Derivation, constraining power, and detectability of the auto-spectra](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30766v1)

Adélie Gorce
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Presents a fast, physically-parameterised analytical model for the three main CMB imprints of the Epoch of Reionisation — spatial fluctuations of the Thomson optical depth ($C_\ell^{\tau\tau}$), the patchy kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect, and the scattering/screening $B$-modes — all derived from a common set of physical reionisation parameters (typical ionised-bubble size, duration of reionisation) and calibrated against high-resolution hydrodynamical simulations. Unlike earlier analytical treatments, the shared physical basis lets the three observables be modelled and analysed jointly rather than piecemeal. The paper shows $C_\ell^{\tau\tau}$ and $C_\ell^{BB}$ are within reach of CMB-S4-like and CMB-HD-like experiments respectively, and quantifies how the three probes complement each other in constraining reionisation history *and* morphology. The code is released as the `preion` Python package.

### [Super-Eddington Little Blue Dots May Reionize Helium Too Early](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28745v1)

Christopher Cain, Brent Smith, Gibson B. Bowling et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

Tests the super-Eddington-accretion scenario proposed for JWST "Little Blue Dots" — faint $z\gtrsim4$ broad-line AGN argued to be the same population as Little Red Dots seen at a different viewing angle — against the observed timing of He II reionization. Super-Eddington accretion produces hard extreme-UV and weak X-ray emission, which would doubly-ionise helium too early; requiring He II reionization to end no earlier than $z\approx2.8$ disfavours scenarios in which most $3\lesssim z\lesssim7$ LBDs are highly super-Eddington. For the fiducial SED, at most ~10% of the population can be in that state unless escape fractions are ≲1.5% or LBDs are less abundant than observed. The conclusion is explicitly sensitive to the assumed EUV spectral shape of super-Eddington black holes.

### [DINOspec: Efficient Multimodal Alignment of Vision and Spectral Foundation Models for Astronomy](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30503v1)

Erica Lastufka, Mariia Drozdova, Daniel Schaerer et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

Introduces DINOspec, which aligns a frozen DINOv3 image encoder with a pre-trained AION-1 spectral tokenizer using only lightweight adapters and contrastive learning on ~20k paired galaxy images and spectra — composing two independently-trained foundation models without retraining either encoder. Training at most 21M parameters, it improves galaxy morphology classification (F1 0.72→0.78) and spectral classification (0.70→0.74), while spectroscopic redshift prediction is unchanged (R²≈0.9). The asymmetric transfer between separately-learned representations is itself the interesting result: alignment helps some downstream tasks and not others.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [SPT-3G D1: Quadratic-Estimator CMB Lensing Reconstruction and Cosmology](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31136v1)

Y. Omori, W. L. K. Wu, Y. Nakato et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Reconstructs the CMB lensing potential from the 1500 deg² SPT-3G Main field (2019–2020) using a quadratic estimator that jointly accounts for $T$, $E$ and $B$ and their covariance, yielding the highest signal-to-noise-per-mode lensing measurement reported to date (polarisation-dominated for $L\lesssim600$). From SPT-3G lensing alone they measure $\sigma_8\Omega_{\rm m}^{0.25}=0.6046\pm0.0096$, tightening to $0.6020\pm0.0084$ with ACT DR6 and Planck PR4. Combining with primary CMB and DESI DR2 BAO gives $\sum m_\nu<0.072$ eV (95% C.L.); the improved consistency with DESI DR2 both relaxes that bound and reduces the previous ~2σ preferences for nonzero curvature and for $(w_0,w_a)$ departures from ΛCDM. With DES Y3 3×2pt they obtain $S_8=0.811\pm0.011$.

### [Radio-dust connection in quasars driven by powerful ionised outflows](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28775v1)

V. A. Fawcett, C. M. Harrison, T. Costa et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Uses DESI optical spectra of 3418 quasars at $0.5<z<0.9$, fit with PyQSOFit, to test whether ionised outflows drive the reported link between dust reddening and radio emission in quasars. Comparing [O III] λ5007 outflow velocity against line-of-sight extinction $E(B-V)$, optical-to-mid-IR colour $g-W2$, and the MIR-excess proxy $L_{6\mu\rm m}/L_{5100\rm \AA}$, and adding LOFAR LoTSS DR2 radio data, they find the radio-dust connection is driven by the sources hosting high-velocity outflows. Crucially there is *no* significant trend of outflow velocity with $E(B-V)$, but a clear positive trend with $g-W2$ that survives luminosity matching (and holds even at $E(B-V)=0$), plus a positive trend with the MIR excess. They argue outflows shock the surrounding dust and gas, heating the dust and boosting both MIR and radio emission.

### [Measuring peculiar velocity and tomographic redshift dipole with DESI DR1 catalogs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30914v1)

Yi-Wen Wu, Jun-Qing Xia
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Measures the kinematic dipole from DESI DR1 using the redshift-dipole method, which exploits the Doppler modulation of observed redshifts and is intrinsically less sensitive to the imaging systematics and selection-function errors that bias number-count dipoles. A tomographic analysis across four tracers (BGS, LRG, ELG, QSO) over $0.1<z<2.1$, with geometry and errors from 1000 EZmock realisations, gives $v=358^{+55}_{-48}$ km/s from the high-$z$ quasar sample — in excellent agreement with the CMB value $369.82\pm0.11$ km/s. The complementary number-count analysis instead yields an anomalously large dipole, attributed to large-scale power leakage and DR1 footprint incompleteness.

### [Learning Radio Astronomical Representations with LeJEPA and Very Small Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30594v1)

Erica Lastufka, Mariia Drozdova, Vitaliy Kinakh et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

Asks whether self-supervised pretraining directly on domain data can let very small vision models (~6M parameters) match large foundation models transferred from natural images. Pretraining on Radio Galaxy Zoo images with LeJEPA and evaluating morphology classification across three datasets, the small domain-specific model matches a substantially larger foundation model while producing representations that stay more consistent between training and evaluation datasets. The authors conclude that the choice of self-supervised objective — not parameter count — is what lets small domain models compete.

### [Beat-Synchronous Tokenization for ECG Transformers](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30367v1)

Ahmed Sameh, Nolan Wilson, Max Enderlein et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: eess.SP

Studies how to tokenize a continuous physiological waveform for a transformer, comparing conventional fixed temporal patches against three morphology-aligned schemes in which token boundaries follow cardiac cycles (resampled beats, adaptive pooled beats, and resampled beats augmented with R–R interval timing). Evaluation spans 12-lead diagnostic classification on PTB-XL after masked pretraining on MIMIC-IV-ECG, and single-lead rhythm classification on Icentia11k after patient-level contrastive pretraining. Resampled beat tokens give the best mean macro AUROC (0.8945) and nearly match the best fixed-patch AUPRC, while cutting average sequence length from 100 to 11.2 tokens; on the second dataset beat-synchronous tokenizers match fixed patching with better run-to-run stability.

### [The Illusion of Replacement: Rethinking Specialized Machine Learning Models in the Foundation Model Era](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28980v1)

Kiyan Rezaee
**Primary category:** cs.CL | also: cs.LG

A review of 159 papers (2016–2026) across nine data modalities asking whether specialized architectures for structured data can be replaced by language-based models. It separates *performing* a task from *preserving and computing* the structure that makes the task tractable, and organizes existing work into eight representational regimes from language-only to fully specialized. Language-mediated models are found highly competitive in extreme few-shot prediction, discretized symbolic tasks, textually annotated knowledge graphs, and large-scale single-modality pretraining — but whenever structural representation or computation is evaluated directly rather than by accuracy alone, no evidence of general architectural replacement appears. The recurring pattern is that missing structure gets reintroduced via a graph module, structural tokens, or specialized attention: specialization relocates rather than disappears.

### [The information geometry of product-reference discrete diffusion: Interaction growth complexity and optimal scheduling](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28949v1)

Martin J. Wainwright
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG, math.ST

Analyses product-reference discrete diffusion samplers through a path-based measure of data geometry the author calls interaction growth complexity (IGC). A bivariate IGC kernel gives an exact representation of the KL discretization error along with a simple one-step upper bound, while the univariate IGC density characterises how step-size choices set the iteration complexity needed for ε-accurate samples. Samplers taking equi-spaced steps in log-squared-reliability-odds have complexity governed by the aggregate IGC mass, whereas refined step-size schedules achieve a lower square-root functional, with both characterisations becoming sharp in the fine-grid limit. Allowing general product reference distributions, the choice of reference substantially reshapes the IGC profile — references far from both the uniform and the data marginals can yield dimension-dependent improvements — and the aggregate IGC mass is bounded via total correlation and dual total correlation.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Agentic research is oxymoronic](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31161v1)

Natalie B. Hogg
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

A short polemical essay by a cosmology postdoc (Cambridge / Handley Research Group) arguing that using agentic LLMs to perform scientific *interpretation* — as distinct from constrained "grunt work" like debugging code, which the author does daily and finds genuinely faster — misunderstands what research is. Invoking the OECD Frascati definition, under which research requires human creativity and interpretation, she argues that computation can be delegated because its correctness is verifiable, whereas interpretation cannot, since there is no ground truth and questioning an interpretation is itself an act of interpretation. The result, she predicts, is a "hollow" literature and a process she names *deliteration*: the gradual erosion of trust in astronomy's literature as LLM-generated papers proliferate. The piece deliberately foregrounds early-career stakes.

### [Ad Astra White Paper: A Pitch for the Next 25 Years of NASA's Physics of the Cosmos Program](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31160v1)

Eric Burns, Ivan Agullo, Igor Andreoni et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.CO

A community white paper giving a quarter-century status update on the 2003 NRC report *Connecting Quarks with the Cosmos: Eleven Science Questions for the New Century*, assessing progress on each question and identifying which space-based facilities are needed for continued progress. It is written to guide NASA's Physics of the Cosmos program and its preparatory work for the Astro2030 Decadal Survey.
