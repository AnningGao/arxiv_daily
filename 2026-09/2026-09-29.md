## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Tide-and-Seek: Anisotropic scale-dependent bias in the Lyman-alpha forest and designer samples](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35713)

Yong Sheng Yap, William R. Coulton, Vid Iršič (Iršič is a high-value author)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Spin-2 (anisotropic) primordial non-Gaussianity, $f_{\rm NL}^{s=2}$, normally leaves no scale-dependent bias, because it only makes collapse thresholds anisotropic. The paper shows that any tracer whose selection breaks the Kaiser relation ($b_\eta \neq 1$) picks up a **$(\mu^2-1/3)\,f_{\rm NL}^{s=2} b_{\Phi,\eta}/\mathcal{M}(k)$ term, i.e. a $1/k^2$ signature in the multipoles, including the hexadecapole**, which late-Universe physics cannot mimic. The Lyα forest has $b_\eta \neq 1$ by construction: FGPA mocks from $1280^3$ pkdgrav3 runs at $z=2$ show **$b_{\Phi,\eta} = \mathcal{O}(1)$ and a clean $1/k^2$ response**, so the standard P3D/multipole analyses could be extended simply by adding one term. Large-scale continuum-fitting systematics currently limit $k_{\rm min}$. On BOSS DR12 samples selected by velocity dispersion (a tidal-field proxy), they find **no evidence for $f_{\rm NL}^{s=2}$**.

![](figures/2609.35713/p_1d_all.pdf)

*The fractional response of mock Lyα-forest power multipoles to $f_{\rm NL}^{s=2}=\pm300$ follows the predicted $1/k^2$ rise on large scales in the monopole, quadrupole and hexadecapole. Local-type non-Gaussianity would leave the hexadecapole flat. eBOSS error bars are shown for scale.*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Probing Spatial Variations in IGM Transmission: Higher Lyα Visibility in an Hα-Selected Galaxy Overdensity at z≃6.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35221)

Shunta Shimizu, Nobunari Kashikawa, Junya Arita et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

JWST/NIRCam F470N identifies a projected overdensity of Hα emitters at $z\simeq6.2$ in CEERS, which defines the environment independently of Lyα. Subaru NB872 imaging then measures Lyα for those galaxies. Both the LAE fraction (**0.41 inside vs 0.27 outside**) and the stacked Lyα escape fraction are higher within $2'$ of the density centre. Galaxies there are more massive and have redder UV slopes, which should *lower* Lyα escape. The authors therefore read the excess as **reduced IGM damping inside an ionized bubble around the overdensity**, the opposite sign to $z\sim2.5$ protoclusters. The significance is modest (21 HAEs, and filter-wing effects are not excluded). The authors themselves call for forest transmission along multiple sightlines as the direct test.

### [Can Fingers of God be Resummed?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35757)

Shi-Fan Chen, Alejandro Aviles
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The paper recasts FoG damping in terms of the **single-particle characteristic function (SPCF) of small-scale velocities, $W(\lambda|\mathbf{x})$ with $\lambda = k\mu$**. The usual damping-function models are its mean-field limit. The environmental dependence of the SPCF generates an expanded bias basis: at leading order it is exactly the selection-effect basis, and at higher order there are parity-odd operators obeying selection rules. The coefficients are free functions of $\lambda$. Halo-model estimates for DESI-like galaxies show these **response terms are as large as the mean-field correction itself** in both the power spectrum and the bispectrum. The answer to the title is **"potentially, but only by promoting coefficients to free functions of $k\mu$"**. The same anisotropic-bias structure is what the Lyα-forest EFT already carries.

### [Joint measurements of thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich and cosmic infrared background cross-correlations with cosmic shear](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31774)

Amy Wayland, Adrien La Posta, David Alonso et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The CIB is modelled with a physically calibrated main-sequence plus starburst SED (four parameters, anchored to external $U(z)$ radiation-field data). It is **marginalised at the level of multi-frequency cross-spectra rather than deprojected in the map**, for DES Y3 shear × Planck PR4. The recovered WL×tSZ bandpowers move by at most $0.19\sigma$ under full SED marginalisation and are stable against radio sources and against CIB SED evolution. Against FLAMINGO, the data **disagree with the fiducial D3A cosmology at 27σ (still 20σ for the strongest feedback) but match the low-$S_8$ run**. The preference comes from the $\ell<300$ modes, which are nearly immune to AGN feedback.

![](figures/2609.31774/FLAMINGO_SHEAR_TSZ.pdf)

*In every DES tomographic bin, the reconstructed WL×tSZ spectrum sits below all FLAMINGO fiducial-cosmology curves (red) whatever the feedback strength, and tracks the low-$S_8$ curves (blue). Cosmology, not feedback, drives the mismatch.*

### [Observational Evidence of Velocity Anisotropy Assembly Bias in Galaxy Clusters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31852)

Facundo Rodriguez, Andrea Biviano, Ravi K. Sheth et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Orbital anisotropy $\beta$ is measured by MAMPOSSt Jeans modelling for **91 eROSITA clusters with DESI member spectroscopy**, under three anisotropy-profile models. The sample is split into isotropic and radially anisotropic halves with matched $r_{200}$ distributions. In projected cross-correlations, **isotropic clusters cluster more strongly than radial-orbit-dominated ones**. This is the sign simulations predict for the secondary property most tightly tied to halo bias. The significance ranges from a mild tendency to a pronounced signal depending on the $\beta$ profile, so this is a first detection that larger DESI cluster samples will need to confirm.

### [Cool Embeddings: Predicting Galaxy Cluster Cooling Times in IllustrisTNG and TNG-Cluster with AstroCLIP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32582)

Rawan Karam, Carter Lee Rhea, Julie Hlavacek-Larrondo et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

**Frozen AstroCLIP embeddings, pretrained on optical DESI galaxy images and spectra, are applied unchanged to mock Chandra X-ray cluster images**, a change of both wavelength and physical scale. In t-SNE, the embedding separates clusters by central cooling time but not by halo mass. A dot-product kNN-attention head on the embeddings predicts $\log t_{\rm cool,0}$ to RMSE 0.23–0.24. Trained on TNG-Cluster and tested on TNG300, it reaches **RMSE 0.50 vs 0.73 for a CNN fine-tuned on the raw images**. The out-of-distribution test is weaker than it looks, because both sets share TNG physics, and the authors do not show which features the ViT is using.

### [Exploring the observed properties of JWST Little Red Dots with cosmological simulations and dust radiative transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34903)

Saksham Chandna, Fabio Di Mascia, Simona Gallerani et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Two zoom-in simulations of the same massive $z=6$–9 halo are compared, one with black-hole growth and AGN feedback and one without. They are post-processed with SKIRT over a grid of dust models and intrinsic AGN spectra, then run through the JWST LRD selection. The AGN run, though **not tuned for LRDs, naturally reproduces V-shaped continua, compactness, overmassive BHs, sub-Eddington accretion and Balmer breaks of ~1–2**. Spectral decomposition gives **a rest-UV dominated by stars and a rest-optical dominated by a dust-attenuated AGN**. On this reading, LRDs are not a separate class but a phase of massive AGN hosts set by dust geometry. Super-Eddington accretion or dust-free gas columns are still needed to cover the full observed diversity.

### [Accelerating massive ensembles of ordinary differential equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35661)

Lawrence Berry, Will Handley, Oliver Hahn et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.CO

modax is a JAX-compatible library of **thread-level CUDA ODE solvers for large ensembles of small (<200D) independent systems**. It targets the Monte-Carlo, BBN and Einstein–Boltzmann-mode workloads common in cosmological inference. Against diffrax, the explicit Tsit5 solver is **30× faster on Lorenz ensembles** and the implicit **Rodas5P is >700× faster on stiff Robertson systems**. The key design point is that linearly implicit Rosenbrock–Wanner methods remove Newton iterations and hence thread divergence on GPUs. End-to-end demos cover BBN parameter estimation, global-21cm uncertainty quantification and primordial power-spectrum modes. SDE and neural-ODE extensions are planned.

### [Prompting Particle Physics: Tokenized Multi-modal Foundation Models for Combinatorially Many Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31862)

Nilotpal Kakati, Daniel Murnane, Baran Hashemi et al.
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: cs.LG

Every object in a jet (tracks, calorimeter cells, topoclusters, particles, jets) is **tokenized with residual VQ-VAEs into one shared vocabulary**. A single encoder–decoder is trained over all combinations of up to three input and three output modalities, so particle flow, fast simulation and unfolding are just different prompts to the same weights. The authors report that **tokenisation made multi-task training stable with little tuning**. The autoregressive decoder beats a state-of-the-art particle-flow algorithm on jet resolution and is hardest for a classifier to tell apart from Geant4. The model also transfers zero-shot to an unseen four-modality input. The design lesson for continuous scientific data is explicit: **some iterative (autoregressive) decoding is needed to capture the multimodal posterior**, and models should be compared on fidelity-vs-compute Pareto fronts.

### [When Is an SAE Feature Interpretable? A Validation Ladder for EEG Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34091)

Yucong Cao, Chenqi Li, Tingting Zhu
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Sparse autoencoders are trained on three EEG foundation-model backbones, across three datasets and three depths (27 settings). Band-stop removal of alpha changes latent firing **7.3× more than an equal-width sham notch**, which suggests "alpha detectors". **After normalising by the spectral energy each filter removes, the ratio drops to 0.28× and exceeds 1 in none of the 27 settings**. On clean data, the selected latents are slightly anti-correlated with alpha power. The proposed ladder asks, in order, whether the latent responds, survives energy-matched controls, is specific, and shows the property on unperturbed data. The ladder applies directly to claims that a spectral-transformer latent "detects" an absorption feature after masking it.

### [JEPA Learns What the Mask Leaves Unrecoverable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32481)

Peng Xie, Amr Alanwar
**Primary category:** cs.CV | also: cs.LG

The paper treats a mask as a linear measurement and asks **how much target content no low-level (wavelet-scale) prior could fill in**. Strips and scattered masks fail because a smooth prior recovers them; blocks work because it cannot. Across 22 mask configurations (46 runs), this unrecoverable-content measure **ranks linear-probe accuracy at ρ = +0.84**. Good masks need two things: enough unrecoverable content and enough "reachable" context near the target. The masking ratio decides which of the two limits performance. For masked pretraining on spectra, where neighbouring forest pixels are strongly correlated, this is a concrete criterion for choosing mask geometry.

![](figures/2609.32481/fig_phase.pdf)

*Only configurations that clear both thresholds, unrecoverable content and reachability (shaded quadrant), reach the high linear-probe plateau. Failing either condition gives a distinct failure mode.*

### [First Learn, Then Memorize: The Spectral Bias of Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35377)

Raphaël Urfin, Tony Bonnaire, Giulio Biroli et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cond-mat.dis-nn

Score-matching dynamics are governed exactly by the **NTK Gram matrix on noisy training data**. The paper shows that using $m$ noised copies per sample splits its spectrum into two bulks. A large-eigenvalue bulk carries the global features and drives generalization. A small-eigenvalue bulk aligned with sample-specific noise directions drives memorization, and **the gap between them grows with training-set size $n$**. Closed-form bias–variance results are derived in the lazy regime at linear and polynomial sample complexity. The two-bulk structure also appears in CNTKs and finite-width U-Nets. Causally, **truncating the Gram matrix at rank $r$ or an $L_2$ penalty on the second bulk suppresses memorization**.

### [Rethinking Circuit Evaluation: Do Circuits Explain Model Errors?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35686)

Li Zhang, Chuqin Geng, Mark Zhang et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.AI

Circuits validated by ablation (manual and automated, on IOI, Docstring and six Mechanistic Interpretability Benchmark settings) are scored separately on the model's successes and its failures. On GPT-2-small IOI under mean ablation they **agree with the model on 97.3–99.5% of its correct answers but only 11.4–41.7% of its errors**. Restoring the omitted attention heads raises error reproduction from 14.2% to 75.1% on held-out prompts, at a cost of only 0.4 points of correct-answer agreement. The proposed standard is **exact reproduction of the model's errors as well as its successes**, a stricter test for any attention-attribution or circuit claim.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Machine learning for the LHC physics program: a 2025-2026 stocktake](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32874)

Jesse Thaler
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: cs.LG, hep-ex

This is an LHCP 2026 review whose **slides and write-up were produced by an agentic AI pipeline** under the author's direction. The pipeline surveyed the 569 HEPML Living Review papers from the past year, read 103 in full, and had independent reviewer agents check the claims. It makes six claims: **HEPML has become infrastructure that physics results depend on** (e.g. the CMS charm-Yukawa limit); SBI and foundation models are starting to merge; agents are the new front (47 papers, no adopted measurement yet); **"do we trust it?" is the fastest-growing agenda (one paper in five)**; equivariance has been absorbed into standard tools; and theory ML has crossed a capability threshold. It is a candid case study of the method as well as the field, and the author flags where the evidence is thin.

### [Comment on "Fermi-LAT Galactic Center Excess Morphology of Dark Matter in Simulations of the Milky Way Galaxy"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34155)

Kevork N. Abazajian, Jason Kumar, Oscar Macias
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE | also: astro-ph.GA, hep-ph

A PRL claim that dark-matter annihilation and old-star emission give indistinguishable Galactic Center Excess morphologies is traced, through the public code, to an error. The code **squares the projected mass, $[\int\rho\,s^2 ds]^2$, instead of integrating $\int\rho^2 ds$**. Squaring a map cannot change its isophote shapes, which explains the matching axis ratios. Redone on the same HESTIA halos over 216 views, the annihilation maps are **clearly more centrally concentrated (median $C=0.43$ vs 0.23 for stars)**. The authors disclose that the reanalysis was implemented and partly drafted with Claude Code, making it a concrete example of AI-assisted replication via public code.
