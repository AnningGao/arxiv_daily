## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Self-Supervised Representation Learning: From Spectral Foundation Models to Auroral Emission Spectra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31206)

Matthieu Le Lain, Gaël Cessateur, Sébastien Lefèvre
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: physics.space-ph

A two-channel 1D ViT (line channel with a running-median continuum removed, plus a downsampled continuum channel; 64 tokens of ~4.4 nm, about one emission line each) is **pretrained as a masked autoencoder on 223k unlabelled auroral spectra**, then probed on 811 expert-labelled ones. The frozen embedding ties 13 hand-built line-ratio features under a linear probe (mAP 0.863 vs 0.861) and recovers the physical line ratios (R² 0.91 vs 0.77 for an untrained encoder). With 10% of the labels, pretraining beats the same architecture trained from scratch by +0.159 mAP. The result that matters for a quasar-spectrum transformer is the frozen-transfer test: **transfer tracks overlap in wavelength window, not the kind of source**. SpecFormer (AstroCLIP, optical DESI galaxies) is the only external model above the untrained floor but still falls short of in-domain pretraining, and **SpectraFM (infrared APOGEE) falls below a randomly initialised encoder**.

![](figures/2609.31206/P6M_interpretability_composite_v2.png)

*Panel (b) shows that the MAE embedding, learned without labels or a line catalogue, recovers the diagnostic line ratios far better than an untrained encoder. Panel (c) shows the fine-tuned classifier putting its attribution on the physically correct emission systems, e.g. both N₂⁺ first-negative bands even though the annotator only checks one.*

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [KBSS-InCLOSE II: First Detailed Insights on the Inner CGM of Low-Mass $z\sim2.3$ Galaxies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30834)

Evan Haze Nunez-Cravin, Charles C. Steidel, Gwen C. Rudie et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Two low-mass ($M_* \lesssim 10^9\,M_\odot$) star-forming galaxies at z ≈ 2.245, found with KCWI and MOSFIRE within 50 kpc (< 0.75 R_vir) of background QSOs, have their inner CGM decomposed in Keck/HIRES spectra. The absorption shows no low ions, ubiquitous C IV and O VI, and ≥ 7 kinematic components over ±150 km/s. Tying Doppler widths across ions separates **thermal from turbulent broadening**. The median absorber is at **log T = 4.0, consistent with UV-background photoheating**, with subsonic internal turbulence (median b_turb ≈ 5 km/s). The fastest components carry short-lived intermediate-temperature gas (**4.6 ≤ log T ≤ 5.1**), which needs extra heating or rapid replenishment. Neither halo shows unambiguously unbound gas. It is a clean example of the ion-tied T/b_turb Voigt method used in IGM thermal-state work, applied here to CGM gas.

![](figures/2609.30834/Tlosv_scatter_vTanalysis_2026September21.pdf)

*Absorbers near the galaxies' systemic velocity sit at photoionisation temperatures (~10⁴ K). The components at large line-of-sight velocity are the hot ones (10^4.6–10^5.1 K), consistent with gas recently ejected by an outflow.*

### [A tidal disruption event in a quasar at redshift 7.19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30441)

S. Fujimoto, Q. Fei, G. B. Brammer et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.HE

Twenty years of HST, Spitzer and JWST imaging of the red quasar GNz7q ($M_{BH} \sim 3\times10^7\,M_\odot$) at z = 7.19 show a smooth, coherent **fade of Δm ≈ 1.1 mag in the rest-UV over ~2 rest-frame years**. That places it above the 99.9th percentile of SDSS quasar variability. A damped-random-walk quasar model reproduces it with probability ≲ 10⁻⁵, and a scaled SLSN fades too fast and falls short of the energy by an order of magnitude. A thermal continuum with a t^(−5/3) decline fits all bands, giving a peak L_bol ≈ 3×10⁴⁵ erg/s and **~1.5×10⁵³ erg radiated, implying the disruption of a few-solar-mass star**. Supporting evidence: a redshifted extra broad Balmer component (FWHM ≈ 11,900 km/s, +2,500 km/s) in two NIRSpec epochs, and a delayed MIRI dust echo from ~1500 K dust at sub-parsec radius. The practical caveat for quasar samples: single-epoch "quasar" spectra at the highest redshifts can include transient, non-virialised broad-line emission.

![](figures/2609.30441/lc_fit_single_v1.pdf)

*One panchromatic model (Gaussian rise plus t^(−5/3) decay) fits the baseline-subtracted HST/Spitzer/JWST light curves from 1 to 5 μm. Stochastic quasar variability cannot produce that kind of coherent, wavelength-dependent evolution.*

### [Probing the cosmic web with fast radio bursts: II. Scintillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31106)

Sharon Lapiner, Nir Mandelker, Paz Beniamini et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.HE

Using a "shattering" model for **~10⁴ K cloudlets condensing out of shock-heated sheets, filaments and massive-halo CGM**, the authors ask whether those small-scale plasma screens leave a mark on FRB scintillation. Scattering by a single cosmic-web object is hard to detect, and chance intersections with cloudy hot filaments and sheets are rare and mostly at high z. The better handle is indirect: an extended cosmic-web screen broadens the apparent source size and so **suppresses Milky Way ISM scintillation**. Filaments and sheets can do this for sources at z_S ≳ 2 and 3, and turbulent cold CGM cloudlets for sources down to z_S ~ 0.5, reaching **cold-gas volume-filling fractions as low as f_v ~ 10⁻⁵**. They give the expected fraction of FRBs with suppressed MW scintillation as a function of mean extragalactic DM, as a test that works on non-localised samples. It is a forward-modelling paper with no data yet, but it is a small-scale multiphase-gas probe complementary to absorption lines.

### [Natural Saturation Of The Sterile Neutrino Dark Matter Resonant Production By a High Lepton Flavor Asymmetry In Primordial Plasma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31340)

Dmitry Gorbunov, Dmitry Kalashnikov
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: astro-ph.CO

Resonant (Shi–Fuller) sterile-neutrino production is computed with the lepton-flavour asymmetries evolved self-consistently. The active-neutrino quantum kinetic equations (COFLASY) are coupled to the background, including a simplified pion-condensate treatment. A larger initial asymmetry pushes the resonance to lower temperature, where it overlaps the onset of active-neutrino oscillations. **Those oscillations redistribute or erase the asymmetry, so the relic abundance saturates** instead of growing with the initial asymmetry. The result is a lower bound on the mixing angle for resonant production, leaving a **~two-order-of-magnitude window in sin²2θ** (explored at m_s = 40 keV and across mass) for next-generation X-ray line searches. The hotter spectra from late production mean that Lyα-forest and small-scale-structure limits are what exclude the lighter masses in this scenario.

### [Cosine Similarity Is Not Evidence: Measuring the Noise Floor of Interpretability Transfer Under Quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30275)

Pranav Varshney
**Primary category:** cs.LG

This is a methodological paper on reporting representation-similarity statistics. For a difference-in-means direction estimator, the split-half cosine between two independent estimates is set by one number, **κ = nρ²/d, with E[cos] ≈ (1 + 4/κ)⁻¹**. Here n is sample count, d is width and ρ is class separation, which prior compression-transfer papers never report. Measuring ρ = 33–61 on Qwen2.5-1.5B activations, two runs of the same estimator already agree at 0.978–0.994 from sampling noise alone. A published cosine of 0.996 between full-precision and quantised refusal directions therefore says nothing without its n. Tested against each quantised model's own null, **the INT4 direction genuinely rotates, while INT8 shows no detectable movement**. The author also shows that scale-invariant scores cannot tell translation from attenuation of a transferred direction. The lesson applies to any claim that two spectral embeddings or probe directions "agree".

![](figures/2609.30275/fig_floor.pdf)

*Monte Carlo runs over nearly three decades in κ all land on the analytic cosine-floor curve (A). At realistic width, the floor for the unreported sample size can exceed the published 0.996, so that number cannot certify that the direction survived quantisation (B).*

### [A Flow Matching Framework for Neural Representational Dissimilarity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31544)

Zeyuan Ye, Xue-Xin Wei
**Primary category:** cs.AI | also: cs.LG, math.IT

The standard representational-distance metrics all come out of one estimator: **fit a conditional flow-matching model from a Gaussian source, then take the Jeffreys divergence between the fitted condition-specific distributions**. The metric is fixed by constraining the velocity class. A constant velocity gives Euclidean distance (or cosine/correlation with norm constraints), a shared affine map gives Mahalanobis, a per-condition affine map gives Gaussian Jeffreys, and an unconstrained velocity gives the full Jeffreys divergence or full Fisher information for continuous conditions. The unified estimator does best on synthetic benchmarks for Fisher information and time-resolved divergences in high dimension. It is also applied to mouse visual-cortex (Stringer) and Allen Neuropixels/two-photon recordings. Choosing a template source plus a velocity class yields **new metrics by design**, demonstrated with a geometry-preserving (similarity-transform) distance. It is a principled alternative to CKA-style comparisons of learned representations.

### [BeatGraph: Self-Supervised Heartbeat Graphs for Infant ECG Representations from the Home Environment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31546)

Mohammad Nur Hossain Khan, M. S. Krafczyk, Beverly G. Bolster et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

This is a tokenisation-design result for 1D scientific signals. Instead of fixed-length patches that split heartbeats and whose content drifts with heart rate, BeatGraph **uses each detected heartbeat as the token**. A shared beat encoder, a temporal Transformer and residual graph attention over all beats in a 30 s window are pretrained by **masked beat-embedding prediction** on ~3,400 h of unlabelled infant ECG. One backbone improves macro-F1 over the strongest baseline by 0.076–0.158 on four downstream tasks. Trained only on infants, it matches the best self-supervised ECG models on a pediatric benchmark (AUROC 0.892) and on adult PTB-XL under linear evaluation. The ablations make the point: **beat-aligned tokens beat fixed patches on every task**, and extra Transformer depth does not recover what dropping graph attention costs. That is directly analogous to line- or feature-aligned tokens for spectra.

### [A Flatness-Generalization Relation in the Teacher-Student Tree-Committee Machine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31101)

Brandon Livio Annesi, Davide Straziota, Enrico Maria Malatesta
**Primary category:** cond-mat.dis-nn | also: cs.LG, stat.ML

In a teacher-student tree-committee machine, both the empirical-risk minimizers (replica method, including RSB for classification) and **the full Hessian spectrum at those minimizers** are computed analytically in the proportional limit, and both match N = 2000 gradient-descent runs. That allows a direct test of whether flatness (spectral mean, left edge, right edge) tracks test error as the sample ratio α varies. **The answer depends on the phase.** In regression the spectral mean and right edge track error, and the left edge only for α < 1. In classification the correlation holds only when highly overparametrised, disappears in the RSB phase (where the left edge is pinned at −λ, i.e. marginal minima), and can reverse at large α. It is a solvable counterexample to treating sharpness as a universal proxy for generalisation.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [SN Helios: A Multiply Imaged Type II Supernova Opening Time-Delay Cosmography beyond redshift of 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30440)

Seiji Fujimoto, Conor Larison, Justin D. R. Pierel et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE

JWST/VENUS imaging of the cluster RXC J0018.5+1626 found a red F150W-dropout transient. A DDT NIRSpec prism spectrum shows it is a **strongly lensed Type II supernova at z = 3.34** (Hα FWHM ≈ 7,900 km/s with a P-Cygni trough at 8,800 km/s, plus Hβ and He I 10830), about 27 rest-frame days past maximum. Lens models give μ ≈ 10–20 and **predict another image in ~2–6 yr**, enabling a time-delay H₀ measurement with a source beyond z = 3. The host is undetected (de-lensed M_UV ≳ −13). The striking part is the impostor test: without multi-epoch data, **the demagnified SED fits as a z ≈ 16 Lyman-break galaxy** with P(z > 10) > 0.9999, and z < 7 is rejected at Δχ² = 30. A Balmer break plus Hα at z = 3.34 mimics a Lyman break, which is a warning for single-epoch ultra-high-z candidate lists.

![](figures/2609.30440/fig_z16_impostor.pdf)

*Demagnified to blank-field depth, SN Helios's discovery photometry gives a single-peaked photo-z at z ≈ 16 with galaxy templates. The overplotted spectrum shows how a z = 3.34 supernova produces that fake Lyman break.*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [FOTO: Figure-Level Semantic Search for Astronomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30536)

Hurum Maksora Tohfa, Francisco Villaescusa-Navarro
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

FOTO indexes **482,750 figure captions from 53,839 peer-reviewed astro-ph papers** as "title | caption" strings, embedded with a local 110M-parameter open model. An LLM is used only to verify a retrieved shortlist, never to generate references. Prepending the paper title is the key trick (pilot recall@20 rises from 0.491 to 0.914), and rewriting captions with an LLM adds nothing. Under the harder figure-level criterion, FOTO recovers the target in **29–79% of queries at recall@20, against 6–20% for Pathfinder and 8–16% for Semantic Scholar** at paper level. LLM reranking lifts recall@1 from 0.667 to 0.875 on detailed queries and from 0.071 to 0.571 on vague ones. Open-weight judges match a frontier model, and caption-only verification matches caption-plus-image. The index currently stops at June 2023, and vague queries remain the failure mode.
