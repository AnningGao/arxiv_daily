## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [The gradual decline of Lyα visibility in the CANDELS fields: evidence for the combined effects of galaxy evolution and reionization](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00199v1)

L. Napolitano, L. Pentericci, A. Ferrara et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectroscopy of 3,446 sources across the five CANDELS fields (539 secure Lyα emitters), the authors trace the Lyα emitter fraction X$_{\mathrm{Lyα}}$ from z = 4 to 14.2 and find it rises from z = 5 to 6 and then declines monotonically at z > 6. Crucially, LAEs occupy a stable, extreme corner of galaxy-property space (low mass, bluer, more metal-poor, more bursty) that does *not* evolve with redshift, whereas the full star-forming population drifts toward that corner at earlier epochs — so galaxy evolution alone raises the intrinsic Lyα escape/production toward high z. Feeding this into an attenuation-free framework linking galaxy properties to IGM Lyα transmission, they show that both galaxy evolution and IGM attenuation must be modeled jointly to infer the neutral fraction x$_{\mathrm{HI}}$. The comparatively gradual decline of X$_{\mathrm{Lyα}}$ between z = 6 and 8 favors reionization histories that build up the neutral fraction progressively rather than in a rapid transition.

This is the closest paper to the core program: it is squarely about the IGM/reionization use of Lyα (primary focus #2), it directly addresses inferring x$_{\mathrm{HI}}$ from Lyα visibility, and it explicitly points to using this as a target-selection and cross-check diagnostic for wide-area Lyα campaigns including DESI.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Baryonic feedback suppression of the matter power spectrum: a three-parameter fitting formula and its single-parameter reduction](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00807v1)

Pengjie Zhang
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Zhang measures the hydro-to-DM-only power ratio S(k,z) across FLAMINGO, IllustrisTNG, Illustris, and the CAMELS-TNG/SIMBA one-parameter variation suites, and proposes a compact three-parameter fitting formula (with a clear physical reading for each of B₀, n, and a) that reproduces essentially all runs to |ΔS| < 0.03 (typically < 0.01) for k ≤ 3 h/Mpc and z ≤ 2. For FLAMINGO, TNG, and CAMELS-TNG the suppression floor exponent n ≈ 1, collapsing the description to a single parameter with the same accuracy, and the formula stays accurate across Ω_m ∈ [0.2,0.5], Ω_b, and σ₈ ranges well away from the calibration cosmology. The upshot is that despite the diversity of feedback implementations, baryonic suppression needs at most three effective degrees of freedom.

Baryonic feedback on the small-scale matter power spectrum is the leading systematic for weak-lensing cosmology and a general precision-cosmology methods concern (interest #4, techniques/simulations shelf); a low-dimensional, physically-interpretable nuisance parameterization is exactly the kind of tool that can be marginalized internally without killing constraining power.

*No figures extracted for 2609.00807 (the key content is the two-equation fitting formula quoted above; the paper's figures are fit-residual scatter plots that add little at a glance).*

### [The Halo Gas of Local Spiral Galaxies and the Link to Gaseous Satellites](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00151v1)

Yong Zheng, Jingyao Zhu, Ben Wertz et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Combining 21 nearby (<15 Mpc) spiral galaxies with 26 HST/COS QSO sightlines at impact parameters 0.1–0.9 R₂₀₀, the authors characterize the cool circumgalactic medium and its connection to gaseous satellites. They find large intrinsic scatter (~1–2 dex) in ion column densities and estimate a total cool CGM mass of ~2.5×10⁹ M⊙ for an L* spiral (integrating Si II/III/IV profiles), consistent with z~0.2 samples like COS-Halos. Hosts with gaseous satellites show higher metal-ion detection rates, but CGM column densities do not correlate with satellite number or total H I mass, implying satellites contribute little relative to the intrinsic profile scatter; detection rates rise mainly when a sightline passes within half the virial radius of a massive (LMC-like) satellite.

This is quasar-absorption-line CGM science — the natural sibling of the IGM program (interest #6), using the same background-quasar probe and photoionization modeling machinery, and a useful reference point for how cool, metal-enriched gas is distributed around galaxies.

*No figures extracted for 2609.00151 (the mass estimate and null satellite-correlation are the headline results and are fully captured in text).*

### [Connecting radio pulsars, magnetars, and XDINSs in a unified evolutionary framework using simulation-based inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00962v1)

Michele Ronchi, Celsa Pardo-Araujo, Vanessa Graber et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE

The authors build a full population-synthesis forward model of isolated neutron stars (birth properties, dynamical + magneto-thermal evolution, radio and X-ray emission, plus survey selection) and fit it to the observed radio-pulsar, magnetar, and XDINS populations using truncated sequential neural posterior estimation (TSNPE), an amortized simulation-based inference method. They infer a two-component log-normal birth magnetic-field distribution with peaks near 5×10¹² G and ~10¹⁴ G and a Galactic neutron-star birth rate of 3–4 per century, and show the same magnetic-field-decay parameters reproduce both young and old classes simultaneously. They are candid about where SBI struggles — the second (high-field) component's parameters are poorly constrained by the small X-ray sample, and the ~20 s magnetar spin-period cutoff resists reproduction.

A clean, end-to-end worked example of SBI/TSNPE applied to an expensive astrophysical forward model with realistic selection effects — directly relevant to the ML-inference-for-cosmology toolkit (interest #3), where the same neural-posterior machinery is what one would deploy on forest/LSS simulations.

### [Exact Global MCMC with Denoising Diffusion](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00279v1)

Mitch Hill
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

This paper shows that a denoising diffusion model trained with the standard loss defines a Markov chain whose stationary distribution is the target density, and makes this *exact* for any (imperfect) denoiser by wrapping it in a Metropolis-Hastings step whose acceptance ratio uses the forward/reverse path densities of a discrete-time SDE. The proposed Denoising Diffusion Monte Carlo (DDMC) trains the diffusion model on locally-converged MALA samples to learn global proposals, then composes that global path sampler with a local MALA sampler. Experiments on high-dimensional multimodal targets (Gaussian mixtures, double-well, Lennard-Jones, a Bayesian neural network posterior) show high acceptance and good global mixing, with evidence that standard diffusion-training scaling transfers to exact sampling from unnormalized densities.

A generic but on-target inference method: the interest profile explicitly tracks normalizing-flows / diffusion / stochastic-interpolant samplers and MCMC tooling (interest #3), and a diffusion-based *global* proposal with an exact MH correction is a plausibly borrowable ingredient for sampling stiff, multimodal cosmological posteriors.

*No figures extracted for 2609.00279 (figures are toy-target benchmark densities that do not illustrate the method itself).*

### [First tomographic measurements of the angular clustering and bias of photometric quasars from S-PLUS](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01238v1)

Maria Lopes, Felipe Avila, Armando Bernui et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using 23,402 photometric quasars from the S-PLUS DR4 QuCatS value-added catalog over 1.1 ≤ z_phot ≤ 2.6, the authors perform a tomographic angular-clustering analysis in four redshift bins, propagating photo-z uncertainties via inverse sampling and estimating covariances from 1,000 lognormal mocks. They measure a linear quasar bias rising from b_Q ≈ 2.17 at z_eff = 1.26 to b_Q ≈ 4.05 at z_eff = 2.38, consistent with prior measurements and best matching the Laurent et al. (2017) b(z) parameterization. The sample alone cannot constrain two-parameter bias models, but the measurements extend the observational mapping of quasar bias and serve as a calibration check on the QuCatS photo-z distributions.

Quasar clustering, bias evolution, and (ML-based) quasar target selection are part of the LSS/surveys thread feeding the forest program (interest #4); this is a modest but on-theme photometric-survey result relevant to using quasars as tracers and as forest sightline sources.

*No figures extracted for 2609.01238 (the four bias values and their agreement with literature b(z) fits are the substance).*

### [TDCOSMO XXVIII. The Hubble constant from the quadruply lensed quasar J1537−3010 with precise time delays](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00179v1)

A. Galan, A. G. Schweinfurth, S. H. Suyu et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

This is an end-to-end, blind time-delay cosmography of the quad-lensed quasar J1537−3010, which has ~2% optical time delays. Combining HST multi-band imaging, MUSE resolved stellar kinematics, and a full line-of-sight/environment analysis, and adopting a mass profile maximally degenerate with H₀ to fold the mass-sheet degeneracy fully into the error budget, they measure H₀ = 75.5⁺⁹·³₋₅·₈ km/s/Mpc — a 10% measurement from a single system in flat ΛCDM. The relatively large uncertainty is deliberate: it conservatively turns the mass-sheet degeneracy into a statistical error and includes differences between two independent lens-modeling teams/codes. Incoming JWST imaging and spectroscopy are expected to tighten this substantially.

Time-delay cosmography is an independent handle on the H₀ tension and thus on the broader cosmological context the forest program sits in (interest #4); the careful, blind treatment of the mass-sheet systematic is also a methodological reference for degeneracy-aware inference.

*No figures extracted for 2609.00179 (the single-system H₀ value and its conservative error budget are the takeaways; the paper's figures are system-specific lens/kinematics maps).*

### [Quantifying Sky Map Resolution Requirements for Beam Chromaticity Correction in Sky-Averaged 21 cm Experiments](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01125v1)

Aleksandra Dragović, Dominic Anstey, Harry T. J. Bevins (interest-file high-value 21cm author) et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

For global (sky-averaged) 21 cm experiments, antenna-beam chromaticity must be corrected using a base sky map, and this work quantifies how accurate that map must be. Using simulated data and progressively degraded base maps under three foreground models (difference polynomial, log-log polynomial, EDGES-style power-law expansion), they find chromaticity-correction residuals stay stable down to map resolution N_side = 64 (~1°), so imperfect sky knowledge is not the dominant limitation. Instead, foreground-model choice matters more: the physically-motivated EDGES power-law needs fewer terms and yields cleaner signal posteriors, and a shallow (0.155 K) Gaussian signal cannot be recovered because of signal–foreground degeneracy, while a deeper flattened Gaussian is partially recoverable.

The 21 cm global signal is the complementary high-z / cosmic-dawn probe to the forest-based reionization interest (interest #7), and this is a concrete systematics/calibration study (with interest-file author Bevins) on what actually limits signal recovery.

*No figures extracted for 2609.01125 (the resolution threshold and the foreground-model comparison are the key results; figures are technical residual panels).*

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Higgsino Dark Matter Interpretation of the LUX-ZEPLIN 248 keV Nuclear-Recoil Event](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01583v1)

Katherine Freese, Dionysios P. Theodosopoulos
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: astro-ph.CO, hep-th

The newsworthy item behind this short Letter is that LUX-ZEPLIN, in a 2.84 tonne-year extended-energy (5.4–270 keV) search, reported an unexplained nuclear-recoil "event of interest" at 248 keV — background-only disfavored at 2.6σ global / 3.4σ local, with no identified likely background. Freese & Theodosopoulos argue a nearly-pure ~1 TeV thermal Higgsino realizes inelastic dark matter through the off-diagonal Z coupling of two near-degenerate Majorana states, which *fixes* (rather than fits) the inelastic Higgsino–nucleon cross section; for a mass splitting δ ≈ 350 keV the prediction approaches LZ's published two-sided 90% interval. They note the thermal-mass prediction sits just outside the estimated bounds, motivating a targeted extension of LZ's inelastic analysis.

Off the forest program, but a genuinely surprising development worth tracking: an unexplained possible signal from the leading WIMP direct-detection experiment, with dark matter as a secondary interest (interest #5). Treat with caution — it is one of several rapid theory interpretations of a sub-discovery-significance event, not a detection.

*No figures extracted for 2609.01583 (a brief theory Letter; the content is the LZ event and the cross-section comparison).*
