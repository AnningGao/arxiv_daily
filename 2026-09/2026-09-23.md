## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Exploring the influence of reionisation-era galaxies on their local intergalactic medium using the Sherwood-Relics simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26483)

Hamish Anderson, Laura C. Keating, Luke Conaboy et al. (incl. James S. Bolton & Ewald Puchwein, forest/IGM authors flagged in the profile)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using the Sherwood-Relics patchy-reionisation radiation-hydrodynamic suite, this is the first theoretical prediction of how Lyα-forest opacity *near galaxies* compares to random sightlines at the end of reionisation (5.4 < z < 6.1), with mock [O III] emitters and Lyα spectra matched to the JWST/NIRCam ASPIRE geometry. At small influence radii the galaxy-biased sightlines are more opaque than random (they trace overdensities); at larger radii they tentatively show enhanced transmission from the locally boosted photoionisation rate, but with large realisation-to-realisation scatter and no statistically significant KS separation. They estimate that an ASPIRE-like survey needs roughly 4× (small-scale) to 10× (large-scale) more Lyα path length to overcome cosmic variance, and find broad agreement with the observed ASPIRE opacity distributions. This sits squarely on the galaxy–IGM connection and late-reionisation-relic systematics that matter for percent-level forest cosmology.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [The KAGG survey: KCWI Analysis of Gas around Galaxies at z~3-5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25221)

Grecco A. Oyarzún, Marc Rafelski, J. Xavier Prochaska et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

A KCWI integral-field survey of Lyα emission around 36 Damped Lyα Absorbers (DLAs) at z~3–5, reaching Lyα luminosities ≲10⁴² erg/s within impact parameters ≲120 kpc. They detect 7 DLA-associated Lyα emitters and, combining with the MAGG sample, find that emitter impact parameters shrink with increasing DLA metallicity (b>50 kpc at [M/H]~−2 down to b<50 kpc at [M/H]~−1), which they interpret as higher-metallicity DLAs tracing more massive halos. The low detection rate at small impact parameters points to most DLA hosts being low-mass (M⋆≲10⁸ M⊙) or having Lyα suppressed by resonant scattering/dust. Relevant as a direct handle on the DLA/CGM environment that feeds the same quasar-absorption systematics as the forest work.

### [Gone with the wind? Potential gas inflow in the broad-line region of a QSO at z=0.287 traced by time-varying Balmer absorption](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25224)

Xihan Ji, Dominic J. Walton, Francesco D'Eugenio et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

DESI spectroscopy of a QSO showing variable hydrogen Balmer absorption over ~10 months, decomposed into a stationary and a dynamic component, both with N_H~10²² cm⁻² and densities n_H≳10⁸ cm⁻³ implying sub-milliparsec absorbers located in the broad-line region. The redshifted centroids imply gas inflow at a rate comparable to the single-epoch accretion rate, with a 10% column-density change over two months suggesting continuous feeding, while new Einstein Probe X-ray data show the source is X-ray weak and heavily obscured (N_H~4×10²³ cm⁻²). The authors read it as an active feeding phase following a gas-rich merger. It connects to the profile's rising interest in Balmer-absorption AGN and in DESI quasar spectral variability as a continuum systematic.

### [The First Blind Survey of Intervening NaI Absorbers: Incidence and Cosmic Evolution with DESI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26794)

Abhijeet Anand, Sowgat Muzahid
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The first blind statistical survey of intervening Na I D absorbers, mining 214,035 DESI DR1 quasar sightlines to find 3,636 doublets at 0.01≲z≲0.27, with the selection function calibrated by end-to-end injection–recovery (50% completeness at W_5891≃0.95 Å, ~77% purity). From z≃0.25 to z≃0.03 the absorber incidence dN/dX and the cosmic Na I mass density both rise by a factor ~3.4, with roughly constant column density per absorber, implying growth in the product of host number density and Na-bearing cross-section toward late times. They find preliminary evidence that the absorbers sit near foreground galaxies and redden the sightlines, consistent with Na I tracing dense neutral gas. A clean example of squeezing an absorption-line survey out of DESI quasar spectra with careful injection-recovery completeness — the same methodology that underpins forest systematics.

### [The BRIDGE Survey. I. Metallicities and Physical Properties of Gas Probing the Circumgalactic Medium at Cosmic Noon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26785)

Saloni Deepak, Nicolas Lehner, J. Christopher Howk et al. (incl. N. Lehner, a CGM absorption-line author flagged in the profile)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

A metallicity survey of 105 H I-selected "CGM-like" absorbers (16.4 ≤ log N_HI < 20.3) at Cosmic Noon (1 ≤ z ≤ 2.2), combining archival HST UV with Keck/HIRES and VLT/UVES optical spectra. The median metallicity ([X/H]=−1.19) is statistically indistinguishable from z<1 but far above z>2.2 (−2.02), a plateau driven by the most diffuse absorbers reaching their z<1 enrichment already by Cosmic Noon while denser systems stay metal-poorer; no pristine (< −3.5) gas remains, unlike at z>2.2. A metallicity floor of ~−2.0 emerges for log N_HI>19, indicating denser gas is well mixed as a population while diffuse gas retains an unmixed metal-poor component. Useful context for the ionization/metal state of the intervening absorbers that sit alongside the IGM in quasar-absorption cosmology.

### [Basis function expansions of the two-point correlation function and reconstruction of the cosmological distance scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25320)

Fatima Abd Alrahman, Farnik Nikakhtar, Marcello Musso et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Proposes measuring the galaxy two-point correlation function without binning by projecting pair counts onto orthogonal basis sets (polynomials and half-integer generalized Laguerre functions), and — importantly — estimating the reconstructed BAO distance scale in the *same* single step rather than in a separate fit to binned counts. The analysis carries through shot-noise and cosmic-variance effects and shows the practical advantages of the orthogonal-basis projection. Sidesteps the perennial optimal-bin-size problem in clustering estimators. Directly relevant to the profile's interest in estimator methodology and information compression for large-scale-structure and BAO analyses.

### [HierArchical Wavelet Coefficients on the Sphere (HAWCS): the Scattering Transform on Spherical Maps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25548)

Arefe Abghari, Lukas T. Hergt, Douglas Scott et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

Introduces HAWCS, a healpy-based Python package for computing wavelet scattering-transform coefficients on full-sky maps, giving compact non-Gaussian summary statistics sensitive to higher-order, cross-scale structure. It is validated on controlled Gaussian/non-Gaussian fields and applied to Planck-derived thermal SZ maps (versus simulations) and to lensing signatures in CMB temperature maps, positioning the coefficients as a tool for validating component-separation pipelines and simulations. The authors note the coefficients could also be used to constrain or generate maps matching real data statistics. On-thread for the profile's interest in scattering transforms and beyond-power-spectrum summary statistics for cosmological fields.

### [Constraining Inflation with Little Red Dots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26553)

Dionysios Karagiannis, P. Daniel Meerburg
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Forecasts primordial non-Gaussianity from a hypothetical 14,000 deg² Little Red Dot spectroscopic survey over 4<z<9, using the joint galaxy power spectrum and bispectrum with 32 marginalised nuisance parameters and the non-Gaussian bispectrum covariance. Because LRDs plausibly combine large volume, high redshift, strong bias and adequate number density, they forecast σ(f_NL^loc)=0.32 — a factor ~16 better than Planck and a factor of three below the σ(f_NL^loc)≈1 single- vs multi-field inflation threshold — plus modest gains for equilateral and orthogonal shapes. It makes a concrete case for wide-field near/mid-IR spectroscopy and ties the profile's LRD interest to LSS bispectrum inference and inflation constraints.

### [Matryoshka attribution: Learning to attribute language model outputs to representations and weights](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25518)

Aryaman Arora, Kirill Acharya, Nathan Hu et al.
**Primary category:** cs.CL | also: cs.LG

Frames interpretability attribution as learning nested subsets of internal components that minimise a downstream loss, using a differentiable sigmoid top-k mask trained across all sparsities simultaneously to yield an ordering of components by attribution score. The method (MAttr) tops the Mechanistic Interpretability Benchmark leaderboard, recovers sparse task-transferable circuits, has a clean mathematical link to Integrated Gradients through its backward operator, and — trained with RL — identifies the ~1% of weights whose restoration removes refusals in a finetuned Llama-3.1-8B. A strong, gradient-based reformulation of mechanistic interpretability directly on the profile's transformer-interpretability / attention-attribution thread and its QGPT interpretability agenda.

### [Double Descent and Malign Overfitting in Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26392)

Raphaël Urfin, Tony Bonnaire, Giulio Biroli et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cond-mat.dis-nn

Resolves why diffusion-model overfitting is catastrophic rather than benign despite training reducing to score-matching regression, combining U-Net experiments on CelebA with a solvable random-features model. With m noise realisations per sample, the interpolation peak shifts from p~n (regression) to p~nm, but the test-loss rise — and the bias mechanism driving it — sets in at p~n independent of m, so real diffusion models (m≫1) sit on the malign rising branch where the score estimator's bias grows and memorises the empirical score. A bias–variance decomposition pinpoints the persistent, saturating bias, and they show optimally regularised overparameterised models still beat any unregularised one. Squarely on two profile threads at once — double-descent / benign-overfitting theory and diffusion generative models.

### [On Basis Function Selection for Sparse Gaussian Process Regression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26624)

Marnix Van Soom, Ivan De Boi
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

Points out that sparse GPs conventionally *truncate* a basis expansion to its first M functions under a compute budget, when one could instead *select* the M functions that matter for the data, and derives three information-theoretic ranking criteria matched to no-data, no-prior and in-between states of knowledge. Across six UCI benchmarks and three basis families (Hilbert-space GPs, variational Fourier features, variational inducing spherical harmonics), the no-data criterion is a strong, safe default that matches or beats truncation everywhere and is much better for spherical-harmonic bases, while the data-aware criteria help most for HSGP. Directly useful for the profile's GP-latent-variable and GP quasar-continuum modelling interests, where the choice and cost of the kernel expansion is the practical bottleneck.

### [Variational objectives for amortized Bayesian inference in inverse problems: The role of posterior conditioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25145)

Abhishek Srivastava, Arijit Hazra, Rajesh Dubbaku
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

Studies how the choice of variational regularizer shapes posterior accuracy in VAE-based amortized inference for inverse problems, comparing reverse-KL, asymmetric Jensen–Shannon, and a Jensen–Shannon–Wasserstein objective that swaps the reverse-KL regularizer for a squared 2-Wasserstein term while keeping forward-KL posterior supervision. A local linear–Gaussian Fisher-basis analysis explains the variance-dependent gradients, and on linear–Gaussian benchmarks plus nonlinear ODE/PDE-constrained problems the Wasserstein variant gives substantially lower posterior error precisely when the inverse problem is ill-conditioned (weakly identified directions). The takeaway — posterior conditioning should drive the variational objective — is relevant to the profile's amortized/simulation-based inference and uncertainty-quantification thread.

### [Penalized Nonreversible Langevin for Constrained Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25381)

Pervez Ali, Weihao Dong, Xiaoyu Wang
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG, math.PR

Develops penalized nonreversible Langevin algorithms for sampling from densities supported on a compact convex set, combining a squared-distance penalty with skew-symmetric perturbations that preserve the penalized Gibbs distribution. For smooth possibly-nonconvex potentials they prove nonasymptotic total-variation bounds under a log-Sobolev inequality and 2-Wasserstein bounds under contraction with stochastic gradients, and show nonreversible acceleration — tuning the skew perturbation improves the iteration bound from linear to logarithmic in the curvature ratio for a quadratic model. Experiments cover constrained Bayesian regression, classification, and neural-network posteriors. On the profile's sampling / Bayesian-computation thread (nonreversible/Langevin samplers of the kind used for neural-network posteriors).

### [Surveying the Universe in 4D: Beating Cosmic Variance with Wide-Field Slitless Spectroscopy from HST, JWST, Euclid, Roman, and Beyond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23148)

Andreea Petric, Gaël Noirot, Stacey Alberts et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.IM

A summary report from an STScI workshop on wide-field slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) across HST, JWST, Euclid, Roman and Rubin-LSST, laying out the science case (transients, spatially resolved galaxy evolution, the cosmic web, rare populations) and the shared data-analysis challenges of spectral overlap, crowded fields and very large data volumes. It singles out forward modeling and physics-informed machine learning as essential for extraction pipelines, and — notably for the field's practice — calls for systemic changes to properly recognize the early-career researchers who build the pipelines, code and calibration that enable WFSS science. Worth reading as a snapshot of where survey-spectroscopy infrastructure and ML-for-extraction are heading, and of the credit/career issues around pipeline work.

### [Before WIMPs: Neutrinos and the origins of particle dark matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25239)

Jaco de Swart
**Primary category:** physics.hist-ph | also: astro-ph.CO, hep-ph

A history-of-science account tracing today's particle dark-matter paradigm back to the neutrino-dominated universe proposed in the early 1970s, which became the leading "missing matter" explanation after the 1980 hints of neutrino mass before being superseded by WIMPs. The paper argues that this short-lived neutrino model set the conceptual and methodological standards — a coherent picture of cosmic evolution and structure formation — that gave particle cosmology its impetus, and reflects on how those inherited aims still steer dark-matter inquiry after four decades of null results. A concise piece of intellectual history for anyone whose work touches dark matter and structure formation.
