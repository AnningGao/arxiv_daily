## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Cosmic web Lyα emission in a sample of overdense regions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20938)

Davide Tornotti, Michele Fumagalli, Matteo Fossati et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

Using the deep MUSE MUDF and MXDF fields, the authors assemble 41 Lyα-emitter overdensities (δ ≈ 2–10) at 3 ≲ z ≲ 5 and search for extended intergalactic Lyα down to surface brightness (3–5)×10⁻²⁰ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² arcsec⁻². Diffuse filamentary emission beyond the circumgalactic scale appears preferentially in the densest environments (δ ≳ 3.5), reaching intrinsic SB ≈ 2×10⁻¹⁹, while δ < 2 regions are largely non-detections, and an oriented stack aligned along the LAE-traced web recovers the signal statistically. They infer a cosmological incidence of cosmic-web Lyα emission ℓ(Lyα) ≈ 1.22 — comparable to that of partially-neutral Lyman-limit systems — and argue the emitting gas is partially ionized at moderate densities (n_H ≈ 10⁻³–10⁻¹ cm⁻³), tracing the denser filament spines.

This is the closest paper to the core program today: it probes the same z ≈ 3–5 intergalactic medium the Lyα forest samples in absorption (primary focus #1–2), quantifies its ionization state and covering factor, and connects directly to optically-thick-gas statistics — useful emission-side context for the forest/IGM picture ahead of next-generation wide-field spectroscopy.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [High Resolution Grid-based Simulations of the Warm-Hot Intergalactic Medium](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21784)

Lile Wang, Renyue Cen
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This work runs a uniform 4096³ grid cosmological hydro simulation in a (100 h⁻¹ Mpc)³ box with the GPU code Kratos, reaching ~24.5 h⁻¹ kpc resolution — enough to resolve the Jeans scale of ~10⁴ K, n_H ~ 10⁻³–10⁻² cm⁻³ gas. They find only ~23.4% of cosmic baryons in the WHIM phase (10⁵–10⁷ K) at z = 0, well below the 40–50% of earlier lower-resolution runs, and show via a resolution series that resolving the Jeans scale lets gas reach higher densities where radiative cooling drains it out of the WHIM range. Synthetic Lyα, O VI, O VII, and O VIII maps trace the phase structure across the temperature–density plane, with O VI marking filament–halo interfaces.

Not the forest regime, but squarely IGM-thermodynamics work (primary focus #2): the temperature–density phase-space treatment, the Jeans-scale-resolution argument, and the O VI/Lyα synthetic observables are methodologically relevant to how the photoheated IGM and its thermal state are modeled and resolved in simulations.

### [Intrinsic Tidal Shear at the Largest Scales: Galaxy Multiplet Alignment in DESI DR2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21839)

C. Lamman, J. Aguilar, S. Ahlen et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors measure intrinsic alignment of galaxy *multiplets* (small groups whose configuration traces the local tidal field) against the large-scale structure in DESI DR2 BGS, LRG, and ELG samples over 0.01 < z < 1.6. They detect multiplet tidal alignment ℰ₊ beyond 100 h⁻¹ Mpc in every sample and out to 200 h⁻¹ Mpc (up to 4.5σ in the highest-z LRG bin), and present a bias-independent shape–shape autocorrelation ℰ₊₊ as a consistency check on the NLA model; no baryon-acoustic feature is detected in the projected estimator. Multiplet alignment gives direct access to the tidal shear field even in samples where individual-galaxy alignment is unavailable.

Adjacent LSS methodology on the owner's primary survey (DESI DR2, focus #3): intrinsic alignment is both a nuisance for and a novel probe of the tidal field, and a bias-independent large-scale estimator is a useful addition to the DESI-era clustering toolkit.

### [Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1): Euclid spectroscopy of quasars. 2. Physical properties from spectral fitting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20947)

Euclid Collaboration: J. Calhau et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

From Euclid Q1 spectroscopy the team fits 5,489 quasars (H_E ≤ 22.5, 0.01 ≤ z ≤ 4.8), selected via DESI/Gaia/WISE/QUBRICS cross-matches, successfully fitting 5,387 and flagging ~45% as artefact-free for trustworthy measurements. They deliver line fluxes, FWHM, and velocity offsets for the main emission lines, single-epoch black-hole masses for 1,213 sources (mean log(M_BH/M_⊙) ≈ 8.7, ⟨λ_Edd⟩ ≈ 0.34), and a geometric-mean composite whose continuum is fit by two power laws with a break near rest-frame ~5610 Å. The catalogue and fitting software are released.

Rising-secondary quasar-spectra/AGN interest (focus #4): the continuum-slope distributions, composite construction, and survey-scale line/BH-mass measurements are directly usable for QSO-continuum characterization and cross-survey (DESI) quasar-sample work that feeds forest continuum-fitting systematics.

### [Patchy Reionization Delays Quenching in Isolated Ultra-faint Dwarf Galaxies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21333)

Juyoung Kim, Myoungwon Jeon, Yumi Choi et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors run high-resolution cosmological zoom-in simulations contrasting a spatially uniform UV background with an on-the-fly radiative-transfer patchy-reionization model, sourced from 40 nearby massive galaxies, to study ultra-faint dwarf star-formation histories. Patchy reionization delays star-formation quenching by ~440 Myr on average relative to the instantaneous-uniform-UVB case, easing the tension with the extended, diverse SFHs seen in Local Group UFDs, though the extra star formation leaves little imprint on the stellar metallicity distribution. The result argues that idealized uniform-UVB treatments oversimplify how reionization truncates star formation.

Adjacent reionization physics (primary focus #2): the comparison of uniform vs. patchy/inhomogeneous UV-background modeling and the associated photoheating/photoionization histories are the same ingredients that matter for late-reionization opacity fluctuations and reionization relics in forest statistics.

*No figures extracted for 2609.21333 (the informative figures are multi-panel schematics of the RT source-placement scheme and per-halo SFH grids; the ~440 Myr delay is stated directly in the summary above).*

### [FLUMEN: Neural Emulator of an Advanced Stochastic Weak Lensing Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21806)

Galymzhan Baltabay, Ville Vaskonen
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This paper builds an improved stochastic model for the weak-lensing magnification distribution dP/dμ, resolving individual lenses down to 10⁷ M_⊙ and adding subhalos and large-scale clustering of the lens population, both of which broaden the distribution. On top of it they train FLUMEN, a conditional normalizing-flow emulator predicting dP/dμ as a function of source redshift (to z_s = 12) and six cosmological parameters, with the empty-beam cutoff and fold-caustic power-law tail enforced analytically. The emulator reproduces held-out Monte Carlo distributions at median KL ≈ 0.0048 at negligible cost.

Adjacent on the method axis (§6 flows/emulation): a conditional normalizing-flow emulator that hard-codes known analytic structure (cutoffs, tails) is exactly the emulation pattern the owner tracks for forest/cosmology surrogate modeling, here demonstrated end-to-end on a cosmological distribution.

### [Abstention and Noise Filtering: Two Missing Primitives of Softmax Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22005)

Richard Zhe Wang
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.CL

The author argues that value-pathway gating in attention supplies two distinct capabilities softmax attention lacks: abstention (a head can output nothing, escaping the sum-to-one constraint, via a learned per-head sink logit) and noise filtering (a value gate suppresses interference from superposed residual-stream features). In matched 10M–350M-parameter models, the abstention benefit shrinks with scale while the noise-filtering benefit grows — abstention accounts for nearly all the gain at 10M, filtering for most of it at 350M — and the best model at every scale has both. Controlled interference injection confirms the gate removes superposition noise and exposes each gate form's characteristic blind spot.

Directly on a §6 thread (transformer/mechanistic interpretability): a clean mechanistic decomposition of what attention gating does, with scale-dependent evidence, is squarely the kind of attention-mechanism analysis feeding the owner's quasar-transformer interpretability work.

### [Beyond Gaussian Worlds: Latent Geometry Matters for JEPAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21656)

Léo Nicollier, Enric Meinhardt-Llopis et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures avoid representation collapse by matching learned representations to a prescribed target distribution; prior work showed a Gaussian target uniquely guarantees linear recovery of Gaussian latents under Euclidean assumptions. This paper extends the analysis to latents on embedded Riemannian manifolds, deriving conditions under which alignment plus exact distribution-matching guarantees recovery up to an orthogonal transformation — e.g., uniform-on-a-sphere latents matched to the same spherical target — showing Gaussian uniqueness is not universal. It also gives a tighter approximate-recovery bound for the spherical case, with experiments on Gaussian, spherical, and toroidal latent spaces confirming that geometrically compatible targets improve linear recovery.

On a §6 thread (self-supervised representation learning + identifiability): the owner's active quasar self-supervised/foundation-model project turns on exactly this question of when a self-supervised objective recovers the true latent factors, and here the answer is made geometry-dependent.

*No figures extracted for 2609.21656 (a theory paper; figures are latent-geometry schematics and per-manifold recovery scatter plots that add little beyond the summary).*

### [Neural composite likelihood estimation: simulation based inference for time series](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21762)

Grace Yan, Mark Beaumont, Dennis Prangle
**Primary category:** stat.CO | also: stat.ML

Neural likelihood estimation (NLE) enables simulation-based inference for intractable likelihoods but only scales to low-dimensional data. The authors introduce Neural Composite Likelihood Estimation (NCLE), which splits a long sequence into equal-sized batches, learns a neural density estimate of each batch sub-likelihood, and multiplies them into an approximate composite likelihood; frequentist point estimates come from maximizing it and confidence intervals from a Godambe (sandwich) information estimate. Experiments on time-series models demonstrate that this recovers inference for long sequences with complex dependency structure where full-sequence NLE is infeasible.

Directly on a §6 thread (simulation-based inference, sequential data): SBI that scales to long dependent sequences is a natural fit for spectrum/forest inference problems, and the composite-likelihood factorization plus sandwich-based uncertainty is a transferable recipe.

### [MOSAIC-SR: Transformer-Guided Symbolic Regression for Scientific Equation Recovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20997)

Peiyi Zheng, Yanming Kang, Hans De Sterck et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

MOSAIC-SR combines a pretrained transformer, which proposes several initial expression sketches, with structured search that refines each sketch through scale-aware constant optimization and local symbolic repair — using the learned priors to seed searches in promising regions rather than random restarts. On the SRSD-Feynman benchmark (with and without dummy variables) and six further benchmarks it attains the highest symbolic-solution rate on every dataset while ranking top-two in predictive accuracy, and the advantage persists under irrelevant inputs. The result argues that learned priors plus numerical optimization and symbolic repair together drive recovery.

On a §6 thread (symbolic regression): a hybrid learned-prior + search approach to recovering closed-form equations is the interpretable surrogate-modeling tooling the owner flags, useful for compact analytic descriptions of simulation or data relations.

*No figures extracted for 2609.20997 (the paper's figures are benchmark-rate bar charts and pipeline schematics; the solution-rate result is stated above).*

### [Kinks vs. Smoothness: Identifiability of Real Analytic nICA for Laplace-like Sources](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21926)

Isaac Manring, Kejun Huang
**Primary category:** cs.LG

The authors prove identifiability (exact recovery up to trivial ambiguities) for nonlinear ICA when the generating function is real-analytic and the source densities have finitely many first-derivative discontinuities — the Laplace distribution being the prime example. The proof exploits the contrast between "kinks" in the source density and the smoothness of real-analytic mixing, and because such mixings are approximable by normalizing flows or VAEs with standard activations (tanh, softplus, GELU), the result transfers to existing training pipelines with minimal change. Experiments with flows and VAEs, including recovery of interpretable latent factors on CelebA, illustrate the guarantee.

On a §6 thread (generative/latent-variable models, identifiability): recovering the *true* independent factors rather than a scrambled version underpins interpretable representation learning, and a clean identifiability condition tied to source non-smoothness is a principled tool for the owner's latent-factor/foundation-model work.

*No figures extracted for 2609.21926 (a theory paper; figures are synthetic-recovery and CelebA latent-traversal panels that add little at a glance).*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [When AI Reviews Train AI Reviewers: Scientific-Judgment Collapse and Mitigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20942)

Sy-Tuyen Ho, Minghui Liu, Furong Huang
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Starting from Llama 3.1 8B, the authors fine-tune a reviewer on official ICLR 2018–2023 reviews and then train successor models on 2024 data with varied mixtures of official vs. model-generated reviews, simulating one step of a recursive AI-peer-review loop. Introducing synthetic reviews compresses rating distributions and reduces both same-paper and corpus-level semantic diversity — a pattern they call "scientific-judgment collapse." Their TrustReviewer system intervenes with a curated single-stage training corpus (prevention) and paired activation steering at test time (correction) to preserve judgment diversity.

Relevant meta-research: as LLM-assisted reviewing and model-generated text enter training corpora, this quantifies a concrete degradation of scientific judgment under recursive training — a direct concern for how AI is reshaping peer review and evaluation in the owner's field.

*No figures extracted for 2609.20942 (the paper's figures are distribution-compression and diversity-metric plots; the collapse mechanism and mitigation are captured in the summary).*
