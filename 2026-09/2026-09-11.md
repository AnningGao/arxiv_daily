## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [$w_0w_a$ or $bc$: DESI constraints on relative baryon-CDM perturbations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10794v1)

Ananda F. Smith, Mikhail M. Ivanov, Roger de Belsunce et al. (high-value co-authors: Ivanov and de Belsunce, both on the forest-EFT author watchlist)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors search DESI DR1 full-shape clustering for relative baryon–CDM density and velocity modes — a known baryon-acoustic-oscillation contaminant — and test whether they could be faking DESI's preference for evolving dark energy. They build the first holistic one-loop EFT model of the galaxy power spectrum plus tree-level bispectrum that consistently includes the baryon–CDM bias operators ($b_{\delta_{bc}}$, $b_{\theta_{bc}}$, $b_{v^2_{bc}}$), with an efficient FFTLog implementation that lets the relative-mode response track cosmology, and fit $P(k)+B(k)$ for all DR1 tracers via MCMC. The bias coefficients are consistent with zero at 95% CL (e.g. LRG2: $b_{\delta_{bc}}=5\pm6$, $b_{v^2_{bc}}=-0.031\pm0.023$), and marginalizing over them leaves the $(w_0,w_a)$ posterior essentially unchanged. DESI's dynamical-dark-energy preference is therefore robust against relative baryon–CDM perturbations.

This is the closest paper to the core program: DESI full-shape cosmology analyzed with a one-loop EFT of the power spectrum and bispectrum, by the forest-EFT group, addressing a perturbation systematic that is a direct sibling of the continuum/BAO systematics studied in the Lyα forest.

### [Learning JWST. I. A Foundation Model for New Population Discoveries and Morphology-Aware Photometric Redshift Measurements in the JADES Survey](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11879v1)

Jiani Ding, Minghao Yue, Yongda Zhu et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

FM-JADES-v1 is a self-supervised, multimodal foundation model (an AION-1-style transformer over tokenized 16-band NIRCam image patches plus photometric-catalog groups, ~1,456 tokens/object) trained from scratch on 482,444 JADES DR5 sources with masked-token reconstruction, InfoNCE alignment, and VICReg regularization — no class labels. Applied blindly, UMAP+HDBSCAN on the learned embeddings yields detached "islands" that turn out to be high-$z$ galaxies (recovering 91/100 spectroscopic $z>7$, ~55–89× enrichment) and Little Red Dots (~212–252× enrichment), with a nearest-neighbor seed search expanding LRD candidates from 19 to 113. A frozen encoder plus an attention head reaches $\sigma_\mathrm{NMAD}=0.157$ on a strict three-band photo-$z$ test versus 0.44 for template fitting, and the embeddings recover non-parametric morphology not present in the catalog.

This is a direct hit on the active self-supervised transformer / foundation-model thread (§6): a from-scratch scientific foundation model with masked-token + contrastive pretraining, representation-space discovery, and explicit transfer claims to Rubin/LSST, Roman, and Euclid — the same architectural family (AION-1, AstroCLIP) the QGPT quasar-spectrum project draws on.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [BIND (Baryonic INpainting with Deep learning): A Field-level Emulator for Galaxy Groups and Clusters](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10709v1)

Max E. Lee, Shy Genel, Zoltan Haiman et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

BIND is a conditional flow-matching (stochastic-interpolant/rectified-flow) generative model that maps a projected dark-matter-only halo field to its paired hydrodynamical dark-matter, gas, and stellar fields, conditioned on redshift and the full 35-dimensional CAMELS/IllustrisTNG parameter vector (trained on ~360k halo patches from the 1024 SB35 runs). Halo mass is never supplied, yet BIND recovers component masses at the percent level, azimuthally averaged profiles to ≲10% at all radii, halo shapes, and emergent scaling relations, and reproduces the correct sign and morphology of field-level responses to individual subgrid parameters. Applied halo-by-halo to a $(50\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc})^3$ $N$-body box it recovers the projected matter-power-spectrum suppression up to the ceiling set by pasting in the true hydro halos, in minutes on one GPU. Models and generated halos are released open-source.

This sits at the intersection of two interests: flow-matching / stochastic-interpolant generative modeling (§6) and baryonic-feedback modeling of the matter power spectrum, the systematic that couples directly to forest and weak-lensing cosmology.

### [Aletheia: Emulating the halo mass function with evolution mapping](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10665v1)

Andrea Fiorilli, Ariel G. Sánchez, Andrés N. Ruiz et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors build a two-stage Gaussian-process emulator of the halo mass function that exploits the evolution-mapping framework: shape parameters plus the clustering amplitude $\sigma_{12}$ set the multiplicity-function shape, and a first-order correction captures the residual dependence on integrated growth history. Trained on only 100 flat-$\Lambda$CDM $N$-body simulations, it nonetheless applies to curvature, quintessence, and $w_0w_a$CDM cosmologies by construction. Validated on independent $\Lambda$CDM and dynamical-dark-energy suites, it reaches per-cent-level accuracy across mass and redshift, degrading only mildly at early times. It outperforms the Aemulus-$\nu$, Dark Emulator, and CSST emulators in nearly all tested cases.

Relevant on two fronts: it is GP-based emulation with built-in uncertainty (an §6 method interest), and the evolution-mapping compression of the cosmological parameter space is a useful trick for any LSS/dark-energy emulator, including forest statistics.

### [Nitrogen-Loud Quasars from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument. I. Sample Selection and Basic Properties](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11104v1)

Shuo Zhai, Wei-Jian Guo, Yong-Jie Chen et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using DESI DR1, the authors assemble the largest nitrogen-loud quasar sample to date — 1,993 objects at $1.6<z<4.3$ with strong broad N IV]$\lambda1486$ and/or N III]$\lambda1750$, about 1.2% of the parent quasar sample — via spectral fitting plus visual inspection. The N-loud quasars share the parent $L_{1450}$ distribution but cluster more strongly around $z\sim2.5$–3, and their composite shows enhanced N V, N IV], and N III] on a similar UV continuum. Against a redshift- and luminosity-matched SDSS control sample they have systematically narrower broad C IV and Mg II, lower single-epoch virial black-hole masses, and higher Eddington ratios, hinting at a rapid-accretion phase; the radio-loud fraction is 10.1%, peaking among objects with both N III] and N IV].

This is squarely in the rising quasar-spectra / AGN-demographics interest (§4) with direct DESI survey relevance — the kind of survey-scale quasar catalog that feeds continuum modeling and target-selection work for the forest.

### [Beyond the BLUE I: the advantage ceiling — how much can any estimator beat the matched filter in mm/submm survey data?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10475v1)

Kaustuv Basu
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.CO

This methods paper defines a single diagnostic, the "advantage ceiling" $\eta\ge1$ — the Fisher information for a source amplitude in units of the matched filter's — that upper-bounds how much any unbiased estimator (a CNN included) can beat the matched filter, and is computable from noise-only simulations before any network is trained. Using a variational score-matching ladder (linear → mixture → quadratic → cubic → convolutional rungs), the author computes $\eta$ across a taxonomy of mm/submm survey noise: Gaussian noise gives $\eta\equiv1$ ($\pm0.04$), covariance mixtures give ceilings up to 10.2, and source confusion gives the largest gain ($\eta\ge3.8$ extended, $\ge2.4$ compact) reachable only above the bispectrum rung. A ResNet regressor is shown to be 6–15% *less* efficient than the matched filter on Gaussian noise once prior shrinkage is divided out.

The framework — Fisher/Cramér–Rao ceilings, score-matching, and a before-you-train diagnostic of when a neural estimator can actually help — is directly transportable to P1D/LSS amplitude estimation and to the owner's inference-methodology and SBI interests.

### [Hierarchical Population Inference with Normalizing Flows for Binary Black Holes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11885v1)

Leonardo Iampieri, Simone Mastrogiovanni
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE | also: gr-qc

The authors represent the binary-black-hole source-frame population in $(z,m_1,m_2)$ with a normalizing flow whose parameters are optimized directly through the hierarchical population likelihood, with per-event posteriors propagating measurement uncertainty and an injection campaign supplying the selection correction. Because restricting inference to the injection-supported region can itself manufacture an apparent mass–redshift association, they introduce a mutual-information diagnostic comparing the reconstruction against a redshift-independent reference under the same support restriction. Validated on mock catalogs that differ only in whether the primary-mass peak evolves with redshift, the method recovers both cases; applied to GWTC-5.0 it recovers features near $10$ and $35\,M_\odot$ and finds no evidence for intrinsic evolution of the primary-mass spectrum.

The GW application is incidental — the method is a general §6 recipe (normalizing flows + hierarchical/simulation-based inference + selection-effect handling + a mutual-information diagnostic for spurious correlations) for recovering a population from noisy, indirect, selection-biased measurements, which is exactly the shape of forest-population and summary-statistic inference problems.

*No figures extracted for 2609.11885 (the headline — mass features and the absence of redshift evolution — is stated in the text, and the key figure is a standard posterior-slice plot).*

### [Resonantly Scattered CIV Emission in Local Star-forming Galaxies: Radiative Transfer Constraints on High-ionization Gas in Reionization Analogs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11497v1)

Zhihui Li, Danielle A. Berg, Simon Gazagnes et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

This is the first systematic radiative-transfer modeling of resonantly scattered nebular C IV $\lambda\lambda1548,1550$ in 18 local star-forming dwarfs (reionization analogs) observed with HST/COS. Using the clumpy RT code PEACOCK, the authors show that profile morphology is governed by C IV column density, outflow kinematics, intrinsic equivalent width, and aperture-dependent recovery of scattered emission, reproducing four morphological classes, and fit the spectra with a neural-network-accelerated Bayesian pipeline. Emission infilling biases kinematic inferences (the blueshifted trough minimum does not cleanly trace bulk outflow), and aperture losses make observed net EW systematically underestimate intrinsic line production. Combining RT-inferred intrinsic EWs with rest-UV diagnostics, most galaxies are consistent with stellar photoionization.

Relevant as resonant-line radiative transfer methodology — a close sibling of Lyα transfer — applied to reionization-analog systems, touching both the reionization theme (§2) and the CGM/absorption-line interest (§7).

*No figures extracted for 2609.11497 (the actionable result — aperture losses biasing measured EW — is summarized in the text; the mechanism plots are multi-panel parameter sweeps that need heavy context).*

### [Impact of LSST systematics on stellar-stream density fluctuations for dark matter](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10897v1)

Matthieu Pélissier, Peter S. Ferguson, Alex Drlica-Wagner et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The authors build a forward-modeling framework that injects realistic stellar streams into LSST-like catalogs — photometric errors, depth maps, background contamination, and imperfect star–galaxy classification — and apply a likelihood-ratio analysis to assess the detectability of density gaps used as dark-matter subhalo probes. After four years, LSST is sensitive to ~50% density reductions for 5°-wide gaps in streams of ~33 mag/arcsec², a factor ~5 worse than the idealized case. Translated through a simple gap↔subhalo mapping, the minimum detectable subhalo mass is ~$10^7\,M_\odot$, shifted upward by a factor ~16 by observational effects (dominated by star–galaxy classification). They stress that neglecting these effects biases forecasts given the steep subhalo mass function.

This is dark matter from small-scale structure (§5) via the subhalo mass function — the stellar-stream sibling of the forest WDM/fuzzy-DM program — plus a forward-modeling treatment of survey systematics that is methodologically reusable.

### [Constraints on the fuzzy dark matter mass using globular clusters in dwarf galaxies from Euclid ERO data](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11328v1)

Lisa Besnard, Pierre Boldrini, Paola Di Matteo et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

Using ~4500 globular-cluster candidates across ~760 dwarfs from Euclid Early Release Observations in the Fornax and Perseus clusters, the authors constrain the fuzzy-dark-matter particle mass by modeling GC orbital evolution with galpy, combining an $m_{22}$-dependent solitonic density profile with the wave-nature-modified dynamical-friction force. A bisection search over $0.1\le m_{22}\le100$ finds the maximum mass for which GCs avoid sinking within 10 Gyr, solving the long-standing GC timing problem. The 5th-percentile upper constraints are $m_{22}\lesssim1.46$ (Fornax cluster), $1.09$ (Perseus), and $1.38$ (Fornax dSph). These are consistent with Milky-Way-disk limits but in tension with stellar-stream and Lyman-α-forest bounds ($m_{22}\sim7$–20).

Directly relevant to the fuzzy/ultralight dark matter interest (§5), and notable for the explicit tension it opens with the Lyman-α-forest FDM mass bounds that are central to the profile.

### [MUtE: A Dual Framework for Concept Erasure and Counterfactual Interventions](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11253v1)

Antoine Saillenfest
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.CL

This paper characterizes maximum-utility concept erasure as a set of class-conditional diffeomorphisms that push each class-conditional representation distribution onto a shared invariant post-erasure distribution, and shows this structure intrinsically induces a deterministic dual counterfactual map. The implementation is a class-conditional Rotation-Based Iterative Gaussianization (a normalizing flow): alternating global orthogonal rotations with per-class marginal Gaussianization, gradient-free and robust to noisy routing. On GloVe, Bias-in-Bios, DIAL, and Jigsaw it drives probe accuracy to chance while retaining utility (e.g. WordSim-353 correlation preserved at 0.71 vs 0.70) and generates counterfactuals with higher substitution rates than mean-difference or linear-OT steering.

A strong double hit on §6 method threads: representation editing / null-space-style concept erasure (the INLP/AlphaEdit family the profile tracks) built on a Gaussianization normalizing flow — directly relevant to interpretability and feature-control for the quasar-spectrum transformer project.

### [Deep operator learning for efficient sampling from invariant measures of stochastic differential equations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11376v1)

Lin Guo, Li Lei, Jingtong Zhang
**Primary category:** math.NA | also: cs.LG

The authors propose an amortized neural sampler that learns an operator mapping SDE coefficient functions to a flow-matching velocity field whose ODE pushes a reference measure to the SDE's invariant (stationary Fokker–Planck) measure, so a new SDE instance needs only one encoder pass plus a few ODE steps — decoupling inference cost from mixing time. To scale without a mesh, coefficients are probed by random Lagrangian-trajectory sensors, aggregated by a Perceiver cross-attention encoder into a fixed latent context, and decoded by an AdaLN-conditioned flow network. They prove expressivity (coefficient→flow continuity plus neural-operator universal approximation) and resolution invariance. Experiments on 1D/2D SDE families and a 64D interacting-particle Langevin system show competitive Sinkhorn/$W_2$ accuracy with large speedups over MCMC in slow-mixing regimes.

This spans several §6 threads at once — neural operators, flow matching, and amortized/simulation-based sampling from invariant measures — and the slow-mixing-sampler angle is directly relevant to Bayesian-computation interests (HMC/Langevin posteriors).

*No figures extracted for 2609.11376 (the architecture diagram helps but the multi-stage pipeline is adequately described in the text).*

### [SPURS: An Ultra-deep View Inside the Compact, Nitrogen-Enriched Nuclei of Little Red Dots](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11094v1)

Mengtao Tang, Daniel P. Stark, Charlotte A. Mason et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The team presents the first ultra-deep (29 hr) JWST/NIRSpec rest-UV spectroscopy of four UV-bright Little Red Dots at $z\approx7$. They report the first detections of broad high-ionization UV lines in LRDs — broad C IV (FWHM ≈ 2700–2830 km/s) in two sources and broad He II in a third — plus extremely dense narrow-line gas ($n_e\sim10^4$–$10^6$ cm⁻³), nitrogen enhancement in all four, and Lyα damping wings implying the UV continuum is deeply embedded in neutral gas. An archival analysis of 122 $z>4$ LRDs finds N IV]/N III] ~8× more common than in the general galaxy population. They conclude the UV-emitting region sits within ≲8 pc of the nucleus, consistent with an assembling nuclear star cluster, with orientation-dependent broad-C IV transmission.

Little Red Dots are flagged explicitly in the rising quasar/AGN interest (§4); this is the clearest new spectroscopic characterization of their UV nuclear physics, and the nitrogen-enrichment demographics connect to the N-loud quasar work above.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [More than half of recent astronomy papers are written with language-model assistance](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10664v1)

Serat M. Saad, Yuan-Sen Ting (high-value co-author: Ting, on the ML-for-astrophysics watchlist)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: cs.CL

The authors measure LLM-assisted writing across ~433k astro-ph papers (2015–mid-2026) by counting "marker" words per token and fitting a hierarchical Bayesian mixture of assisted/unassisted components, calibrated on pre-2020 papers and ~400 papers that disclose model use. Unlike prior document-frequency estimators, they use counts-per-token, let the background drift by subfield and time, and let the assisted excess fade as authors adapt. Under the conservative specification, $67^{+12}_{-11}$% of 2025 astro-ph papers carry an LLM trace (never below ~60% across variants), rising toward ~88% by early 2026, while only about one in eight to ten papers discloses such use. The marker excess more than halves between 2023 and 2026, evidence that the trace is actively being masked.

Directly in the meta-research interest: a quantitative, astronomy-specific measurement of how LLMs are reshaping the field's writing practice and the large gap between use and disclosure.
