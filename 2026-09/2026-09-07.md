## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Model Predictive Scoring Shows Specific BAO Observations (not SNIa) Drives $w_0w_a$ Tension](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04334v1)

Tanveer Karim, Joshua S. Speagle, Renée Hložek
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Rather than the usual in-sample tests (Δχ²_MAP or the prior-sensitive Bayes factor), the authors score ΛCDM against $w_0w_a$CDM by how well each *predicts unseen data*, using the Expected Log Predictive Density (ELPD) with a leave-one-redshift-block-out cross-validation estimator on DESI DR2 BAO, Planck CMB, and several supernova datasets. Aggregated, they find only a modest preference for $w_0w_a$CDM, and it is driven entirely by BAO. Decomposing ΔELPD by redshift, a single BAO block — LRG2 at $z=0.706$ — supplies the whole BAO preference, while the biggest outlier point LRG1 contributes little because both models miss it equally. All SNIa datasets score ΔELPD consistent with zero, meaning the evolving-dark-energy "tension" is, at the out-of-sample predictive level, one BAO point.

This sits squarely at the owner's meeting point of DESI DR2 dark-energy cosmology and inference methodology: a clean, prior-independent statistical dissection of exactly where the reported $w_0w_a$ evidence lives — directly relevant to how the same DESI data feed Lyα full-shape/BAO conclusions.

### [It's All About the Environment: Local $f_{\rm NL}$ from a Dark Matter Conditioned Multitracer Analysis](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04313v1)

Julius Adolff, Uroš Seljak
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors develop a multitracer approach to local primordial non-Gaussianity that splits a single tracer sample by its large-scale dark-matter environment — reconstructed from the halos themselves via linear theory — rather than by halo mass or unobservable secondary properties. They derive the $f_{\rm NL}$ response of environmentally selected tracers and validate it against N-body and Quijote simulations, confirming consistency between separate-universe predictions and direct power-spectrum inference. Forecasts for the DESI LRG sample show environmental multitracing improves $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})$ by a factor of 2-3 for high-density tracers, comparable to gains from knowing halo formation time perfectly. An accompanying analytic model explains why an environment split gains less than a mass split and why separate-universe predictions fail in that case.

A large-scale-structure inference method from a listed high-value author (Seljak) that requires only linear theory and observable quantities — a concrete route toward $\sigma(f_{\rm NL})\sim1$ with DESI-era surveys, the owner's working data.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Tracing the Cosmic Origins: Machine Learning Reconstruction of the Primordial Density Field from EoR Observations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05412v1)

Anchal Saxena, P. Daniel Meerburg, Guochao Sun et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

A 3D U-Net reconstructs the $z\sim300$ initial density field from simulated $z\sim8$ 21-cm and CO(1-0) line-intensity maps (generated with LIMFAST), after which simulation-based inference on power-spectrum summaries quantifies the cosmological-information gain. The two tracers are complementary — 21-cm follows neutral, low-density IGM while CO traces overdense star-forming peaks — so combining them recovers a cross-correlation $|C(k)|\gtrsim0.90$ (noiseless) and $\gtrsim0.70$ with SKA1-Low/COMAP-ERA-like resolution and thermal noise for $k\lesssim0.3\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$; crucially, the joint recovery is nearly independent of the mean neutral fraction. The reconstruction tightens $\sigma_8$ and $n_{\rm s}$ by roughly $2\times$, confirmed via KL-divergence diagnostics.

This fuses reionization-era observables (the owner's §2/§8 interest) with ML reconstruction and SBI (§6): an initial-conditions/LSS-information recovery method whose SBI-on-summaries machinery transfers directly to forest P1D/P3D inference.

### [A guide to choosing data compression methods for cosmological inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04829v1)

Susan Pyne, Benjamin Joachimi, Calum Gordon et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

A pedagogical guide to selecting data-compression methods for cosmological inference and numerical covariance estimation, categorizing techniques by loss-function form (Fisher information, mutual information, or mean squared error) and by linearity, with tomographic weak-lensing power spectra as the worked example constraining $\Omega_{\rm m},\sigma_8,w_0,w_a$. MOPED and a Fisher-information-based linear neural network achieve lossless compression, whereas mutual-information objectives reach at most 84% of the uncompressed figure of merit and MSE objectives at most 72%. The authors show how to test whether MOPED retains information when the fiducial cosmology is uncertain, and demonstrate a CCA strategy that nearly matches MOPED needing only approximate knowledge of where the posterior mass sits.

Information compression is an explicit Lyα sub-theme in the profile; this is a practical reference map for the owner's estimator/compression and covariance-estimation work, where MOPED-style lossless compression is a standard tool.

### [The Super-Sample Covariance of Line-Intensity Mapping Power Spectrum](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04488v1)

Sefa Pamuk, José Luis Bernal, Azadeh Moradinezhad Dizgah
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors provide the first first-principles derivation of the line-intensity-mapping (LIM) power-spectrum super-sample covariance (SSC), and as a by-product the non-Gaussian in-box covariance contributions, using a halo-model plus standard-perturbation-theory combination that captures nonlinearity and non-Gaussianity. The SSC (equivalently field-to-field variance) cannot be estimated from the data itself yet is essential for correctly inferring ensemble-averaged global parameters rather than the values in the observed patch. Validated against painted N-body simulations, the newly derived terms dominate at intermediate and small scales whenever the covariance is not instrumental-noise-limited; their relative importance is roughly independent of survey volume but depends on the power-spectrum response to large-scale modes for each line and redshift.

Covariance estimation is a named LSS technique in the profile, and the same SSC / beyond-Gaussian covariance reasoning applies directly to forest P1D/P3D covariance modelling for current and next-generation surveys.

### [TNFlow: Amortized Posterior Inference for Trans-Neptunian Object Surface Composition](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04305v1)

Agastya Gaur, Cristina M. Dalle Ore, Alessandra Ricca
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.EP, cs.LG

TNFlow couples a transformer to a normalizing flow to perform amortized posterior inference of Trans-Neptunian-object surface composition from reflectance spectra, trained on Shkuratov-radiative-transfer synthetic spectra so as to act as the model's inverse. It inverts one spectrum in ~0.7 s on a single CPU core, returning a multimodal posterior over simplex-valid compositions and grain sizes, and generalizes to unseen combinations of known components (mean total-variation distance 0.149 on the synthetic test split). On real JWST spectra it shows blindness or bias toward some materials, which the authors attribute to either simulator fidelity or training-set coverage.

The astronomy target is off-focus, but the method is a clean §6 match — a transformer-plus-flow amortized/simulation-based inversion of a physical forward model from spectra — the same pattern behind data-driven quasar-continuum and forest SBI, and a useful reference architecture for the QGPT project.

*No figures extracted for 2609.04305 (method-demonstration paper; the PIT and exemplar-spectrum plots are diagnostic rather than a single clarifying result).*

### [An Analysis of Self-supervised Pre-training with Dependent Samples](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05031v1)

Maximilian Fleissner, Debarghya Ghoshdastidar, Samory Kpotufe
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

This is a statistical-learning analysis of self-supervised pretraining that asks whether pooling many mutually dependent augmentations of the same datapoint — as done in practice — is harmful for learning a low-complexity invariant subspace. The authors prove that naive pooling is never statistically worse than the theory-standard baseline of partitioning data into independent subsets, and in some regimes (masking or noise-injection augmentations over a shallow network) yields strictly faster estimation rates in the number of augmentations. The benefits are most pronounced when correlations between augmentations mildly affect estimation or actively reduce its variance.

A §6 self-supervised / contrastive representation-learning result with genuine theory behind it — directly relevant to the owner's self-supervised transformer-for-spectra (QGPT) project, where masking-style augmentations of continuous spectral data are exactly the dependent-sample regime analyzed here. No astronomy hook required, per the profile.

*No figures extracted for 2609.05031 (a theory paper; figures are illustrative masking/augmentation examples, not central results).*

### [H I Absorbers as Beacons of Hidden Structure at $z\sim3$: Multi-Component, Metal-Rich Absorption System near a Protocluster](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04349v1)

Kentaro Koretomo, Nobunari Kashikawa, Toru Misawa et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

A background quasar at $z=3.09$ sightline confirms a protocluster at $z=3.079$ that shows no strong H I absorption, while the same spectrum serendipitously reveals a prominent, exceptionally broad ($\sim2000$ km/s, $\sim40$ cMpc) H I absorber offset by $\sim60$ cMpc at $z\sim3.01$. Kinematic modelling decomposes this absorber into five distinct components rather than a single cloud, one of which has super-solar metallicity ($[\mathrm{O/H}]=+1.19$). The authors propose either a hidden line-of-sight protocluster or metal-rich outflows and metal-poor inflows driven by a single massive galaxy, and argue that strong H I absorbers can serve as beacons for otherwise-hidden massive structures.

Quasar-absorption-line work through the same IGM/CGM lens the owner uses; notably their continuum handling employs QFA and SpenderQ — the data-driven quasar-continuum models tracked in the profile — making both the absorber science and the pipeline choices relevant to forest continuum-fitting systematics.

### [Widespread Inflows Reveal Baryonic Cycling in Star-forming and Quiescent Galaxies](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04327v1)

Hassen M. Yesuf, Ravi Joshi, Yuxuan Zou et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

Using DESI spectroscopy of ~30,000 galaxies, the authors identify coherent inflowing gas (~100 km/s) in 20-50% of the sample and uncover a striking inversion: inflows are detected preferentially in quiescent galaxies, while star-forming systems are dominated by gravitationally bound outflows. Gas-flow state correlates with stellar-population age and recent evolutionary history rather than mass, environment, or structure, and broad metallicity distributions (with no pristine-dilution signature) imply the detected inflows are mostly recycled or enriched. Star-forming disks drive galactic-fountain outflows recycling on ~0.5 Gyr timescales, while quiescent systems show low-level "drizzling" inflows consistent with slowly cooling halo gas.

A circumgalactic-medium / baryon-cycling census at DESI survey scale — the emission-line sibling of the owner's quasar-absorption IGM/CGM interest (§7), and a demonstration of what large spectroscopic samples reveal about gas flows around galaxies.

*No figures extracted for 2609.04327 (the census result is stated cleanly in text; the main figures are multi-panel population summaries that do not reduce to one key plot).*

### [Recovering Ionizing Photon Escape and Galaxy Scaling Relations in the LzLCS via Si II and C II Absorption Lines and Mock Spectra from a Radiation-Hydrodynamic Simulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04324v1)

John Trevino, Simon Gazagnes, Danielle A. Berg et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors use a radiation-hydrodynamic simulation of a single ~$10^9\,M_\odot$ galaxy, plus a library of 22,500 mock spectra, to interpret Si II and C II low-ionization absorption profiles in stacked HST/COS spectra of 58 LzLCS+ galaxies across three mass bins and three stacking methods. Simulated and observed profiles agree well ($\chi^2<1$), and extracting line properties (equivalent width, residual flux) from best-fit mock profiles provides a robust, noise-resistant alternative to direct empirical trends for indirectly estimating the ionizing escape fraction $f_{\rm esc}$. The best-matching mocks come predominantly from peak-UV-luminosity starburst phases, and simulation-based $f_{\rm esc}$ reproduces the observed mass-dependent trends.

Ionizing sources and the escape fraction feed the UV background that sets IGM opacity — a §2 (reionization astrophysics) concern — and this is a transferable forward-modeling framework for inferring $f_{\rm esc}$ from absorption-line spectra of the galaxies that reionized the IGM.

*No figures extracted for 2609.04324 (stacked-spectrum and scaling-relation panels are supporting detail; the methodology and result are captured in the summary).*

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Ion abundances in the plasma tail of 3I/ATLAS show that it is N2-rich](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05393v1)

Lea Ferellec, Cyrielle Opitom, Colin Snodgrass
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP | also: astro-ph.GA

Post-perihelion WHT/WEAVE-LIFU integral-field spectroscopy of the third known interstellar object, 3I/ATLAS, simultaneously detects the ions N2+, CO+, CO2+, H2O+, and CH+ in an anti-solar plasma tail. The measured N2+/CO+ ratio yields a lower limit N2/CO$>0.023\pm0.001$, making 3I/ATLAS N2-rich relative to Solar-System comets — a property linked to very cold formation conditions around its parent star — while other ion ratios are consistent with enrichment in hypervolatiles. Integral-field spectroscopy of the tail (rather than the neutral coma) is what makes these ion measurements accessible.

Well outside the owner's subfield, but a genuinely notable comparative-planet-formation result: only the third interstellar object ever observed, carrying a chemical fingerprint of cold formation around another star — the kind of once-a-decade object worth knowing about as a scientist.
