## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [The Lumina Project: Morphology of Ionized Bubbles and Neutral Islands](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28390)

Meredith Neyer, Aaron Smith, Ali Sadain et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

A 3D watershed segmentation (with a 10% shared-surface merging criterion) applied to the ionization field of Lumina, a (500 cMpc)³ AREPO-RT radiation-hydrodynamics reionization simulation, measuring sizes, shape tensors and interior gas properties of ionized bubbles and late neutral islands. Volume-weighted bubble radii grow from ~1 cMpc at z≳10 to ≳100 cMpc by z≲6, and the watershed sizes exceed line-of-sight mean-free-path (MFP) sizes by roughly 3× at intermediate redshift — a direct warning when comparing MFP-style statistics to bubble-size models. Bubbles are mostly prolate, but at z≈6 the triaxiality distribution turns bimodal with an oblate peak (T≈0.25) from a few percolated giants. Residual neutral islands at z<6 peak near R_eff≈10 cMpc, stay prolate, and shrink to the resolution limit within Δz≲0.5 below z≈5.4 — the structures behind late-reionization dark gaps and opacity fluctuations.

### [Detection of metal absorption lines in quasar spectra: a neural network approach using U-Net](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28072)

Elena Sofia Mangola, Francesco Pistis, Michele Fumagalli et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

A 1D U-Net that flags metal-absorption pixels redward of Lyα (rest-frame 1230–3095 Å) in WEAVE-QSO-like mock spectra, trained on 50,000 mocks. Completeness, purity and F1 all reach ~90% at line S/N≈4 and ~100% at S/N≥8; line centres are recovered with σ≈0.2 Å (73.5% within one pixel), and every false positive at S/N≥5 sits in DLA damping wings. Crucially, swapping the true continuum for the companion autoencoder continuum barely moves performance (F1 within ~5% at S/N≈4, identical by S/N≈6), so the detector survives realistic continuum error. Ion identification is deferred to a companion paper; this is a survey-scale absorber finder directly usable for WEAVE-QSO/DESI metal masking and forest contamination studies.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Radio Quasars in the Cosmic Dawn: A Spectroscopic Sample from the DESI Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26931)

Anniek J. Gloudemans, Jinyi Yang, David M. Alexander et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Cross-matches 646 DESI-confirmed quasars at 4.7<z<6.7 (from a dedicated secondary target program) with LoTSS-DR3 at 144 MHz, finding 83 radio detections and 26 radio-loud objects (5–15% radio-loud fraction). UV composites and CIV velocity shifts are indistinguishable between radio-detected and non-detected quasars; radio sources show slightly lower Lyα/CIV equivalent widths, but the weak-line-quasar excess is only ~1.3σ. BAL quasars (106 in the sample) are predominantly radio-quiet with no correlation between absorption index and radio loudness, and a luminosity-matched z~2 comparison shows no evolution in radio-loudness — useful DESI high-z quasar demographics and BAL context.

### [Multivariate Continuous-Time Autoregressive Moving Average Processes for Astronomical Multiband Time Series](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28419)

Izak Schmidlkofer, Zhirui Hu, Lishan Shi et al.
**Primary category:** stat.AP | also: astro-ph.IM

Generalizes CARMA (the damped-random-walk family) to multiband light curves: each band keeps its own CARMA dynamics while cross-band dependence enters through correlated Brownian drivers, with Kalman-filter likelihoods, a two-stage stabilized MLE, and AICc order selection for irregular, heteroscedastic, partially observed data. In simulations, joint fitting improves parameter recovery in 23/27 and PSD recovery in 26/27 settings over separate single-band fits, while higher-order structure becomes weakly identifiable under coarse cadence or near pole–zero cancellation. Three SDSS Stripe 82 quasars select MCARMA(1,0), (2,0) and (2,1), with joint modelling changing inferred order and PSD for two of them; released as the `mcarma` package.

### [Differentiable astrophysics at scale: solving and differentiating ODE ensembles on the GPU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28458)

A. Spurio Mancini
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.CO

Introduces GRADSOLVE, a JAX library that integrates each ODE ensemble member in its own GPU thread with its own adaptive Runge–Kutta step size and returns forward-mode parameter sensitivities in the same pass (reverse mode also supported). At fixed accuracy on stellar-stream orbits, a dark-energy expansion history (with a DESI DR2 + Pantheon+ profile likelihood), and binary-black-hole spin precession, it runs 8.5–1500× faster than 128-core CPU codes for 10⁶ trajectories and 11–15× faster than diffrax on the same GPU. Aimed at small non-stiff systems, it makes HMC/gradient-based inference over million-member ODE ensembles routine.

### [From starlight to dark matter: a stochastic interpolation approach to map dark matter from stellar density](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27028)

Xiaowei Ou, Lina Necib, Carolina Cuesta-Lazaro et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.IM

Trains a conditional stochastic interpolant (U-Net drift) to map 2D stellar-density maps of Milky-Way-mass galaxies to dark-matter density maps using the DREAMS zoom-in suite. Held-out DREAMS galaxies are recovered to ~0.09 dex, and 100 stochastic draws per galaxy give calibrated ~0.1 dex per-pixel uncertainties, including a recovered m=2 anisotropy orientation. Out-of-domain tests transfer qualitatively to TNG50 but over-predict FIRE inner profiles (r≲5 kpc) by 0.2–0.4 dex, a clear demonstration of how sensitive such generative inverse maps are to the galaxy-formation model — a well-diagnosed example of the stochastic-interpolant/UQ toolkit.

### [JWST evidence for a sharp "Cosmic Daybreak" at z = 15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28257)

Jiashuo Zhang, Tom Broadhurst, Tzihong Chiueh et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

Using 22 spectroscopically confirmed z>10 JWST galaxies with stellar ages, the authors convert ages to formation redshifts and find none above z_f≈15.1, arguing for an abrupt onset of star formation rather than a gradual cosmic dawn. They interpret this as the wave-dark-matter (ψDM) Jeans-mass threshold (~10⁹ M⊙ at z=15), implying m_ψ≈2.2×10⁻²² eV after simulation calibration, and reproduce the M_UV–z distribution with a toy constant-SFH model. The inference rests on a small sample and a very simple model, and the implied boson mass sits well below published Lyα-forest fuzzy-DM lower limits — worth reading critically as a counterpoint to forest constraints.

### [Hidden not Deleted: How Networks Suppress Entangled Features](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27593)

Akash Samanta, Manish Pratap Singh, Debasis Chaudhuri
**Primary category:** cs.LG

In a toy superposition autoencoder where two features share an antipodal subspace (entanglement ρ), linear-projection concept erasure (INLP-style) damages the non-target feature, raising its reconstruction MSE from 0.098 at ρ=0 to 0.411 at ρ=−1, while gradient-based excision keeps it near 0.02. Gradient descent converges to one of two circuit-level "mirror" or "shadow" solutions, with a basin-prevalence crossover near ρ≈−0.75 (100 seeds per point). Causal interventions show both solutions retain the erased feature, recoverable with a single scalar activation patch — a mechanistic account of why suppression-not-deletion lets unlearned features resurface. Toy-scale, but directly on the null-space projection/concept-erasure thread.

### [What Converges in the Platonic Representation Hypothesis? Structure over Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27252)

Junwon You, Mihyun Jang, Sangwoo Mo et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.CV

Separates two confounded axes in representation-similarity studies — local vs global scale, and relational structure (which samples are related) vs metric geometry (distances) — introducing H₀ skeleton overlap from persistent homology as a global analogue of mutual kNN, plus distance-aware variants, all permutation-calibrated. Across 204 LLM–ViT pairs, relational structure converges at both scales (mKNN vs H₀ Spearman 0.968), while demanding distance agreement sharply weakens alignment and flattens the capacity trend (89.7% vs 40.2% significant at the strictest tolerance). Results replicate under a Riemannian metric and on video–text models; a useful framework for choosing similarity measures when comparing spectral foundation-model embeddings.

### [Improving Ensemble Filters with Flow Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28015)

Haoyuan Chen, Alexandre Thiéry
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

FlowEF learns a nonlinear analysis update by conditional flow matching: training transports a localized Gaussian source built from the forecast ensemble, and at deployment the forecast members themselves are transported, with the velocity field conditioned on a classical baseline filter's ensembles and the observation. It improves RMSE and CRPS over EnKF, ETKF, DEnKF and LETKF on Lorenz-96, Kuramoto–Sivashinsky and Kolmogorov flow (64×64 with 1.6% of the state observed), e.g. LETKF RMSE 1.32→1.18 with coverage 0.72→0.90 on Lorenz-96, and beats learned DA baselines. Coverage still falls below nominal in some settings and each system/filter pair needs retraining; a clean template for flow-matching posterior correction on top of Gaussian approximations.

### [A Scaling Study for fMRI Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27232)

Wenhao Ye, Xuanye Pan, Junfeng Xia et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

A controlled scaling grid (>10,000 GPU-hours, >200 source datasets) for a JEPA-style fMRI time-series foundation model, varying pretraining data (2.6k–63k recordings), model size (~2M–92M parameters) and training duration under a fixed downstream protocol. Compute alone does not predict performance: extra data helps more at larger model sizes, and at matched compute more data beats more parameters on 8 of 12 tasks. Configurations locked using in-distribution results then give the best average out-of-distribution performance among compared fMRI foundation models at lower compute — a transferable recipe for data/model/duration trade-offs when pretraining a spectral foundation model on a finite survey.
