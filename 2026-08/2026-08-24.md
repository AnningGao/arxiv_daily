## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Physical Calibration of a Minimal Effective Field Theory of the Three-Dimensional Lyman-$\alpha$ Forest](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21226v1)

Gabriele Autieri, Vid Iršič, Tomáš Šoltinsky et al. (Iršič & Viel — both high-value forest/IGM co-authors from the interest file)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors calibrate a deliberately *minimal* EFT of the 3D Lyman-$\alpha$ forest against the Sherwood and Sherwood–Relics hydrodynamical simulations, modelling both the flux auto-power spectrum $P^{\alpha\alpha}(k,\mu)$ and its cross-power spectrum with the dark matter density field. Rather than the full 19-parameter one-loop model, they truncate at tree level and keep only the leading counterterms and stochastic terms, and show this suffices to fit the simulated auto-spectrum to $k_{\rm max}=3\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ and the cross-spectrum to $2\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$. Even in this reduced model they find strong parameter degeneracies (an unconstrained $P_{\rm shot}$ can go negative and drag the counterterms and biases with it), and they trace the redshift evolution of the linear biases $b_{\rm F}$, $b_\eta$ primarily to the effective optical depth $\tau_{\rm eff}$, with reionisation history affecting mainly the linear biases. They also report empirical correlations between EFT parameters and $b_{\rm F}$ that could reduce the free-parameter count in future fits.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [One-point matter PDFs beyond TopHat filters](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20457v1)

Anton Chudaykin, Alexander M. Kayssi, Sergey Sibiryakov
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph, hep-th

This paper builds an analytic path-integral model for the one-point PDF of the matter density smoothed with an *arbitrary* spherically symmetric window, factorizing it into a spherical-collapse exponential, a monopole prefactor, and an aspherical prefactor that carries the EFTofLSS counterterm sensitivity. A numerical pipeline computes the spherical part (validated to sub-percent against known TopHat results) and shows that, once filters are normalized to a common linear variance, the PDFs are remarkably filter-independent — a potential $\Lambda$CDM null test — while the most-probable density profiles differ strongly for overdensities but collapse to a universal curve for underdensities (relevant for void interiors). Comparison against the high-resolution Quijote suite confirms the weak filter and redshift dependence, and a 1-loop expression approximates the aspherical prefactor to $\lesssim15\%$ up to $\delta_*\simeq1$. The authors note the EFT counterterm sensitivity is essentially the same for smooth and sharp filters, only reduced by using larger cells.

### [On what scales does strong lensing robustly constrain the mass profile of low-mass perturbers?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20454v1)

Maryam Tajalli, Simona Vegetti
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

Starting from the Fisher information matrix, the authors give a physically motivated definition of the "robust radius" at which the projected enclosed mass of a low-mass lensing perturber is measured most precisely — the pivot radius where the local amplitude and logarithmic slope of the profile decorrelate. They show this radius is, to leading order, a weighted geometric mean of the arc-pixel distances from the halo (weighted by deflection angle, macro-model magnification, and source surface-brightness gradient), and that any sufficiently flexible profile that fits the data recovers nearly the same enclosed mass there, making it model-independent. Beyond the amplitude, they show resolved lensed emission can constrain the local slope and even curvature of a perturber's profile given enough radial leverage and signal-to-noise. The framework applies to both subhaloes and line-of-sight haloes and contrasts explicitly with the earlier geometric "perturbation radius" of Minor et al.

### [Model independent lensing sub-structure detection with multiply-imaged star cluster constellations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21253v1)

Leo W. H. Fung, Tom Broadhurst, Sung Kei Li et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Fung et al. propose a fully model-independent, data-driven way to detect dark-matter substructure in strong lenses, without ever fitting a lens or source model. They exploit the optical Liouville theorem — that a compact sub-halo makes the size magnification $\mu_{\rm size}$ and flux magnification $\mu_{\rm flux}$ of point-like sources disagree ("geometrical duality" breaking), even when the sub-halo's Einstein radius is below the telescope's diffraction limit and produces no visible astrometric shift. Applied as a proof of concept to a "constellation" of 11 lensed compact star clusters in two giant arcs of SMACS0723 (JWST), the method finds self-consistency with non-detection except for one outlier pair, which realistic simulations show can be reproduced by a $10^8$–$10^9\,M_\odot$ sub-halo — though other explanations are not yet excluded. The dominant systematic is the precision on $\mu_{\rm size}$ when the arc is highly stretched, motivating future use of near-isotropic "umbilic" cluster configurations.

### [Diversity of Galaxy Centers from Small-Scale Isocurvature](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21323v1)

Jessica N. Lopez-Sanchez, Wen Yin
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc, hep-ph

Using N-body simulations, the authors follow a subdominant ultralight dark matter (ULDM) component that carries large-amplitude small-scale isocurvature perturbations, and ask how its abundance varies within galaxies. Although nonlinear evolution largely homogenizes the ULDM fraction on galactic scales, they find ULDM can still be strongly enhanced — dominating more than half of the central dark-matter density beyond $0.01R_{\rm vir}$ — in the centers of small halos, because large initial ULDM fluctuations seed the early potential wells that later become halo cores. This central segregation can produce either cusp-like or core-like centers depending on the initial fluctuations, offering a single multicomponent-DM route to the observed diversity of galaxy centers. The authors note this also undermines the usual assumption that a direct-detection signal scales with a component's *cosmological* fraction, since the local fraction need not match it.

### [NestyNet. III. Symbolic Regression from Analytic Neural Surrogates](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21051v1)

Rodrigo Ibata, Wassim Tenachi, Foivos Diakogiannis et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: physics.data-an

NestyNet-SR trains a neural surrogate with analytic derivatives, then uses those exact gradients and Hessians to detect separability and recursively decompose a multivariate problem into simpler "neural atoms," which a tiered symbolic-search stack distills into closed form. Its final tier is a factorized symbolic search that separates structure from calibration, so nested constants are *fitted* rather than searched. On the SRBench AI-Feynman benchmark it reports exact symbolic recovery of all 120 noiseless equations — claimed as the first such result — and the standalone factorized search alone recovers 88/115 in a hard one-shot oracle setting. As a real-data vignette, given only the separate mass-model components of SPARC galaxies it rediscovers the baryonic acceleration coordinate and the radial acceleration relation, with held-out generalization and a calibrated abstention.

### [Mapping the z>=5 SiIV Column Density Distribution onto the Galaxy Stellar Mass Function Using the Cumulative Absorption Cross Section](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20609v1)

Kristian Finlator, Sam Patterson, Nora Nava et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors introduce a technique that connects the observed metal-absorber column density distribution (CDD) to the galaxy stellar mass function (SMF) through a six-parameter model for the cumulative absorption cross section (CACS), calibrated on cosmological reionization simulations rather than assuming a monotonic absorber-strength–host-mass relation. A maximum-likelihood analysis reconciles the observed $z\sim5$–6 SiIV CDD with the observed SMF, and shows that galaxies of all masses host absorbers of all strengths, with only a weak association of strong absorbers with massive galaxies. Faint galaxies ($M_*<10^8\,M_\odot$) host the majority of weak SiIV absorbers, emphasizing the complementarity of emission- and absorption-selected samples at the end of reionization. The framework is general to any ion/redshift given a well-constrained SMF, though current data leave real parameter degeneracies.

### [A comparative study of emergent dark energy models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20415v1)

Andrés García-Rivera, A. Hernández-Almada, Miguel A. García-Aspeitia et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

This paper runs a uniform comparison of late/emergent dark-energy models — PEDE, GEDE, the graduated (GDE) and variable-curvature (VC) models — each augmented with a curvature term, against Cosmic Chronometers, SN Ia, DESI-DR1 BAO, and CMB. By AIC/BIC, the flat/curved $\Lambda$CDM baseline remains preferred overall, with PEDE and GEDE competitive (PEDE shares $\Lambda$CDM's degrees of freedom and marginally beats it for CC data) and GDE most penalized. The variable-curvature model is statistically the least favored but is the *only* one that produces a decelerating phase near $z=0$, which the authors argue is consistent with recent DESI hints, and it best fits Planck's near-flat curvature. The reconstructions of $w_{\rm eff}(z)$ place all models except VC in the phantom regime.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Efficient Event Generation for High-Multiplicity LHC Processes: An End-to-End GPU Workflow with Normalizing Flows](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21338v1)

Enrico Bothmann, Joshua Isaacson, Claudius Krause et al.
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: hep-ex

The authors present the first end-to-end, GPU-resident event-generation workflow that couples normalizing-flow proposals to a parton-level generator (Pepper), with helicity-conditioned coupling flows trained online (with sample replay) and deployed across all subprocesses of full proton–proton collisions with many jets. Flow sampling, proposal-density evaluation, and unweighting run on-device while the matrix elements and phase-space factors defining the target density are evaluated in the same memory, avoiding host–device transfer bottlenecks. Benchmarked on processes up to $e^+e^-+5j$ and $t\bar t+4j$, it generates $10^9$ unweighted events per process on four H100 GPUs, achieving end-to-end speedups of up to two orders of magnitude and turning multi-week runs into sub-day computations.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [From Timbuktu to SKA: Who owns the Astronomy knowledge Africa produces?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21119v1)

Rhea Koch, Amare Abebe
**Primary category:** physics.soc-ph

Using Web of Science bibliometric data on Astronomy & Astrophysics publications from 2000–2025, the authors examine where African astronomical knowledge is produced, validated, and circulated. They document a paradox: African researchers are active contributors to global astronomical discovery, yet the intellectual capital they generate is overwhelmingly stored, peer-validated, and circulated through publishing infrastructures located outside the continent. Framing this through a decolonial lens on citation impact and "ownership," they argue that dominant publishing systems shape visibility and authority, and call for a strategic shift in publication choices toward building a sovereign African knowledge archive — set against a long African intellectual heritage from Timbuktu manuscript astronomy to the SKA.
