## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Cosmology in the Einstein Telescope era: comparing traditional and simulation-based methods for population inference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04005v1)

Giovanni Antinozzi, Guillermo Franco Abellán, Davide Sciotti et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM, gr-qc

This is a head-to-head of Hierarchical Bayesian Inference (HBI) against simulation-based inference (SBI) for cosmological population inference, using a mock catalogue of $O(10^4)$ Einstein Telescope binary-black-hole mergers (dark sirens, no electromagnetic counterparts). Inferring $H_0$ and $\Omega_m$ in flat $\Lambda$CDM with both an analytic hierarchical likelihood and Marginal Neural Ratio Estimation (MNRE, via their `Sireeni` code), they find SBI reproduces the HBI posteriors to high accuracy while costing orders of magnitude less once simulation and training are amortized. They further show SBI extends straightforwardly to a joint cosmology-plus-astrophysics fit (adding the star-formation-rate-density parameters) at negligible extra cost, where HBI would become prohibitive.

### [The One-Loop Power Spectrum of Fast Radio Burst Dispersion Measures](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03235v1)

Haruki Ebina, Martin White
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Ebina & White build the one-loop effective-field-theory (EFT) description of the free-electron auto-spectrum $P_{ee}$ and the electron-galaxy cross-spectrum $P_{eg}$, placing FRB dispersion-measure clustering on the same theoretical footing as spectroscopic galaxy analyses. Validating jointly against $P_{ee}$, $P_{eg}$, and $P_{gg}$ from the FLAMINGO hydrodynamical simulations for DESI-like BGS/LRG samples at $z=0.2$ and $0.5$, the model fits all three spectra to $k\sim0.2\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ with an electron linear bias $b_{e,1}\simeq0.92$, higher-order biases consistent with zero, and near-perfect electron-matter correlation $r_{em}\simeq1$ — establishing free electrons as nearly unbiased, feedback-robust tracers of matter. They also identify which spectroscopic samples suffice for cross-correlation and argue Hybrid EFT (HEFT) would extend the modeling reach a further factor of 2–3.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [SCRIPT in the Cosmic Dawn: Distinguishing Redshift-Evolving Galaxy Populations with 21-cm Fluctuations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03441v1)

Janakee Raste, Tirthankar Roy Choudhury
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors extend the explicitly photon-conserving semi-numerical framework SCRIPT to self-consistently compute spin-temperature fluctuations driven by inhomogeneous Lyman-α coupling and X-ray heating during Cosmic Dawn. Comparing a JWST-informed model with a redshift-evolving star-formation efficiency against a static baseline, they show the *global* 21-cm signal is badly degenerate — a static population can mimic the evolving one's absorption trough if a steep, mass-dependent X-ray efficiency shifts IGM heating onto abundant low-mass halos. The degeneracy is broken definitively by the 21-cm *power spectrum*, whose amplitude retains the structural memory of source-halo clustering bias and is substantially lower for the tuned static model.

### [Tomographer: End-to-end Redshift Distribution Estimation for Source Catalogs and Intensity Maps](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03415v1)

Yi-Kuan Chiang, Yu Voon Ng, Yu-Ren Lin et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

Tomographer is an end-to-end clustering-redshift framework that estimates the bias-weighted redshift distribution $b(z)\,{\rm d}N/{\rm d}z$ of an arbitrary tracer via spatial cross-correlation with a spectroscopic reference. Its key design move is a set of precomputed "activation maps" encoding the spatial pair information of 3 million SDSS spectroscopic galaxies and quasars up to $z\sim4$ over $10{,}000\,{\rm deg}^2$, which eliminates user-side pair counting and collapses the computational scaling from $O(N\log N)$ to $O(1)$ map multiplications. The framework is validated against known-redshift samples, shown robust to footprint, spatially varying selection, beam smoothing, and foreground contamination, and applies to both source catalogs and diffuse intensity maps from radio to X-rays.

### [Bridging perturbation theory and simulations: initial conditions and fast integrators for cosmological simulations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03400v1)

Oliver Hahn
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

These are pedagogical lecture notes on generating initial conditions for cosmological N-body simulations, starting from Gaussian random fields and their efficient Fourier-space generation, through the Vlasov-Poisson system and its solution via Lagrangian perturbation theory (LPT). Hahn emphasizes high-order LPT and late-time starts to minimize truncation and discreteness errors, and closes with time-integration schemes including PT-informed integrators. The stated aim is to bridge theoretical perturbation methods and practical simulation technique.

### [Fisher Forecasting for the DESC with Augur](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03876v1)

Paul Rogozenski, Sankarshana Srinivasan, Javier Sánchez et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

This paper introduces `Augur`, the LSST Dark Energy Science Collaboration (DESC) Fisher-forecasting tool, built on DESC's own software frameworks so forecasts stay consistent with the collaboration's analysis pipelines. The authors validate it against external forecasting code and against direct posterior sampling via nested sampling, finding good agreement across methods, and use a harmonic-space $3\times2$pt analysis to explore how modeling and hyperparameter choices (e.g. redshift-bin knots) affect the forecast figure of merit. The tool is positioned as a continually updated, low-cost way to gauge proposed analysis choices before committing to full inference.

### [Horizontal Magnetic Fields Dominate the Quiet Sun Internetwork in Sunrise III Observations: Evidence from Traditional and Transformer-Based Inversions](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03776v1)

Ryan J. Campbell, Carlos Quintero Noda, Manuel Collados et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.SR

The science result is a solar one — quiet-Sun internetwork fields are strongly horizontal-dominated (median $B_h/|B_{\rm LOS}|\approx9$ near $\log\tau=-1.2$) — but the methodological result is what travels: the authors run a transformer-based inversion (SINN), trained *exclusively* on synthetic Stokes profiles from a radiative-MHD simulation, alongside the traditional DeSIRe code on the same Sunrise III/SCIP spectropolarimetric data. Both fundamentally different methods agree quantitatively (internetwork medians 64/7 G vs 71/8 G), demonstrating that a transformer trained on simulations transfers cleanly to real observations while running inference orders of magnitude faster (4.4 s on a GPU vs traditional inversion times).

### [Deep Divide-and-Reduce in Symbolic Regression](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02628v1)

Yusong Deng, Yanjie Li, Weijun Li
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: cs.AI

DDRSR is a symbolic-regression method that generalizes the decomposition strategy of AI Feynman: rather than relying on brute-force searches over sub-expressions, it uses rigorously derived mathematical properties to decompose and reduce expressions with wider applicability and provable correctness. The authors show the approach broadens where expression decomposition is valid, circumvents the sub-structure search that limits AI Feynman on complex equations, and yields empirical gains on both decomposition and numerical-regression benchmarks. They also delimit the applicable regime and remaining limitations.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Iron Kα signatures from accretion disks around fermionic dark matter cores](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03797v1)

V. Crespi, F. L. Vieyro, C. R. Argüelles et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE | also: astro-ph.GA

Within the extended RAR model — where a self-gravitating fermionic dark-matter distribution forms a compact, highly degenerate core surrounded by a diffuse halo of the same particle — the authors compute relativistic iron Kα line profiles from an accretion disk around the core using the Skylight ray-tracing code, and contrast them with the standard Kerr black-hole prediction. The most compact fermion cores produce line broadening comparable to rapidly spinning black holes, but with distinctive extra features arising from emitting matter at radii smaller than a gravitational radius. For MCG-06-30-15, a near-critical-compactness fermion core reproduces the observed broad iron line as well as a Kerr fit.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Rings in the Sky: Orbital Data Centres and Potential Impacts to Astronomy and the Sky](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02757v1)

Aaron C. Boley, Samantha M. Lawler, Hanno Rein
**Primary category:** astro-ph.EP | also: astro-ph.IM

The authors model the sky impact of proposed megaconstellations of orbital data centres (ODCs) using three real company designs, showing these systems would form large ring structures that each sweep through the sky twice per day and persist through the day in polar regions. Using simplified but calibrated brightness models, they find the rings would be visible during twilight and parts of the night, produce persistent infrared and radio source regions, and — depending on orbital-node confinement — could either concentrate into dense arcs or spread sky-wide interference. Their headline warning is that without brightness mitigation or hard caps on satellite numbers, certain times of night could host more visible satellites than visible stars.

### [The Research Impact of the Jodrell Bank Observatory and Other Facilities affected by the UK Science Funding Cuts in 2025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02723v1)

Rommulus Francis Lewis, Amruth Alfred, Hetansh Shah
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.GA

Following UKRI/STFC's 2025 funding cuts to facilities including the e-MERLIN radio network and the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope, the authors quantify the research impact of the affected facilities using a dataset of every astrophysics paper submitted to arXiv in 2025. They compare citation distributions of papers mentioning the de-prioritized facilities against those of a heavily used benchmark (JWST), finding the impact is not skewed by a few outliers but follows a distribution comparable to a highly cited flagship. The intent is to give the community and stakeholders an evidence base for judging the output of facilities losing support.
