### [How X-rays heat the IGM in different 21-cm simulation codes: a comparison between Licorice and Beorn](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14423v1)

Romain Meriot, Jonathan R. Pritchard, Timothée Schaeffer
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors process the same ionizing/X-ray sources through a 3D radiative-transfer code (Licorice) and a fast 1D-RT code (Beorn) with identical physics and parameters, then compare the resulting IGM temperature fields and 21-cm signal. Mean temperature histories agree to within Δz ≈ 0.2, but the 21-cm power spectra differ by ~20% (realistic) to 11% (idealised) on average — and by factors of 10–50 on some scales near the z ≈ 11–12 absorption trough — driven by 1D-RT approximations (homogeneous density in the optical-depth calculation, constant heating fraction f_heat) compounded by the source-binning needed to compress Licorice sources into Beorn profiles. Feeding these spectra through the Loremu II emulator and running inference at SKA-100h noise, the modeling differences bias recovered astrophysical parameters by ≳1σ, reaching ~4σ for the fiducial model. They conclude the codes are close enough for SKA pathfinders but that a ~20–30% modeling floor threatens unbiased SKA-era inference.

This is the closest paper of the day to the profile's core: IGM/reionization physics (primary focus) coupled to emulator-based inference and posterior-bias quantification (rising-primary ML-for-cosmology focus), and it speaks directly to the "joint Lyα + 21cm reionization inference" expansion candidate. The concrete demonstration that simulator choice alone shifts posteriors by several σ is a cautionary result for any SBI/emulator pipeline built on expensive RT simulations.

### [Cosmicflows-4 and the Cosmological Consistency of Large-Scale Motions](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14265v1)

Adi Nusser, Brent Tully
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using Cosmicflows-4 and its constituent catalogs, the authors fit monopole and dipole moments in non-overlapping radial bins out to 300 h⁻¹Mpc with a log-distance radial-velocity estimator, comparing each against a Planck-2018 ΛCDM prediction that folds in actual positions, weights, and velocity/measurement covariance. The full "All Individual" sample is broadly consistent (dipole deviation 2.29σ), but a localized feature at 120–160 h⁻¹Mpc reaches 3.40σ with |V_dip| = 628 ± 82 km/s, concentrated toward negative supergalactic X and strongly dependent on the survey window (TFR and 6dFGS favor larger dipoles; SDSS and SN smaller). Removing 6dFGS drops the upper-bin deviation to 1.56σ; removing SN raises it to 3.77σ. The conclusion is not a uniform bulk-flow anomaly but localized, window-dependent features that need selection-matched mocks to interpret.

Relevant as secondary-focus LSS cosmology and a careful case study in peculiar-velocity systematics: the covariance treatment, window dependence, and honest accounting for look-elsewhere effects are exactly the precision-cosmology methodology worth borrowing when assessing "anomalies" in survey data.

### [Constraining Primordial Power Asymmetry from Galaxy Clustering and Peculiar Velocity Information](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14170v1)

Keita Minato, Atsushi Taruya, Teppei Okumura et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors build a unified bipolar-spherical-harmonic (BipoSH) framework for the auto- and cross-spectra of galaxy density and line-of-sight peculiar velocity, and Fisher-forecast dipolar and quadrupolar primordial power asymmetry for a Euclid-like spectroscopic survey combined with Simons-Observatory-like kSZ velocity reconstruction. For the dipole, galaxy clustering dominates and velocities mainly provide systematics cross-checks. For the quadrupole, the galaxy auto-spectrum is strongly degenerate with the anisotropic galaxy-bias response; the density–velocity cross-spectrum breaks this degeneracy (different degeneracy direction) and the velocity auto-spectrum — independent of the bias parameter — gives a competitive, bias-immune constraint that beats BOSS for steep (k⁻¹, k⁻²) scale dependences.

Squarely in the secondary LSS / spectroscopic-survey focus, and useful on two methodological axes the profile emphasizes: Fisher forecasting for DESI/Euclid-class surveys, and kSZ velocity–reconstruction cross-correlations, which the paper notes have just moved from forecasts to real ACT DR6 / DESI DR2 detections.

*No figures extracted for 2608.14170 (the result is a set of Fisher constraint contours; the degeneracy-breaking logic is clear from the summary).*

### [Cosmographic Reconstruction of the Quintessence Potential in Scalar-Tensor Gravity](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14183v1)

G. Antonopoulos, L. Perivolaropoulos
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

Generalizing an existing cosmographic-reconstruction programme to scalar-tensor gravity, the authors derive exact closed-form expressions for the quintessence potential slope and curvature (λ_U, Γ_U) and the coupling slope/curvature (λ_F, Γ_F) in terms of the cosmographic parameters (q, j, s), Ω_m, and the Planck-mass running α_M, and recast α_M into the observable Ġ/G "gravitational tower." Evaluated against DESI DR2 BAO, supernovae, and Lunar Laser Ranging, the potential slope is recovered at the few-tenths level while its curvature is essentially unconstrained; Lunar Laser Ranging pins |g₁| ≲ 2×10⁻⁴, and a non-minimal coupling reopens the phantom region of cosmographic space through a single higher-order coefficient g₂.

Adjacent secondary-focus material: dynamical dark energy anchored to DESI DR2 BAO (a live topic in the library, cf. the DESI DR2 dark-energy addition) and a model-independent reconstruction approach that sidesteps assuming a specific dark-energy parameterization.

*No figures extracted for 2608.14183 (reconstructed-potential curves; the constraint story is captured by the text).*

### [Abundant Heavy Black Hole Seeds from Moderate Lyman-Werner Radiation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13656v1)

Ryan Hazlett, Eli Visbal, Greg L. Bryan et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

A suite of 65 high-resolution cosmological zoom-in simulations of 15 pristine atomic-cooling halos, spanning Lyman-Werner backgrounds J₂₁ = 0–300 and halo assembly rates 0.01–7 M⊙/yr, is used to test what regulates supermassive Pop III star (and hence heavy black-hole seed) formation. Introducing a new prescription that estimates the final Pop III mass from the gas infall profile at runaway collapse (validated against sink-particle results to within a factor ~2), they find a sharp transition between J₂₁ ≲ 1 and ≳ 10, with the high-J₂₁ halos sustaining inflow rates that yield stellar masses up to 10⁵ M⊙, while halo assembly timescale shows no significant correlation. A semi-analytic model shows intermediate-LW halos (1 ≲ J₂₁ < 10) are orders of magnitude more common than the high-J₂₁ tail, so heavy seeds may form in far more common environments than classical direct-collapse (J_crit ~ 10³–10⁴) requires.

Adjacent early-universe context: the ionizing-source populations and high-z quasar/AGN seeds tie into the reionization-era physics that underpins the IGM/forest program, and the "abundant seeds" picture speaks to the rapidly growing JWST high-z AGN population.

*No figures extracted for 2608.13656 (the key result is a J₂₁-vs-inflow bifurcation; adequately conveyed in prose).*

### [ATLAS. IV. A JWST+MUSE Demographic Study of Lyα Profiles in Little Red Dots](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14534v1)

Yuta Kageura, Masami Ouchi, Hiroto Yanagisawa et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Combining VLT/MUSE and JWST/NIRSpec grating spectroscopy of 31 little red dots (LRDs) at z = 3–9, the authors measure Lyα in 14 of them and find only 2 with broad Lyα (FWHM > 1000 km/s); the rest show narrow Lyα (FWHM 300–600 km/s), comparable to ordinary high-z star-forming galaxies. The broad-Lyα emitter fraction above 10⁴² erg/s is ~10%, about five times the ≤2% upper limit for star-forming galaxies but far from ubiquitous. They interpret the scarcity of broad Lyα as absorption by a high-column-density (log N_H ≳ 23–24), near-unity-covering-factor gas envelope around the central engine, with broad Lyα escaping only through lower-covering or clumpy/porous sightlines (e.g. Abell2744-QSO1).

Adjacent via Lyα radiative transfer and neutral-gas covering: although this is Lyα in emission from high-z sources rather than the forest in absorption, the physics of Lyα photons escaping (or being resonantly reprocessed by) dense neutral hydrogen is conceptually cousin to the IGM/CGM absorption modeling at the heart of the profile.

*No figures extracted for 2608.14534 (the paper's clearest visual is a schematic of Lyα escape geometry; the covering-factor argument is self-contained in the summary).*

### [Posterior Inference of Hamiltonian Parameters from RIXS Spectroscopy](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13848v1)

Samuel Klein, Thomas M. Linker, Louis Conreux et al.
**Primary category:** cond-mat.str-el | also: stat.ML

The first application of simulation-based inference to Hamiltonian-parameter extraction from resonant inelastic X-ray scattering (RIXS) spectra. Using truncated marginal neural ratio estimation (TMNRE) with a column-ViT spectral encoder and a flow-matching density estimator, they recover the full joint posterior over 11 Hamiltonian parameters for NiPS₃ and K₂NiF₄ at a simulation budget comparable to conventional optimization, treating the energy-loss broadening as a marginalized nuisance. The amortized posterior lets independent datasets on the same material be fused by simply multiplying posteriors, identifies loosely constrained parameters for active experimental design, and — because it targets an explicit prior/likelihood rather than minimizing a norm — keeps assumptions auditable; a single trained model generalizes across compounds of the same symmetry class.

Non-astronomy, but a near-direct methods transplant for the profile's rising-primary focus: this is a clean, reusable template for SBI on spectroscopic data — amortized TMNRE, ViT encoders on spectra, nuisance marginalization, posterior fusion — the exact machinery the library is accumulating (SpecPT, foundation models, flows, "Trust Crisis in SBI") for spectroscopic cosmology and forest inference.

*No figures extracted for 2608.13848 (the deliverable is an 11-parameter corner posterior; the methodological transfer is the point, and it is described in the text).*

### [Observational Constraints on Horizonless Compact Objects from Thermal Emission in AGNs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13645v1)

Ekin Oran, Avery Broderick, Kiana Salehi
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE

Any accreting horizonless "black-hole mimicker" — boson star, gravastar, fermion star, naked singularity — should develop an accretion-powered thermal photosphere; a true event horizon cannot. The authors select 104 radiatively inefficient, optically thin low-luminosity AGN from the Swift/BASS DR2 catalog (which uniquely supplies both the mass/distance/bolometric-flux needed to predict the photosphere and the optical spectra that would reveal it) and search for the predicted inverted-spectral-index feature. None is found; in 39 of 104 sources the optical flux falls conclusively below the photosphere prediction, giving strong population-level evidence for horizons, and archival VLBI already rules out the large, cool photospheres that could otherwise hide in 17 more.

Held to the Tier 3 bar because it extends horizon tests from the two imaged sources (M87*, Sgr A*) to a whole AGN population, turning "is there a horizon?" into a statistical statement across 100+ objects — a genuinely broad constraint on exotic-compact-object physics worth a cosmologist's awareness.

*No figures extracted for 2608.13645 (the result is a non-detection across a flux–prediction comparison; no single plot is needed to convey it).*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Unknown Unknowns: Model Misspecification in Machine Learning for Physics](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13633v1)

Juan Cruz-Martinez, Carolina Cuesta-Lazaro, Alexander Held et al.
**Primary category:** physics.data-an | also: astro-ph.CO, cs.LG

A perspective on model misspecification when ML models trained on simulation are deployed on real physics data. The central distinction: sometimes misspecification *is* the discovery (new physics as a failed model), and sometimes it is contamination to be absorbed (detector effects, foregrounds) without biasing the measurement — and a robust analysis must preserve sensitivity to the former while absorbing the latter. The authors stress that no diagnostic can confirm correct specification (only distributional adequacy), that high-dimensional ML both amplifies simulator defects and surfaces failures invisible to low-dimensional checks, and that detection and mitigation form an iterative loop backed by a battery of complementary diagnostics, blind analysis, and the recognition that data-driven methods relocate assumptions rather than remove them.

Meta-research squarely on the practice the profile is investing in: it is a methodology critique of ML/SBI-based inference in physics and astronomy, extending the "Trust Crisis in SBI" thread in the library and offering a concrete disposition for auditing emulator- and simulation-based cosmology pipelines against unknown unknowns.

### [Aether.jl: A High-Performance 3D MHD and Multifluid Dust Code Written in a Dynamic Language with an Interactive Human-AI Development Framework](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14048v1)

Ka Wai Ho
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

Aether.jl is a GPU-first finite-volume code for compressible hydro/MHD with constrained transport and an arbitrary number of dust fluids, written in Julia and built from scratch through an interactive workflow between the author and coding agents (Claude Code, Codex). Beyond the numerical methods, the paper documents a "human-AI development framework": it argues that current agents excel at one-shot, single-session tasks but that sustained development needs persistent project memory (via agent-harness configuration) to stop characteristic errors from recurring across sessions and model versions. On the performance side, the dynamic-language code matches or beats equivalent C++ single-GPU throughput and holds >93% weak-scaling efficiency to 4096 GCDs on Frontier.

A Tier 4 data point on how AI is changing the practice of computational astronomy: a concrete, documented case of agent-assisted development of production simulation software — the kind of methods-and-tooling shift (like foundation models entering the pipeline) the profile flags as a live "how AI changes the field" interest.
