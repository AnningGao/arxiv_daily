## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [A new measurement of the FRB DM-galaxy cross correlation and a first joint analysis with the kinematic SZ effect](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11296v1)

Samuel McCarty, Liam Connor, Kritti Sharma et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors cross-correlate the dispersion measures (DM) of 130 localized fast radio bursts — including new DSA-110 spectroscopic host redshifts — with 27.3 million DESI Legacy Survey Bright Galaxy Sample galaxies, detecting the DM-galaxy correlation at 6.5σ, the highest significance to date, in both configuration and harmonic space. Fitting a halo model (with the BCEmu baryonification prescription) and comparing to the FLAMINGO simulations, they disfavor a no-feedback scenario in which gas traces dark matter at ~9σ and directly measure electron clustering out to scales beyond a few Mpc. They show the FRB and recent kSZ optical-depth measurements agree at ~1σ across stellar-mass bins despite the very different systematics. Finally, they make a first attempt at breaking the kSZ optical-depth degeneracy by combining FRB (density-only) and kSZ (density×velocity) signals to jointly constrain the growth rate fσ8.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [CIBER × galaxy cross-correlations reveal a bright, low-redshift NIR background](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12116v1)

Richard M. Feder, Grigory Heaton, James J. Bock et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

This is the first tomographic analysis of near-IR extragalactic background light (EBL) anisotropies, cross-correlating CIBER 1.1 and 1.8 μm maps with DESI Legacy Survey DR8 and Hyper-Suprime-Cam photometric galaxy catalogs in redshift slices across 0 < z < 1. The measured cross-power exceeds an integrated-galaxy-light model on scales ℓ < 2000 and is concentrated at z ≲ 0.6, with cluster-member galaxies accounting for 15–20% of the large-angle signal. A parametric halo-model decomposition detects both one-halo and two-halo clustering at high significance, implying low-mass halos with satellites and/or diffuse intra-halo light amplify the low-z EBL. They also show correlated low-z structure contributes substantially to the previously reported CIBER auto-power.

### [Evidence for the First Globular Cluster Stellar Stream beyond the Milky Way](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12254v1)

Julie Kiel Holm, Sarah Pearson, Jacob Nibauer et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The authors report the first extragalactic globular-cluster stellar stream ("Oyashio"), identified independently in deep HST and CFHT imaging of the ultra-diffuse galaxy UGC9050-Dw1, with a 7.3σ prominence and a measured width of ~72 pc. Using generative stream modelling (the X-Stream sampler) that fits dynamical models directly to the stream morphology, they infer the host halo mass (log₁₀ M_halo/M_⊙ ≈ 11.3) and an inner density slope γ ≈ 0.9, the first stream-based halo constraint for a UDG. The result favors a massive dark-matter host and an inner profile less cored than typical low-surface-brightness dwarfs.

### [Distillation of Foundation Models for Time-dependent PDEs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11937v1)

Daniel Musekamp, Boshra Ariguib, Andrei Manolache et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG

Foundation models for time-dependent PDEs generalize across physical systems and fine-tune well in low-data regimes, but are too large to serve as fast surrogates. The authors propose TREX (Teacher Rollout Extension), a knowledge-distillation scheme that augments limited target-domain data by generating long synthetic trajectories from a fine-tuned teacher (optionally with periodic noise injection), exposing a compact student to long-horizon states and letting it add task-specific inductive biases such as equivariance. Across multiple PDE benchmarks the distilled students match or beat the teacher while cutting parameters by orders of magnitude and delivering >10× inference speedup.

### [Variational Parameter Calibration with Physics-Aware Latent-Space Surrogates](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11435v1)

Qiyao Zhou, Xujia Zhu, Pierre Joli et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: physics.comp-ph, physics.data-an

The authors build an autoencoder-based, end-to-end differentiable reduced-order surrogate that maps physical parameters to predicted fields through a latent space, then solve the inverse (parameter-calibration) problem variationally in parameter space via the surrogate-induced observation operator. A key finding is that reconstruction accuracy alone is insufficient for inversion — "observable supervision" during training is needed so latent variables retain parameter-correlated information and become separable. On CFD benchmarks under noisy, low-resolution, masked, and partial observations, the method reduces calibration error and variability relative to standard surrogates.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Constraints on ultralight bosons from merging binary and remnant black holes observed during the second and third parts of the fourth LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA observing run](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11620v1)

The LIGO Scientific Collaboration, the Virgo Collaboration, the KAGRA Collaboration et al.
**Primary category:** gr-qc | also: astro-ph.HE, hep-ph

Using O4b/O4c data, the collaboration runs directed searches for long-transient gravitational waves from ultralight vector-boson clouds formed by superradiance around the merger remnants of GW250114 and GW250207, finding no signal and disfavoring vector-boson masses in [2.80, 3.95]×10⁻¹³ eV at >90% confidence. They additionally derive constraints from the inferred high spins of black holes in three binaries, excluding (at 10⁵-yr ages) scalar bosons in [1.39, 6.94]×10⁻¹³ eV and vector bosons in [0.32, 14.4]×10⁻¹³ eV.

### [Testing Covarying Coupling Constants (CCC) against the full SPARC rotation-curve sample: a like-for-like comparison with MOND and NFW](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11575v1)

Rajendra P. Gupta, Nikolaos Samaras
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

The authors test the Covarying Coupling Constants framework — a density-keyed effective-mass field that modifies galactic dynamics without particle dark matter — against all 175 SPARC disc galaxies, on identical footing with MOND and one- and two-parameter NFW halos using reduced χ². Replacing an earlier unphysical sharp density turn-off with a smooth transition (introducing no new parameter), one-parameter CCC performs comparably to galaxy-by-galaxy-fitted MOND (lower χ² in 56% of galaxies; mean 2.58 vs 2.65, not significantly different), while two-parameter NFW is markedly worse. Recast as an acceleration, the CCC turn-off scale has 0.33 dex scatter, matching the MOND a₀ scale (0.34 dex).

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Which is the best day of the week to submit to arXiv:astro-ph?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11268v1)

Chirag Sharma, Shantanu Desai
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: physics.soc-ph

The authors examine whether arXiv preprint release day correlates with eventual citations, using ADS citation data for 37,173 refereed astro-ph papers (2020–2023) across six journals (MNRAS, ApJ, ApJL, ApJS, A&A, JCAP) matched to arXiv v1 submission timestamps. Grouping by submission weekday and by announcement-batch day, and using median citations with bootstrap confidence intervals, they find weekend submissions — which all land in the crowded Monday listing — receive 12–26% fewer median citations in five of six journals, while the five weekdays are statistically indistinguishable. The link is correlational, consistent with either compositional differences in weekend submissions or reduced visibility in enlarged Monday listings.
