## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Low Lyα Visibility in Galaxy Overdensities: Reionization Topology and Neutral-Fraction Ceilings from DIVER over 4.8<z<11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19311v1)

Yongda Zhu, Xiaohui Fan, Laura C. Keating et al. (incl. high-value IGM authors Y. Zhu & G. D. Becker)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

Deep JWST/NIRSpec observations (DIVER, GOODS-N) measure Lyα visibility for 250 galaxies at 4.8<z<11, with 84 Lyα detections including 44 strong emitters (W_Lyα>25 Å), and map the local density field using NIRCam-slitless Hα and [OIII] emitters. Galaxies with high Lyα equivalent width or high effective escape fraction preferentially lie *farther* from neighbors, and near the z~5.2 overdensity fewer than 15% show strong Lyα — opposite to the naive inside-out expectation that overdensities enhance Lyα visibility. The authors derive an empirical upper envelope for the effective Lyα escape fraction and calibrate it against reionization simulations to obtain one-sided neutral-fraction ceilings ⟨x_HI⟩_max = 0.36, 0.76, 0.74, 0.84, 1.0 at z~5.2, 5.8, 6.7, 7.7, 9.8. The z~8 ceiling disfavors an almost fully neutral IGM at that epoch and supports patchy reionization already underway by z~8.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [A Prediction for DESI Full-Shape: Increasing Tomographic Ωm(z) Trend](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19883v1)

Eoin Ó Colgáin, M. M. Sheikh-Jabbari
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors combine the recent DESI DR2 full-shape (FS) Lyman-α Ωm constraint at z_eff=2.33 (Ωm=0.325±0.018) with DR1 FS tracer constraints spanning 0.295≤z_eff≤1.491 and fit a line Ωm(z)=mz+c, finding slope m=0.022±0.012 — 1.8σ from a constant Ωm, up from ~1σ with the DR1 Lyman-α point. AIC and Bayesian evidence show the line and the constant are currently statistically indistinguishable, i.e. we may be right at the crossover point. Via the Om(z) diagnostic they argue an increasing Ωm(z) maps to phantom dark energy, whereas the mixed Ωm hierarchies of DESI-plus-external datasets make a phantom crossing inevitable if interpreted as dynamical DE. They forecast how the constraint tightens toward the final DESI release.

### [The Challenge of Observing Patchy Reionization with CMB Optical-Depth Fluctuations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19307v1)

Nick Takoudes, Hanjue Zhu, Nickolay Y. Gnedin
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using five Cosmic Reionization on Computers (CROC) radiation-hydrodynamical volumes, the authors build past lightcones and compute the Thomson optical-depth angular power spectrum C_ℓ^ττ directly from the simulated electron-density field, decomposing it into a patchy component (ionization-fraction fluctuations) and a density component (baryon-density fluctuations at fixed ionization). Baryon-density fluctuations dominate the high-redshift signal over most angular scales, and the accumulated low-redshift, fully-ionized contribution exceeds the high-z signal across the full multipole range. The implication is that a large fraction of C_ℓ^ττ power is *not* uniquely tied to reionization morphology, so future interpretations must model the density contribution explicitly.

### [A blind spot in transverse BAO calibration](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20296v1)

Domenico Sapone
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The paper shows that a constant calibration error in the transverse (angular) BAO scale is *exactly* degenerate with the combination r_d·h: it leaves the goodness of fit and recovered parameters unchanged, so it is invisible to any inference using transverse BAO alone. The radial BAO sector breaks the degeneracy by supplying D_M/r_d through integration without reference to H0, reducing a flat-FLRW consistency test to a straight line whose slope measures a relative transverse calibration ε. Applied to two SDSS transverse-BAO compilations, one gives ε=1.073±0.021 (3.8σ from unity) while the other is consistent with unity, and the two differ at 3.9σ with a redshift-independent offset — explaining why some analyses report 3.5–3.7σ tensions and others find consistency.

### [Does Early Dark Energy Absorb the DESI Late-Time Dynamics Signal? A Combined Analysis](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19432v1)

Mohammad Malekjani, Saeed Pourojaghi
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

The authors test whether DESI's preference for late-time dynamical dark energy (DDE) is genuinely a low-redshift phenomenon or an artifact of unresolved early-universe physics, by simultaneously allowing an Early Dark Energy (EDE) component and a CPL (w0-wa) late-time parametrization. Excluding DESI BAO, a joint CMB+Pantheon+ analysis within the EDE-extended framework keeps the CPL parameters statistically consistent with ΛCDM — i.e. a low-z DDE signal can be mimicked by an EDE component. However, once DESI BAO are included, a statistically significant departure from a cosmological constant re-emerges, favoring a phantom-to-quintessence transition that EDE cannot absorb.

### [Cluster finding with outskirt stellar masses and percolation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19768v1)

Pablo Avalos, Conghao Zhou, Tesla Jeltema et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

Optical cluster cosmology is limited by projection effects in galaxy-population finders such as the red sequence; this work proposes selecting clusters by the outer stellar mass M_out (e.g. the 50–100 kpc annulus of the central galaxy), which depends only on the central galaxy and is therefore largely free of projection effects. Its dominant systematic is instead satellite contamination, which the authors quantify with IllustrisTNG300 at z=0.4 (FoF satellite fractions ≤15% for M_out>10^10 M_⊙, <10% above 10^11) and mitigate with a simple proximity-based percolation that removes lower-M_out galaxies within a chosen radius of a more massive one. A moderately high cut (~4×10^10 M_⊙) with a ~2 R_200c percolation radius recovers a sample that is both complete and pure for halos ≳10^14 M_⊙.

### [Detectable subhalo impacts in Milky Way streams](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19321v1)

Junyang Lu, Elias Bernreuther, Tongyan Lin et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO, hep-ph

The authors build a pipeline and statistical framework to forecast the number of detectable dark-matter subhalo impacts on Milky Way stellar streams using morphological and kinematic data from LSST and Via, focusing on 14 well-modeled streams. Defining a detectable impact as a ≥95% CL deviation from a best-fit polynomial proxy (marginalizing over stream-modeling uncertainties), they find five streams with expected detectable impacts >0.2: Jet (5.15), Orphan-Chenab (1.40), ATLAS-Aliqa Uma (1.25), GD-1 (0.55), and Palomar 5 (0.40). Warm and fuzzy dark matter at current mass bounds suppress the count by a factor ~4, while a toy self-interacting DM model gives an O(1) enhancement.

### [Tracing Lyman alpha escape in the CRISTAL-02 galaxy at z~5.3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19439v1)

Arshi Ali, Lucia Guaita, Manuel Aravena et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Combining VLT/MUSE Lyα with JWST/NIRSpec IFU Hα/Hβ and NIRCam UV imaging, the authors construct spatially matched emission-line, extinction, escape-fraction and ionizing-photon-efficiency maps for the five-clump star-forming galaxy CRISTAL-02 at z~5.3. Lyα emission extends preferentially along the [CII] outflow direction while Hα and UV trace the galactic disk; the low-dust Clump A shows enhanced Lyα/Hα and a higher Lyα escape fraction, whereas the more UV/Hα-bright Clump B has suppressed Lyα. The evidence favors feedback-driven, anisotropic outflow-cleared escape, though the data cannot uniquely separate AGN- from star-formation-driven feedback.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Imaging the vacuum fluctuations of a quantum field](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20311v1)

Yansheng Zhang, Feiyang Wang, Yi Jiang et al.
**Primary category:** cond-mat.quant-gas | also: astro-ph.CO, hep-th

The authors directly image the spatial vacuum (ground-state) fluctuations of a bosonic quantum field using a homogeneous planar two-component Bose–Einstein condensate whose coherently-coupled spin degrees of freedom emulate a massive relativistic sine-Gordon field in the interaction-dominated regime. By quenching the coherent coupling and imaging the structure factor's post-quench oscillations, they extract scale-dependent fluctuation amplitudes that follow the theoretical prediction for a vacuum state (up to a ~0.7 detection-efficiency factor). This realizes a laboratory platform for relativistic-field vacuum states in the sine-Gordon limit, a regime that is presently not theoretically tractable.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [The Cross-Survey Decade: A Call to Action](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19272v1)

Gioia Rau, Robert Benjamin, Federica Bianco et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA

This community white paper argues that by 2027 three flagship wide-field surveys (representing more than $6 billion in US and European public investment) will observe overlapping sky and produce overlapping petabyte-scale datasets, shifting the bottleneck from observation to shared computational infrastructure and coordination. It issues a call to action built on four pillars: joint pixel-level processing and validation, an AI-ready data substrate for scientific foundation models, standardized and interoperable cross-survey data access, and dedicated personnel and career pathways. The authors outline concrete steps for agencies, observatories, universities, and philanthropy, arguing the moment to align foundational technical choices is now.
