## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [The optimal redshift for dark energy I: formalism and interpretation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25229v1)

Mustapha Ishak, Travis Seth Rippentrop, Kristian Gonzalez
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Introduces an "optimal redshift" for testing dark energy: within a generalized CPL parameterization they derive the redshift (scale factor) that maximizes the departure of the equation of state $w(a)$ from $-1$ relative to its uncertainty, i.e. that maximizes $|w(a)+1|/\sigma(w(a))$ rather than simply minimizing the variance as the usual pivot redshift does. Applied to DESI DR2 BAO + DES-Y6 BAO + the recalibrated DES-Dovekie supernovae, the optimum yields a $\sim2.8\sigma$ tension with $\Lambda$CDM versus $\sim2.6\sigma$ at the pivot. They give a clean geometric picture in CPL space: the pivot is the projection minimizing $w$'s variance, while the optimum maximizes squared distance from $w=-1$ over that variance. The formalism is dataset- and parameterization-dependent but the optimization principle generalizes beyond CPL.

Directly in the DESI large-scale-structure / dark-energy program that is one of your recent primary directions — a compact statistical reframing of how strongly current BAO+SN data actually disfavor a cosmological constant, and how to report it.

### [The optimal redshift for dark energy II: application to cosmological data and the evidence for the phantom crossing of the CPL equation of state](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25278v1)

Travis Seth Rippentrop, Mustapha Ishak, Kristian Gonzalez
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The companion application paper: it uses the optimal-redshift formalism to sharpen the evidence for a phantom crossing ($w(z)=-1$) in CPL fits to combinations of BAO, CMB, and supernova data. By choosing, for each dataset combination, the redshift that maximizes tension on each side of the crossing, they report $w(z)<-1$ before the crossing at $3.01$–$3.22\sigma$ and $w(z)>-1$ after it at $3.30$–$3.55\sigma$ — higher significances than at the conventional pivot. The paper is careful to frame this as a statistical-significance-maximization exercise: whether the crossing is physical/intrinsic versus an artifact of the CPL form is left as a separate question.

Same DESI-era dynamical-dark-energy thread as paper I, and squarely the kind of BAO+CMB+SN result that drove your recent additions (DESI DR2 dynamical dark energy). Useful as a cautionary note too: significances quoted "at the optimal redshift" are optimized quantities, not pivot-value tensions.

### [What would it take for dark matter to be literally warm?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24990v1)

Katelin Schutz
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

A single-author note dissecting the WDM benchmark that underlies small-scale-structure constraints (including Lyman-α forest bounds now approaching $\sim$10 keV). Schutz argues that literal warm dark matter — a thermal relic that decoupled while relativistic — requires one of three fine-tuned coincidences: $\sim10^4$ relativistic degrees of freedom at decoupling (far beyond the Standard Model), an early-matter-domination entropy dilution (which would itself alter the transfer function away from the radiation-dominated form assumed in WDM fits), or a never-thermalized dark sector born cold via asymmetric reheating. The conclusion is that the WDM transfer function is a poor physical stand-in for realistic models, whose structure suppression differs both quantitatively and qualitatively. She advocates moving to more expressive parameterizations and simulation-based inference to exploit upcoming small-scale data.

A direct hit on two of your threads at once — small-scale dark matter via the forest (§5) and the SBI methods program (§3). It is a pointed argument that the "$m_{\rm WDM}$" number your community quotes from P1D is a benchmark of convenience, and a nudge toward SBI on physically motivated dark-sector parameterizations.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Enhancing dark siren cosmology via Gaussian process reconstruction of incomplete galaxy catalogs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24992v1)

Matteo Tagliazucchi, Jonathan Gair, Riccardo Barbieri et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

Replaces the standard "uniform-in-comoving-volume" completion of incomplete galaxy catalogs — used in dark-siren $H_0$ inference from gravitational waves — with a non-parametric line-of-sight redshift distribution drawn from a Gaussian process, fitted to the observed catalog through a hierarchical Bayesian likelihood that explicitly models the survey selection function. On mock GW + galaxy catalogs to $z\le0.4$, the GP method tightens $H_0$ by $\sim$23% for a 24%-complete catalog and $\sim$37% for an 8%-complete one, reaching 66% in configurations where redshift over/under-densities matter most. The gain comes from restoring the clustering information that homogeneous completion discards.

The science target (GW $H_0$) is adjacent, but the machinery — a GP prior on a 1D radial density modulated by a selection function, inside a hierarchical Bayesian likelihood — is exactly the kind of data-driven completion you could borrow for continuum/selection modeling or for LOS density reconstruction in forest and LSS inference.

*No figures extracted for 2608.24992 (method is well conveyed by the text; figures are schematic/parameter-study plots).*

### [Distinguishing cold and self-interacting dark matter through topological analysis](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25632v1)

Adrian Szpilfidel, Clotilde Laigle, Pierre Boldrini et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Uses the genus statistic — a topological measure of the density field — to separate self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) from CDM on intermediate scales, above galactic scales where baryonic degeneracies dominate but below where the models converge. On the Darkium DM-only simulations (one CDM plus four SIDM cross-sections), computed around a few hundred halos of $10^{12}$–$10^{14}\,M_\odot/h$ over $z=0$–$2$, the density field is systematically clumpier in CDM than SIDM up to $\sim0.05\,{\rm Mpc}/h$. They also test an observation-like configuration using only the halo distribution in thick 2D projection, arguing the genus is a viable topological probe of DM self-interaction in real halo catalogs.

A dark-matter-nature probe (§5) built from a field-topology summary statistic on N-body output — the same "which summary statistic best distinguishes DM models" question you engage with in the forest, applied to the cosmic-web density field instead of P1D.

### [Filaments within filaments: unraveling the hierarchy of the cosmic web](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25840v1)

Saee Dhawalikar, Aseem Paranjape, Shadab Alam
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Applies a hierarchical reconstruction scheme to identify sub-filaments nested inside larger parent filaments, extending the familiar halo/sub-halo hierarchy to the filamentary web. Comparing the radial phase-space (density and velocity) profiles of parent versus sub-filaments, the authors argue sub-filaments are a statistically distinct structural class rather than scaled-down parents. They frame a universal filament hierarchy as something that must account for these profile differences, with consequences for cosmology and dark matter.

Cosmic-web / large-scale-structure territory (§4) with an explicit dark-matter hook: a new structural decomposition that could feed environment-dependent bias models or provide a fresh field statistic for LSS inference.

### [Tracing Galaxy Bias Through the Cosmic Web: The Role of Filaments](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25060v1)

Constanza A. Soto-Suárez, Antonio D. Montero-Dorta, Daniela Galárraga-Espinosa et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

Maps large-scale galaxy bias through the cosmic web in TNG300, using DisPerSE to extract filaments and an object-by-object bias estimator. Galaxies in node outskirts reach bias up to $\sim4\times$ the halo-mass expectation, and low-mass red galaxies in filaments show enhanced bias that largely vanishes once galaxies near nodes are excluded — pointing to proximity to massive nodes as the driver of environmental secondary bias. Bias also decreases with filament length (mean $1.4$ for short filaments down to $-0.5$ for long ones) at fixed halo mass and color, with long filaments showing a rising bias profile from saddle toward node.

Directly relevant to the LSS/bias systematics that sit under any clustering or full-shape analysis (§4): a quantification of how much cosmic-web environment, not just halo mass, modulates the tracer–matter relation.

### [Revealing Galactic dust beneath the cosmic infrared background anisotropies with Wavelet Phase Harmonics](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25617v1)

Srijita Sinha, Tuhin Ghosh, Erwan Allys et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Builds a component-separation algorithm based on Wavelet Phase Harmonics (WPH) statistics to extract Galactic dust emission from regions dominated by cosmic-infrared-background (CIB) anisotropies, where dust and CIB share spectral properties. Applied to Planck 353 GHz over three sky regions spanning a range of HI column density, it recovers the dust signal without significant dust–CIB leakage, and outperforms the standard linear dust–HI template fit in the low-S/N validation region while preserving the dust–HI statistical correlation. The recovered maps are then used to characterize scale-dependent dust/HI variations.

The WPH generative/statistical framework is in the scattering-transform family that appears in your methods shelf (§3, §4): a non-Gaussian field statistic used here for foreground separation, transferable to component separation and field-level modeling in cosmological map analysis.

### [Constraining primordial non-Gaussianity and energy injection with the thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect and integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect cross-correlation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25809v1)

Ayodeji Ibitoye, Yin-Zhe Ma, Prabhakar Tiwari
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Uses the ISW–tSZ cross-correlation to jointly constrain local primordial non-Gaussianity ($f_{\rm NL}$) and early-Universe energy injection. For scale-independent PNG they find $f_{\rm NL}=-358^{+140}_{-114}$ (68% C.L.), and for a scale-dependent model $f^0_{\rm NL}=-296^{+173}_{-157}$ with tilt $n_{\rm NL}=0.62^{+1.02}_{-0.64}$ — both consistent with Gaussian initial conditions given the large errors. They also report the first ISW–tSZ constraint on an exotic energy-injection amplitude, tightened by a factor $\sim2.6$ when a Planck $f_{\rm NL}$ prior is imposed, and project improvements from Simons Observatory and Euclid.

Early-Universe / inflation-adjacent cosmology (a secondary but recurring context in your library): a complementary, cross-correlation-based handle on $f_{\rm NL}$ and on non-standard thermal-history energy injection.

### [Cosmic topology. Part Va. Information content of the observable Universe](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25033v1)

Deyan P. Mihaylov, Mikel Martin Barandiaran, Stefano Anselmi et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Quantifies how much information a compact spatial topology imprints on observable perturbations. Deriving the covariance between perturbation modes in the full topology volume and projecting it to the observable subvolume, the authors estimate the topology information available in the full set of linear perturbations. For two Euclidean examples (the cubic 3-torus $E_1$ and a quarter-turn 3-torus $E_3$), the 3D density field carries enough information to detect topology on scales up to 25% larger than the 2D CMB alone can reach — motivating deep galaxy surveys and 21-cm intensity mapping as topology probes.

Nongalactic cosmology with a survey/21-cm angle (touches your adjacent 21-cm interest, §7): a Fisher-style information argument that the same 3D density fields you analyze for LSS carry additional large-scale information, here for topology rather than dark energy.

*No figures extracted for 2608.25033 (a formal information-content argument; figures are illustrative diagrams).*

### [The ionizing properties of JWST's compact broad-line emitters](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25021v1)

Sara Mascia, Jorryt Matthee, Alberto Torralba et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.HE

Assembles a clean sample of 20 compact, blue broad-line emitters at $4\le z\le7$ from JWST NIRSpec/PRISM + NIRCam, selecting sources where the UV continuum and broad Hα arise from the same compact region. These objects show hot ionizing continua, high-ionization UV lines, and elevated Lyα-emitter fractions (50–67%), with optical spectra resembling Little Red Dots but at lower column density. Modeling with Sirocco yields high ionizing-photon production efficiency ($\log\xi_{\rm ion}\sim25.4$) and escape fraction $f_{\rm esc}\sim0.2$ for the bluest sources (vs $\sim0.01$ for LRDs), implying broad-line sources do not dominate reionization globally but can dominate on few-Mpc scales.

Reionization-budget question (§2): a quantitative constraint on whether AGN-like broad-line sources contribute to the ionizing background — relevant context for the UV-background and late-reionization physics that sets the forest's astrophysical foreground.

### [Finding and using interpretable latents in a neutrino foundation model with sparse autoencoders](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26090v1)

Raphaël Bonnet-Guerrini, Johann Ioannou-Nikolaides, Inar Timiryasov et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE | also: cs.LG, hep-ex

A first application of sparse-autoencoder mechanistic interpretability to a physics foundation model: an IceCube-pretrained neutrino model fine-tuned for direction reconstruction. Using held-out tests, matched nuisance controls, and replication across dictionary seeds, they build a validated atlas of physical concepts in the model's representation, and show causally that the direction head barely uses this atlas. Motivated by that unused information, they train an uncertainty head on the same event-level representation that depends causally on named quality/brightness features; at 20% selection efficiency it improves the median angular resolution from $20.2^\circ$ to $3.2^\circ$.

Squarely in your foundation-model / representation-learning interest (§3): a concrete, rigorously validated recipe for probing what an astro foundation model has learned and turning interpretable latents into a better downstream (uncertainty) task — directly transferable to spectroscopic foundation models.

### [EncoTESS: Age-Sensitive Encodings from Raw TESS Light Curves](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25019v1)

Phil R. Van-Lane, Joshua S. Speagle, Ryan Cloutier et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.SR | also: astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.IM, cs.LG

A compact time-series foundation model trained on TESS 2-minute light curves, designed to handle heteroskedastic noise, irregular sampling, and large data gaps. EncoTESS encodes light curves into a fixed-size latent space that infers physical stellar properties and recovers light-curve summary statistics; notably it is $\sim$1% the size of a typical time-series foundation model and runs on a laptop. As an application it outperforms rotation period and variability amplitude as an age indicator for stars not yet on the slow-rotator sequence, with the framework and encodings released publicly.

The stellar-age science is peripheral, but the architecture is on-profile (§3, foundation models / self-supervised representation learning of spectroscopic-style time series): a small, gap-robust encoder that is a useful reference design if you build representation models for survey light curves or spectra.

*No figures extracted for 2608.25019 (a methods/architecture contribution; the released code and encodings are the deliverable).*

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [An Inverse Lyth Bound for Non-Attractor Inflation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25911v1)

William H. Kinney
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The paper derives a genuine inflation result — a complementary *upper* bound on the inflaton field excursion during a canonical non-attractor phase (whenever the second Hubble-flow parameter $\epsilon_2<-3$, i.e. the anti-damping regime that includes ultra-slow-roll), independent of the potential and not requiring slow roll. The striking part is the framing: the author (a working cosmologist) states in the abstract that the result was "generated entirely autonomously by ChatGPT 5.6 Sol," and the four-page derivation reads as a self-contained physics note. It stands as a documented case of an AI system producing a novel, non-trivial theoretical result in cosmology.

Worth knowing not for the inflation bound itself but as a concrete data point on AI's changing role in doing (not just assisting) theoretical astrophysics — the sociology-of-method question of what autonomous AI derivations mean for authorship, verification, and practice in the field.
