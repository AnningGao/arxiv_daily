## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [Beyond dynamical dark energy: the role of dark sector interactions after DESI DR2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21938v1)

Weiqiang Yang, Sibo Zhang, Supriya Pan et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors ask whether the DESI-DR2 preference for time-varying dark energy could instead (or also) be a signature of energy exchange in the dark sector. They model dark matter as diluting like $\rho_{\rm dm}\propto a^{-3+\delta}$ and pair the interaction strength $\delta$ either with a constant $w\neq-1$ or with a CPL $w_0w_a$ equation of state, fitting against Planck CMB, DESI-DR2 BAO, and three SN Ia compilations (PantheonPlus, Union3, DES-Dovekie). For constant $w$, CMB+DESI+SN prefer a small negative interaction at above $2\sigma$; once the equation of state is allowed to evolve, the evidence for interaction weakens and the data instead favor a quintessence-like evolving component. In every case Bayesian model comparison still prefers plain $\Lambda$CDM.

### [Light Dark Matter from Self-cooling Dark Sectors](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22307v1)

Esau Cervantes, Andrzej Hryczuk, Stefan Lederer
**Primary category:** hep-ph

Thermally produced light dark matter is normally boxed in by the Lyman-α forest: its free-streaming suppresses small-scale structure, which for warm dark matter sets a mass floor near 5.7 keV (or 1.9 keV under a weaker recast). This paper introduces a "self-cooling" mechanism in which number-changing $2\to3$ inverse-cannibal reactions in the dark sector trade kinetic energy for mass during freeze-in, plus a second "decay-cooling" phase, lowering the dark-matter velocity dispersion. As a result the Lyman-α mass bounds can be relaxed by up to two-plus orders of magnitude, so frozen-in dark matter saturating $\Omega h^2$ can be as light as ~0.7–1.9 keV while remaining consistent with the forest. The authors also flag a spurious velocity-dispersion enhancement that appears when second-moment Boltzmann equations are pushed past kinetic decoupling.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Strong Lensing Cosmology with Population-level Calibrated Neural Ratio Estimation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23534v1)

Sreevani Jarugula, Brian D. Nord, Aleksandra Ćiprijanović et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.CO

The authors apply Neural Ratio Estimation (NRE), an amortized simulation-based-inference technique, to jointly infer the dark-energy equation of state $w$ and $\Omega_m$ from populations of galaxy-galaxy strong lenses plus companion spectroscopy. Individual per-lens likelihood ratios are combined into a population-level posterior, and — crucially — they add a post-hoc coverage-calibration step to fix the overconfidence that neural density estimators typically exhibit. For 100 lenses in $\Lambda$CDM the calibrated model reaches ~22.8% fractional uncertainty in $w$ and ~2.9% in $\Omega_m$, with errors shrinking as the lens population grows; quadrupling the training set (to 2M images) did *not* cure overconfidence, so calibration remained necessary.

### [Guidance for Prior Change via Density Ratio Estimation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21729v1)

Yichen Zang, Song Liu, Jiun-Yi Lin
**Primary category:** stat.ML | also: cs.LG

Amortized SBI trains a posterior estimator against a fixed prior, so updating the prior normally means retraining. This paper proposes an inference-time fix: learn the gradient of the joint density ratio via Density Ratio Estimation (DRE) to reweight an already-trained amortized posterior toward a new prior, without extra simulator calls. Unlike the earlier PriorGuide method — which relies on Gaussian and Gaussian-mixture approximations that inject bias — the DRE guidance term is unbiased and estimator-agnostic, and it drops the Langevin correction PriorGuide needs at every observation. On several benchmarks and a Bayesian-updating exoplanet light-curve task it matches or beats PriorGuide, especially under out-of-distribution prior shifts.

### [Neural Boltzmann Equations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23022v1)

Jonas Spinner, Jack Shergold
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: cs.LG, stat.ML

Early-universe Boltzmann equations involve high-dimensional phase-space integrals that are normally evolved on a fixed momentum grid, which scales badly for complex reaction networks and parameter scans. The authors replace the grid with physics-inspired *neural distribution functions* whose parameters are predicted by neural networks, evaluate collision integrals via Monte Carlo with collider-physics importance sampling, and evolve the system with natural-gradient optimization. They report ~1000× more efficient importance sampling than a recent MC Boltzmann solver, evolve a non-equilibrium system with number-changing $2\leftrightarrow3$ processes to per-mille precision, and deliver a precision computation of $N_{\rm eff}$ (the effective number of relativistic neutrino species) plus a 2D scan of an extended-$N_{\rm eff}$ scenario.

### [When a neural surrogate cannot accelerate a solver: runtime share, closed-loop drift, and the economics of uncertainty gating in a stiff coupled simulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23075v1)

L. Thümmler, T. Kuroda
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: astro-ph.HE, cs.LG, physics.comp-ph

A carefully controlled *negative* result on learned surrogates for expensive inner-solver blocks — here, the implicit Newton solve coupling neutrino radiation to matter in a GR radiation-hydrodynamics code. The authors identify three structural barriers, none of them a failure of the trained network: (1) per-call cost is not the same as share of runtime, and Amdahl's law caps the speedup — their target block is the single most expensive routine yet only 16.9% of critical-path wall clock, so a 5.8×-cheaper surrogate merely ties the solver; (2) offline accuracy does not rank surrogates for deployment (the apparent correlation vanishes under proper controls); and (3) an out-of-distribution gate cannot rescue a loop that leaves its training distribution — with visited states 73× off the data manifold, the gate defers 96.8–99.7% of cells, and a never-crashing gated run still accumulates a directed −19.9% density bias over 6000 steps.

### [What AstroPT knows about galaxies, and what that can teach us about LLMs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22614v1)

UniverseTBD Collaboration (Kshitij Duraphe, Aman Kumar, Michael J. Smith, et al.)
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: astro-ph.IM

AstroPT is an LLM-like transformer trained by patch reconstruction on millions of galaxy images. The authors use it as an interpretability *testbed* where — unlike natural language — the difficulty ordering of concepts is known in advance. Probing frozen representations across training checkpoints, layers, model sizes, and objectives, they find galaxy properties become decodable in a fixed order that tracks their physical difficulty: band magnitude (written almost directly into pixels) emerges early and shallow, while redshift and specific star-formation rate emerge later and deeper. The ordering is invariant to the training objective and scales in magnitude but not in sequence with model capacity, and linear-probe directions recover the known sign structure among galaxy properties.

### [Dynamics of Wave Structures in Multifield Fuzzy Dark Matter Halos](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23162v1)

Yu-Ming Yang, Xiao-Jun Bi, Peng-Fei Yin
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA, hep-ph

High-resolution simulations of fuzzy dark matter (FDM) halos built from multiple scalar fields — the axiverse-motivated generalization of single-field FDM. Relative to the single-field case, the central-core density oscillations develop multiple spectral peaks shifted to higher frequency; the component cores undergo nearly synchronized random walks (with subdominant components wandering more); and granule density fluctuations are suppressed roughly as the inverse of the number of fields, largely independent of how mass is partitioned among components. Consequently granule-driven dynamical heating of embedded stellar systems weakens as fields are added — though once the oscillating central core is included, the dependence on field number nearly cancels.

### [On the importance of the IGM/CGM enrichment in determining chemical abundances during the first 600 Myr](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21515v1)

Annalisa Citro, Yong-Zhong Qian, Claudia Scarlata
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

JWST finds $z>8$ galaxies with a wide spread of C/O and systematically elevated N/O at fixed metallicity. The authors argue that the chemical state of the *inflowing* gas — pre-enrichment of the IGM/CGM — must be modeled alongside in-situ star formation, because at these early epochs the star-formation histories are too brief to erase the imprint of the gas feeding the galaxy. Their chemical-evolution model, applied to eight $z>8$ galaxies, finds that the low-C/O, high-N/O ratios of half the sample (including GN-z11 and CEERS-1019) can only be jointly reproduced if the inflowing gas was pre-enriched by supermassive stars to an oxygen abundance $Z_{\rm inf,O}\gtrsim10^{-5}\,Z_\odot$; the rest are consistent with CCSN or Pop III pre-enrichment. They also note the N/O vs O/H plane constrains enrichment pathways more tightly than C/O vs O/H.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Anisotropies in the PTA gravitational wave background: what can they teach us about supermassive black hole binaries?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22164v1)

Matias Zaldarriaga, Gabriela Sato-Polito
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

A sharp, partly deflationary re-examination of what pulsar-timing-array (PTA) anisotropy measurements can actually deliver. Across models normalized to the measured background amplitude, the expected anisotropy is produced by the single brightest binary — a dipole at the current NANOGrav upper limit would need one source to supply ~60% of the band power, yet no model that also fits the strain spectrum contains a source that bright. Because the signal is dominated by one source, compressing the sky into an angular power spectrum discards the phase information that would localize it, so such a search is never more sensitive than a direct continuous-wave search. Most pointedly, the authors argue the published angular-power-spectrum upper limits reflect the analysis *prior* (the square-root spherical-harmonic basis) rather than the data — while the strain-spectrum *shape* already disfavors mass functions dominated by binaries above ~$10^{10}\,M_\odot$.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Particle Dark Matter in the 1980s and 1990s](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22087v1)

Michael S. Turner
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

A first-person historical account, by one of the architects of the field, of how "inner space" (particle physics) and "outer space" (cosmology) fused over two decades. Turner traces the arc from 1980 — when the Hubble constant was uncertain by a factor of two and the hot big bang was "the standard model" — to 2000, when $\Lambda$CDM with inflation, particle dark matter, and dark energy had become the new standard model with $H_0$ known to better than 10%. The essay situates particle dark matter as the central idea that bound the two communities together and argues it remains a live question in both.
