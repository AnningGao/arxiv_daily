## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [The impact of the IGM thermal state on the Lyα flux 3D power spectrum from linear to highly non-linear scales](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24784v1)

Tomáš Šoltinský, Gabriele Autieri, Vid Iršič et al. (incl. Iršič, an interest-file high-value forest/IGM author)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using the Sherwood and Sherwood-Relics cosmological hydrodynamical simulations (boxes up to 160 h⁻¹ cMpc), the authors quantify how the three-dimensional Lyα forest flux power spectrum P₃D,α over 2.4 ≤ z ≤ 4.8 responds to numerical configuration (box size, mass resolution, optical-depth grid) and to the IGM thermal/reionization history, applying a Zel'dovich control-variate correction to suppress single-realization cosmic variance. They find numerical effects can rival or exceed the astrophysical signal: mass-resolution differences reach ~13%, and computing τ on an already-coarsened grid can spuriously enhance small-scale power by ~35%. The strongest genuine astrophysical imprint is spatially patchy HI reionization, which boosts large-scale power by up to ~70% at z = 4.2, while doubling/halving photoheating shifts large-scale power by ~4–8%; later-ending reionization lowers P₃D,α by only ~1% at z = 2.4. They also demonstrate a splicing-based resolution correction that recovers the high-resolution result to ≲2%.

This is a direct hit on the primary Lyα-forest-cosmology focus: it is a P3D measurement-methodology paper tying the forest to the IGM thermal state and reionization, built on the Sherwood-Relics suite, and explicitly framed toward interpreting DESI and WST P3D data — precisely the "Lyα × DESI" spine of the library, and companion to the EFT treatment of the same simulated spectra.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Gaussian process reconstruction of scalar field dynamics from recent cosmological data](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24433v1)

Afaq Maqsood, Sonej Alam, Md. Wali Hossain
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors perform a model-independent Gaussian-process reconstruction of the late-time expansion history and effective quintessence scalar-field dynamics, combining 32 cosmic-chronometer measurements, DESI DR2 BAO, three SN Ia compilations (Pantheon+, Union3, DES Y5), and compressed CMB distance priors. From the reconstructed H(z) they extract the scalar potential V(z), kinetic term, equation of state w(z), and slope |λ(z)|: the potential is nearly constant (Ṽ(0) ≈ 0.68–0.70 ρ_c,0), the kinetic term stays small, and w(z) hugs −1, with a mild phantom-crossing hint near z ~ 0.5 whose significance depends strongly on the SN sample (DES Y5 ~3.1–3.3σ, Union3 ~1σ).

Relevance: DESI DR2 BAO dynamical dark energy is a flagged recent interest, and GP reconstruction is exactly the model-independent, ML-flavored inference machinery the library collects for expansion-history data — a clean template for compression-free reconstruction from DESI-anchored datasets.

### [Beyond w₀–wₐ and Phantom Crossing: Testing Models of Coupled Dark Sector](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23712v1)

Kaynan R. de O. Pompeu, João Rebouças, Rogerio Rosenfeld
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: hep-ph

This paper asks whether the DESI-driven apparent phantom crossing of the dark-energy equation of state can be produced by dark-sector interactions rather than genuine phantom behavior. Extending a φ²χ² dark-matter–dark-energy coupling with an inverse-power-law potential V ∝ φ^−β and a generalized mass coupling m_χ ∝ φ^α, implemented at background and perturbation level in a Boltzmann solver and constrained with CMB+BAO+SN Ia, they show coupled quintessence reproduces w₀wₐ-quality fits (best at α = 0.1) while the uncoupled case cannot. They stress that dark-matter interactions modify structure formation (Ω_m, σ₈), so extracting full information will require N-body calibration of the nonlinear matter power spectrum.

Relevance: connects the DESI BAO dynamical-dark-energy signal to dark-sector physics and, importantly, to nonlinear structure-formation modeling — the LSS-inference context surrounding the forest program, and a reminder that these models live or die on the matter power spectrum.

*No figures extracted for 2608.23712 (key content is posterior contours and a mirage-w argument better carried by the text).*

### [First application of weak lensing peak steepness statistics to HSC Y1 data: effectively probing halo density profiles](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24591v1)

Ziwei Li, Xiangkun Liu, Tianyu Zhang et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors introduce weak-lensing peak "steepness" statistics — the radial slope of the convergence field around high peaks — as a probe sensitive to halo density profiles, and apply it for the first time to HSC Y1 data in combination with peak-height statistics. Within the HMcode2016 framework (baryonic feedback encoded as a reduction of halo concentration) and focusing on high peaks dominated by massive clusters, they obtain S₈ = 0.76₋₀.₀₇⁺⁰·⁰⁸ and low concentrations (A = 1.93₋₁.₁₆⁺¹·³³ vs. 3.34 for DM-only mocks), phenomenologically favoring strong baryonic feedback at cluster scales. Systematics from cluster-member contamination, intrinsic alignments, and B-modes are tested.

Relevance: LSS precision-cosmology statistics, S₈, and baryonic feedback sit squarely in the secondary-focus "techniques" shelf; the peak-steepness estimator is a new summary statistic worth knowing for statistical inference from imaging-survey data.

### [Chebyshev interpolation in Einstein-Boltzmann codes](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24682v1)

Herman Sletmoen
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.IM

This methods paper implements barycentric Chebyshev-polynomial interpolation in wavenumber k and multipole ℓ inside the approximation-free Einstein-Boltzmann code SymBoltz, solving the perturbation ODEs and line-of-sight integrals at Chebyshev nodes. For smooth CMB and matter spectra this reaches up to four orders of magnitude lower interpolation error than cubic splines at fixed sample count (relative error 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ with only 50–80 points per variable vs. 200 for splines, i.e., 2.5–4× speedup), and a node-rounding trick avoids the non-integer-ℓ spherical Bessel functions that standard Chebyshev ℓ-interpolation would require.

Relevance: a concrete, borrowable numerical-methods advance for the theory codes underpinning cosmological inference and emulation — the kind of high-precision, approximation-free spectrum computation that pairs naturally with emulator training and Fisher/likelihood pipelines.

*No figures extracted for 2608.24682 (the payoff is convergence-error curves; the numbers are stated in the summary).*

### [From Numerical Simulators of PDEs to Neural Emulators and Back](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24547v1)

Felix Koehler
**Primary category:** cs.LG

This thesis-format synthesis argues that neural PDE emulators and classical numerical solvers are two views of the same object, unified through mode-wise Fourier analysis in which solver error, architectural inductive bias, and training objective can all be read off together. It contributes APEBench (a differentiable pseudo-spectral benchmarking suite in JAX for autoregressive neural emulators), "Progressively Refined Differentiable Physics" (showing unconverged solvers can suffice for surrogate training because the network cannot exploit the extra precision), and an analysis of "neural emulator superiority" — why an emulator can beat its own training data via implicit regularization of solver errors.

Relevance: the neural-operator / emulator surrogate-modeling backbone (the FNO/DeepONet lineage the library tracks for replacing expensive hydro simulations) gets a rigorous framework here for building and trusting emulators — directly applicable to forest/LSS emulation, with an explicit eye toward PDE foundation models.

*No figures extracted for 2608.24547 (a conceptual/methodological synthesis; no single plot is central).*

### [A New Probe of Dark Matter Subhalos: Stellar Aberration with TESS](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23795v1)

Matthias Daniel, Xiao Xue, Kris Pardo et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

The authors propose stellar aberration — the coherent dipolar pattern of apparent stellar-position shifts induced by an acceleration of the observer — as a new channel to search for dark-matter subhalos, distinct from the localized distortions targeted by astrometric lensing or pulsar timing. Fisher-matrix forecasts for TESS (stars with Tmag ≤ 10 in the ecliptic-pole continuous-viewing zones, with field-of-view rotations/translations as the dominant systematics) reach a sensitivity of 6.3×10⁻⁹ m/s², probing concentrated NFW subhalos from ≳10⁻⁶ M⊙ at AU-scale distances to ≳10⁷ M⊙ at ~10 pc; TESS's high cadence uniquely opens sensitivity to transient accelerations, and a PLATO-like strategy could push to a few×10⁻¹¹ m/s².

Relevance: small-scale dark-matter structure is a secondary focus normally approached via the forest; this is a methodologically fresh, complementary probe of the same "nature of DM via substructure" question, worth tracking as an independent cross-check.

*No figures extracted for 2608.23795 (main figures are sky-sensitivity maps; the forecast numbers carry the point).*

### [Cool CGM MgII Absorption Across the Star-Forming, Green Valley, and Quiescent Transition](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23687v1)

Simon Xinlin Wu, Rongmon Bordoloi, Robert A. Simcoe et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Using MgII absorption toward 716 galaxies over 0.07 < z < 2.7 (169 new + 547 archival), the authors trace cool circumgalactic gas across the star-forming-to-quiescent transition, introducing a star-formation-offset metric σ_SFO that isolates green-valley galaxies as a distinct population at every epoch. MgII absorption strength (as a virial-radius-normalized radial profile), its scatter, and the inner-CGM covering fraction all decline monotonically from star-forming through green-valley to quiescent systems, while star-formers show a bimodal (polar + disk) azimuthal dependence consistent with outflows plus co-planar accretion. The cool CGM fades gradually across the green valley rather than vanishing abruptly at quenching.

Relevance: CGM absorption-line work viewed through the same quasar/background-source absorption lens as the IGM program — a natural sibling of the library's IGM/CGM branch, and a useful reference point for how cool gas around galaxies is tracked with MgII.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Spatially resolved spectral properties of M87* on event horizon scales](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24110v1)

Shan-Shan Zhao, Ru-Sen Lu, Rocco Lico et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.HE | also: astro-ph.GA

Combining near-simultaneous 2018 images at 3.5 mm (GMVA including ALMA and the Greenland Telescope) and 1.3 mm (EHT), the authors produce the first spatially resolved spectral-index map of M87* on horizon scales (≤100 μas). The map is ring-like with a clear radial gradient: the spectral index rises modestly inward, peaks near ~20 μas (just inside the 1.3 mm ring), then declines, flipping from positive to negative near ~30 μas. This is consistent with frequency-dependent synchrotron opacity in the innermost accretion flow — optically thick inside, optically thin outside — and broadly matches GRMHD/RIAF predictions once realistic observational resolution is applied, though the sharp photon-ring spectral spike is smoothed out.

Relevance: outside my area, but a genuinely groundbreaking horizon-scale black-hole imaging result — the first resolved spectral-index map of an event-horizon-scale source — worth knowing as a scientist.

### [More Numerous and Bluer: Companions of the Brightest Little Red Dots Differ from Control Galaxies at >3σ, Hinting at Synchronized Black Hole Seed Formation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23671v1)

Fabio Pacucci, C. Megan Urry
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

In a controlled JWST experiment, 253 photometrically selected Little Red Dots (4.0 ≤ z ≤ 8.5) are compared against >7000 redshift-matched control galaxies observed in the same mosaics with identical pipelines. The brightest LRD quartile hosts a close (0.5–2 kpc) blue companion at 27.7% versus 11.6% for controls (×2.39, 3.3σ), and those companions are significantly bluer (median β ≈ −2.32 vs. −1.92, 3.4σ) and overwhelmingly young (92% with β < −1). The excess is confined to the brightest LRDs and to <2 kpc — exactly what the "synchronized pair" scenario predicts, in which a young neighbor's Lyman-Werner radiation enables direct-collapse massive black-hole seed formation.

Relevance: a striking, statistically clean result on early massive-black-hole seeding at high redshift — adjacent to the reionization-era ionizing-source interests and worth flagging as a notable early-universe finding, even though it is outside the forest-cosmology core.
