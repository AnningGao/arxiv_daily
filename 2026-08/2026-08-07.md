## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [On the Origin of the Lyα Damping Wing in Galaxies at 8 ≤ z ≤ 10: Explorations using the NINJA Simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05281)

Sukanya Mallik, Raghunathan Srianand, Nishikanta Khandai
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

Using the NINJA cosmological hydrodynamical suite (three boxes spanning 50–140 h⁻¹ Mpc), the authors decompose the Lyα damping wing seen in JWST/NIRSpec galaxies at z ∼ 8–10 into contributions from the diffuse IGM, the clustered gas around galaxies, and residual neutral hydrogen inside the virial radius, via three idealized bubble-in-IGM models. They find the overdense envelope around galaxies extends to a characteristic radius R_eq ≈ 100 pkpc (set by halo mass, not redshift), and that a uniformly ionized diffuse IGM cannot reproduce the observed incidence of damped absorbers: even a fully neutral IGM yields no sightlines with N_HI > 10²² cm⁻², whereas ~18% of observed z > 8 galaxies show them. Only by adding residual neutral gas within the virial radius (Case III) do the strongest absorbers appear — f₂₂ rising to ~12% for 10¹⁰·⁵–10¹¹·⁵ M⊙ halos — so the strongest DLAs probe the ISM/CGM ionization state rather than the global IGM neutral fraction.

This is a direct hit on the interest file's fused Lyman-α / IGM-reionization primary focus: it addresses exactly the degeneracy between IGM x_HI, ionized-bubble size, and galaxy-associated HI that governs how JWST damping-wing measurements are turned into reionization constraints — and it sharpens the systematic that any forest-based x_HI inference at z > 5 has to control.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [A Gaussian Covariance Matrix for Joint Pre- and Post-Reconstruction Full-Shape Power Spectrum Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05504)

Yuting Wang, Ruiyang Zhao, Gong-Bo Zhao et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors build a semi-analytical Gaussian covariance for the joint analysis of pre-reconstruction, post-reconstruction, and cross full-shape galaxy power spectra, modelling the reconstruction-reduced scale-dependent cross shot noise from displacement-field statistics and introducing a new estimator that measures it directly. Validated against mock catalogues and with emulator-based inference, the semi-analytical covariance recovers cosmological constraints consistent with the mock-based numerical covariance over the fitting ranges (k_max = 0.18 h/Mpc pre/post, 0.12 h/Mpc cross). It captures the dominant covariance structure of the full three-spectrum data vector without needing thousands of mocks.

Relevant to the LSS-methods secondary focus: covariance estimation and full-shape/BAO pipelines are squarely on the interest file's "precision-cosmology methods shelf," and a mock-cheap covariance for jointly fitting pre- and post-reconstruction spectra is directly usable machinery for DESI-style analyses (the author list is DESI/eBOSS-adjacent).

### [Luminosity function of quasars at 1.0 < z < 3.5 from SDSS and DESI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06000)

Gaocheng Yin, Linhua Jiang, Zhiwei Pan et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Combining SDSS and DESI spectroscopy over a ~1700 deg² wide field and a ~265 deg² deep field, the authors assemble 62,426 type-1 quasars at 1.0 < z < 3.5 and derive quasar luminosity functions using a "library of all known quasars" strategy with simple rest-frame UV/optical colour cuts, achieving ~96% completeness and ~93% purity. The binned QLFs are fit with a double power law; they find slightly higher densities at the faint end and at the bright end for 2.5 < z < 3.5 than previous work. Pure luminosity evolution describes 1.0 < z < 2.5, while either pure luminosity or pure density evolution fits 2.5 < z < 3.5.

Relevant as context for the DESI/quasar side of the forest program: the QSOs whose sightlines carry the Lyα forest are drawn from exactly this population, so an updated, high-completeness QLF and a clean colour-based selection recipe bear on target-selection completeness and the available sightline density for forest analyses.

*No figures extracted for 2608.06000 (the QLF result is split across per-redshift-bin panels; the double-power-law trend is adequately conveyed in text).*

### [Reanalyzing Megamasers: a low value of H₀ from a local probe changes our view of the Hubble Tension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06247)

Richard Watkins, Hume A. Feldman
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors re-analyze the six-galaxy megamaser distance sample (all z < 0.04), replacing the previous Gaussian-noise treatment of peculiar velocities with a constrained-simulation reconstruction of the local velocity field that captures coherent flows and non-Gaussian infall tails. Correcting each galaxy's redshift with the reconstructed peculiar velocity shifts the inferred Hubble constant down to H₀ ≈ 69.7 ± 2.6 km/s/Mpc — consistent with the CMB value and now in > 2σ tension with the local distance ladder, the reverse of the earlier megamaser result. They argue prior megamaser H₀ estimates were biased by peculiar-velocity mismodelling.

Useful cosmology-context reading: megamasers are a rare one-step-geometric H₀ anchor independent of the distance ladder, and a result that flips which side of the Hubble tension a local probe lands on — pointing to a distance-ladder systematic rather than new physics — matters for how any DESI-era background-cosmology inference is interpreted.

### [A space of inference spaces in the space sciences — Parametric Bayesian inference in astronomy, cosmology and particle physics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06078)

Johannes Buchner
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: stat.CO

Buchner (author of UltraNest) collates a sample of real parametric Bayesian inference problems from astronomy, cosmology, and particle physics — plus mock and toy problems — and characterizes each along seven axes: dimensionality, Gaussianity, tail weight, informativeness (posterior-to-prior volume), mixed constrained/unconstrained parameters, multimodality, and whether the inference undergoes phase transitions. He shows astrophysical inference spans this entire "space of inference problems," from low- to high-dimensional and uni- to multi-modal, with model-evaluation costs from milliseconds to tens of seconds, and proposes the collated set (with a Docker image) as a standard benchmark for new Monte Carlo samplers.

Relevant to the ML/statistical-inference rising-primary focus: the interest file's SBI/nested-sampling/flows toolkit lives or dies on sampler reliability across awkward posteriors, and a reproducible, physics-grounded testbed for comparing samplers is directly useful for choosing or validating inference machinery on forest/LSS problems.

*No figures extracted for 2608.06078 (conceptual/benchmark paper; the figures are per-problem corner plots and an abstract problem-space scatter).*

### [NestyNet. I. Physics Functions Are Hard to Fit with Neural Networks: A Framework for Accurate Surrogates and Analytic Derivatives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05862)

Rodrigo Ibata, Wassim Tenachi, Foivos Diakogiannis et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM | also: physics.data-an

NestyNet is a coupled model-and-optimizer framework for fitting the smooth, sharply structured functions common in physics to high accuracy while returning gradients, Hessians, Laplacians, and antiderivatives analytically: a deterministic segmented analytic surrogate trained with a tailored second-order Levenberg–Marquardt scheme. On the AI Feynman benchmark of 120 equations it beats standard Adam-trained networks by median factors of ~2100× (values), ~1400× (first derivatives), and ~780× (second derivatives), and runs up to ~44× faster than autograd for the derivatives. As an astrophysics demonstration, it reconstructs the vertical acceleration K_z(z) of the Galactic disk from a fitted stellar phase-space distribution function — a dynamical-mass / solar-neighbourhood dark-matter measurement requiring accurate derivatives of the fit.

Relevant to the emulator/surrogate and symbolic-regression threads of the ML-for-cosmology focus (the interest library already holds AI Feynman and Tenachi-style symbolic work): accurate, differentiable, cheap surrogates with analytic derivatives are exactly the substrate needed to replace expensive hydro/forest simulations and to do derivative-based inference on them.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Kastor: An efficient fine-tuning strategy for generative emulation of PDE simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06107)

Guillaume Couairon, Alexis Jacq, Yu-Han Wu et al.
**Primary category:** cs.LG | also: math.NA

Kastor adapts a deterministic physics foundation model into a fast generative surrogate for PDE simulations through three ingredients: a two-stage inference scheme pairing a large-stride causal auto-regressive model with a non-causal temporal super-resolution network to curb error accumulation; a "mean prediction regularization" objective that constrains the generative model to reproduce the deterministic distribution mean under null-noise conditioning (stabilising both functional-generative and diffusion emulators); and spatial gradient matching to improve power-spectrum fidelity. On the 2D datasets of The Well benchmark it cuts forecasting error by 42.9% on average versus a strong fine-tuning baseline.

Outside astronomy (the domains are fluid/weather PDEs), but notable for the forest-emulation angle in the interest file: error accumulation over long auto-regressive rollouts and matching the small-scale power spectrum are precisely the failure modes of neural emulators for hydrodynamic simulations, and the regularization + temporal-super-resolution recipe is borrowable for that setting.

*No figures extracted for 2608.06107 (the informative figures are domain-specific fluid-field rollouts; the 42.9% headline is conveyed in text).*

### [First-principles upper bounds on dark matter–electron scattering rates from condensed matter sum rules](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05282)

Bradford A. Barker, Jay Epstein, Luke James et al.
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: cond-mat.mtrl-sci, hep-ex

The authors derive rigorous, material-independent upper bounds on the achievable dark-matter–electron scattering rate in any detector target using condensed-matter sum rules (f-sum-rule / energy-loss-function constraints), rather than relying on a specific first-principles calculation of a given material's response. This turns the question "how good can a target possibly be?" into an analytic bound, letting one benchmark real candidate materials against the theoretical ceiling and flag where published DFT-based rate predictions may violate physical constraints.

Off the interest file's forest-based dark-matter focus (this is sub-GeV direct-detection theory, not small-scale structure), but held up here as a genuinely foundational move: replacing model-dependent material response with first-principles sum-rule bounds is the kind of rigorous methodological result worth knowing about even from an adjacent DM community.

*No figures extracted for 2608.05282 (the constraint/response plots are material-specific; the conceptual result — an analytic upper bound — needs no figure at a glance).*
