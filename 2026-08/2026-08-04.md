## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [Evidence for a thermal pressure deficit in galaxy groups from the tSZ effect and weak lensing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00164)

Jared Siegel, Alexandra Amon, Jenny E. Greene et al. (incl. E. Quataert, I. G. McCarthy)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.GA

Stacks the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) signal around DESI Luminous Red Galaxies using ACT single-channel maps, directly modeling dust and radio emission to isolate the tSZ from foregrounds, and pairs it with galaxy-galaxy lensing (GGL) to enable a like-with-like comparison against the FLAMINGO hydrodynamical simulations. Across halo masses $M_{500}=10^{13}$–$10^{14}\,M_\odot$ and $0.4<z<1$, the fiducial FLAMINGO run overpredicts the observed tSZ at $\lesssim3'$; even the strongest-feedback variant — which already matches the kSZ-inferred gas density of the same sample — overpredicts the thermal pressure. Because the strongest-feedback model reproduces the gas density, the residual factor-of-two pressure excess cannot be explained by additional gas expulsion alone, pointing instead to missing non-thermal pressure support or departures from hydrostatic equilibrium.

Relevant to the DESI/LSS secondary focus: this is a DESI-sample data product that quantifies where state-of-the-art feedback simulations mispredict the thermodynamic state of group-scale halos — the same simulations and gas physics that set baryonic systematics for LSS and cross-correlation cosmology.

### [Spin-1 Ultralight Dark Matter under Cosmological Scrutiny: Mass Constraints from CMB and Distance Probes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00331)

Guadalupe Ahumada Acuña, Tomas Ferreira Chase, Diana López Nacir, Rafael C. Nunes
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Derives cosmological constraints on spin-1 (vector) ultralight dark matter (VFDM) using Planck CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and SNIa, including a full derivation of the CMB temperature covariance with the off-diagonal anisotropic terms induced by the background vector field's preferred direction. Using the diagonal covariance plus CMB lensing, they obtain $\log_{10}(m_{\rm A}/\mathrm{eV})>-24.07$ (95% C.L.) in the pure-VFDM case, and in the mixed VFDM+CDM case a clear correlation between the DM fraction $f$ and $m_{\rm A}$ (smaller fractions allow lighter masses). They also compute the Bipolar Spherical Harmonic coefficients of the off-diagonal signal and show mixed VFDM+CDM models can produce anisotropy at or above Planck sensitivity, motivating dedicated searches.

Hits the small-scale/ultralight dark matter thread of the interest file (cf. the Rogers & Peiris ultralight-axion forest bounds): a fresh mass constraint on a wave-DM candidate using DESI DR2 BAO as a geometrical probe, and a template for the kind of bound the Lyman-α forest complements at higher masses.

### [Revisiting Metastable Dark Energy in Light of DESI DR2 BAO and DESI DR1 Full-Shape Measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01844)

Purba Mukherjee, Arman Shafieloo, Varun Sahni
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

Constrains three "radioactive-decay-law" metastable dark energy models — an effective decaying-DE component, DE decaying into dark matter, and DE decaying into dark radiation — using DESI DR2 BAO, SNIa, CMB, and, for the first time, the DESI DR1 full-shape (FS) clustering measurements. BAO+SNIa combinations show a mild ($\gtrsim2\sigma$) preference for positive decay rate $\Gamma/H_0$ (quintessence-like low-$z$ behavior), but adding Planck or P-ACT CMB pulls the constraints back to consistency with $\Gamma/H_0=0$. The FS data probe the growth sector and cleanly separate the interacting DM–DE model (Model 2, strongest clustering response) from the background-only decaying-DE and dark-radiation cases.

Squarely in the DESI/LSS secondary focus: one of the first analyses to fold the DESI DR1 full-shape power-spectrum multipoles into a beyond-$\Lambda$CDM inference, illustrating how the growth sector — not just BAO geometry — breaks degeneracies between dark-sector models.

*No figures extracted for 2608.01844 (results are constraint tables and multi-panel MCMC corner plots; the headline is the ≳2σ→null shift when CMB is added, which the text states directly).*

### [Boosting the optical depth to Thomson scattering with primordial black hole evaporation at high redshift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01919)

Isaac Sierra, Gabriel P. Lynch, Lloyd Knox et al. (incl. V. Poulin)
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Tests whether extra optical depth $\tau$ from exotic high-redshift (partial) reionization can resolve the mild $\Lambda$CDM tension between BAO and CMB — the "matter density deficit" and "CMB lensing excess." Using Hawking radiation from a monochromatic primordial-black-hole spectrum as a concrete injection source and retaining the high-$\ell$ $TT/TE/EE$ data plus the low-$\ell$ polarization reionization bump, they jointly vary the PBH history and reionization redshift. They find the CMB does not prefer the extra signal: the achievable boost is at most $\Delta\tau\simeq0.008$, far short of the $\Delta\tau\simeq0.03$ needed, because the stellar-reionization redshift and residual PBH signal at $\ell>30$ compensate, leaving the matter deficit and lensing excess essentially unrelieved.

Touches the reionization primary focus and the DESI-era BAO–CMB tension at once: it is a careful null result on whether the reionization optical depth has enough freedom to absorb the BAO-vs-CMB discrepancy, which matters for how much weight to put on that tension when interpreting DESI results.

*No figures extracted for 2608.01919 (the key outcome is a null result — extra τ cannot close the BAO–CMB gap — stated quantitatively in the text; the figures are ionization-history and posterior plots that add little at a glance).*

### [Kinetic backreaction cannot suppress axion quantum pressure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00472)

Kaleb Anderson, Savvas M. Koushiappas
**Primary category:** hep-ph | also: astro-ph.CO

Asks whether backreaction from field oscillations in string-motivated ultralight axions — whose non-canonical kinetic term is set by a modulus-dependent coupling function — could shrink the Jeans length and thereby enhance small-scale structure growth. Through an adiabatic-invariant analysis, confirmed by direct numerical integration, they prove a no-go: for any smooth positive coupling function, the oscillation-averaged backreaction always drives the coupling to larger values, strengthening the quantum pressure and growing the Jeans length with time. Kinetically coupled ultralight dark matter therefore cannot self-generate enhanced collapse or seed sub-Jeans structure.

Relevant to the fuzzy/ultralight dark-matter sub-branch of the forest program (free-streaming and the Jeans/quantum-pressure cutoff that the Lyman-α forest constrains): it closes off a loophole by which wave-DM might have evaded small-scale suppression, reinforcing that the forest's small-scale power cutoff remains a clean probe of the ultralight-DM mass.

*No figures extracted for 2608.00472 (analytic no-go theorem; the single figure is a numerical confirmation of the proof and adds nothing beyond the stated result).*

### [Discovery of the Distant, Ultra-Faint Milky Way Satellite Aquarius IV with the Vera C. Rubin Observatory Early Data Preview 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02601)

William Cerny, Aashay Pai, Alex Drlica-Wagner et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA

Reports the first ultra-faint Milky Way satellite discovered with Vera C. Rubin Observatory data, detected at $\sim8\sigma$ in Rubin Early Data Preview 2 photometry and independently confirmed at $\sim6\sigma$ in archival DECam imaging. Joint morphology-and-distance fitting yields a low-luminosity ($M_V=-1.9$), compact ($r_{1/2}=19$ pc) stellar system in the outer halo ($D_\odot=109$ kpc) with an ancient, metal-poor population ($\tau=13$ Gyr, $Z=0.0001$). Its properties match the smallest confirmed ultra-faint dwarf galaxies, though a globular-cluster classification is not yet ruled out pending spectroscopy.

In the small-scale-structure dark-matter thread: the abundance and central densities of ultra-faint dwarfs are a leading complement to the Lyman-α forest for constraining warm/fuzzy DM free-streaming, and this is the proof-of-concept that Rubin's depth is about to expand that census — worth tracking as the survey ramps up.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Measurement of the Hubble constant with high-energy neutrinos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00923)

Gonzalo Herrera, Nicholas Kamp, Carlos A. Argüelles
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: astro-ph.HE, hep-ph

Introduces — and reports the first observational realization of — a distance-ladder measurement of $H_0$ that uses high-energy astrophysical neutrinos from point sources as standardizable candles, exploiting the disk–corona correlation $L_\nu=\kappa\,L_X^\beta$ between neutrino and X-ray luminosity. Using 12 X-ray-selected Seyfert galaxies with significant per-source excesses in IceCube's 14-year public release, anchored by non-redshift distances to NGC 1068 (Cepheid + TRGB), they find $H_0=49^{+40}_{-30}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and a corona slope $\beta=0.67^{+0.16}_{-0.25}$ that disfavors the calorimetric limit $\beta=1$ at $\sim2\sigma$. The uncertainty is large but the measurement is entirely free of the electromagnetic propagation systematics (extinction, absorption, radiative transfer) that affect photon-based probes.

Held to a high bar for Tier 3: this is a genuinely new cosmographical probe — a neutrino distance ladder — that opens a systematics-orthogonal axis on the Hubble tension, notable to any observational cosmologist even though it is far from the forest/IGM program.

## Tier 4 — Meta-research about the field

### [Foundation Models for Astrophysics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02573)

Xiaosheng Zhao, Yuan-Sen Ting
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

A pedagogical chapter that develops the idea of foundation models for an astronomical reader from first principles, centered on the notion of a *transferable representation* rather than the fitted task — surveying architectures, self-supervised objectives, scaling laws, adaptation (linear probing, LoRA, fine-tuning), and cross-modal learning. Its through-line is a careful distinction between the machinery and the goal it serves: the presence of a transformer, a self-supervised objective, and large-scale pretraining does not by itself make a model a foundation model, since the defining property is that the learned representation *transfers* (few-shot and zero-shot). It offers a cautious reading of the current astronomy literature, noting that many models adopt the architecture while clear demonstrations of transfer across instruments, populations, and tasks remain rare — as expected, since robust transfer beyond language is still uncommon in vision and the physical sciences.

Directly relevant to the interest file's rising ML focus, where astronomical foundation models (AION-1, AstroCLIP, SpecPT, spectral tokenizers) are the newest additions: this is a level-headed framing of what these models are actually delivering versus what the architecture promises — useful for calibrating expectations before betting a spectroscopic-inference pipeline on one.
