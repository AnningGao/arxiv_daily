## Tier 1 — Highly relevant

### [A Sequentially-Valid Reanalysis of DESI's Dynamical Dark Energy Signal](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28918v1)

Jinyoung Kim, David F. Mota, Andrius Tamosiunas
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: stat.ME

The authors reanalyse the DESI DR1→DR2 BAO evidence for a time-evolving dark-energy equation of state using an *e-process* — a likelihood-ratio measure of evidence that stays valid under repeated "looks" as a survey releases data in stages, with no trials-factor penalty across releases. Under a pre-specified alternative aligned with the DR2-preferred direction the running evidence reaches M = 33.97 (crossing their illustrative 20 threshold), but removing the LRG2 redshift bin collapses it to M = 0.49, and allowing any of the seven bins to have produced the excess kills the rejection under a look-elsewhere correction. They conclude the signal is a fragile, single-bin, specification-dependent effect rather than robust evidence for dynamical dark energy, and recommend future DESI/Euclid/Roman/LSST releases report an anytime-valid e-value alongside conventional σ. To their knowledge this is the first application of anytime-valid sequential inference to a cosmological dataset.

### [A Simulation Based Inference Approach to Modelling of Type Ia Supernova Populations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28725v1)

B. Popovic, M. Grayling, M. O'Callaghan et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The paper presents *Stjörnumál*, a simulation-based inference (SBI) pipeline that replaces the MCMC core of the earlier Dust2Dust SN Ia modelling with Neural Posterior Estimation for parameters and Neural Ratio Estimation for model comparison. Applied to the DES 5-year sample, it recovers previously published dust-model parameters, tests seven models of SN Ia intrinsic scatter, and finds evidence (log₁₀ Bayes factor +1.9) against a two-population high-redshift model while noting current data cannot break degeneracies between R_V models. The authors argue neither frequentist χ² nor Bayesian model comparison alone suffices for model selection here, and release the code publicly. Results are shown to be robust to |Δw| < 0.10 in the assumed cosmology.

## Tier 2 — Adjacent / useful context

### [An optimal quadratic estimator for window-free cosmic shear power spectra](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29652v1)

Taisei Terawaki, Masahiro Takada
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

The authors derive a quadratic maximum-likelihood estimator for the *unwindowed* cosmic-shear power spectrum, using the full covariance (sample variance + shape noise) rather than the shape-noise-only inverse-variance weighting that makes the standard pseudo-C_ℓ estimator optimal only on small scales. Combining flat-sky FFTs, conjugate-gradient solves, and Monte-Carlo realizations of Gaussian ancillary fields, they make the otherwise expensive Fisher-matrix computation tractable. On ray-tracing simulations the method recovers the input E-mode spectrum with statistically optimal precision at all scales, tightening errors by 5–15% at ℓ ≲ 500 over pseudo-C_ℓ and strongly suppressing E→B leakage.

### [Emulating the nonlinear effects of modified gravity on the matter power spectrum for reconstruction](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29683v1)

Lanyang Yi, Kazuya Koyama, Qi Xiong, Gong-Bo Zhao
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

To make model-independent reconstruction of modified-gravity functions affordable, the authors train a CosmoPower neural-network emulator for the nonlinear correction R_MG(k,z) = P_MG^NL/P_MG^L (rather than the full spectrum), on ~9×10⁵ MGCAMB+ReACT samples. The emulator reproduces reference spectra to within 1.5% over the full k range for ΛCDM and moderate MG, holds that accuracy to k < 0.8 Mpc⁻¹ in the extreme-nonlinear case, and passes synthetic-data MCMC recovery tests. They demonstrate three uses: distinguishing models with degenerate linear spectra, feeding 3×2pt photometric likelihoods, and forecasting DESI+CSST constraints via PCA.

### [Searching for Population III stars with line intensity mapping cross-correlations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28713v1)

Sahil Hegde, Guochao Sun, Julian B. Muñoz, Steven R. Furlanetto
**Primary category:** astro-ph.GA | also: astro-ph.CO

The authors extend the analytic LIM framework Zeus21/oLIMpus to include Population III stars and their diagnostic emission lines (Hα, HeII), building a flexible model that maps a low-metallicity top-heavy IMF to line luminosities. They forecast the sensitivity of SPHEREx and next-generation intensity-mapping surveys to the Pop III star-formation efficiency and IMF shape, and run a Fisher analysis of SPHEREx×JWST synergies. They find SPHEREx can begin to constrain Pop III demographics but that next-generation instruments are needed to survey the full "classical" and "exotic" Pop III landscape.

### [Direct shear × kSZ correlation: controlling baryons without modeling galaxies](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29091v1)

Anoma Ganguly, Emmanuel Schaan, Elisabeth Krause, Tim Eifler
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Baryonic feedback suppresses the small-scale matter power spectrum and is now the leading systematic for weak-lensing surveys. The authors build a kSZ template from tomographic weak-lensing convergence maps and reconstructed radial-velocity fields (via the continuity equation), then cross-correlate it with the observed CMB temperature — directly probing the redistributed gas without tying the measurement to any galaxy sample. Forecasts give SNR ~5–15 for LSST-Y10/Roman combined with ACT/SO and ≳100 with CMB-HD, corresponding to few-percent (sub-percent for CMB-HD) constraints on the baryonic suppression of P(k).

### [Cosmological constraining power of the redshifts, heights, and angular clustering of weak gravitational lensing peaks](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29128v1)

Jeger C. Broxterman, Matthieu Schaller, Ian G. McCarthy et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO

Using a forthcoming dark-matter-only hypercube spanning a 10-dimensional cosmology (evolving dark energy, neutrino mass, decaying dark matter, running of the spectral index), the authors study three weak-lensing-peak statistics — height distribution, redshift distribution, and angular clustering of high-value peaks — in a Euclid-like Bayesian forecast. The three are complementary: the redshift distribution best constrains Ω_m, w₀, w_a; heights and clustering pin A_s; combining them also reaches Ω_b and h. All beat the shear two-point function individually, and combining scales roughly doubles the figure of merit.

### [Ab Initio Cosmological Simulations: From Inflation to Present-Day Structure Formation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28800v1)

Drew Jamieson, Angelo Caravano, Eiichiro Komatsu
**Primary category:** astro-ph.CO | also: gr-qc

The authors present what they describe as the first simulation spanning the entire history of the universe — a lattice code (ALEF) evolves inflaton quantum fluctuations deep in the inflationary epoch into primordial curvature perturbations, which then seed an N-body simulation run to z=0. Applied to axion-U(1) inflation, they find that nonlinear inflationary interactions enhance small-scale structure at high redshift, imprinting the matter power spectrum and halo mass function with signatures of a modified primordial power spectrum and non-Gaussianity. They argue high-redshift galaxy surveys, 21-cm observations, and line-intensity mapping are the natural probes of these signatures.

### [End-to-end differentiable retrieval of molecular spectra using hydrodynamics, chemistry, and radiative transfer](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28727v1)

T. Grassi, G. Vermariën, J. E. Pineda et al.
**Primary category:** astro-ph.IM

The authors build a fully differentiable (JAX) pipeline that couples a custom hydrodynamical code, a differentiable chemical network (Carbox), and a custom radiative-transfer code, so that all physical and chemical parameters can be optimized jointly by gradient descent against observed molecular line emission. On controlled synthetic data they recover hydrodynamical shock-model parameters directly from line spectra and even tune selected chemical reaction-rate coefficients, all while preserving the full time-dependent evolution.

## Tier 3 — Outside my area but notable

### [Gravity Probe-DM: The Gravitational Laboratory for Dark Matter](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28722v1)

Yu-Dai Tsai, Hayden R. Foote
**Primary category:** hep-ph

The authors propose detecting dark matter purely gravitationally within the Solar System. The Sun focuses incident unbound dark matter into an irreducible downstream "wake" whose structure encodes the local DM density, velocity distribution, and — for wave dark matter — the de Broglie scale. They show that precision inter-spacecraft ranging measures the differential wake acceleration (a wake tidal tensor for short baselines), and estimate that a realistic heliocentric two-spacecraft configuration could probe this gravitational-only signature, offering a detection channel independent of any non-gravitational DM coupling.
